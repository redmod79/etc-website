# Word Studies

## Question
What does the 'day and night' (hemeras kai nuktos) formula mean in Revelation? Does 'no rest day nor night' (Rev 14:11) describe eternal conscious experience, or the thoroughgoing nature of a judgment during its execution?

### G372 — anapausis

**Source:** `D:/Bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G372-anapausis.md`

**Occurrences:** 5

Rest, intermission, cessation — from anapauo (G373). All 5 NT occurrences in accusative singular (anapausin). Rev 4:8 and Rev 14:11 share the identical construction: ouk echousin anapausin hemeras kai nuktos. In Rev 4:8 the absence of rest is positive (ceaseless worship); in Rev 14:11 it is negative (no relief during judgment). The identical grammatical construction in the same book by the same author demands the same functional reading.

**Semantic categories:** spiritual rest offered by Christ, rest sought by unclean spirits, ceaseless activity (absence of rest), absence of rest as punishment

**Key verses:** Matt 11:29, Matt 12:43, Luke 11:24, Rev 4:8, Rev 14:11

**Relevance:** Core word — the 'no rest' formula in Rev 4:8 and 14:11. The identical construction in opposite contexts (worship vs. judgment) is the central evidence that 'no rest day nor night' describes quality of activity, not eternal duration.

### G373 — anapauo

**Source:** `D:/Bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G373-anapauo.md`

**Occurrences:** 12

Cognate verb of anapausis (G372) — to cause to rest, give rest, refresh. Rev 14:13 uses anapahsontai (future passive indicative 3P) — 'they shall rest from their labours.' This is the deliberate antithesis to Rev 14:11's 'no anapausis': same word family, adjacent verses, opposite outcomes.

**Semantic categories:** rest given by Christ, physical rest/sleep, refreshment by believers, eschatological rest of the dead

**Key verses:** Matt 11:28, Rev 6:11, Rev 14:13

**Relevance:** The verb form of the 'rest' concept. Rev 14:13 (blessed dead rest) vs. 14:11 (beast-worshippers have no rest) creates the anapausis antithesis — the sharpest evidence for what 'no rest' means.

### G2250 — hemera

**Source:** `D:/Bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2250-hemera.md`

**Occurrences:** 388

Day — 365 unique verses, 388 tokens. Critical collocation: nyx (G3571) co-occurs 28 times in 'day and night' merism expressing continuous action. Hebrew source: H3117 yowm with 1,485 LXX co-occurrences (score 25.24, PMI 3.46), the highest-confidence Hebrew-to-Greek bridge in day/time vocabulary.

**Semantic categories:** calendar/literal day, daylight hours vs night, eschatological day, last days, historical epoch

**Key verses:** Rev 4:8, Rev 7:15, Rev 12:10, Rev 14:11, Rev 20:10, Acts 26:7

**Relevance:** Core word — 'day' in the hemeras kai nuktos formula. The genitive singular form hemeras appears in all 5 Revelation 'day and night' passages.

### G3571 — nyx

**Source:** `D:/Bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G3571-nyx.md`

**Occurrences:** 65

Night — 65 NT occurrences across 13 books. The 'day-and-night merism in Revelation' is identified as a distinct semantic category: hemera kai nyx functioning as a merism for ceaseless, uninterrupted activity. Used for worship (4:8), service (7:15), accusation (12:10), torment (14:11, 20:10). The abolition of night in 21:25 and 22:5 signals the end of the present order. Hebrew source: H3915 layil with score 34.83 (PMI 6.64) — one of the most stable LXX mappings.

**Semantic categories:** literal temporal night, spiritual vigilance/devotion, spiritual darkness metaphor, day-and-night merism in Revelation, night watches

**Key verses:** Rev 4:8, Rev 7:15, Rev 12:10, Rev 14:11, Rev 20:10, Rev 21:25, Rev 22:5

**Relevance:** Core word — 'night' in the hemeras kai nuktos formula. The genitive singular form nuktos appears in all 5 Revelation passages. The abolition of nyx in Rev 22:5 is the eschatological endpoint.

### G305 — anabaino

**Source:** `D:/Bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G305-anabaínō.md`

**Occurrences:** 86

To go up, ascend — from ana (up) + baino (to walk). 13 Revelation occurrences including Rev 14:11 (smoke of torment ascending) and Rev 19:3 (Babylon's smoke ascending). Rev 14:11 uses present tense anabainei — gnomic present, timeless assertion, same kind of present as Rev 4:8.

**Semantic categories:** physical ascent to Jerusalem, Christological ascension, apocalyptic ascent and rising, natural growth

**Key verses:** Rev 8:4, Rev 14:11, Rev 19:3

**Relevance:** The verb for smoke 'ascending' in Rev 14:11. The gnomic present tense makes this a timeless assertion about smoke as testimony, not a report of ongoing burning.

### G928 — basanizo

**Source:** `D:/Bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G928-basanizo.md`

**Occurrences:** 12

To torment, test, vex — from basanos (touchstone). 12 NT occurrences: 7 non-judgment (illness, waves tossing a ship, toiling at rowing, moral distress), 5 in Revelation's apocalyptic framework. The word does not inherently require eternal duration (Rev 9:5 has a 5-month limit). Matt 14:24 applies it to waves tossing a ship (no conscious subject required).

**Semantic categories:** physical suffering, demonic encounters, moral distress, eschatological torment

**Key verses:** Matt 8:29, Matt 14:24, 2 Pet 2:8, Rev 9:5, Rev 14:10, Rev 20:10

**Relevance:** The torment verb in Rev 14:10 and 20:10. Genre-restricted to Revelation for judgment contexts. The word's semantic range includes non-punitive distress and time-bounded torment.

### G929 — basanismos

**Source:** `D:/Bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G929-basanismos.md`

**Occurrences:** 6

Torment (noun) — all 6 occurrences exclusively in Revelation. Rev 14:11 ('smoke of their torment'), Rev 9:5 (five-month limited torment), Rev 18:7,10,15 (Babylon's torment). Time-bounded in Rev 9:5; applied to destroyed entity in Rev 18.

**Semantic categories:** demonic/supernatural torment, eschatological judgment torment, commercial/imperial torment

**Key verses:** Rev 9:5, Rev 14:11, Rev 18:7, Rev 18:10, Rev 18:15

**Relevance:** The torment noun in Rev 14:11. Genre-restricted to Revelation. 4 of 6 occurrences are time-bounded or applied to entities that no longer exist.

### G2586 — kapnos

**Source:** `D:/Bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2586-kapnos.md`

Used for incense smoke (Rev 8:4), abyss smoke (Rev 9:2), judgment smoke (Rev 14:11, 19:3), Babylon's burning smoke (Rev 18:9,18), and theophanic smoke (Rev 15:8). Hebrew source: ashan (H6227) with score 30.28 (PMI 9.95)

**Semantic categories:** prophetic sign, incense smoke ascending, smoke from the abyss, judgment instruments, eternal consequence, divine glory, Babylon's destruction

**Key verses:** Rev 8:4, Rev 14:11, Rev 19:3, Rev 18:9

**Relevance:** The smoke noun in Rev 14:11. Smoke in biblical thought is testimony of completed transformation — evidence that fire has done its work.

### G2725 — kategoros

**Source:** `D:/Bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2725-kategoros.md`

**Occurrences:** 7

Accuser, formal legal prosecutor — from kata (against) + agora (assembly). 7 NT occurrences: 5 in Acts (Roman judicial accusers), 1 in Revelation (Satan as cosmic accuser, Rev 12:10), 1 in John 8:10 (disputed text). Rev 12:10 is the theological climax: Satan is 'the accuser of our brethren' whose 'day and night' accusation is terminated when he is 'cast down.'

**Semantic categories:** Roman judicial accusers, Satan as cosmic accuser, confrontation of earthly accusers

**Key verses:** Rev 12:10, Acts 23:30, Acts 25:16

**Relevance:** The accuser noun in Rev 12:10. The accuser's 'day and night' activity was terminated — proving the formula describes bounded activity.

### G2723 — kategoreo

**Source:** `D:/Bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2723-katēgoréō.md`

**Occurrences:** 24

To accuse, bring formal charges — the verb cognate of kategoros (G2725). 24 NT occurrences. In Rev 12:10, the present active participle kategoron describes the ongoing character of Satan's accusation 'day and night.' The participle is present tense (continuous aspect) but the activity is terminated by the aorist eblethe (was cast down).

**Semantic categories:** legal/judicial accusation, hostile surveillance, spiritual/cosmic accusation

**Key verses:** Rev 12:10, Matt 27:12, Acts 24:2

**Relevance:** The accusation verb in Rev 12:10. Present participle + aorist termination = bounded 'day and night' activity.

### G737 — arti

**Source:** `D:/Bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G737-árti.md`

**Occurrences:** 36

Now, at this moment, this instant — adverb marking the present moment with emphasis on immediacy. In Rev 12:10, arti marks the cosmic turning point: 'NOW is come salvation... for the accuser is cast down.' Functions as a temporal boundary marker declaring that the 'day and night' accusation has ended at this very instant.

**Semantic categories:** punctiliar present, now-then contrast, epoch-marking transition, forward-looking present

**Key verses:** Rev 12:10, Matt 3:15, 1 Cor 13:12

**Relevance:** The temporal boundary marker in Rev 12:10 that terminates the 'day and night' accusation. Proves the formula can describe bounded activity.

### G166 — aionios

**Source:** `D:/Bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G166-aionios.md`

**Occurrences:** 71

Eternal, everlasting — adjective from aion (G165). 71 NT occurrences. ~43 for 'eternal life.' Applied to God's attributes (genuinely eternal), to believers' inheritance (genuinely eternal), and to judgment (3 uses in Revelation — Rev 14:6,11; subject-determined meaning). Hebrew source: owlam (H5769) with PMI 26.16.

**Semantic categories:** eternal life, eternal judgment, divine attributes, eternal realities, eternal purpose

**Key verses:** John 3:16, Matt 25:41, Matt 25:46, Rev 14:6

**Relevance:** The 'ages of ages' formula co-occurs with 'day and night' in Rev 14:11 and 20:10. Subject-determined meaning: applied to eternal God = genuinely eternal; applied to judgment on non-eternal subjects = permanent result.

### G4352 — proskyneo

**Source:** `D:/Bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G4352-proskyneo.md`

**Occurrences:** 65

To worship, prostrate — 54 unique verses, 65 tokens. Revelation dominates with 22 occurrences (41%). Includes both proper worship (throne room) and false worship (beast/dragon). Rev 14:11's 'who worship the beast' identifies the subjects of the 'no rest day nor night' formula.

**Semantic categories:** prostration before Jesus, recognition of divine sonship, true worship discourse, exclusive worship command, heavenly throne room worship, eschatological false worship

**Key verses:** Rev 4:10, Rev 13:4, Rev 14:9, Rev 14:11

**Relevance:** The worship verb identifying the subjects of Rev 14:11 — those who worship the beast. False worship is the sin that brings the 'no rest day nor night' judgment.

### H5769 — owlam

**Source:** `D:/Bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H5769-owlam.md`

**Occurrences:** 432

Forever, eternity, perpetual — 406 unique verses (432 tokens) across 34 books. Core meaning: 'the vanishing point' — time that recedes beyond the reach of human perception. Subject-determined: applied to God = genuinely eternal; applied to slave's lifetime = 'for ever' (bounded); applied to Ai's destruction = permanent desolation. Isa 34:10 uses owlam for Edom's smoke — but Edom is not burning today.

**Semantic categories:** eternal duration of God, everlasting covenant, perpetual cultic ordinances, immemorial past, eschatological age to come, permanent judgment/desolation

**Key verses:** Psa 90:2, Isa 34:10, Josh 8:28, Dan 12:2

**Relevance:** The Hebrew 'forever' behind Rev 14:11's 'for ever and ever.' Subject-determined: Isa 34:10's 'for ever' smoke comes from a fire that is demonstrably out (animals inhabit the ruins, Isa 34:11-17).

### H3915 — layil

**Source:** `D:/Bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H3915-layil.md`

**Occurrences:** 241

Night — 223 unique verses (241 tokens) across 30 books. First occurrence: Gen 1:5. Nearly all renderings contain 'night.' Forms the OT half of the yom v'laylah merism. LXX bridge to nyx (G3571) with 189 co-occurrences (score 34.83, PMI 6.64) — one of the most stable in the LXX.

**Semantic categories:** creation ordinance, day/night merism for totality, night of divine encounter, night as adversity

**Key verses:** Gen 1:5, Gen 8:22, Isa 34:10, Psa 32:4

**Relevance:** The Hebrew 'night' behind the OT yom v'laylah merism. The same pattern crosses into the NT via the LXX bridge layil → nyx.

### H6227 — ashan

**Source:** `D:/Bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H6227-ashan.md`

**Occurrences:** 25

Smoke — 25 occurrences across 13 OT books. Five semantic categories: theophanic smoke (Gen 15:17, Exo 19:18), divine wrath/judgment (Isa 34:10, 2 Sam 22:9), transience/vanishing (Psa 37:20, Psa 102:3), military signal (Josh 8:20), and eschatological sign (Joel 2:30). LXX maps to kapnos (G2586) with score 30.28 (PMI 9.95)

**Semantic categories:** theophanic smoke, divine wrath and judgment, transience and vanishing, military signal and destruction, eschatological sign

**Key verses:** Isa 34:10, Gen 19:28, Psa 37:20, Joel 2:30

**Relevance:** The Hebrew smoke word behind Rev 14:11. In the Hebrew mind, smoke = visible trace of transformation — evidence that fire has done its work.

### G3000 — latreuo

**Source:** `D:/Bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G3000-latreuo.md`

**Occurrences:** 21

To serve/worship (God) — from latris (hired menial). 21 NT occurrences across 8 books. In Rev 7:15, the great multitude latreuousin (PAI-3P, 'serve') God hemeras kai nuktos ('day and night') en to nao autou ('in his temple'). The verb designates exclusive, whole-life religious service. Distinguished from proskyneo (reverence act) in Matt 4:10. Acts 26:7 uses latreuo for the twelve tribes 'instantly serving God day and night.'

**Semantic categories:** exclusive devotion to God, covenant service, personal devotion, perverted latreuo, old covenant cultic service

**Key verses:** Matt 4:10, Rev 7:15, Acts 26:7, Heb 8:5

**Relevance:** The service/worship verb in Rev 7:15. The great multitude's 'day and night' service (latreuo) in God's temple parallels Rev 4:8's 'day and night' worship — both positive uses of the formula.

### G930 — basanistes

**Source:** `D:/Bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G930-basanistēs.md`

**Occurrences:** 1

Tormentor/torturer — agent noun from basanizo (G928). Hapax legomenon: only Matt 18:34. The master delivers the unforgiving servant 'to the tormentors' until the debt is paid. The basanistes is the agent who carries out punishment.

**Semantic categories:** punitive authority/judicial torment

**Key verses:** Matt 18:34

**Relevance:** Part of the basanizo word family. The parable's use shows torment as bounded — 'till he should pay all that was due.'

### G931 — basanos

**Source:** `D:/Bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G931-basanos.md`

**Occurrences:** 3

Touchstone, then torture/torment — 3 NT occurrences. Matt 4:24 (physical suffering), Luke 16:23,28 (torment in Hades parable). Root of the basanizo word family. Originally a stone for testing metal purity, extended to testing by torture, then to torment generally.

**Semantic categories:** physical suffering/disease, torment in afterlife parable, testing/examination (etymological)

**Key verses:** Matt 4:24, Luke 16:23

**Relevance:** Root noun of the basanizo family. The etymological origin as 'touchstone' (testing) illuminates the word family's semantic range.
