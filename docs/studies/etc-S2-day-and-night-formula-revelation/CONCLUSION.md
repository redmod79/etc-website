# Conclusion: The "Day and Night" Formula in Revelation

## Question
What does the "day and night" (hemeras kai nuktos) formula mean in Revelation? Does "no rest day nor night" (Rev 14:11) describe eternal conscious experience, or the thoroughgoing nature of a judgment during its execution?

## Summary Answer
The hemeras kai nuktos ("day and night") formula in Revelation is a genitive-of-time construction that characterizes the thoroughgoing, uninterrupted quality of an activity during its occurrence -- not its eternal duration. All five Revelation occurrences use the identical genitive case, the same construction appears in Rev 4:8 where no one reads it as eternal conscious torment of the living creatures, Rev 12:10 proves the formula can describe activity that has definitively ended, and Rev 22:5 dissolves the day/night temporal framework entirely in the new creation. The "no rest day nor night" of Rev 14:11 describes the thoroughgoing nature of a judgment during its execution -- complete, without relief or intermission -- not eternal conscious experience.

## Key Verses

> **The Ceaseless Worship Formula (Revelation 4:8)** And the four beasts had each of them six wings about him; and they were full of eyes within: and they rest not day and night, saying, Holy, holy, holy, Lord God Almighty, which was, and is, and is to come.

> **The Perpetual Temple Service (Revelation 7:15)** Therefore are they before the throne of God, and serve him day and night in his temple: and he that sitteth on the throne shall dwell among them.

> **The Terminated Accusation (Revelation 12:10)** And I heard a loud voice saying in heaven, Now is come salvation, and strength, and the kingdom of our God, and the power of his Christ: for the accuser of our brethren is cast down, which accused them before our God day and night.

> **The Crux Text (Revelation 14:11)** And the smoke of their torment ascendeth up for ever and ever: and they have no rest day nor night, who worship the beast and his image, and whosoever receiveth the mark of his name.

> **The Anapausis Antithesis (Revelation 14:13)** And I heard a voice from heaven saying unto me, Write, Blessed are the dead which die in the Lord from henceforth: Yea, saith the Spirit, that they may rest from their labours; and their works do follow them.

> **The Lake of Fire Formula (Revelation 20:10)** And the devil that deceived them was cast into the lake of fire and brimstone, where the beast and the false prophet are, and shall be tormented day and night for ever and ever.

> **The Dissolution of Night (Revelation 22:5)** And there shall be no night there; and they need no candle, neither light of the sun; for the Lord God giveth them light: and they shall reign for ever and ever.

## Analysis

### 1. The Greek Grammar: Genitive of Time, Not Accusative of Extent

The grammatical foundation of this study is the case form used in all five Revelation "day and night" passages. The Greek parser confirms that every occurrence uses hemeras (G2250, N-GSF, genitive singular feminine) kai nuktos (G3571, N-GSF, genitive singular feminine). The genitive of time in Greek expresses "time within which" or "time during which." It characterizes the temporal backdrop of an action -- what kind of time-frame the activity fills. It answers the question "during what kind of time?" rather than "for how long?"

Greek has a different tool for measuring duration: the accusative of extent of time, which answers "for how long?" Luke demonstrates this distinction in Luke 2:37, where Anna serves God nykta (N-ASF, accusative singular) kai hemeran (N-ASF, accusative singular). Luke uses the accusative to measure the extent of Anna's service. Acts 26:7 similarly uses the accusative (nykta kai hemeran) for the twelve tribes serving God "instantly day and night."

The diagnostic contrast is exact. The same words -- nyx and hemera -- change grammatical function by case. Accusative = how long. Genitive = what kind of time. John uses the genitive in all five Revelation passages, consistently characterizing the quality of the activity rather than measuring its duration. This is not an incidental detail. It is a grammatical choice available to a Greek author who wants to express either quality or duration, and John consistently chooses the quality-characterizing construction.

### 2. The Five Occurrences as a Unified Pattern

Revelation contains exactly five hemeras kai nuktos passages. Mapped together, they form a coherent literary pattern:

**Rev 4:8** -- the living creatures worship God "day and night" with the trishagion ("Holy, holy, holy"). This is positive, continuous praise. No interpreter reads this as the creatures being consciously tormented by their worship. The "no rest" (ouk echousin anapausin) describes the thoroughgoing quality of their devotion -- they fill every part of the day-night cycle with worship.

**Rev 7:15** -- the great multitude serves God "day and night in his temple" (latreuousin auto hemeras kai nuktos en to nao autou). This is the second positive use. The verb latreuo (G3000) designates sacral, whole-life worship. The setting is God's inner temple (naos, G3485). The "day and night" describes the quality of their access -- perpetual, uninterrupted, complete.

**Rev 12:10** -- Satan "accused them before our God day and night" (kategoron autous... hemeras kai nuktos). This is the negative use that resolves the entire question. The present participle kategoron (G2723) describes the continuous character of Satan's accusation during the period it was occurring. But the aorist eblethe (G906, "was cast," aorist passive indicative) marks the decisive end of that activity. The adverb arti (G737, "now, at this very moment") declares the turning point: "NOW is come salvation... for the accuser is cast down."

Satan's "day and night" accusation was not paused, rescheduled, or reduced in intensity. It was permanently terminated. The heavenly court has changed. The accuser has been removed. The "day and night" activity is over. This single verse proves that hemeras kai nuktos can describe an activity that is continuous and thoroughgoing during its occurrence and yet has a definite, final endpoint. If "day and night" meant "forever without end," Satan would still be accusing. He is not.

**Rev 14:11** -- the beast-worshippers "have no rest day nor night" (ouk echousin anapausin hemeras kai nuktos). The crux text, using the identical construction as Rev 4:8.

**Rev 20:10** -- the devil, beast, and false prophet "shall be tormented day and night for ever and ever" (basanisthesontai hemeras kai nuktos eis tous aionas ton aionon). All three subjects are non-human or symbolic: the devil (a supernatural spirit being), the beast (a seven-headed composite entity), and the false prophet (the two-horned earth beast of Rev 13:11-17). Humans in the same passage receive different vocabulary -- "devoured" (Rev 20:9, katephagen, aorist active), "cast into the lake of fire" (Rev 20:15), and "the second death" (Rev 20:14). Revelation maintains a consistent vocabulary distinction between non-human/symbolic entities (torment language) and human beings (death/destruction language).

### 3. The Rev 4:8 / Rev 14:11 Parallel -- The Same Grammar Demands the Same Reading

The identical grammatical construction in Rev 4:8 and Rev 14:11 is the most direct evidence available. The word study for anapausis (G372) confirms that the word appears only 5 times in the NT, and the two Revelation uses share exact grammatical form:

- **Rev 4:8:** anapausin ouk echousin hemeras kai nuktos
- **Rev 14:11:** ouk echousin anapausin hemeras kai nuktos

Same verb (echousin, present active indicative 3P). Same noun (anapausin, accusative singular feminine). Same negation (ouk). Same genitive of time (hemeras kai nuktos, both genitive singular feminine). Same book. Same author.

In 4:8, the absence of rest is positive -- the creatures worship without pause. In 14:11, the absence of rest is negative -- the beast-worshippers have no relief from judgment. The function of the formula is the same in both: it describes the quality and completeness of the activity. The creatures' worship fills the entire day-night cycle without interruption. The beast-worshippers' judgment fills the entire day-night cycle without relief.

If someone insists that "no rest day nor night" in 14:11 means eternal conscious experience, they must explain why the identical phrase in 4:8 does not mean eternal conscious existence of the living creatures in a state of restless distress. The grammar is the same. The meaning must be the same unless compelling contextual evidence overrides the parallel -- and no such evidence exists.

### 4. Rev 12:10 -- The Decisive Counter-Example

Revelation 12:10 is the single most powerful argument in this study. Satan "accused them before our God day and night." The participle kategoron is present tense, describing the ongoing character of the accusation. The genitive hemeras kai nuktos characterizes the accusation as continuous and relentless. And then -- arti egeneto he soteria -- "NOW is come salvation." The aorist eblethe ("was cast down") marks the completed, irreversible expulsion of the accuser.

This is not a pause. It is not an interruption. The accusatory ministry is permanently ended. The OT background (Job 1:6-12, Zech 3:1-2) shows Satan in the role of courtroom prosecutor, standing before God and bringing charges against the saints. Rev 12:10 declares that this "day and night" prosecutorial activity has been terminated at Christ's victory.

The implications are straightforward: if hemeras kai nuktos described the *eternal nature* of an activity, Satan would still be accusing. The formula describes the *character* of the activity during its occurrence (unceasing, relentless, thoroughgoing) and says nothing about its guaranteed eternal continuation. The "day and night" accusing was absolute -- without pause, without exception, filling the entire temporal framework -- and it ended.

### 5. Rev 22:5 and 21:25 -- The Temporal Framework Dissolves

Rev 22:5 declares: nyx ouk estai eti -- "night shall not be any more." Rev 21:25 confirms: nyx gar ouk estai ekei -- "for night shall not be there." The day/night framework that God established in Gen 1:5 ("God called the light Day, and the darkness he called Night") is abolished in the new creation.

This dissolution has direct consequences for the "day and night" formula. If "night" no longer exists in the eternal state, then activities described as occurring "day and night" cannot be literal temporal descriptions of eternal realities. The saints' "day and night" service (Rev 7:15) continues in eternity -- but not because night continues. The service is perpetual in character, described using the "day and night" merism because that is the present-age framework within which John writes. The judgment activities described as "day and night" (Rev 14:11, 20:10) operate within a framework that is itself temporary.

The bookend is complete: Gen 1:5 creates the day/night distinction. Rev 22:5 abolishes it. Everything described as "day and night" within Revelation operates inside this created framework that has a beginning and an end.

### 6. The Anapausis Antithesis -- Rev 14:11 vs. Rev 14:13

John places the sharpest possible contrast two verses apart. In 14:11, the beast-worshippers have no anapausis (G372, rest/intermission/cessation). In 14:13, the blessed dead anapahsontai (G373, future passive of anapauo, the cognate verb) ek ton kopon auton -- "they shall rest from their labours."

Same word family: ana (up, again) + pauo (to cease, stop). Noun in 14:11, cognate verb in 14:13. Adjacent verses. Opposite groups. Opposite outcomes. This is a deliberate literary antithesis -- John places the two side by side so that each defines the other.

If the blessed dead's "rest" means cessation of labor, the end of struggle, entry into reward -- and it does, since their "works follow them" -- then the beast-worshippers' "no rest" means the absence of this relief. They receive no intermission, no pause, no cessation from the judgment's pressure. The antithesis defines "no rest" as the opposite of "rest from labours" -- not as eternal conscious existence, but as thoroughgoing judgment without relief.

Jesus himself offers anapausis in Matt 11:28-29: "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest (anapausin). Take my yoke upon you... and ye shall find rest (anapausin) unto your souls." If Christ's rest is spiritual refreshment and the cessation of burden, then the denial of rest in Rev 14:11 is the absence of this refreshment -- no relief from the judgment that is coming.

### 7. The Broader NT Pattern -- Every "Day and Night" Is Qualitative

Outside Revelation, the NT uses the "day and night" / "night and day" formula in at least ten passages. Every one describes the quality or intensity of an activity:

Paul labored "night and day" (nuktos kai hemeras, genitive, 1 Thess 2:9; 2 Thess 3:8) -- working hard, not literally without sleeping. The widow "continueth in supplications and prayers night and day" (1 Tim 5:5, genitive) -- devout, not literally non-stop prayer. Paul remembers Timothy "in my prayers night and day" (2 Tim 1:3, genitive) -- faithful, persistent remembrance. God's elect "cry day and night unto him" (Luke 18:7, genitive) -- fervent, persistent prayer. The enemies "watched the gates day and night to kill" Paul (Acts 9:24, genitive) -- thorough surveillance. Paul "ceased not to warn every one night and day with tears" over three years (Acts 20:31, genitive) -- devoted pastoral vigilance.

Even the strongest possible case -- Mark 5:5, where the demoniac is "always, night and day" (dia pantos nuktos kai hemeras) crying and cutting himself -- is qualitative. The demoniac did sleep, eat, and pause. The formula describes the relentless, pervasive character of his torment. And his "day and night" torment ended when Jesus delivered him.

Luke 2:37 and Acts 26:7 use the accusative (measuring extent) rather than the genitive (characterizing quality). Even these accusative uses do not claim literal unbroken activity -- Anna did not pray without a single pause for 84 years. The accusative measures the span; the genitive characterizes the quality. Revelation uses the genitive every time.

### 8. The OT Merism -- The Foundation John Inherited

The Hebrew yom v'laylah / yomam va-laylah merism provides the OT foundation:

Gen 8:22: "Day and night shall not cease" -- the created order expressed as merism. Josh 1:8 / Psa 1:2: "Meditate therein day and night" -- devotional completeness, not literal 24-hour meditation. Psa 32:4: "Day and night thy hand was heavy upon me" -- thoroughgoing divine conviction that ended when David confessed (Psa 32:5). Isa 62:6: Watchmen who "shall never hold their peace day nor night" -- continuous vigilance. Neh 1:6: Praying "day and night" -- intensity of intercession. Jer 14:17: Tears "night and day" -- depth of grief. 1 Ki 8:29: God's eyes "open toward this house night and day" -- continuous divine attention.

The OT pattern is uniform: "day and night" expresses completeness, thoroughness, and pervasive quality. It is a merism -- a figure naming two poles to express totality, like "heaven and earth" meaning "everything" or "searched high and low" meaning "searched everywhere." The formula crosses into the NT via the LXX bridge (yowm H3117 to hemera G2250, PMI 3.46; layil H3915 to nyx G3571, PMI 6.64 -- among the most stable mappings in the LXX).

### 9. Isaiah 34:10-15 -- The OT Source That Resolves the Question

The most telling OT evidence is Isa 34:10, the direct source for Rev 14:11. Both share the same three elements: (1) fire not quenched "night nor day," (2) smoke ascending "for ever," (3) permanent desolation. The Hebrew reads: laylah ve-yomam lo tikhbeh ("night nor day it shall not be quenched"); le-owlam ya'aleh ashana ("forever its smoke shall ascend").

But Isaiah 34:11-15 describes the aftermath. Cormorants and bitterns possess the land. Owls and ravens dwell in it. Thorns grow in the palaces. Wild beasts meet in the ruins. The screech owl rests there and finds a place of rest. Owls make nests, lay eggs, hatch young. Vultures gather with their mates.

Animals cannot inhabit a place that is still on fire. Nesting birds cannot raise young in burning ruins. Thorns cannot grow in active flames. The fire that was "not quenched night nor day" is demonstrably out. The smoke that "shall go up for ever" (le-owlam) is the permanent testimony of completed destruction, not ongoing burning. The subject of owlam (H5769) determines its scope: applied to God, it means genuinely eternal; applied to Edom's fire, it means a permanent result -- destruction that cannot be reversed, not fire that burns without end.

This is the imagery John draws on in Rev 14:11. If the OT source describes a fire that has completed its work, the NT passage that echoes it should be read the same way.

### 10. The Convergence of Evidence

Eight independent lines of evidence converge on the same conclusion:

(1) The genitive of time characterizes quality, not extent. (2) Rev 4:8's identical formula is read as quality by everyone. (3) Rev 12:10 proves "day and night" can describe terminated activity. (4) Rev 7:15's "day and night" service occurs where "there shall be no night." (5) Every NT "day and night" outside Revelation is qualitative. (6) Every OT yom v'laylah is qualitative. (7) Isa 34:10's same formula describes a fire that is out. (8) The anapausis antithesis (14:11 vs. 14:13) defines "no rest" as absence of relief, not eternal duration.

No single argument is decisive in isolation. Together, they form an overwhelming case. The formula means the same thing everywhere it appears: thoroughgoing, uninterrupted activity filling the entire day-night framework. It describes the character of the action, not its guaranteed eternal continuation.

## Word Studies

**Anapausis (G372):** Rest, intermission, cessation. 5 NT occurrences. The identical construction ouk echousin anapausin hemeras kai nuktos in Rev 4:8 and 14:11 is the central grammatical parallel. In 4:8 it describes positive ceaseless worship; in 14:11 it describes negative judgment without relief. Same grammar, same function.

**Anapauo (G373):** To cause to rest, give rest. 12 NT occurrences. The cognate verb creates the antithesis in Rev 14:13 -- the blessed dead "rest from their labours," directly contrasting the beast-worshippers' "no rest" two verses earlier.

**Hemera (G2250) and Nyx (G3571):** Day (388 tokens) and night (65 occurrences). The paired merism appears 28 times in the NT, expressing continuous activity. The genitive forms hemeras and nuktos appear in all five Revelation passages. The abolition of nyx in Rev 22:5 dissolves the framework.

**Basanizo (G928):** To torment. 12 NT occurrences. Seven non-judgment uses (illness, waves, rowing, moral distress). Five judgment uses, all in Revelation's apocalyptic framework. The word does not inherently require eternal duration -- Rev 9:5 limits it to five months.

**Basanismos (G929):** Torment (noun). 6 occurrences, exclusively in Revelation. Four are time-bounded or applied to destroyed entities.

**Kapnos (G2586) and Ashan (H6227):** Greek smoke (13 tokens) and Hebrew smoke (25 occurrences). Smoke in biblical thought is the visible trace of completed transformation -- evidence that fire has done its work. The verb anabaino (G305) is used for both worship smoke (Rev 8:4) and judgment smoke (Rev 14:11) ascending before God.

**Owlam (H5769):** Forever, eternity. 432 OT tokens. Subject-determined: applied to God = genuinely eternal; applied to Edom's smoke = permanent result of completed destruction.

**Kategoreo (G2723):** To accuse, 24 occurrences. The related noun kategoros (G2725, accuser) appears in Rev 12:10 alongside the verb. Rev 12:10 is the theological climax: the accuser's "day and night" prosecutorial role is permanently terminated by Christ's victory.

**Arti (G737):** Now, at this moment. 36 occurrences. In Rev 12:10, it marks the temporal boundary where "day and night" accusation ends -- the cosmic turning point.

**Latreuo (G3000):** To serve, worship. 21 occurrences. The sacral service verb in Rev 7:15 describes positive "day and night" temple worship, paralleling Rev 4:8.

## Difficult Passages

### Rev 20:10 -- "Tormented Day and Night For Ever and Ever"
The combination of hemeras kai nuktos with eis tous aionas ton aionon ("for ever and ever") appears to press toward eternal duration. This is the strongest passage for the eternal-conscious-torment reading.

However, several factors qualify this: (1) All three subjects (devil, beast, false prophet) are non-human/symbolic. No literal human being is the subject of this formula. (2) The "ages of ages" formula is subject-determined -- applied to God it means genuinely eternal; applied to judgment on non-eternal subjects it means permanent result (cf. Isa 34:10). (3) Ezek 28:18-19 describes the same Satan-figure as becoming "ashes upon the earth" and ceasing to exist: "never shalt thou be any more." The didactic oracle governs the apocalyptic vision. (4) Humans in the same passage receive different vocabulary: "devoured" (v.9), "second death" (vv.14-15). (5) The genitive of time still functions as a quality-characterizer, and the Rev 12:10 counter-example still proves the formula can describe terminated activity.

### Matt 25:41, 46 -- "Everlasting Fire" and "Everlasting Punishment"
Though not "day and night" passages, these are frequently connected to Rev 14:11 in theological discussion. "Everlasting fire" and "everlasting punishment" use aionios (G166), the adjective form. The Hebrews pattern governs: "eternal redemption" is redemption obtained once with a permanent result, "eternal judgment" is judgment rendered once with a permanent result. "Everlasting punishment" is punishment with a permanent result -- not punishment that is consciously experienced forever. The result (death, the second death) is what is everlasting, not the process.

### Mark 5:5 -- The Strongest Negative "Day and Night"
The demoniac's "always, night and day" (dia pantos nuktos kai hemeras) crying and self-harm is the most intense "day and night" formula outside Revelation, reinforced by dia pantos. Even this is qualitative -- the demoniac was not literally awake and active every second. He slept. He paused. The formula describes the pervasive, relentless character of his distress. And his "day and night" torment ended when Jesus cast out the demons. The activity was thoroughgoing during its occurrence and reached a definite endpoint.

### Rev 21:4 -- "No More Pain" in the Eschatological State
If the wicked are consciously tormented forever, then "there shall be no more pain" (Rev 21:4) is not universally true -- pain exists eternally somewhere in the universe. The eschatological vision of Revelation envisions complete resolution: no death, no sorrow, no crying, no pain. "The former things are passed away." If eternal conscious torment continues, the former things have not fully passed away. The eschatological endpoint requires that judgment reaches completion and pain ceases everywhere.

## Conclusion

The "day and night" formula in Revelation is a genitive-of-time construction inherited from the OT yom v'laylah merism, carried through the LXX into the NT, and deployed by John as a quality-marker for continuous, uninterrupted activity. It appears five times in Revelation, always in the genitive case, always characterizing the thoroughgoing nature of the activity it describes rather than measuring its duration.

The evidence converges from grammar, internal Revelation parallels, broader NT usage, OT source imagery, and literary structure. The genitive of time characterizes quality, not extent (Rev 4:8, 7:15, 12:10, 14:11, 20:10 all use the genitive; Luke 2:37 and Acts 26:7 use the accusative for duration). Rev 4:8's identical construction is universally read as describing the quality of worship. Rev 12:10 proves the formula can describe an activity that has been permanently terminated. Rev 22:5 dissolves the day/night temporal framework itself. The anapausis antithesis (Rev 14:11 vs. 14:13) defines "no rest" by contrast with "rest from labours." Isa 34:10-15 uses the same formula for a fire that is demonstrably out, with animals nesting in the cold ruins. Every NT "day and night" outside Revelation is qualitative. Every OT yom v'laylah is qualitative.

"No rest day nor night" (Rev 14:11) describes the thoroughgoing nature of a judgment during its execution -- complete, without relief, without intermission, filling the entire day-night cycle -- not eternal conscious experience. The judgment is real, severe, and without respite while it lasts. But the formula tells us about the quality of the judgment, not its infinite duration.

This study builds on the foundations established in prior studies: the anapausis parallel (etc7-11), the smoke-ascending formula (smoke-imagery-hebrew-mind), the basanizo genre restriction (etc7-11, etc7-12), the non-human subjects of Rev 20:10 (etc7-12), the "ages of ages" subject-determination (for-ever-and-ever-revelation, etc7-08), and the bounded "day and night" accusation of Rev 12:10 (hist-11, revelation-12-timing). Its unique contribution is the systematic mapping of all five Revelation "day and night" occurrences as a unified literary pattern, the genitive-of-time grammatical analysis, the anapausis antithesis as deliberate literary structure, the broader NT and OT "day and night" evidence, and the eschatological dissolution of the day/night framework in Rev 22:5.
