# Verse Analysis: Matthew 10:28 — Same-Author Usage Study

## 1. The Verse Itself: Matthew 10:28

**Context:** Jesus is sending out the twelve disciples (Matt 10:1-5) and warning them of persecution (10:16-23). The immediate pericope (10:26-31) is a structured fear-contrast: fear of human persecutors vs. fear of God.

**Direct statement:** "And fear not them which kill the body, but are not able to kill the soul: but rather fear him which is able to destroy both soul and body in hell."

**Key observations:**
- Two verbs for two actions: [apokteino](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g615/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (G615, "kill outright") for what men do to the body; [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (G622, "destroy fully") for what God does to soul and body.
- Two subjects of fear: men (who can only kill the body) and God (who can destroy the whole person in [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}).
- The verse uses [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (G5590) twice: once for what men cannot kill, once for what God can destroy.
- The location of God's destruction: en geenne (G1067), "in [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}."
- Greek grammar: both [apokteino](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g615/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} and [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} are aorist active infinitives — punctiliar action, not ongoing process.

---

## 2. Surrounding Verses: Matthew 10:26-33

The immediate context contains four uses of phobeo (G5399, "fear") in six verses:
- v.26: "Fear them not" (persecutors will be exposed)
- v.28a: "Fear not them which kill the body"
- v.28b: "Rather fear him which is able to destroy"
- v.31: "Fear ye not therefore" (you are valuable to God)

The pericope moves from persecution (vv.16-25) to fear-instruction (vv.26-31) to confession/denial (vv.32-33). The point: do not fear those who can only end your physical life; fear the one whose judgment extends to the whole person.

**Matthew 10:39** (11 verses after 10:28): "He that findeth his life [psyche] shall lose [apollymi] it: and he that loseth [apollymi] his life [psyche] for my sake shall find it."

This verse uses the same two Greek words ([psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} and [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}) within the same discourse. Here [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} is translated "life" (not "soul"), and [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} means "lose" (not "destroy"). The same-author proximity is a key datum for understanding the semantic range of both words in this passage.

## 3. The Chapter: Matthew 10

Matthew 10 is a single continuous discourse of Jesus to the twelve. Three occurrences of [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (G622) appear in this chapter:
- v.6: "lost [apollymi] sheep" — those perishing, whom Jesus came to save
- v.28: "destroy [apollymi] both soul and body in hell" — God's eschatological judgment
- v.39: "lose [apollymi] his life" — forfeiture of life/self for Christ's sake
- v.42: "lose [apollymi] his reward" — forfeiture of reward

The chapter also contains two occurrences of [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}:
- v.28: translated "soul" (2x)
- v.39: translated "life" (2x)

### Translation Pattern Summary

- **Translated "life" (8 occurrences):** 2:20; 6:25 (2x); 10:39 (2x); 16:25 (2x); 20:28
- **Translated "soul(s)" (8 occurrences):** 10:28 (2x); 11:29; 12:18; 16:26 (2x); 22:37; 26:38

The distribution is 8 "life" and 8 "soul(s)" — an even split. The "life" occurrences consistently refer to physical life that can be sought, lost, saved, given as ransom, or found. The "soul" occurrences are more varied: inner self (11:29; 22:37; 26:38), God's delight (12:18), the person/self (16:26), and the contested 10:28.

### Key Pattern: The [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}-[apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} Word Pair

In three passages within Matthew, [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} and [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} appear together:
1. **10:28** — God can [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (destroy) both [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (soul) and [soma](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g4983/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (body) in [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}
2. **10:39** — He who finds his [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (life) will [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (lose) it; he who [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (loses) his [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (life) will find it
3. **16:25** — Whoever saves his [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (life) will [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (lose) it; whoever [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (loses) his [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (life) will find it

In both 10:39 and 16:25, [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} is translated "life" and [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} is translated "lose." The same word pair used in 10:28 for "destroy soul" is used 11 verses later for "lose life." The same author, in the same discourse, uses the same words with a "life" meaning rather than an "immortal soul" meaning.

### 16:25-26 Transition

Matthew 16:25-26 provides a particularly instructive transition:
- v.25: "whosoever will save his **life** [psyche] shall **lose** [apollymi] it"
- v.26: "For what is a man profited, if he shall gain the whole world, and **lose** his own **soul** [psyche]?"

The same word [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} transitions from "life" to "soul" in consecutive verses of the same passage. The KJV translators chose different English words, but the Greek word is identical. This demonstrates that "soul" and "life" in Matthew's vocabulary are not distinct concepts but are the same word used in overlapping semantic ranges. The question "what shall a man give in exchange for his soul?" (v.26) functions identically to "what shall a man give in exchange for his life?"

### Usage Categories

**A. Destruction/Killing (active, transitive — God or authority figure acting on the wicked):**
- 2:13: Herod seeks to destroy the child (physical killing intended)
- 10:28: God can destroy both soul and body in [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}
- 12:14: Pharisees plot to destroy Jesus (physical killing)
- 21:41: "He will miserably destroy those wicked men" (judgment parable)
- 22:7: King "destroyed those murderers" (judgment parable)
- 27:20: Crowd wants to destroy Jesus (physical killing)

**B. Perishing (intransitive, middle voice — the subject perishes):**
- 5:29-30: One member should perish (rather than whole in [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"})
- 8:25: "we perish" (fear of physical death)
- 9:17: Wineskins perish (physical ruin)
- 18:14: Little ones should not perish
- 26:52: Sword-takers shall perish (physical death)

**C. Lost/ruined (participle — state of being lost, heading toward destruction):**
- 10:6; 15:24: Lost sheep of Israel
- 18:11: To save that which was lost

**D. Lose/forfeit (transitive — one loses something):**
- 10:39; 16:25: Lose one's life/[psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}
- 10:42: Lose one's reward

### Key Pattern: God's [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} Toward the Wicked

When Matthew records God or authoritative figures acting on the wicked with [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}:
- **10:28:** God "destroys" soul and body in [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}
- **21:41:** The lord "miserably destroys" wicked tenants (judgment)
- **22:7:** The king "destroyed" those murderers (judgment)

In every case, the meaning is actual destruction or killing. None of these passages appends torment vocabulary. The 21:41 usage is particularly telling: "kakous kakos apolesei" — "he will miserably destroy those wicked ones" — using the same verb form (future active indicative) as in 10:39 and 16:25.

### The Wineskins Counterexample (9:17)

Matt 9:17 uses [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} for wineskins that "perish" — they burst and become non-functional, but the material still exists. This is cited as evidence that [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} can mean "ruin" rather than "annihilate." This is textually accurate: [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} has a range from "lose" to "ruin" to "destroy." The question is which sense Matthew intends in 10:28, where the verb's object is "both soul and body" and the location is "in [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}." The verb's context (paired with [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}, in a judgment discourse) and its parallels in Matthew (21:41, 22:7) point toward the destruction end of the range.

In all seven Matthean [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} passages:
- The vocabulary paired with [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} is destruction/judgment: [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (5:29-30; 10:28), [krisis](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g2920/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (5:22; 23:33), "cast into" (5:29-30; 18:9).
- The alternative to [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} is consistently "enter into life" (18:8-9; cf. 7:13-14).

The pattern extends to the remaining 5 NT [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} occurrences (Mark 9:43,45,47; Luke 12:5; James 3:6). None of them uses [basanizo](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g928/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} or [basanismos](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g929/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}.

## 7. Synoptic Comparison: Matt 10:28 vs. Luke 12:4-5

### Parallel Texts

| Element | Matthew 10:28 | Luke 12:4-5 |
|---------|---------------|-------------|
| Addressees | The twelve disciples | "My friends" (disciples) |
| Negative command | "Fear not them which kill the body" | "Be not afraid of them that kill the body" |
| Limitation of men | "but are not able to kill the soul" | "and after that have no more that they can do" |
| Positive command | "Rather fear him..." | "Fear him..." |
| God's action | "...which is able to destroy [apollymi] both soul and body in [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}" | "...which after he hath killed [apokteino] hath power to cast into [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}" |
| Word for God's action | [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (G622) — "to destroy fully" | [apokteino](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g615/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (G615) — "to kill outright" + emballo (G1685) — "to cast into" |
| Mentions [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (soul)? | Yes — "kill the soul" / "destroy soul" | No — soul not mentioned |
| Mentions [soma](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g4983/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (body)? | Yes — "kill the body" / "destroy body" | Yes — "kill the body" |
| Temporal sequence? | Not specified | "after he hath killed" — temporal sequence |

### Key Differences

1. **Verb for God's action:** Matthew uses [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (destroy); Luke uses [apokteino](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g615/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} + emballo (kill + cast into). Matthew's verb shift from [apokteino](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g615/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (for men's killing) to [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (for God's destroying) is absent in Luke, which uses [apokteino](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g615/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} in both clauses.

2. **Soul/body distinction:** Matthew has God destroying "both soul and body." Luke omits any reference to the soul. Luke's version describes God killing ([apokteino](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g615/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}) and then casting into [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} — a two-step process.

3. **Temporal structure:** Luke specifies "after he hath killed" (meta to apokteinai) — a temporal sequence where killing precedes casting into [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}. Matthew presents destruction of soul and body in [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} as a single action.

4. **Vocabulary implications:** Luke's version describes something God does after killing — the body is already dead, and what remains is "cast into" [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}. Matthew describes God destroying the whole person (both soul and body) in [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}.

## 8. Greek Parsing Insights

### The Verb Shift: [apokteino](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g615/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} to [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}

In Matt 10:28, the verb shift is grammatically significant:
- **Men's action:** [apokteino](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g615/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (G615) — "to kill outright." Aorist active infinitive. This is the standard Greek word for physical killing.
- **God's action:** [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (G622) — "to destroy fully." Aorist active infinitive. This word has broader scope than [apokteino](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g615/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}.

If Matthew intended to say God can "kill" the soul and body (the same action men perform on the body), he had [apokteino](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g615/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} available — the word already used in the first clause. The switch to [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} signals a different kind of action: not merely killing (ending physical life) but destroying (the full, irreversible destruction of the person).

### The kai...kai Construction

The phrase "kai psychen kai [soma](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g4983/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}" (both soul and body) uses the correlative kai...kai construction, which emphasizes totality. The construction means "both X and Y" — the entire person, not one part. God's destruction extends to everything the person is.

### Aorist Aspect

Both apokteinai and apolesai are aorist infinitives — punctiliar, not durative. The grammar describes a completed action, not an ongoing process. "To destroy" (aorist) describes a single event, not continuous destroying.

## 9. Word Study Insights

### [Psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}: The [nephesh](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h5315/kjv/wlc/0-1/){:target="_blank"}-[psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} Lexical Chain

The LXX translates Hebrew [nephesh](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h5315/kjv/wlc/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (H5315) as Greek [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (G5590) 599 times. [Nephesh](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h5315/kjv/wlc/0-1/){:target="_blank"} in the OT means "a breathing creature" — the living being itself (Gen 2:7: "man became a living [nephesh](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h5315/kjv/wlc/0-1/){:target="_blank"}"). [Nephesh](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h5315/kjv/wlc/0-1/){:target="_blank"} is used for animals (Gen 1:20-21,24,30; 2:19), for dead bodies (Lev 21:11; Num 6:6 — "[nephesh](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h5315/kjv/wlc/0-1/){:target="_blank"} meth" = dead soul/body), and can die (Ezek 18:4,20: "the [nephesh](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h5315/kjv/wlc/0-1/){:target="_blank"} that sins shall die").

The concept_context tool categorizes [G5590](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (psyche) under the LIFE concept (Chay/zoe), not under a separate "SOUL" or "IMMORTALITY" concept. This aligns with the [nephesh](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h5315/kjv/wlc/0-1/){:target="_blank"}-[psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} semantic range: the word denotes the living being, the life-principle.

### [Apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}: The [abad](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h6/kjv/wlc/0-1/){:target="_blank"}-[apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} Lexical Chain

The LXX translates Hebrew [abad](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h6/kjv/wlc/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (H6) as Greek [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (G622) 141 times (PMI 30.45). [Abad](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h6/kjv/wlc/0-1/){:target="_blank"} means "to perish, to destroy, to wander away." This is the dominant Hebrew-Greek destruction word pair. [Apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}'s definition (Strong's): "From apo and the base of [olethros](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g3639/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}; to destroy fully." No lexicon defines [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} as "torment" or "ongoing conscious suffering."

### [Gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}: The ge-[Hinnom](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h2011/kjv/wlc/0-1/){:target="_blank"} Background

[Gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} derives from Hebrew ge-[Hinnom](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h2011/kjv/wlc/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (Valley of the son of [Hinnom](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h2011/kjv/wlc/0-1/){:target="_blank"}), a real geographic location (Josh 15:8; 18:16) associated with:
- Child sacrifice to Molech (2 Ki 23:10; 2 Chr 28:3; 33:6; Jer 7:31; 32:35)
- Renamed "valley of slaughter" by Jeremiah (Jer 7:32; 19:6)
- Burial until no more room (Jer 7:32)
- Carcasses as food for birds and beasts (Jer 7:33)

The OT background of [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} is slaughter and corpse disposal, not ongoing torment.

## 10. Patterns Identified

### Pattern 1: Matthew's [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}-[apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} Pair = Life Lost/Destroyed
In the three passages where Matthew pairs [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} and [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (10:28; 10:39; 16:25), the meaning moves along a continuum: destroy one's life/self (10:28), lose one's life (10:39, 16:25). The same Greek words in the same Gospel carry the sense of "losing" or "destroying" one's life/self, not tormenting an immaterial soul.

### Pattern 2: Matthew's [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} + [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} = Destruction, Not Torment
In the two passages where Matthew pairs [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} and [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (5:29-30; 10:28), the vocabulary is consistently destruction. Across all seven Matthean [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} passages, no torment vocabulary appears.

### Pattern 3: Matthew's Judgment [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} = Actual Destruction
When Matthew records authority figures acting on the wicked with [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (10:28; 21:41; 22:7), the meaning is actual destruction — killing or ending. The judgment parables (21:41; 22:7) depict destruction of the wicked, not their ongoing torment.

### Pattern 4: Synoptic Agreement on [Gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} as Post-Death Judgment
Both Matthew and Luke agree that [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} is the post-death judgment administered by God. Luke adds the temporal note "after he hath killed" — [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} follows death. Neither version uses torment vocabulary.

### Pattern 5: The Verb Shift ([apokteino](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g615/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} to [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}) Signals Greater Scope
Matthew deliberately switches from [apokteino](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g615/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (men's killing of the body) to [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (God's destroying of the whole person). The switch marks a qualitative difference: human persecution can end physical life; divine judgment ends the person entirely.

## 11. Connections Between Passages

### Matt 10:28 and Ezek 18:4,20
"The soul [nephesh] that sins shall die" (Ezek 18:4,20). Matthew's statement that God can "destroy the soul" in [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} is consistent with Ezekiel's statement that the soul can die. Both assert the mortality of the soul/[nephesh](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h5315/kjv/wlc/0-1/){:target="_blank"}/[psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} under divine judgment.

### Matt 10:28 and Isa 66:24
The "worm" and "fire" of Mark 9:43-48 (which Matthew records as "[gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} fire" in 18:9) quotes Isaiah 66:24. In the Isaiah source, the worm and fire act on "carcasses" (peger = dead bodies/corpses). The same [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} concept in the same author (Jesus) pairs with destruction vocabulary ([apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}) in 10:28 and with corpse imagery (Isa 66:24) in Mark 9.

### Matt 10:28 and Rom 6:23
"The wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life" (Rom 6:23). Matthew's depiction of God destroying soul and body in [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} is consistent with Paul's binary: death vs. life. Both use destruction/death as the alternative to life.

### Matt 10:28 and 2 Pet 2:12
"Natural brute beasts, made to be taken and destroyed; shall utterly perish in their own corruption" (2 Pet 2:12). Peter uses [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (perish) for the wicked's fate. The same verb Matthew uses for God's destroying of soul and body.

## 12. Difficult Passages / ECT Counterarguments

### A. "Not Able to Kill the Soul" — Does This Require Body-Soul Dualism?

Matt 10:28 states that men can kill the body but are "not able to kill the soul." The ECT inference: this implies the soul survives bodily death as a separable, conscious entity. If the soul survives the death of the body, it could be subject to ongoing torment.

**What the text says:** Men cannot kill the [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}; God can destroy it. The text addresses the scope of human power vs. divine power. Humans can end physical life; they cannot end the whole person. God can.

**Same-author data:** Matthew uses [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} for "life" (2:20; 6:25; 10:39; 16:25; 20:28) and for "soul/self" (10:28; 11:29; 12:18; 16:26; 22:37; 26:38). Luke 12:4-5 (the synoptic parallel) omits any reference to the soul: "after he hath killed [the body] hath power to cast into [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}." Luke's version describes a sequence (kill, then cast) without requiring a separable soul surviving between the two.

**Textual observation:** The text states men cannot kill the [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}. The text also states God can destroy the [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}. Whether [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} here refers to an "immaterial soul" or to "the life/self that only God controls" is a matter of interpretation. Both readings are compatible with the text. What the text does not say is that the [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} is indestructible or immortal — it explicitly states God can destroy it.

### B. [Apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} as "Ruin" Not "Annihilate" — The Wineskins Passage

Matt 9:17 uses [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} for wineskins that "perish" — they burst and become non-functional, but their material still exists. The ECT inference: [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} in 10:28 means "ruin" (rendering non-functional, not ceasing to exist), so God "ruins" the soul and body in [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} without annihilating them.

**What the text says:** Matt 9:17 uses [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} for physical ruin of wineskins. Matt 10:28 uses [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} for what God does to soul and body in [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}.

**Same-author data:** When Matthew uses [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} for God's or authority figures' judgment action against the wicked (21:41; 22:7), it means actual destruction/killing. "He will miserably destroy those wicked men" (21:41) does not mean "he will ruin them while they continue to exist." The king who "destroyed those murderers and burned up their city" (22:7) completed an act of destruction.

**Textual observation:** [Apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} has a semantic range from "lose" to "ruin" to "destroy fully." The 9:17 usage demonstrates that "ruin" is within the range. The judgment-context usages (10:28; 21:41; 22:7) consistently point toward actual destruction. Both readings are within [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}'s range; the same-author judgment pattern favors the destruction reading.

### C. The Warning Implies Something Worse Than Death

The structure of Matt 10:28 — "fear not men who can kill the body; rather fear God who can destroy soul and body in [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}" — implies that [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} is worse than bodily death. The ECT inference: if [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} were mere cessation (the wicked simply cease to exist), it would not be qualitatively worse than the death men can inflict; therefore [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} must involve ongoing suffering.

**What the text says:** The verse presents [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} as something to fear more than physical death. It does not specify whether the "worse" consists in duration, scope, finality, or nature.

**Textual observation:** The text states that what God can do is more comprehensive than what men can do: men can kill the body only; God can destroy both soul and body. The greater scope (the whole person, not just the body) and the finality ([gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} — eschatological, permanent) account for the greater fear without requiring ongoing torment. A permanent, total, irreversible destruction of the entire person is qualitatively different from (and worse than) a temporary physical death from which resurrection is possible.

### D. Matt 16:26 — "What Shall a Man Give in Exchange for His Soul?"

This passage, using the same word [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}, seems to imply the soul has supreme value and eternal significance. The ECT inference: the soul is so valuable that its loss (in hell) must involve an ongoing, conscious experience, not mere cessation.

**What the text says:** "What is a man profited, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul [psyche]? or what shall a man give in exchange for his soul [psyche]?" The text asks about the value of one's [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (life/self) — nothing can compensate for losing it. The previous verse (16:25) uses the same word [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} meaning "life."

**Textual observation:** The passage speaks of irreplaceable loss, not of ongoing experience. Once one's [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} is lost/destroyed, nothing can restore it. This is consistent with both permanent cessation and ongoing torment; the text does not specify which.
