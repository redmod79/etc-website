# Matthew 10:28 -- What Does "Destroy Soul and Body in Hell" Mean?

## What This Study Examined

This study asked: What does Matthew 10:28 teach about the fate of the wicked? Jesus says, "Fear not them which kill the body, but are not able to kill the soul: but rather fear him which is able to destroy both soul and body in hell." The study used same-author analysis -- examining how Matthew himself uses the key Greek words throughout his Gospel -- rather than importing meanings from other contexts.

Three words were tracked across all of Matthew: *[psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}* (G5590, "soul/life"), *[apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}* (G622, "destroy/lose"), and *[gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}* (G1067, "hell").

---

## What the Text Actually Says

### "Soul" and "Life" Are the Same Greek Word

Matthew uses *[psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}* 16 times. Half are translated "life" and half "soul" in English, but they are the same Greek word. Most significantly, Matthew 10:39 -- just 11 verses after 10:28, in the same discourse -- uses the identical word pair *[psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}* + *[apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}*:

> **Matthew 10:39:** "He that findeth his life [psyche] shall lose [apollymi] it: and he that loseth [apollymi] his life [psyche] for my sake shall find it."

Here the same two words clearly mean "lose one's life" -- not "destroy an immaterial soul." Matthew's own usage in the immediate context treats *[psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}* as "life/self" and *[apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}* as "lose/forfeit."

### When Matthew Uses "Destroy" for God's Judgment, It Means Actual Destruction

When Matthew records God or authority figures using *[apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}* against the wicked, the result is actual destruction:

> **Matthew 21:41:** "He will miserably destroy those wicked men." (Vineyard parable -- the wicked tenants are killed.)

> **Matthew 22:7:** "He sent forth his armies, and destroyed those murderers, and burned up their city." (Wedding feast parable -- destruction is the outcome.)

Matthew's judgment *[apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}* consistently means killing and destruction, not ongoing torment.

### [Gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} Is Never Paired with Torment Vocabulary

Matthew has 7 of the 12 total New Testament [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} occurrences -- the largest share of any author. In every Matthean [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} passage, the vocabulary paired with [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} is destruction or judgment:

- Matt 5:29-30: "perish" (*[apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}*) + [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}
- Matt 10:28: "destroy" (*[apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}*) + [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}
- Matt 5:22: "judgment" (*[krisis](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g2920/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}*) + [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} fire
- Matt 18:9: "enter into life" contrasted with [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} fire
- Matt 23:33: "damnation" (*[krisis](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g2920/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}*) + [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}

No Matthew [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} passage uses *[basanizo](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g928/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}* ("torment") or *[basanismos](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g929/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}* ("torment/torture"). This pattern extends to all 12 NT [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} occurrences: no [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} verse in the entire New Testament uses torment vocabulary for human beings.

---

## The Synoptic Parallel: Luke 12:4-5

Luke records the same teaching but with different words:

> **Luke 12:4-5:** "Be not afraid of them that kill the body, and after that have no more that they can do. But I will forewarn you whom ye shall fear: Fear him, which after he hath killed hath power to cast into hell."

Luke omits "soul" entirely and uses "kill" (*[apokteino](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g615/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}*) rather than "destroy" (*[apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}*) for God's action. Luke describes a two-step process: God kills, then casts into [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}. Both authors agree [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} is the place of eschatological judgment; neither uses torment vocabulary.

---

## The Greek Grammar

Both verbs in Matthew 10:28 are aorist active infinitives -- a form that describes completed actions, not ongoing processes. The verb shift from *[apokteino](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g615/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}* ("kill outright," for men's action) to *[apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}* ("destroy fully," for God's action) signals a qualitative difference in scope. The "both...and" construction (*kai...kai*) emphasizes totality: God's destruction encompasses the entire person -- both soul and body.

---

## Addressing the Counter-Arguments

**"Not able to kill the soul" proves body-soul dualism:** The text says men cannot kill the *[psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}*. It also says God CAN destroy the *[psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}*. The text does not say the soul is indestructible or immortal. Luke's parallel omits "soul" entirely. Whether *[psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}* refers to an immaterial soul or to "the life/self that only God can end" is the interpretive question -- and Matthew's own usage pattern in 10:39 supports the "life" reading.

**[Apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} means "ruin," not "annihilate" (citing the wineskins in 9:17):** *[Apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}* has a range from "lose" to "ruin" to "destroy fully." The wineskins passage shows the "ruin" end. But when Matthew uses *[apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}* for divine judgment (10:28; 21:41; 22:7), it consistently means actual destruction. The judgment usage governs the judgment context.

**[Gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} must involve something worse than death:** The text presents [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} as qualitatively different from what men can do. The difference lies in scope (whole person, not just body) and finality (eschatological, permanent). The text does not specify the "worse" as ongoing torment.

---

## Conclusion

Matthew 10:28 states that God can destroy both soul and body in [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}. Same-author analysis reveals three convergent patterns: (1) *[psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}* functions as "life/self" in Matthew's Gospel, with the same word pair meaning "lose one's life" just 11 verses later; (2) when Matthew uses *[apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}* for divine judgment, it means actual destruction; (3) all seven Matthean [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} passages pair [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} with destruction vocabulary, never with torment vocabulary.

The text teaches that God's eschatological judgment is qualitatively different from anything humans can do -- it encompasses the whole person and is permanent. The vocabulary Jesus chose is the vocabulary of destruction, not the vocabulary of ongoing torment.
