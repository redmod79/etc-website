# Matthew 10:28 — Same-Author Usage and the Fate of the Wicked

## Question

Matthew 10:28 — "And fear not them which kill the body, but are not able to kill the soul: but rather fear him which is able to destroy both soul and body in hell." What does this verse teach about the fate of the wicked? Same-author usage analysis of [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (G5590), [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (G622), [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (G1067), and phobeo (G5399) in Matthew's Gospel.

## Summary Answer

Matthew 10:28 states that God is able to destroy ([apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}) both soul ([psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}) and body ([soma](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g4983/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}) in [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}. Same-author analysis of Matthew's Gospel reveals three convergent patterns: (1) [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} functions as "life/self" in the majority of Matthean uses, with the same [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}-[apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} word pair appearing in 10:39 and 16:25 meaning "lose one's life"; (2) when Matthew uses [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} for God's judgment action against the wicked (10:28; 21:41; 22:7), it consistently means actual destruction/killing, not torment; (3) all seven of Matthew's [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} passages pair [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} with destruction/judgment vocabulary, never with torment vocabulary ([basanizo](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g928/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}/[basanismos](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g929/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}). The synoptic parallel (Luke 12:4-5) lacks both [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} and "soul," describing God as killing and then casting into [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}.

## Key Verses

**Matthew 10:28:** "And fear not them which kill the body, but are not able to kill the soul: but rather fear him which is able to destroy both soul and body in hell."

**Matthew 10:39:** "He that findeth his life shall lose it: and he that loseth his life for my sake shall find it." [Same words: psyche = "life"; apollymi = "lose"]

**Matthew 21:41:** "They say unto him, He will miserably destroy those wicked men, and will let out his vineyard unto other husbandmen." [apollymi in judgment context = actual destruction]

**Luke 12:4-5:** "Be not afraid of them that kill the body, and after that have no more that they can do. But I will forewarn you whom ye shall fear: Fear him, which after he hath killed hath power to cast into hell."

**Matthew 5:29-30:** "It is profitable for thee that one of thy members should perish, and not that thy whole body should be cast into hell." [apollymi + gehenna = perishing/destruction]

## Analysis

### A. Matthew's [Psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} Pattern

Matthew uses [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (G5590) 16 times across 11 verses. The translation splits evenly: 8 occurrences as "life" (2:20; 6:25 2x; 10:39 2x; 16:25 2x; 20:28) and 8 as "soul(s)" (10:28 2x; 11:29; 12:18; 16:26 2x; 22:37; 26:38). In all "life" occurrences, [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} refers to physical life that can be sought (2:20), worried about (6:25), lost or found (10:39; 16:25), or given as ransom (20:28). In 16:25-26, the same word transitions from "life" (v.25) to "soul" (v.26) in consecutive verses of the same discourse, demonstrating that "life" and "soul" are not two distinct concepts for Matthew but overlapping uses of a single word.

The most significant datum: Matt 10:39 (just 11 verses after 10:28, in the same discourse) uses the identical word pair [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} + [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} meaning "lose one's life" — not "destroy an immaterial soul." Matthew's own usage pattern in the immediate context treats [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} as "life/self" and [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} as "lose/forfeit."

### B. Matthew's [Apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} Pattern

Matthew uses [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (G622) approximately 18 times across 16 verses. When Matthew records God or authority figures acting on the wicked with [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}, the meaning is actual destruction:
- 10:28: God "destroys" soul and body in [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}
- 21:41: The vineyard lord will "miserably destroy" the wicked tenants
- 22:7: The king "destroyed" those murderers and burned their city

The "lost sheep" usage (10:6; 15:24; 18:11) describes those perishing whom Jesus came to save — the same word denoting their trajectory toward destruction. The "lose one's life" usage (10:39; 16:25) describes forfeiture of [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}. The wineskins passage (9:17) demonstrates that [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} can mean "ruin" (the material persists but the function is lost). The judgment-context usages consistently point toward actual destruction.

### C. Matthew's [Gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}-Destruction Pattern

Matthew has 7 of the 12 total NT [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} occurrences (58%). In every Matthean [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} passage, the vocabulary paired with [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} is destruction/judgment:
- Matt 5:29-30: [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (perish) + [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}
- Matt 10:28: [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (destroy) + [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}
- Matt 5:22: [krisis](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g2920/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (judgment) + [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} fire
- Matt 18:9: "enter into life" contrasted with [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} fire
- Matt 23:33: [krisis](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g2920/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (judgment/damnation) + [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}

No Matthew [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} passage uses [basanizo](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g928/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (torment), [basanismos](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g929/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (torment-noun), or any conscious-ongoing-existence vocabulary. This pattern extends to all 12 NT [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} occurrences: no [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} verse in the entire NT uses torment vocabulary for human beings.

### D. Synoptic Comparison

Luke 12:4-5 records the same teaching but with different vocabulary:
- Luke omits [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (soul) entirely — no reference to what men "cannot kill"
- Luke uses [apokteino](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g615/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (kill) for God's action, not [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (destroy)
- Luke specifies a temporal sequence: "after he hath killed [apokteino] hath power to cast [emballo] into [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}"
- Luke describes a two-step process: kill, then cast into [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}

Matthew describes God's destruction of the whole person (both soul and body) in [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} as a single action. Luke describes God killing the person and then casting what remains into [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}. Both agree [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} is the locus of eschatological judgment; neither uses torment vocabulary.

### E. Greek Grammar

Both [apokteino](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g615/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} and [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} in Matt 10:28 are aorist active infinitives — punctiliar aspect, describing completed actions rather than ongoing processes. The verb shift from [apokteino](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g615/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (for human killing) to [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (for divine destroying) signals a qualitative difference in scope. The correlative kai...kai construction ("both soul and body") emphasizes totality — God's destruction encompasses the entire person.

### F. ECT Counterarguments Addressed

**1. "Not able to kill the soul" implies body-soul dualism:** The text states men cannot kill the [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}. It also states God can destroy the [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}. The text does not state the [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} is indestructible or immortal. Luke's parallel omits any mention of the soul. Whether [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} refers to an immaterial soul or to "the life/self that only God can end" is an interpretive question not settled by this verse alone.

**2. [Apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} means "ruin" not "annihilate" (citing wineskins, 9:17):** [Apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} has a semantic range from "lose" to "ruin" to "destroy fully." The wineskins passage demonstrates the "ruin" end of the range. The judgment passages (10:28; 21:41; 22:7) demonstrate the "destruction/killing" end. Same-author judgment usage consistently means actual destruction.

**3. [Gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} must involve something worse than death:** The text presents [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} as qualitatively different from what men can do. The difference lies in scope (whole person, not just body) and finality (eschatological, permanent). The text does not specify the nature of the "worse" as ongoing torment.

(From Explicit and Necessary Implication tiers)

1. **The text states God can destroy ([apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}) both soul ([psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}) and body ([soma](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g4983/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}) in [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}** (E028).
2. **The text states the same word pair ([psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} + [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}) means "lose one's life" in Matt 10:39, 11 verses later in the same discourse** (E449, N066).
3. **The text states Matthew uses [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} for both "life" and "soul" in equal proportion; the word carries the sense of "the living self/person"** (E448, E450).
4. **The text states the verb changes from [apokteino](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g615/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (kill, for men) to [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (destroy, for God) in Matt 10:28** (E454).
5. **The text states no [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} passage in any author pairs [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} with torment vocabulary** (E453, N068).
6. **The text states the soul that sins shall die** (E016); **the soul is mortal — it can be destroyed** (N005).
7. **The text states Luke's parallel (12:4-5) omits both [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} and [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}, describing God as killing and casting into [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}** (E456, E457).
8. **The text states both verbs in Matt 10:28 are aorist infinitives — punctiliar, completed action** (E455).

## What CANNOT Be Said

(Not explicitly stated or necessarily implied)

1. **The text does not state that the soul is immortal or indestructible.** Matt 10:28 explicitly states God can destroy the soul.
2. **The text does not state that [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} means "annihilate" (complete non-existence of all components).** The word means "destroy fully" with a semantic range including ruin.
3. **The text does not state that [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} means "ruin while continuing to exist in torment."** No [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} passage appends torment vocabulary.
4. **The text does not state whether the "soul" in 10:28 is a separable conscious entity or the person's life/self.** Luke's parallel omits the concept; Matthew's usage pattern supports both readings to varying degrees.
5. **The text does not state that [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} involves ongoing conscious torment.** No [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} passage uses [basanizo](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g928/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} or [basanismos](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g929/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}.
6. **The text does not state that the wicked will cease to exist.** The text says God can destroy them; the duration and nature of that destruction is inferred, not stated.
7. **The text does not state the precise mechanism by which God destroys soul and body in [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}.**
