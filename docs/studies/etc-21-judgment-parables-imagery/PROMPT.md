#### Key Findings from Related Study Conclusions

**From etc-14 (Judgment Passages):**
- Across 8 major judgment passages, NONE uses [basanizo](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g928/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (torment) for human wicked
- 4 of 8 use explicit destruction/death vocabulary (second death, [olethros](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g3639/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}, devour/[apoleia](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g684/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}, [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"})
- Matt 25:46 uses [kolasis](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g2851/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (punishment), NOT [basanismos](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g929/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (torment) -- different Greek words
- "Weeping and gnashing of teeth" OT background (Ps 35:16; 37:12; 112:10; Job 16:9; Lam 2:16; Acts 7:54) = anger and rage, not physical pain. Luke 13:28 identifies cause as exclusion from the kingdom.
- etc-14 already registered E-items for Matt 25:41,46 and related vocabulary

**From etc-06 (Destruction Vocabulary):**
- [katakaio](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g2618/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (G2618, "burn down/consume") is part of the destruction vocabulary family
- The destruction similes (chaff, stubble, wax) depict substances being consumed
- Matt 3:12/Luke 3:17 chaff burned with "unquenchable fire" = fire that cannot be put out until its work is complete

**From etc-19 (Matt 10:28):**
- Same-author (Matthew) analysis: [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} in judgment contexts = actual destruction
- All 7 Matthean [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} passages: destruction/judgment vocabulary only, never torment
- E451: When Matthew records God/authority acting on wicked with [apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}, it means actual destruction (21:41 vineyard; 22:7 burned city)
- The parabolic genre was flagged (Gate 3 in Tree 3) -- judgment parables depict destruction but as illustrations, not direct didactic teaching

**From etc-05 (Four Hell Words):**
- [Gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} in Jesus' usage = eschatological destruction, not ongoing torment
- Matt 10:28: God "destroys" ([apollymi](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g622/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}) soul and body in [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}
- No [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} passage in any NT author pairs [gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} with torment vocabulary

**From eternal-fire:**
- "Eternal fire" = fire from the Eternal God producing permanent results
- Sodom suffered "eternal fire" (Jude 7) but became "ashes" (2 Pet 2:6) -- not still burning
- God IS a consuming fire (Deut 4:24; Heb 12:29)

4. **"Weeping and gnashing of teeth" -- all 7 occurrences:** Matt 8:12; 13:42; 13:50; 22:13; 24:51; 25:30; Luke 13:28. Map the formula across all contexts. What triggers the weeping/gnashing in each passage? Does the OT background for "gnashing of teeth" (anger/hostility) or the judgment context (grief at exclusion) better explain the phrase?

What does "furnace" imagery communicate -- ongoing torment or consuming destruction?

6. **Tension between "outer darkness" and "furnace of fire":** These appear in different parables from the same author (Matthew). Do they describe the same fate with different imagery, or different aspects? How does the darkness imagery relate to fire imagery?

7. **OT background for the imagery:** Malachi 4:1 (furnace/oven burns wicked to stubble/ashes); Daniel 3:6-26 (literal furnace); OT "day of darkness" passages (Amos 5:18,20; Joel 2:2,10; Zeph 1:15).

8. **Same-author pattern in Matthew:** Matthew uses both "furnace of fire" (13:42,50) and "outer darkness" (8:12; 22:13; 25:30) and "[gehenna](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g1067/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} fire" (5:22; 18:9). How do these relate within Matthew's Gospel?
