# Word Studies

## Key Terms for the Immortality Question

### [athanasia](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g110/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (G110) -- "immortality" (deathlessness)
**Original:** [athanasia](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g110/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}
**Transliteration:** [athanasia](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g110/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}
**Part of Speech:** feminine noun
**Definition:** From a-[thanatos](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g2288/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (un-death); deathlessness, immortality
**Occurrences:** 3 total in NT

**Translations:**
- "immortality" -- 3x (1 Cor 15:53, 54; 1 Tim 6:16)

**Key verses:**
- 1 Tim 6:16: God "only hath immortality ([athanasia](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g110/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"})"
- 1 Cor 15:53: "this mortal must put on immortality ([athanasia](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g110/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"})"
- 1 Cor 15:54: "this mortal shall have put on immortality ([athanasia](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g110/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"})"

**Historical significance:** This word appears ONLY 3 times in the NT. It is attributed exclusively to God (1 Tim 6:16) and to resurrected believers (1 Cor 15:53-54). It is never attributed to the human soul. The Greek philosophical concept of an inherently immortal soul uses different terminology (athanatos [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}), but the NT does not apply [athanasia](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g110/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} to the [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}.

### [aphtharsia](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g861/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (G861) -- "incorruptibility"
**Original:** [aphtharsia](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g861/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}
**Transliteration:** [aphtharsia](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g861/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}
**Part of Speech:** feminine noun
**Definition:** From [aphthartos](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g862/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (incorruptible); incorruptibility, unending existence
**Occurrences:** 8 total in NT

**Translations:**
- "incorruption" -- 4x
- "immortality" -- 2x
- "sincerity" -- 2x

**Key verses:**
- Rom 2:7: "seek for glory and honour and immortality ([aphtharsia](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g861/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"})"
- 1 Cor 15:42: "sown in corruption; raised in incorruption ([aphtharsia](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g861/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"})"
- 2 Tim 1:10: Christ "brought life and immortality ([aphtharsia](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g861/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}) to light through the gospel"
- Eph 6:24: "all them that love our Lord Jesus Christ in sincerity ([aphtharsia](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g861/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"})"

**Historical significance:** Like [athanasia](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g110/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}, [aphtharsia](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g861/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} is something to be sought (Rom 2:7), received at resurrection (1 Cor 15:42), and revealed through the gospel (2 Tim 1:10). It is never described as an inherent human property.

### [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (G5590) -- "soul"
**Original:** [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}
**Transliteration:** [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}
**Part of Speech:** feminine noun
**Definition:** From [psycho](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5594/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (to breathe); breath, spirit, the soul; by implication life itself
**Occurrences:** 105 total in NT

**Translations:**
- "soul" (major usage)
- "life" (common usage)
- "mind" (occasional)
- "heart" (occasional)

**Historical significance:** The Greek word [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} was used by Plato for the immortal soul. The same word appears in the NT but with a Hebrew semantic background ([nephesh](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h5315/kjv/wlc/0-1/){:target="_blank"}). In Platonic usage, [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} is the divine, pre-existent, immortal essence imprisoned in the body. In biblical usage, [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}/[nephesh](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h5315/kjv/wlc/0-1/){:target="_blank"} means the whole living person (Gen 2:7 -- man BECAME a living [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}), or life itself (Matt 16:25 -- "lose his [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}"), or appetite/desire. The NT never calls the [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} "immortal" (athanatos). Instead, Jesus says God can DESTROY the [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (Matt 10:28).

The conflation of Platonic [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (inherently immortal) with biblical [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (mortal, whole person) is the central linguistic channel through which Greek philosophy entered Christian theology.

### [thnetos](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g2349/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} (G2349) -- "mortal"
**Original:** [thnetos](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g2349/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}
**Transliteration:** [thnetos](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g2349/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}
**Part of Speech:** adjective
**Definition:** From thnesko (to die); liable to die, mortal
**Occurrences:** 6 total in NT

**Translations:**
- "mortal" -- 5x
- "mortality" -- 1x

**Key verses:**
- Rom 6:12: "Let not sin therefore reign in your mortal ([thnetos](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g2349/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}) body"
- Rom 8:11: "shall also quicken your mortal ([thnetos](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g2349/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}) bodies"
- 1 Cor 15:53: "this mortal ([thnetos](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g2349/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}) must put on immortality"
- 2 Cor 4:11: "the life of Jesus manifest in our mortal ([thnetos](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g2349/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}) flesh"
- 2 Cor 5:4: "that mortality ([thnetos](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g2349/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}) might be swallowed up of life"

**Historical significance:** The NT explicitly calls the human condition "mortal" ([thnetos](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g2349/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}). This directly contradicts the Platonic claim that the human soul is inherently immortal (athanatos). The mortal must "put on" immortality -- it is not already possessed.

## Summary of Word Study Findings

The biblical vocabulary consistently treats immortality as:
1. **Belonging to God alone** ([athanasia](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g110/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} -- 1 Tim 6:16)
2. **Something to be sought** ([aphtharsia](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g861/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} -- Rom 2:7)
3. **Put on at resurrection** ([athanasia](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g110/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} -- 1 Cor 15:53-54)
4. **Brought to light through the gospel** ([aphtharsia](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g861/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} -- 2 Tim 1:10)
5. **NOT an inherent human property** ([thnetos](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g2349/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"} -- humans are mortal)
6. **The soul ([psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}) can be destroyed** (Matt 10:28)

The Platonic concept of an inherently immortal soul (athanatos [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}) has no lexical support in the NT. The NT never combines [athanasia](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g110/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}/athanatos with [psyche](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g5590/kjv/tr/0-1/){:target="_blank"}.
