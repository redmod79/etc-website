    Word Studies¶

    Question¶

    Luke 16:19-31 -- parable or literal? Genre, first-century Jewish context. Apply Tree 3 genre gate. Literary structure (five parables in Luke 15-16).

    G86 — hades¶

    Source: G86-háidēs.md

    Occurrences: 11

    Realm of the dead, unseen world. LXX equivalent of sheol (H7585, 58 co-occurrences, PMI 8.31). Temporary — delivered up and cast into lake of fire (Rev 20:13-14). The one depiction of consciousness in hades (Luke 16:23) is in a parable.

    Semantic categories: grave/realm of dead, judgment association, defeated power

    Key verses: Acts 2:27, Acts 2:31, Luke 16:23, Revelation 20:13-14, Matthew 16:18, 1 Corinthians 15:55

    Relevance: Central location term in Luke 16:23. LXX equivalence with sheol means didactic characterizations of sheol as unconscious (Ecc 9:5,10; Ps 6:5; 115:17) directly inform the meaning of hades.

    G931 — basanos¶

    Source: G931-basanos.md

    Occurrences: 3

    Originally a touchstone for testing metals, then torture/torment. Only 3 NT occurrences: Matt 4:24 (physical disease), Luke 16:23 (parabolic torment), Luke 16:28 (place of torment). 2 of 3 occurrences are in Luke 16.

    Semantic categories: physical suffering/disease, afterlife torment (parabolic), testing/examination (etymological)

    Key verses: Matthew 4:24, Luke 16:23, Luke 16:28

    Relevance: Key vocabulary in Luke 16:23,28. Luke uses basanos (3 total NT occurrences) rather than basanizo (12 occ., the standard eschatological torment verb used in Rev 14:10; 20:10). This vocabulary divergence is a genre indicator.

    G928 — basanizo¶

    Source: G928-basanizo.md

    Occurrences: 12

    Standard NT verb for torment. 5 of 12 occurrences in Revelation (eschatological). ABSENT from Luke 16. Used in Matt 8:6,29; Mark 5:7; Luke 8:28; 2 Pet 2:8; Rev 9:5; 11:10; 12:2; 14:10; 20:10.

    Semantic categories: physical suffering, demonic encounters, moral distress, eschatological torment

    Key verses: Revelation 14:10, Revelation 20:10, Matthew 8:29, 2 Peter 2:8

    Relevance: The standard eschatological torment verb is ABSENT from Luke 16. This absence is significant: if Luke 16 were didactic eschatological teaching, we would expect basanizo, not basanos.

    G929 — basanismos¶

    Source: G929-basanismos.md

    Occurrences: 6

    Torment (noun). All 6 occurrences exclusively in Revelation (9:5; 14:11; 18:7,10,15). ABSENT from Luke 16.

    Semantic categories: demonic torment, eschatological judgment, imperial judgment

    Key verses: Revelation 14:11, Revelation 9:5, Revelation 18:7

    Relevance: The standard eschatological torment noun is ABSENT from Luke 16. Combined with the absence of basanizo, this confirms Luke 16 uses a different vocabulary register from standard eschatological judgment passages.

    G930 — basanistes¶

    Source: G930-basanistēs.md

    Occurrences: 1

    Torturer/tormentor. Single occurrence in Matt 18:34 (parable of unforgiving servant). Agent noun from basanizo.

    Semantic categories: punitive authority

    Key verses: Matthew 18:34

    Relevance: Background: the basanos word family includes this agent noun used in another parable (Matt 18:34).

    G3600 — odunao¶

    Source: G3600-odynao.md

    Occurrences: 4

    To grieve, be in anguish. Used exclusively by Luke (3x Luke, 1x Acts). Luke 16:24,25 ('I am tormented/you are tormented'); Luke 2:48 (Mary's parental anxiety); Acts 20:38 (Ephesian elders' grief at Paul's departure). Grief/sorrow vocabulary, NOT punishment vocabulary.

    Semantic categories: judgment anguish (parabolic), parental grief, separation grief

    Key verses: Luke 16:24, Luke 16:25, Luke 2:48, Acts 20:38

    Relevance: Critical vocabulary evidence. Luke uses odunao for the rich man's experience — the same word he uses for Mary's parental worry and the Ephesian elders' grief. This is grief/sorrow vocabulary, not eschatological punishment vocabulary.

    G3601 — odune¶

    Source: G3601-odyne.md

    Occurrences: 2

    Grief, sorrow (noun form of odunao G3600). Used in Rom 9:2 and 1 Tim 6:10 for grief/sorrow contexts.

    Semantic categories: grief, sorrow

    Key verses: Romans 9:2, 1 Timothy 6:10

    Relevance: Noun form of the verb Luke uses in Luke 16:24,25. The word family is consistently grief/sorrow, not judicial punishment.

    G2859 — kolpos¶

    Source: G2859-kólpos.md

    Occurrences: 6

    Bosom, bay. 6 occurrences: Luke 6:38 (bosom/lap), Luke 16:22 (Abraham's bosom), Luke 16:23 (his bosom), John 1:18 (bosom of the Father), John 13:23 (leaning on Jesus' bosom), Acts 27:39 (bay/inlet). 'Abraham's bosom' as a destination for the righteous dead appears ONLY in Luke 16.

    Semantic categories: intimate relational closeness, Abraham's bosom (unique), physical receptacle, geographical feature

    Key verses: Luke 16:22, Luke 16:23, John 1:18, John 13:23

    Relevance: The uniqueness of 'Abraham's bosom' as a scriptural concept is a significant genre indicator. If this were literal doctrine about where the righteous dead go, we would expect it to appear in other afterlife teaching passages. Its singular occurrence in a passage with multiple parabolic indicators suggests culturally familiar imagery.

    G5490 — chasma¶

    Source: G5490-chásma.md

    Occurrences: 1

    Chasm, gulf, vacancy. Hapax legomenon — appears only once in the entire NT (Luke 16:26). From obsolete chaō (to gape/yawn).

    Semantic categories: impassable spatial separation, geographical void

    Key verses: Luke 16:26

    Relevance: A hapax legomenon in the NT. The word appears nowhere else in Scripture. This unique vocabulary further marks Luke 16 as containing imagery found nowhere else in biblical afterlife teaching.

    G5395 — phlox¶

    Source: G5395-phlóx.md

    Occurrences: 7

    Flame, blaze. 7 occurrences. Used primarily for divine theophany: Christ's eyes (Rev 1:14; 2:18; 19:12), burning bush (Acts 7:30), angels (Heb 1:7). Luke 16:24 ('this flame') and 2 Thess 1:8 ('flaming fire') are the non-theophanic uses.

    Semantic categories: divine theophany, parabolic detail, eschatological judgment (2 Thess)

    Key verses: Revelation 1:14, Acts 7:30, Luke 16:24, 2 Thessalonians 1:8

    Relevance: In Luke 16:24, the 'flame' (phlox) is singular and specific to the parabolic narrative. The majority of phlox uses are theophanic, not eschatological judgment.

    G4145 — plousios¶

    Source: G4145-ploúsios.md

    Occurrences: 28

    Rich, wealthy. 28 occurrences. Luke uses it 11 times (39% of all NT uses) — highest concentration of any book. Key to the 'certain rich man' formula (anthropos tis plousios) at Luke 12:16, 16:1, 16:19.

    Semantic categories: material wealth (literal), spiritual wealth (figurative), Lukan wealth-reversal theme

    Key verses: Luke 12:16, Luke 16:1, Luke 16:19, Luke 6:24, Luke 18:23

    Relevance: The adjective that completes the parabolic formula. Luke's distinctive emphasis on wealth-reversal theology makes plousios a Lukan signature term.

    G4434 — ptochos¶

    Source: G4434-ptōchós.md

    Occurrences: 34

    Poor, destitute, begging. Used in Luke 6:20 (Blessed are ye poor), Luke 16:20,22 (Lazarus the beggar).

    Semantic categories: material poverty, spiritual receptivity

    Key verses: Luke 6:20, Luke 16:20, Luke 16:22

    Relevance: Lazarus's condition connects to Luke's Beatitude ('Blessed are ye poor,' 6:20) — the same wealth-reversal theology.

    G3870 — parakaleo¶

    Source: G3870-parakaleo.md

    Occurrences: 113

    To comfort, encourage, exhort. In Luke 16:25, 'parakaleitai' (V-PPI-3S, 'he is comforted') describes Lazarus's state — contrasted with the rich man's odunao (grief).

    Semantic categories: comfort, encouragement, exhortation

    Key verses: Luke 16:25, 2 Corinthians 1:4, Matthew 5:4

    Relevance: The comfort/grief contrast (parakaleo vs. odunao) in Luke 16:25 echoes the Beatitude reversal: 'Blessed are they that mourn, for they shall be comforted' (Matt 5:4, parakaleo).

    G2976 — Lazaros¶

    Source: G2976-lazaros.md

    Occurrences: 15

    Lazarus (from Hebrew Eleazar, 'God helps'). 15 occurrences: 4 in Luke (all in 16:19-31 parable), 11 in John (brother of Mary and Martha). Nave's lists ONLY the John 11 Lazarus as historical — no separate entry for a historical beggar named Lazarus.

    Semantic categories: named character in parable (Luke 16), historical person raised from dead (John 11-12)

    Key verses: Luke 16:20, Luke 16:23-25, John 11:1, John 11:43, John 12:10

    Relevance: The naming of Lazarus is the steelman argument for the literal reading. However: the name is thematic ('God helps'), the rich man is NOT named, Nave's does not list this Lazarus as historical, and OT parables also name characters symbolically (Ezek 23:4).

    G450 — anistemi¶

    Source: G450-anistemi.md

    Occurrences: 123

    To rise, raise up. In Luke 16:31, anaste (V-2AAS-3S Aorist Active Subjunctive) — 'should rise from the dead.' Standard resurrection vocabulary.

    Semantic categories: physical rising, resurrection, establishment

    Key verses: Luke 16:31, Acts 2:24, 1 Thessalonians 4:16

    Relevance: The climactic verse (16:31) uses resurrection vocabulary (anistemi), not intermediate-state vocabulary. The passage points toward resurrection, not Abraham's bosom, as the eschatological hope.

    G3850 — parabole¶

    Source: G3850-parabole.md

    Occurrences: 50

    Parable, similitude, comparison. 50 tokens across 48 verses. Confined to Synoptic Gospels (46) and Hebrews (2). Luke uses it 18 times. LXX regularly translates mashal (H4912) as parabole.

    Semantic categories: narrative comparison, short saying, object lesson, veiled speech, typological figure

    Key verses: Luke 15:3, Matthew 13:3, Mark 4:2, Hebrews 9:9

    Relevance: Luke 15:3 uses this word (singular, with demonstrative 'this') to introduce the discourse unit containing the Rich Man and Lazarus. The LXX bridge to mashal (H4912) connects the NT concept directly to the OT mashal genre exemplified in Isaiah 14:4.

    G1147 — daktylos¶

    Source: G1147-daktylos.md

    Occurrences: 8

    Finger. 8 NT occurrences. In Luke 16:24, the rich man asks that Lazarus dip 'the tip of his finger' in water — a physical body part attributed to a supposedly disembodied soul.

    Semantic categories: divine action, physical touch, proof by contact, burden-bearing

    Key verses: Luke 16:24, Luke 11:20, John 20:25-27

    Relevance: A physical body part in a scene depicting disembodied post-mortem existence. This is a physical impossibility in the literal reading — a genre indicator.

    G622 — apollymi¶

    Source: G622-apollymi.md

    Occurrences: 102

    To destroy fully, to perish. 102 tokens. The standard NT destruction verb for divine judgment (Matt 10:28). ABSENT from Luke 16.

    Semantic categories: physical destruction, divine judgment, perishing, losing life, lost possession, decay

    Key verses: Matthew 10:28, John 3:16, Luke 13:3, 2 Peter 3:9

    Relevance: The standard destruction vocabulary (apollymi/apoleia) is ABSENT from Luke 16. If Luke 16 were a didactic judgment passage, we would expect this vocabulary.

    G5590 — psyche¶

    Source: G5590-psyche.md

    Occurrences: 105

    Soul, life, self. 105 occurrences. LXX equivalent of nephesh (H5315). Can die (Matt 10:28), be destroyed — not inherently immortal.

    Semantic categories: inner self, physical life, person/individual, living creature, mind/will

    Key verses: Matthew 10:28, Matthew 22:37, Luke 1:46, Revelation 16:3

    Relevance: Background for the anthropological question: if psyche/nephesh can die and be destroyed, there is no inherent immortal component to sustain conscious existence between death and resurrection.

    G4151 — pneuma¶

    Source: G4151-pneuma.md

    Occurrences: 385

    Spirit, breath, wind. 385 occurrences. In death contexts, pneuma is the life-force that departs (Matt 27:50; Luke 23:46; James 2:26).

    Semantic categories: Holy Spirit, evil spirits, human spirit, life-force, disembodied spirits, wind

    Key verses: Luke 23:46, James 2:26, John 3:8, Revelation 11:11

    Relevance: Background for the spirit-at-death question. The life-force (pneuma) returns to God at death (Ecc 12:7/Luke 23:46) but is not depicted as a conscious entity that carries on activity.

    G2288 — thanatos¶

    Source: G2288-thanatos.md

    Occurrences: 119

    Death. 119 tokens. Encompasses physical death, death as sin's consequence, death as cosmic power, Christ's atoning death, spiritual death, and the second death (Rev 20:14).

    Semantic categories: physical death, sin consequence, cosmic power, atoning event, spiritual death, second death

    Key verses: Romans 5:12, Romans 6:23, 1 Corinthians 15:26, Revelation 20:14

    Relevance: The definition of death as cessation/reversal of creation is the foundation. If death = cessation, Luke 16's conscious post-mortem activity is parabolic imagery, not didactic teaching.

    G110 — athanasia¶

    Source: G110-athanasia.md

    Occurrences: 3

    Immortality, deathlessness. Only 3 NT occurrences: 1 Cor 15:53, 15:54 (put on at resurrection), 1 Tim 6:16 (God alone has it). Something received at resurrection, not possessed inherently.

    Semantic categories: resurrection transformation, exclusive divine attribute

    Key verses: 1 Corinthians 15:53-54, 1 Timothy 6:16

    Relevance: If immortality is received at resurrection (not possessed at death), there is no mechanism for conscious intermediate existence. This undermines the literal reading of Luke 16.

    G166 — aionios¶

    Source: G166-aionios.md

    Occurrences: 71

    Eternal, age-pertaining. 71 tokens. Duration determined by subject context. Three passages use aionios for past time that has ended (Rom 16:25; 2 Tim 1:9; Tit 1:2). ABSENT from Luke 16.

    Semantic categories: eternal life, eternal judgment, divine attributes, heavenly realities, pre-temporal reference

    Key verses: John 3:16, Matthew 25:46, Romans 6:23, 2 Thessalonians 1:9

    Relevance: Luke 16 does NOT use aionios. The passage makes no claim about the eternal duration of the rich man's experience. This is another vocabulary absence distinguishing Luke 16 from didactic eschatological passages.

    G684 — apoleia¶

    Source: G684-apoleia.md

    Occurrences: 20

    Destruction, perdition. 20 tokens. Used for final eschatological ruin, son of perdition, and waste. ABSENT from Luke 16.

    Semantic categories: eschatological ruin, son of perdition, beast's end, waste, perishing

    Key verses: Matthew 7:13, John 17:12, 2 Thessalonians 2:3, Revelation 17:8

    Relevance: Standard eschatological destruction vocabulary is ABSENT from Luke 16. Another vocabulary absence distinguishing the passage from didactic judgment teaching.

    G1067 — geenna¶

    Source: G1067-geenna.md

    Occurrences: 12

    Gehenna, Valley of Hinnom. 12 occurrences. 11 are Jesus' direct speech. Consistently associated with fire and destruction (apollymi). ABSENT from Luke 16.

    Semantic categories: fiery destruction, self-denial warnings, destruction of soul and body, judicial judgment, moral corruption

    Key verses: Matthew 10:28, Matthew 5:22, Mark 9:43, Luke 12:5

    Relevance: Jesus' standard judgment-place term is geenna, NOT hades. Luke 16 uses hades, which Revelation shows is temporary and emptied at the final judgment.

    G3709 — orge¶

    Source: G3709-orge.md

    Occurrences: 36

    Wrath, anger. 36 occurrences. Divine judicial wrath and eschatological wrath. ABSENT from Luke 16.

    Semantic categories: eschatological wrath, judicial wrath, wrath and law, deliverance from wrath, human anger

    Key verses: Romans 1:18, Revelation 6:16-17, 1 Thessalonians 1:10, Romans 5:9

    Relevance: Standard judgment vocabulary (orge) is ABSENT from Luke 16. The passage does not use wrath terminology.

    G3992 — pempo¶

    Source: G3992-pémpō.md

    Occurrences: 82

    To send, dispatch. 82 occurrences. Used in Luke 16:24,27 for the rich man's requests to 'send' Lazarus.

    Semantic categories: authorized dispatch, divine mission, representative agency, urgent deployment

    Key verses: Luke 16:24, Luke 16:27, John 4:34, John 20:21

    Relevance: The rich man's requests to 'send' Lazarus serve the narrative's teaching point — building toward the climactic statement about the sufficiency of Moses and the prophets.

    G2198 — zao¶

    Source: G2198-záō.md

    Occurrences: 147

    To live. 147 occurrences. Physical, spiritual, resurrection, and divine life.

    Semantic categories: physical life, spiritual life, resurrection life, the living God, ethical conduct, judgment formula

    Key verses: John 11:25, Romans 1:17, Revelation 1:18, Luke 20:38

    Relevance: Background for understanding 'all live unto him' (Luke 20:38) as eschatological/resurrection life, not intermediate-state consciousness.

    G2222 — zoe¶

    Source: G2222-zoe.md

    Occurrences: 134

    Life. 134 tokens. Dominant usage: eternal/eschatological life as God's gift through Christ. In Luke 16:25, 'en te zoe sou' = 'in thy lifetime' (earthly biographical life).

    Semantic categories: eternal life, present possession in Christ, life vs. death, symbolic objects of life, earthly life, divine attribute

    Key verses: John 3:16, John 5:24, Luke 16:25, Romans 6:23

    Relevance: In Luke 16:25, zoe refers to earthly lifetime, not eternal life — consistent with the passage's reversal motif about earthly wealth and poverty.

    G599 — apothnesko¶

    Source: G599-apothnēskō.md

    Occurrences: 122

    To die. 122 occurrences. Used in Luke 16:22 ('the beggar died... the rich man also died').

    Semantic categories: physical death, Christ's death, dying to sin, death as sin's consequence, death overcome

    Key verses: Luke 16:22, Romans 5:6, 1 Corinthians 15:3, John 11:25

    Relevance: Both the beggar and the rich man 'died' (apethanen) in Luke 16:22 — standard death vocabulary, not specialized eschatological language.

    G861 — aphtharsia¶

    Source: G861-aphtharsia.md

    Occurrences: 8

    Incorruptibility, immortality. 8 occurrences, exclusively Pauline. 4 in 1 Cor 15 (resurrection chapter). Something 'put on' at resurrection, not possessed before.

    Semantic categories: resurrection transformation, aspiration/revealed hope, ethical purity

    Key verses: 1 Corinthians 15:42, 1 Corinthians 15:53-54, Romans 2:7, 2 Timothy 1:10

    Relevance: Paired with athanasia in 1 Cor 15:53-54: incorruption and immortality are both received at resurrection. Reinforces conditional immortality.

    G3306 — meno¶

    Source: G3306-meno.md

    Occurrences: 128

    H4191 — muwth¶

    H5315 — nephesh¶

    Source: H5315-nephesh.md

    Occurrences: 788

    Soul, living being, person, life. 788 tokens. Applied to animals and humans alike (Gen 1:20,24; 2:7 — same phrase 'nephesh chayyah'). Can die (Ezek 18:4; Num 6:6 'dead soul'). Not inherently immortal.

    Semantic categories: breathing creature, whole person, life at risk, inner self/desire, wholehearted devotion, reflexive pronoun, corpse

    Key verses: Genesis 2:7, Ezekiel 18:4, Numbers 6:6, Psalm 146:4

    Relevance: Biblical anthropology: man BECAME a nephesh chayyah (Gen 2:7), he did not RECEIVE a separate nephesh. Nephesh can die. This undermines the prerequisite for conscious intermediate existence.

    H7585 — sheol¶

    Source: H7585-showl.md

    Occurrences: 68

    Underworld, grave, realm of the dead. 68 tokens. LXX translates sheol as hades 58 times (PMI 8.31). Characterized by unconsciousness in didactic passages (Ecc 9:10; Ps 6:5; 115:17). Receives all dead, righteous and wicked.

    Semantic categories: universal destination, descent language, death paired term, fate of wicked, rescue from sheol, insatiable

    Key verses: Ecclesiastes 9:10, Psalm 6:5, Psalm 115:17, Psalm 16:10, Isaiah 14:9-15, Hosea 13:14

    Relevance: The OT counterpart of hades (G86). Didactic passages characterize sheol as unconscious (Ecc 9:10; Ps 6:5; 115:17). The one parabolic depiction of consciousness in hades/sheol (Luke 16:23 / Isa 14:9-10) occurs in mashal/parabolic genre.

    H7496 — rapha¶

    Source: H7496-râphâʼ.md

    Occurrences: 8

    Shades, dead ones in sheol. 8 occurrences. The rephaim are consistently portrayed as weak, powerless shadows — not active, conscious beings. Isaiah 14:9 'stirs up' the rephaim figuratively; Isaiah 26:14,19 discusses their non-rising/rising.

    Semantic categories: dead in sheol, death as destination, resurrection contrast

    Key verses: Isaiah 14:9, Job 26:5, Psalm 88:10, Proverbs 9:18, Isaiah 26:14, Isaiah 26:19

    Relevance: The rephaim of Isaiah 14:9 — 'stirred up' to greet the king of Babylon in the mashal — are the same type of figurative dead-speaking that appears in Luke 16. Both are in mashal/parabolic genre.
