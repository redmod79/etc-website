    Classification Audit Trail — etc7-09-rich-man-lazarus¶

    This document contains the per-item Tree 3/4/5 walkthroughs for every E-item classified Cond. or ECT and every N-item. Each walkthrough uses tool-verified parsing for Gate 2.

    E-Item Classifications (Cond. or ECT)¶

    E16 (Acts 2:34) — "David is not ascended into the heavens" — Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — "not ascended into the heavens" applied to David. The righteous dead have not gone to heaven. This denies post-mortem conscious heavenly existence for the righteous dead. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): David — a literal historical human being, named individual. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: ou G3756 [PRT-N] + gar G1063 [CONJ] + Daueid G1138 [N-PRI] Nom Sg M + anebe G305 [V-2AAI-3S] Aor Act Ind ("ascended") + eis tous ouranous G3772 [N-APM] Acc Pl M ("the heavens"). Emphatic negative particle ou + gar. Grammar is unambiguous: David [subject] + has not ascended [verb] + into the heavens [destination]. No alternative parsing available. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Peter's Pentecost sermon, recorded as didactic prose/direct speech in Acts. Narrative report of apostolic teaching. Didactic. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E15 (David is dead and buried, Acts 2:29), E19 (dead know not anything, Ecc 9:5), E21 (thoughts perish, Ps 146:4), and the full didactic death-state corpus. No conflicting E-items. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands. David, a righteous man, is dead and has not ascended to heaven. This denies conscious heavenly intermediate existence for the righteous dead.

    E19 (Ecc 9:5) — "The dead know not any thing" — Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — "know not any thing" applied to the dead. Cessation of consciousness/knowledge at death. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "The dead" (hammetim, Qal participle of muth H4191 with article) — literal dead humans, universal category. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: vehammetim = muth Verb.Qal.Ptcp.mp.Abs with Art ("the dead") + einam = ain Noun.ms.Abs+3mp ("are not") + yod'im = yada H3045 Verb.Qal.Ptcp.mp.Abs ("knowing") + meumah = Noun.fs.Abs ("anything"). Grammar is unambiguous: the dead [subject] + are not knowing [negated participle] + anything [universal object]. No alternative parsing. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Ecclesiastes 9 — wisdom literature, didactic teaching about the human condition. Direct statement about the nature of death. Didactic. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E20 (no knowledge in sheol, Ecc 9:10), E21 (thoughts perish, Ps 146:4), E22 (no remembrance in death, Ps 6:5), E23 (dead go into silence, Ps 115:17), E24 (dead know not about children, Job 14:21), E25 (grave cannot praise, Isa 38:18-19). Multi-author consensus. No conflicting E-items. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands. The dead lack consciousness and knowledge.

    E20 (Ecc 9:10) — "There is no work, nor device, nor knowledge, nor wisdom, in the grave" — Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — "no knowledge, nor wisdom in sheol" — cessation of cognitive function at death. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): The person addressed ("thou") going to sheol — universal human experience. Literal humans. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: ein = ain Noun.ms.Cst ("there is not") + ma'aseh = Noun.ms.Abs ("deed") + cheshbon = Noun.ms.Abs ("account/device") + da'ath = Noun.fs.Abs ("knowledge") + chokmah = Noun.fs.Abs ("wisdom") + bishe'ol = Prep + sheol Noun.s.Abs ("in sheol"). Four nouns negated by one ain — no work, no device, no knowledge, no wisdom. Grammar unambiguous. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Ecclesiastes 9 — didactic wisdom literature. Direct teaching. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E19, E21-E25 and the full death-state corpus. No conflicting E-items. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E21 (Ps 146:4) — "In that very day his thoughts perish" — Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — "thoughts perish" (abad H6 = "perish") — cessation vocabulary applied to cognitive function at death. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): A human being ("his" = the person whose breath departs). Literal human. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: bayom = Prep + Art + yom Noun.ms.Abs ("in the day") + hahu = Art + hu' PersPron.3ms ("that very") + abdu = abad Verb.Qal.Perf.3p ("they perish") + eshtonotav = eshtonot Noun.fp.Abs+3ms ("his thoughts"). Qal perfect 3rd plural of abad ("perish") with eshtonotav ("his thoughts") as subject. Grammar unambiguous: on the day of death, the person's thoughts perish. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Psalm 146 — hymnic/didactic poetry. Direct statement about human mortality in the context of trusting God rather than princes. Didactic. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E19 (dead know nothing), E20 (no knowledge in sheol), E22-E25. Multi-author consensus. No conflicting E-items. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E22 (Ps 6:5) — "In death there is no remembrance of thee: in the grave who shall give thee thanks?" — Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — "no remembrance" in death — cessation of cognitive function/awareness of God at death. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): The dead — those "in death" and "in the grave." Universal human category. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser confirms negation of remembrance and thanksgiving in death/sheol. Grammar unambiguous: in death [location] + there is no remembrance [negated noun] + of thee [object]; in sheol [location] + who gives thee thanks? [rhetorical question expecting "no one"]. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Psalm 6 — a petition psalm. David's direct statement about death as a state where God is not remembered. Didactic content within poetic genre. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E19-E21, E23-E25. No conflicting E-items. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E23 (Ps 115:17) — "The dead praise not the LORD, neither any that go down into silence" — Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — "dead praise not" + "go down into silence (dumah)" — cessation of activity at death. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "The dead" (hammetim, Qal participle of muth with article) — universal human category. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: lo = Neg ("not") + hammetim = muth Verb.Qal.Ptcp.mp.Abs with Art ("the dead") + yehalelu = halal Verb.Piel.Impf.3mp ("praise") + Yah = PropN ("the LORD"). Grammar unambiguous: the dead [subject] + do not praise [negated verb] + the LORD [object]. Second clause: yoredei dumah = "those descending into silence." PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Psalm 115 — hymnic/didactic poetry. Direct statement about the dead's inability to praise God. Didactic. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E19-E22, E24-E25. No conflicting E-items. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E24 (Job 14:21) — "His sons come to honour, and he knoweth it not; and they are brought low, but he perceiveth it not" — Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — the dead person "knoweth it not" and "perceiveth it not" — cessation of awareness at death. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): The dead man — a literal human being who has died (context: Job 14:10-12 describes man dying). PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: yikbedu = kabad Verb.Qal.Impf.3mp ("they are honored") + banav = ben Noun.mp.Abs+3ms ("his sons") + velo = Conj + Neg ("and not") + yeda = yada Verb.Qal.Impf.3ms ("he knows") + veyits'aru = tsa'ar Verb.Qal.Impf.3mp ("they are brought low") + velo yavin = Neg + bin Verb.Qal.Impf.3ms ("he does not perceive") + lamo = Prep+3mp ("of them"). Grammar unambiguous: his sons [subject] + are honored/brought low [verb], but he [the dead man] + does not know/perceive [negated verbs]. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Job 14 — wisdom literature, didactic teaching about human mortality. Direct statement. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E19-E23, E25. No conflicting E-items. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E25 (Isa 38:18-19) — "The grave cannot praise thee, death can not celebrate thee... The living, the living, he shall praise thee" — Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — the grave/death "cannot praise" — cessation of capacity at death, contrasted with the living. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "The grave" (sheol) and "death" (maveth) as personified states, but the implicit subjects are the humans in those states. The living/dead contrast makes clear the teaching is about dead humans vs. living humans. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: lo = Neg ("not") + she'ol = Noun.s.Abs ("nether world/sheol") + todeka = yadah Verb.Hiphil.Impf.3fs+2ms ("praises you") + maveth = muth Noun.ms.Abs ("death") + yehallelka = halal Verb.Piel.Impf.3ms+2ms ("celebrates you"). Grammar unambiguous: sheol [subject] + does not praise you [negated verb]; death [subject] + does not celebrate you [negated verb]. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Hezekiah's prayer, recorded in Isaiah 38. Personal testimony within prophetic narrative. Didactic content — direct speech about the nature of death. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E19-E24. No conflicting E-items. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E33 (Matt 10:28) — "Fear him which is able to destroy both soul and body in Gehenna" — Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — "destroy" (apolesai, apollymi G622) applied to "soul and body" — destruction vocabulary applied to the whole person. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "Soul and body" (psychen kai soma) — literal human components. Direct reference to human beings. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: ton dunamenon = dynamai G1410 [V-PNP-ASM] Pres M/P Ptcp Acc Sg M ("the one being able") + kai psychen kai soma = G5590 [N-ASF] + G4983 [N-ASN] ("both soul and body") + apolesai = apollymi G622 [V-AAN] Aor Act Inf ("to destroy"). Apollymi in aorist active infinitive — "to destroy." Subject and object are clear: God [the one being able] + to destroy [verb] + both soul and body [objects] + in Gehenna [location]. Grammar unambiguous. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Jesus' direct teaching to his disciples in the Matthean mission discourse. Didactic. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E19-E25 (death-state unconsciousness) and the destruction vocabulary pattern (apollymi). No conflicting E-items. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands. God is able to destroy both soul and body in Gehenna — destruction, not preservation in torment.

    N-Item Classifications¶

    N1 — "The absence of a parabole label at Luke 16:19 is uninformative about genre, given Luke's labeling pattern" — Based on: E1, E2, E3, E4 — Classified: Neutral

    • Gate 0 (Foundation):
    • N-Test 1 (Universal agreement): Would an ECT scholar agree that the absence of the parabole label at 16:19 is uninformative about genre, given that Luke 16:1 also lacks the label and is universally accepted as parabolic? Yes — both sides acknowledge Luke does not label every parable. PASS.
    • N-Test 2 (No interpretation): Is this the only possible conclusion? Yes — the data shows Luke labels some parables but not others (12:16 has label, 16:1 does not, 16:19 does not). The absence of a label cannot establish either reading. PASS.
    • N-Test 3 (Zero added concepts): Does this add anything beyond the cited E-items? No — it is the logical entailment of the observed pattern. PASS.
    • Tree 3 Gates:
    • V1/V2: Neither destruction nor conscious-torment vocabulary. Neither applies.
    • Result: Neutral stands. This is an observation about Luke's labeling convention.

    N2 — "The passage's self-identified teaching point is about hearing Moses and the prophets, not afterlife geography" — Based on: E12, E13 — Classified: Neutral

    • Gate 0 (Foundation):
    • N-Test 1 (Universal agreement): Would an ECT scholar agree that vv.29,31 are the passage's climactic teaching point? Yes — the narrative builds to this exchange. Both sides can identify vv.29,31 as the climactic statements. PASS.
    • N-Test 2 (No interpretation): Is this the only possible conclusion? Some might argue the afterlife setting is also a teaching point. However, the text itself identifies "Moses and the prophets; let them hear them" as the directive. The climactic position (final statement in the narrative) is not disputed. PASS.
    • N-Test 3 (Zero added concepts): Does this add anything beyond the cited E-items? No — it restates what E12 and E13 contain: the passage's final statements are about Scripture's sufficiency. PASS.
    • Tree 3 Gates:
    • V1/V2: Neither destruction nor conscious-torment vocabulary in the teaching point itself. Neither applies.
    • Result: Neutral stands. The teaching point is about the sufficiency of Moses and the prophets.

    N3 — "'Abraham's bosom' as a post-mortem location is unique to Luke 16:22-23 and lacks corroboration elsewhere in Scripture" — Based on: E7 — Classified: Neutral

    • Gate 0 (Foundation):
    • N-Test 1 (Universal agreement): Would an ECT scholar agree that "Abraham's bosom" as a post-mortem destination appears only in Luke 16:22-23? Yes — this is a verifiable distributional fact. PASS.
    • N-Test 2 (No interpretation): Is this the only possible conclusion? Yes — kolpos Abraam as an afterlife location appears nowhere else in the NT or OT. PASS.
    • N-Test 3 (Zero added concepts): Does this add anything beyond the cited E-item? No — it restates the distributional data. PASS.
    • Tree 3 Gates:
    • V1/V2: Neither destruction nor conscious-torment vocabulary. Neither applies.
    • Result: Neutral stands. This is a distributional observation.

    N4 — "Luke 16 uses different torment vocabulary (basanos/odunao) from the eschatological torment passages (basanizo/basanismos)" — Based on: E8, E9, E34 — Classified: Neutral

    • Gate 0 (Foundation):
    • N-Test 1 (Universal agreement): Would an ECT scholar agree that Luke 16 uses basanos G931 and odunao G3600 rather than basanizo G928 and basanismos G929? Yes — this is verifiable from the Greek text. PASS.
    • N-Test 2 (No interpretation): Is this the only possible conclusion? Yes — the lexemes are different. PASS.
    • N-Test 3 (Zero added concepts): Does this add anything beyond the cited E-items? No — it combines the distributional data from E8, E9, and E34. PASS.
    • Tree 3 Gates:
    • V1/V2: Neither destruction nor conscious-torment vocabulary in the observation itself. This is a vocabulary comparison. Neither applies.
    • Result: Neutral stands. This is a lexical observation that both sides can verify.

    N5 — "Since David is dead and NOT ascended (Acts 2:29,34), Luke 16's depiction of the righteous dead in Abraham's bosom cannot be Luke's didactic teaching about the death-state" — Based on: E15, E16 — Classified: Cond.

    • Gate 0 (Foundation):
    • N-Test 1 (Universal agreement): Would an ECT scholar necessarily agree that David is dead and not ascended, and that therefore Luke 16 cannot be Luke's didactic teaching? An ECT scholar would agree David has not ascended into the heavens (the text says so). However, some ECT scholars would argue that Abraham's bosom is not "the heavens" — it is a different compartment. They would deny the implication that this proves Luke 16 is non-didactic. The implication adds a step beyond the E-items: equating "not ascended to heavens" with "not in Abraham's bosom." FAIL.
    • N-Test 2: Not reached (Test 1 failed).
    • N-Test 3: Not reached (Test 1 failed).
    • Gate 0 FAILS. Reclassify to I-tier.
    • Reclassification: N5 fails Gate 0. The implication adds an interpretive step (equating "not ascended to heavens" with "not in Abraham's bosom"). Reclassified as I-A (text-derived, aligns with E/N). Source test: all components from E-items (Acts 2:29,34). Direction test: aligns with death-state E-items. Requires criterion #5 (systematizing). Position: Cond.

    N6 — "Since hades is temporary and emptied at the final judgment (Rev 20:13-14), Luke 16's setting in hades is not the final eschatological state" — Based on: E10, E26 — Classified: Neutral

    • Gate 0 (Foundation):
    • N-Test 1 (Universal agreement): Would an ECT scholar agree that hades is temporary and emptied? Yes — ECT scholars acknowledge that Luke 16:19-31 describes hades (intermediate state), not the final hell/lake of fire (see E422 in master evidence). Both sides agree hades is temporary. PASS.
    • N-Test 2 (No interpretation): Is this the only possible conclusion? Yes — Revelation 20:13-14 states that death and hades deliver up their dead, then are cast into the lake of fire. The text itself establishes hades as temporary. PASS.
    • N-Test 3 (Zero added concepts): Does this add anything beyond the cited E-items? No — it combines E10 (rich man is in hades) with E26 (hades is emptied and destroyed). PASS.
    • Tree 3 Gates:
    • V1/V2: Neither destruction nor conscious-torment vocabulary in the observation itself. It is a statement about the temporal nature of hades. Neither applies.
    • Result: Neutral stands. Both sides agree hades is temporary and is not the final eschatological state.

    I-Item Classifications¶

    I1 — "Luke 16:19-31 is a parable, not a literal account" — Type: I-B — Classified: Neutral

    • Source test: All components from E/N tables — E1-E14, E17-E18, E30-E32, N1-N4, N6. Text-derived.
    • Direction test: Some E/N items support (E1-E4 formula, E7 unique imagery, E8-E9 vocabulary, E17-E18 mashal precedent, N1-N4); the naming of Lazarus (E6) is neutral but is cited by the opposing reading. Both sides have textual observations. Conflicts: I-B.
    • FOR (parabolic): E1 (parabole label at 15:3), E2-E4 (identical formula), E5 (rich man unnamed), E7 (Abraham's bosom unique), E8-E9 (vocabulary divergence), E11 (chasma hapax), E12-E13 (teaching point is Scripture sufficiency), E14 (Pharisees as money-lovers), E17-E18 (mashal precedent), E30 (physical impossibilities), E31-E32 (figurative precedents), N1-N4, N6.
    • AGAINST (literal): E6 (Lazarus named — unique among parables).
    • Result: Type I-B confirmed. Position: Neutral (genre determination, not inherently favoring either eschatological position). [SIS resolution in CONCLUSION.md]

    I2 — "The naming of Lazarus proves the passage is a literal historical account" — Type: I-C — Classified: ECT-direction

    • Source test: The claim that naming = historicity requires an external principle (the assumption that named characters in Jesus' teaching must be historical). This principle is not stated in Scripture. External.
    • Direction test: Compatible with E/N items (E6 confirms the naming) but does not override any E/N item. I-C.
    • Result: Type I-C confirmed. Position: ECT-direction (supports the literal reading, which is the prerequisite for extracting afterlife doctrine from the passage).

    I3 — "The physical details (tongue, water, flame, gulf) describe literal post-mortem conditions" — Type: I-C — Classified: ECT-direction

    • Source test: The claim that parabolic details are literal requires an external framework (literal reading of figurative genre). External.
    • Direction test: Does not override any E/N item directly — E30 observes the physical details but does not classify them as literal. I-C.
    • Result: Type I-C confirmed. Position: ECT-direction.

    I4 — "Luke 16:19-31 teaches the literal geography of the afterlife" — Type: I-C — Classified: ECT-direction

    • Source test: Requires adding the concept "parabolic settings teach doctrine" — not found in any E/N item. External.
    • Direction test: Compatible with some E items (E7, E10, E11 describe the setting) but does not override them. However, it conflicts with N2 (teaching point is Scripture sufficiency, not afterlife geography). Borderline I-C/I-D.
    • Assessment: N2 states the passage's self-identified teaching point. I4 claims the setting is also doctrinal teaching. Since the passage itself identifies its teaching point and I4 must add a concept the text does not state ("parabolic settings are doctrinal"), this remains I-C: compatible external that supplements but does not directly override N2 (N2 does not say the setting teaches nothing; it says the climactic teaching point is Scripture sufficiency).
    • Result: Type I-C confirmed. Position: ECT-direction.

    I5 — "Luke 16:19-31 proves conscious post-mortem suffering consistent with ECT" — Type: I-D — Classified: ECT-direction
