    Word Studies¶

    Question¶

    Hebrew/Greek destruction vocabulary: abad/apollumi (H6/G622), shamad (H8045), kalah (H3615), shachath (H7843), apoleia (G684), olethros (G3639). Lexical definitions, eschatological usage.

    H6 — abad¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H6-abad.md

    Occurrences: 219

    Primitive root verb: 'to wander away, lose oneself; by implication to perish (causative, destroy).' Qal (intransitive, 'perish') dominates at ~61% (119x). Piel 41x and Hiphil 26x provide causative 'destroy.' KJV: 'perish' ~55%, 'destroy' ~32%, 'lost' ~6%. UBS semantic domain: Non-Exist (002002002006). The broadest Hebrew destruction verb, covering simple straying through to total annihilation.

    Semantic categories: Physical loss of objects/animals, Individual death/personal destruction, Covenant-curse/divine judgment, Divine active destruction of enemies/nations, Fate of the wicked (wisdom literature), Loss of abstract qualities, Idiom: ready to perish

    Key verses: Psa 1:6, Psa 37:20, Psa 68:2, Psa 92:9, Deu 7:10, Deu 8:19-20, Deu 28:20, Job 20:7, Pro 2:22

    Relevance: Primary Hebrew destruction verb. Core study word. LXX maps to apollymi 141 times (PMI 6.14, score 30.45) — the strongest Hebrew-Greek verb pairing in the OT-NT destruction chain.

    G622 — apollymi¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G622-apollymi.md

    Occurrences: 102

    From apo (G575) + ollymi ('to destroy'): 'to destroy fully.' Active = destroy, ruin; Middle/Passive = perish, be lost. Louw-Nida: Violence/Destroy (20.003) AND Exist/Non-exist (13.003). KJV: 'perish' 36%, 'destroy' 30%, 'lose/lost' 27%. Critical antonym: sozo (G4982, 'to save') — 14 verses in direct opposition. The primary Greek destruction/perishing verb and canonical NT heir to Hebrew abad.

    Semantic categories: Physical destruction/killing (active), Divine judgment/eschatological ruin, Perishing in eternal judgment (middle/passive), Losing one's life/soul, Losing/finding a possession, Decay/perishability, Safety/preservation (negated)

    Key verses: Mat 10:28, Joh 3:16, Luk 13:3, Luk 13:5, Luk 17:27, Luk 17:29, 2 Pet 3:9, 1 Cor 1:18, Jam 4:12

    Relevance: Primary Greek destruction verb. Core study word. LXX heir to abad (141x co-occurrence). No lexicon places apollymi in a 'torment' or 'ongoing suffering' domain.

    H8045 — shamad¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H8045-shamad.md

    Occurrences: 101

    Primitive root verb: 'to desolate — destroy, destruction, bring to nought, overthrow.' The most absolute Hebrew destruction word — total extermination with no remnant. Almost exclusively Hiphil (69x, causative) or Niphal (21x, passive). No Qal form = always carries intensive/causative force. KJV: 'destroy/destroyed' ~94%. Never means anything less than total destruction.

    Semantic categories: YHWH exterminating Canaanite nations, Covenant curse against apostate Israel, Destruction of dynasties/royal houses, Destruction of idolatry/false worship, Extermination plots against God's people, Eschatological/prophetic final destruction, Divine restraint/non-destruction (negated)

    Key verses: Psa 37:38, Psa 92:7, Psa 145:20, Isa 13:9, Deu 28:20, Deu 28:45, Deu 28:48, Deu 28:51, Deu 28:61

    Relevance: Most extreme Hebrew destruction word. Core study word. LXX maps to apollymi 23 times. UBS domain: Non-Exist. No remnant language — total annihilation.

    H3615 — kalah¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H3615-kalah.md

    Occurrences: 258

    Primitive root verb: 'to end, whether intransitive (cease, be finished, perish) or transitive (complete, prepare, consume).' Broadest usage range of the four Hebrew destruction words. Piel dominates (141x = intensive/factitive). KJV: 'consume/consumed' ~18%, 'made an end' ~20%, 'finished' ~13%. In judgment contexts, consistently means 'consume to nothing.' Three key non-existence passages pair kalah with ayin: Psa 37:20, 59:13, 104:35.

    Semantic categories: Completion of task/work, Finishing speaking, Divine judgment/consumption, Wrath/fury accomplished, Eyes failing/longing, Time/resources spent, Determination/decided evil

    Key verses: Psa 37:20, Psa 59:13, Psa 104:35, Isa 1:28, Nah 1:8-9, Eze 22:31, Jer 14:12, Deu 28:21, Deu 32:23

    Relevance: Core study word. Bridges completion and destruction semantics. LXX maps to synteleo 85 times. In judgment/eschatological contexts, kalah consistently means 'consume to nothing,' paired with explicit non-existence (ayin) language.

    H7843 — shachath¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H7843-shachath.md

    Occurrences: 164

    Primitive root verb: 'to decay, ruin (causatively) — batter, corrupt, destroy, lose, mar, perish, spoil, waste.' Bridges corruption and destruction semantics. Hiphil dominates (98x = deliberate destruction by external agent). Piel (39x = intensive corruption/ruin). KJV: 'destroy/destroyed' ~48%, 'corrupt' ~16%. Gen 6:11-13 threefold use establishes the hermeneutical principle: corruption leads to destruction.

    Semantic categories: Moral/spiritual corruption, Physical destruction of cities/nations/armies, Ruining/marring objects, Divine restraint from destruction (negated), The Destroyer as active agent, Self-destruction/reflexive ruin

    Key verses: Gen 6:11-13, Gen 19:13-14, Psa 14:1, Psa 53:1, Eze 28:17, Deu 31:29, Deu 32:5, Exo 32:7

    Relevance: Core study word. Bridges corruption and destruction. LXX maps to diaphtheiro 48 times. Gen 6:11-13 principle: the same root describes sin (corruption) and judgment (destruction) — corruption naturally results in destruction.

    G684 — apoleia¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G684-apoleia.md

    Occurrences: 20

    Feminine noun from G622 apollymi: 'ruin or loss (physical, spiritual, or eternal).' Louw-Nida: 20.031 (Violence/Destroy). KJV: 'destruction' 25%, 'perdition' 25%. Concentrated in 2 Peter (5x in 61 verses) and eschatological contexts. All 20 occurrences classified: eschatological destruction, 'son of perdition' (destiny-genitive), beast's end, waste/squandering, false teachers' ruin.

    Semantic categories: Final/eschatological ruin, Son of perdition (destiny-genitive), Beast goes into perdition, Waste/squandering, Perishing/immediate ruin, False teachers and their followers, Apostasy (drawing back)

    Key verses: Mat 7:13, Phi 3:19, 2 Th 2:3, 2 Pet 2:1, 2 Pet 2:3, 2 Pet 3:7, Rev 17:8, Rev 17:11, 1 Tim 6:9, Joh 17:12

    Relevance: Core study word. NT noun form of apollymi's action. Matt 7:13 structurally decisive: two ways → destruction vs. life. 2 Peter epicenter (5x in 61 verses).

    G3639 — olethros¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G3639-olethros.md

    Occurrences: 4

    Masculine noun from ollymi (to destroy): 'ruin, death, punishment.' Only 4 NT occurrences, all Pauline. KJV: uniformly 'destruction.' Four semantic categories: (1) disciplinary destruction of flesh (1 Cor 5:5), (2) sudden eschatological destruction (1 Thess 5:3), (3) everlasting destruction from God's presence (2 Thess 1:9), (4) destruction paired with perdition (1 Tim 6:9). Hebrew source (LXX): eid (H343, calamity), shod (H7701, violence/devastation).

    Semantic categories: Disciplinary destruction, Sudden eschatological destruction, Everlasting destruction from God's presence, Destruction paired with perdition

    Key verses: 2 Th 1:9, 1 Th 5:3, 1 Cor 5:5, 1 Tim 6:9

    Relevance: Core study word. Specifically Pauline destruction vocabulary. 2 Thess 1:9 is the most important single verse for this focus area: olethros aionios = 'everlasting destruction.'

    G4982 — sozo¶

    Occurrences: 120

    Verb: 'to save, deliver, heal, make whole.' The primary NT salvation verb and critical antonym of apollymi. 14 verses set apollymi and sozo in direct opposition (Mat 16:25; Luk 9:24; 19:10; Jas 4:12; 2 Cor 2:15 etc.). What sozo rescues from is what apollymi inflicts. KJV: 'save' ~52%, 'be saved' ~17%, 'whole/healed' ~8%.

    Semantic categories: Spiritual salvation from sin/judgment, Physical healing, Deliverance from danger, Preservation of life

    Key verses: Mat 16:25, Luk 9:24, Luk 19:10, Jam 4:12, 1 Cor 1:18, 2 Cor 2:15

    Relevance: Critical antonym of apollymi. The apollymi-sozo pair defines the two destinies: perishing vs. being saved. If sozo = preservation of life/existence, then apollymi = loss of life/existence.

    G166 — aionios¶

    Occurrences: 71

    Adjective: 'eternal, everlasting.' From aion (G165, 'age'). KJV: 'eternal' 45%, 'everlasting' 30%. Applied to both life (zoe aionios, ~40x) and judgment/destruction contexts (olethros aionios 2 Thess 1:9, pur aionion Matt 25:41, kolasis aionios Matt 25:46). The Hebrews pattern (eternal redemption, judgment, salvation — all permanent results) is decisive for interpretation.

    Semantic categories: Eternal life, Everlasting punishment/fire, Everlasting destruction, Age-lasting duration

    Key verses: 2 Th 1:9, Mat 25:41, Mat 25:46, Joh 3:16, Heb 9:12, Heb 6:2, Heb 5:9, Jud 1:7

    Relevance: Duration modifier applied to destruction vocabulary. Key for temporal vs. permanent analysis. Hebrews pattern: aionios + result-noun = permanent result, not ongoing process.

    H5769 — olam¶

    Occurrences: 432

    Noun/adjective: 'concealed, eternity, long duration, perpetual, everlasting, for ever.' Context-dependent duration word. Can mean '3 days' (Jon 2:6), 'until death' (Exo 21:6), 'for a generation' (Isa 34:10 — Edom), or 'truly endless' (of God's nature). In eschatological destruction contexts, olam describes the permanence of the RESULT, not the duration of the process.

    Semantic categories: Truly eternal (of God), Age-lasting/generational, Life-long, Permanent result

    Key verses: Jon 2:6, Exo 21:6, Isa 34:10, Psa 92:7

    Relevance: Hebrew duration word. Context-dependent — not always 'endless.' Proves that 'forever' language in destruction contexts can describe permanent result rather than endless process.

    G928 — basanizo¶

    Occurrences: 12

    Verb: 'to test by rubbing on a touchstone; to torture, torment, vex.' From basanos (G931, 'touchstone'). KJV: 'torment/tormented' ~75%, 'tossed/vexed/pained' ~25%. Used in Rev 14:10 for torment of beast-worshipers. DIFFERENT word family from apollymi/apoleia/olethros. Torment vocabulary ≠ destruction vocabulary.

    Semantic categories: Physical torment, Testing/examination, Distress/vexation

    Key verses: Rev 14:10, Rev 20:10, Mat 8:29, Luk 8:28

    Relevance: Must be distinguished from destruction vocabulary as a DIFFERENT word family. basanizo/basanismos = torment; apollymi/apoleia/olethros = destruction. The Bible uses destruction words ~30x more frequently than torment words for the fate of the wicked.

    G4931 — synteleo¶

    Occurrences: 7

    Verb: 'to complete entirely, consummate, execute.' From syn (G4862, 'together/completely') + teleo (G5055, 'to end'). Primary LXX translation of kalah (H3615) at 85 co-occurrences (PMI 27.17). The syn- prefix doubles the completeness emphasis. Cognate noun synteleia (G4930) = 'consummation' (Matt 13:39, 'end of the age').

    Semantic categories: Completing/finishing discourse, Fulfilling prophecy, Consummating/making covenant

    Key verses: Mat 7:28, Rom 9:28, Heb 8:8, Mar 13:4

    Relevance: LXX bridge word for kalah. Preserves kalah's dual semantic of completion and consumption. synteleo = 'to complete entirely' — when applied to judgment, means complete consumption/finishing.

    G1311 — diaphtheiro¶

    Occurrences: 6

    Verb: 'to rot thoroughly, corrupt utterly, destroy.' From dia (G1223, 'through/thoroughly') + phtheiro (G5351, 'to corrupt/decay'). Primary LXX translation of shachath (H7843) at 48 co-occurrences (PMI 7.00, score 27.24). Rev 11:18 creates a Flood echo: 'destroy them which destroy (diaphtheiro) the earth' — same word as Gen 6:11 LXX 'the earth was corrupt (diaphtheiro).'

    Semantic categories: Physical corruption/decay, Moral corruption, Eschatological destruction, Moths corrupting

    Key verses: Rev 11:18, 2 Cor 4:16, 1 Tim 6:5, Luk 12:33

    Relevance: LXX bridge word for shachath. Preserves shachath's corruption-destruction semantic bridge. Rev 11:18 = the corrupt are destroyed by the same word that describes their corruption.

    G5356 — phthora¶

    Occurrences: 9

    Noun: 'corruption, decay, destruction, perishing.' From phtheiro (G5351, 'to corrupt'). Connected to shachath (H7843) through the LXX diaphtheiro chain. Used in 2 Pet 2:12 ('shall utterly perish in their own corruption'), Gal 6:8 ('of the flesh reap corruption'), 1 Cor 15:42,50 (resurrection body contrasted with corruption).

    Semantic categories: Physical corruption/decay, Moral corruption leading to destruction, Perishability of the body

    Key verses: 2 Pet 2:12, Gal 6:8, 1 Cor 15:42, 1 Cor 15:50, 2 Pet 1:4

    Relevance: NT corruption/destruction noun connected to shachath through LXX. 2 Pet 2:12: wicked 'perish in their own corruption' — the corruption-destruction principle from Gen 6:11-13 extends into the NT.

    H3772 — karath¶

    Occurrences: 360

    Verb: 'to cut off, cut down.' Frequently used for covenant-curse destruction: being 'cut off' from the people, from the land, from existence. Shares 'extinction' sense with abad in Psa 37:9,34 ('evildoers shall be cut off'). LXX maps to apollymi 21x, showing overlap with the destruction vocabulary chain.

    Semantic categories: Covenant-curse cutting off, Judicial excision from community, Physical cutting down, Making/cutting covenant

    Key verses: Psa 37:9, Psa 37:22, Psa 37:28, Psa 37:34, Psa 37:38, Pro 2:22

    Relevance: Related Hebrew destruction term. 'Cut off' = removal from existence. Appears 5x in Psalm 37 alongside abad, kalah, and shamad. LXX maps to apollymi 21x.

    H1820 — damah¶

    Occurrences: 18

    Verb: 'to cease, be silent, be destroyed, be brought to silence.' Emphasizes ceasing to speak/exist — the silence of death and non-existence. Used in judgment contexts for complete cessation. Related to domiyyah ('silence').

    Semantic categories: Ceasing to exist, Being silenced, Being destroyed/cut off

    Key verses: Hos 4:6, Oba 1:5, Jer 47:5

    Relevance: Related Hebrew destruction term. Emphasizes the silence and cessation aspect of destruction — the destroyed cease to speak, act, or exist.
