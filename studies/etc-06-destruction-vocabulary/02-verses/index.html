    Verses¶

    Question¶

    Hebrew/Greek destruction vocabulary: abad/apollumi (H6/G622), shamad (H8045), kalah (H3615), shachath (H7843), apoleia (G684), olethros (G3639). Lexical definitions, eschatological usage.

    First Death Threat in Scripture¶

    But of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it: for in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die.

    Context: The foundational mot tamut construction establishing death as the penalty for sin. All destruction vocabulary builds on this original sentence.

    Notes: mot tamut = infinitive absolute + imperfect = emphatic 'you shall certainly die.' Foundation for all destruction vocabulary.

    First Divine Destruction Declaration¶

    And the LORD said, I will destroy man whom I have created from the face of the earth; both man, and beast, and the creeping thing, and the fowls of the air; for it repenteth me that I have made them.

    Context: God's first declaration of intent to destroy humanity. Uses machah (H4229, 'blot out') rather than shachath. The Flood is the paradigm case for divine destruction of the wicked.

    Notes: Uses machah (blot out) — the destruction language escalates to shachath in vv. 11-13.

    Threefold Shachath — Corruption-to-Destruction Pattern¶

    The earth also was corrupt before God, and the earth was filled with violence. And God looked upon the earth, and, behold, it was corrupt; for all flesh had corrupted his way upon the earth. And God said unto Noah, The end of all flesh is come before me; for the earth is filled with violence through them; and, behold, I will destroy them with the earth.

    Context: The same root shachath (H7843) describes both the sin (corruption, vv.11-12) and the judgment (destruction, v.13). Three forms: Niphal wayyiqtol (v.11, 'was corrupt'), Niphal perfect (v.12, 'it was corrupt'), Hiphil perfect (v.12, 'had corrupted'), Hiphil participle (v.13, 'I will destroy them'). This creates a principle: corruption leads to destruction, and destruction is the natural end of corruption.

    Notes: Three forms of shachath: v.11 Niphal wayq 3fs (earth was corrupt), v.12 Niphal perf 3fs (was corrupt) + Hiphil perf 3ms (had corrupted), v.13 Hiphil ptcp ms+3mp (I will destroy them).

    Destruction of Sodom — Paradigm Case¶

    Then the LORD rained upon Sodom and upon Gomorrah brimstone and fire from the LORD out of heaven; And he overthrew those cities, and all the plain, and all the inhabitants of the cities, and that which grew upon the ground.

    Context: Sodom's destruction is the paradigm case for eschatological judgment (cf. Jude 1:7, 2 Pet 2:6). Fire and brimstone produced ashes, not ongoing torment. The verb 'overthrew' (haphak) emphasizes complete reversal/annihilation.

    Notes: Key paradigm: Sodom reduced to ashes (2 Pet 2:6), proving 'eternal fire' (Jude 1:7) produces permanent destruction, not endless burning.

    Divine Destruction of the Wicked — abad H6¶

    And repayeth them that hate him to their face, to destroy them: he will not be slack to him that hateth him, he will repay him to his face.

    Context: Direct divine destruction language. Uses abad (H6) in Hiphil — causative 'to destroy.' God directly and deliberately destroys those who hate him.

    Notes: abad H6 Hiphil — causative active: God actively destroys the wicked.

    Covenant Curse — abad H6¶

    And it shall be, if thou do at all forget the LORD thy God, and walk after other gods, and serve them, and worship them, I testify against you this day that ye shall surely perish. As the nations which the LORD destroyeth before your face, so shall ye perish; because ye would not be obedient unto the voice of the LORD your God.

    Context: Covenant-curse language using abad. The infinitive absolute intensification (abad + tabad = 'surely perish') emphasizes certainty. V.20 draws explicit parallel: as the destroyed nations perished, so will apostate Israel.

    Notes: Infinitive absolute construction: abad toadedun = 'you shall surely perish.' Same fate as destroyed nations.

    Covenant Curse Cluster — abad/shamad H6/H8045¶

    The LORD shall send upon thee cursing, vexation, and rebuke, in all that thou settest thine hand unto for to do, until thou be destroyed, and until thou perish quickly; because of the wickedness of thy doings, whereby thou hast forsaken me. The LORD shall make the pestilence cleave unto thee, until he have consumed thee from off the land, whither thou goest to possess it. The LORD shall smite thee with a consumption, and with a fever, and with an inflammation, and with an extreme burning, and with the sword, and with blasting, and with mildew; and they shall pursue thee until thou perish.

    Context: Dense covenant-curse passage using shamad ('until thou be destroyed') and abad ('until thou perish'). Both destruction words operate in the same judgment context, showing they share the same semantic domain of total destruction.

    Notes: V.20 uses both shamad (Niphal, 'be destroyed') and abad (Qal, 'perish') together. V.22 adds abad again.

    Righteous/Wicked Contrast — Chaff Simile + abad H6¶

    The ungodly [are] not so: but [are] like the chaff which the wind driveth away. Therefore the ungodly shall not stand in the judgment, nor sinners in the congregation of the righteous. For the LORD knoweth the way of the righteous: but the way of the ungodly shall perish.

    Context: First psalm establishes the binary: righteous flourish like a tree (v.3), wicked are chaff that blows away (v.4). The way of the ungodly 'shall perish' (abad, Qal imperfect) — the way itself ceases to exist. Chaff is a cessation simile: it does not continue to exist in a ruined state.

    Notes: abad Qal impf 3fs — intransitive: the way itself perishes. LXX: apollymi.

    Non-Existence Declaration — ayin¶

    For evildoers shall be cut off: but those that wait upon the LORD, they shall inherit the earth. For yet a little while, and the wicked [shall] not [be]: yea, thou shalt diligently consider his place, and it [shall] not [be].

    Context: Explicit non-existence statement. Hebrew ayin (H369) = 'not be, non-existent.' The wicked 'shall not be' — not 'shall be in torment' or 'shall be ruined.' The place of the wicked is also non-existent. Combined with Jesus' quotation of v.11 (Matt 5:5, 'the meek shall inherit the earth').

    Notes: v.10 parsed: ayin rasha ('not-be wicked') = the wicked SHALL NOT BE. ayin = noun of non-existence. hitbonanta al-meqomo we-einennu = 'you look at his place and it is not (there).'

    Triple Destruction — abad + kalah — Smoke Simile¶

    But the wicked shall perish, and the enemies of the LORD [shall be] as the fat of lambs: they shall consume; into smoke shall they consume away.

    Context: Two destruction words in one verse: abad (Qal impf 3mp, 'shall perish') and kalah (Qal perf 3p, 'shall consume,' twice). The 'fat of lambs' simile is cessation imagery: fat on the altar is completely consumed, nothing remains. 'Into smoke shall they consume away' — smoke dissipates into nothing.

    Notes: Parsed: yobedu (abad Qal impf 3mp, 'shall perish') + kalu (kalah Qal perf 3p, 'consumed') + be'ashan kalu (kalah Qal perf 3p, 'in smoke consumed'). Double kalah intensifies the cessation.

    Non-Existence Statement — 'he was not'¶

    Yet he passed away, and, lo, he [was] not: yea, I sought him, but he could not be found.

    Context: Third non-existence statement in Psalm 37 (with v.10 and v.20). The wicked 'passed away and was not' — emphatic cessation. 'I sought him, but he could not be found' — the wicked person has ceased to exist.

    Notes: Hebrew: einennu = 'he is not.' Same ayin construction as v.10.

    shamad H8045 — Transgressors Destroyed Together¶

    But the transgressors shall be destroyed together: the end of the wicked shall be cut off.

    Context: Third destruction word in Psalm 37: shamad (Niphal perf 3p, 'shall be destroyed'). Combined with karath (Niphal perf 3fs, 'shall be cut off'). The 'end' (acharith) of the wicked is cutting off — termination, not continuation in torment.

    Notes: Parsed: nishmedu (shamad Niphal perf 3p, 'were destroyed') + nichratah (karath Niphal perf 3fs, 'was cut off'). Prophetic perfect = future certainty.

    Kalah + Non-Existence — 'that they may not be'¶

    Consume [them] in wrath, consume [them], that they [may] not [be]: and let them know that God ruleth in Jacob unto the ends of the earth. Selah.

    Context: Prayer using kalah (H3615) with explicit non-existence result clause: 'that they may not be' (ayin). The purpose of the consumption (kalah) is non-existence (ayin). This verse directly connects destruction vocabulary to cessation of existence.

    Notes: kalah Piel imperative + we-einemo ('and they are not') = consume them so that they do not exist.

    Wax/Fire Simile — abad H6¶

    As smoke is driven away, [so] drive [them] away: as wax melteth before the fire, [so] let the wicked perish at the presence of God.

    Context: Double cessation simile: smoke driven away + wax melting before fire. Both describe substances that cease to exist. The wicked 'perish' (abad, Qal impf) at the presence of God — God's presence causes destruction, not preservation in torment.

    Notes: v.2 parsed: yaqum elohim (Qal impf 3ms, God arises) + yobedu (abad Qal impf 3mp, perish). The KJV v.2 is the wax/fire verse; v.1 in Hebrew numbering.

    shamad H8045 — 'Destroyed Forever'¶

    When the wicked spring as the grass, and when all the workers of iniquity do flourish; [it is] that they shall be destroyed for ever:

    Context: shamad with adey-ad duration modifier. 'Destroyed for ever' — the 'for ever' modifies the permanence of the RESULT (destroyed state), not the duration of a process. The wicked flourish temporarily but are destroyed permanently.

    Notes: Hebrew: lehishamdam adey-ad = 'to be destroyed to perpetuity.' shamad Niphal infinitive + adey-ad. The grass simile (spring, flourish) emphasizes temporary existence followed by permanent destruction.

    abad H6 — Enemies Perish¶

    For, lo, thine enemies, O LORD, for, lo, thine enemies shall perish; all the workers of iniquity shall be scattered.

    Context: abad (Qal impf 3mp) in parallel with 'scattered.' Two destruction words in Psalm 92 (shamad v.7, abad v.9). The enemies of the LORD perish — the same verb used of substances that cease to exist.

    Notes: abad Qal impf 3mp = intransitive 'shall perish.' Parallel: enemies perish AND workers of iniquity are scattered = total dissolution.

    Non-Existence — 'Let the wicked be no more'¶

    Let the sinners be consumed out of the earth, and let the wicked be no more. Bless thou the LORD, O my soul. Praise ye the LORD.

    Context: Uses tamam (H8552, 'be consumed/completed') with explicit non-existence result: 'let the wicked be no more' (ayin). Third passage pairing destruction vocabulary with explicit non-existence language (with Psa 37:10, 59:13).

    Notes: tamam (consumed/completed) + ayin (be no more). The prayer is for the wicked to cease existing, not to be tormented.

    Preserve/Destroy Contrast — shamar vs. shamad¶

    The LORD preserveth all them that love him: but all the wicked will he destroy.

    Context: Binary contrast using antonyms: shamar (Qal ptcp, 'preserves/guards') vs. shamad (Hiphil impf 3ms, 'will destroy'). The same God who preserves the righteous utterly annihilates the wicked. The contrast is preservation vs. destruction, not preservation-in-blessing vs. preservation-in-torment.

    Notes: Parsed: shomer (shamar Qal ptcp ms, 'preserves') vs. yashmid (shamad Hiphil impf 3ms, 'will destroy'). Hiphil = causative active: God actively destroys.

    abad H6 — 'Perish forever like his own dung'¶

    [Yet] he shall perish for ever like his own dung: they which have seen him shall say, Where [is] he?

    Context: abad with permanence indicator ('for ever') and cessation simile (dung — decomposing substance). The rhetorical question 'Where is he?' emphasizes absence/non-existence, not continued suffering.

    Notes: abad Qal impf 3ms + lanetsach ('forever'). The dung simile = decomposition to nothing.

    Wicked Cut Off from the Earth¶

    But the wicked shall be cut off from the earth, and the transgressors shall be rooted out of it.

    Context: karath (cut off) + nacah (rooted out). Both verbs describe removal from existence, not preservation in suffering. Rooting out a plant = it ceases to exist in that location.

    Notes: karath Niphal impf + nacah Qal impf = double removal/cessation.

    kalah + Fire Destruction¶

    And the destruction of the transgressors and of the sinners [shall be] together, and they that forsake the LORD shall be consumed. And the strong shall be as tow, and the maker of it as a spark, and they shall both burn together, and none shall quench [them].

    Context: v.28 uses kalah (consumed). v.31 provides the fire simile: the strong become tow (flax fiber), the maker becomes a spark — both burn together. 'None shall quench' does not mean endless burning but that nothing stops the consumption until it is complete.

    Notes: kalah = consumed. 'Unquenchable' fire = fire that cannot be stopped until it has consumed its fuel, not fire that burns forever.

    shamad H8045 — Day of the LORD Passage¶

    Behold, the day of the LORD cometh, cruel both with wrath and fierce anger, to lay the land desolate: and he shall destroy the sinners thereof out of it.

    Context: Eschatological Day-of-the-LORD passage using shamad (Hiphil impf 3ms). God destroys the sinners OUT OF the land — removal/elimination, not preservation. The Day of the LORD consistently uses destruction vocabulary.

    Notes: shamad Hiphil impf 3ms = 'he shall destroy' — causative active. Day of the LORD = eschatological judgment context.

    Dead Shall Not Rise — Destruction Language¶

    [They are] dead, they shall not live; [they are] deceased, they shall not rise: therefore hast thou visited and destroyed them, and made all their memory to perish.

    Context: Triple negation: dead + shall not live + shall not rise. Their very memory perishes (abad). God has 'visited and destroyed them' — shamad used for the destruction. This is permanent non-existence: no resurrection, no memory.

    Apoleia G684 — Broad Way to Destruction vs. Narrow Way to Life¶

    Enter ye in at the strait gate: for wide [is] the gate, and broad [is] the way, that leadeth to destruction, and many there be which go in thereat: Because strait [is] the gate, and narrow [is] the way, which leadeth unto life, and few there be that find it.

    Context: Binary contrast: two ways, two gates, two destinations — destruction (apoleia) vs. life (zoe). If life means continued conscious existence with God, then destruction means the opposite: cessation of existence. Jesus frames the choice as existence vs. non-existence, not two forms of eternal existence.

    Notes: apoleia (G684) as destination. Structural binary: destruction ≠ life. If life = conscious existence, destruction = non-existence.

    Apollymi G622 — God Destroys Soul and Body in Gehenna¶

    And fear not them which kill the body, but are not able to kill the soul: but rather fear him which is able to destroy both soul and body in hell.

    Context: Jesus' most explicit destruction statement. (2) God destroys BOTH soul AND body in gehenna — the entire person is destroyed, not just the body while the soul survives in torment.

    Apollymi G622 — Central Perish-vs-Life Contrast¶

    For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.

    Context: The central NT soteriology verse. apollymi (Aor Mid Subj 3s, 'should not perish') contrasted with echō zōēn aiōnion ('have everlasting life'). The subjunctive mood (me apolelatai) in a purpose clause: the PURPOSE of God's gift is that believers NOT perish. Perishing is the default; eternal life is the alternative.

    Notes: Parsed: apoletai (apollymi G622, Aor Mid Subj 3s, 'should perish') + eche zoen aionion (echō G2192 Pres Act Subj 3s + zōē G2222 + aiōnios G166). Structural binary: perish ≠ eternal life.

    Counter-Evidence — apollymi as 'Lost' (Recoverable)¶

    What man of you, having an hundred sheep, if he lose one of them, doth not leave the ninety and nine in the wilderness, and go after that which is lost, until he find it? And when he hath found [it], he layeth [it] on his shoulders, rejoicing. And when he cometh home, he calleth together [his] friends and neighbours, saying unto them, Rejoice with me; for I have found my sheep which was lost.

    Context: apollymi in its 'lost/straying' semantic category (recoverable). The sheep is apollymi (lost) but still alive and recoverable. ECT proponents argue this shows apollymi's core meaning is 'lost/ruined' rather than 'destroyed.' Counter-argument: illegitimate totality transfer — a word used in four semantic categories does not carry all four in every context. Luke 15 uses 'recoverable loss'; Matt 10:28 uses 'destroy utterly.'

    Notes: apollymi here = 'lost' (recoverable). Context: parable of seeking the lost. In judgment contexts (Matt 10:28, John 3:16), no recovery is possible.

    Counter-Evidence — Prodigal Son 'Lost and Found'¶

    For this my son was dead, and is alive again; he was lost, and is found. And they began to be merry. ... It was meet that we should make merry, and be glad: for this thy brother was dead, and is alive again; and was lost, and is found.

    Context: apollymi for the prodigal son — 'lost' but recoverable. The father describes him as 'dead and alive again, lost and found.' This is the 'recoverable loss' sense of apollymi, not the 'destroy utterly' sense. The parable context (seeking sinners) determines the meaning.

    Notes: apollymi = 'lost' (recoverable). Dead/alive language is metaphorical (spiritual death). Not applicable to eschatological judgment passages.

    Apollymi G622 — Historical Destructions as Paradigms¶

    They did eat, they drank, they married wives, they were given in marriage, until the day that Noe entered into the ark, and the flood came, and destroyed them all. ... But the same day that Lot went out of Sodom it rained fire and brimstone from heaven, and destroyed [them] all.

    Context: Jesus uses apollymi for two historical destructions: the Flood and Sodom. Both describe people who CEASED TO EXIST — the flood victims drowned and died; the Sodomites were incinerated. apollymi in these contexts unambiguously means 'killed/destroyed,' not 'lost but still existing.'

    Notes: apollymi Aor Act Ind 3s = 'destroyed them all.' Historical referent: people who ceased to exist. This fixes the eschatological meaning of apollymi.

    Death vs. Eternal Life — The Wages Statement¶

    For the wages of sin [is] death; but the gift of God [is] eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.

    Context: Paul's definitive contrast: death (thanatos) vs. eternal life (zōē aiōnios). The 'wages of sin' is DEATH — not torment, not suffering, not separation, but death. The contrast is existence (eternal life) vs. non-existence (death). Death is the natural result of sin; eternal life is a gift.

    Notes: thanatos (death) vs. zoe aionios (eternal life). Binary: death ≠ life. If eternal life = conscious existence, death = non-existence.

    Apoleia G684 — 'Whose End Is Destruction'¶

    Context: apoleia as the TELOS (end/outcome) for enemies of the cross. The end is destruction — not ongoing existence in a ruined state. Paul uses telos (end) + apoleia (destruction) to describe the final outcome.

    Notes: hon to telos apoleia = 'whose end [is] destruction.' telos = final outcome/termination. apoleia as the end state.

    Olethros G3639 — Sudden Eschatological Destruction¶

    Context: olethros (G3639) with aiphnidios ('sudden'). The destruction is sudden and inescapable — not gradual or ongoing. The labor-pains simile emphasizes surprise and inescapability, not duration.

    Notes: aiphnidios olethros = 'sudden destruction.' olethros here is a single catastrophic event, not an ongoing process.

    Olethros Aionios — Everlasting Destruction¶

    [Which is] a manifest token of the righteous judgment of God, that ye may be counted worthy of the kingdom of God, for which ye also suffer: Seeing [it is] a righteous thing with God to recompense tribulation to them that trouble you; And to you who are troubled rest with us, when the Lord Jesus shall be revealed from heaven with his mighty angels, In flaming fire taking vengeance on them that know not God, and that obey not the gospel of our Lord Jesus Christ: Who shall be punished with everlasting destruction from the presence of the Lord, and from the glory of his power; When he shall come to be glorified in his saints, and to be admired in all them that believe (because our testimony among you was believed) in that day.

(2) 'from the presence of the Lord' (apo prosopou) = 'away from' (separation) or 'proceeding from' (source)? Greek apo can mean either; if source, the Lord's presence CAUSES the destruction.

Evaluation: this is a living church member under discipline, not an impenitent person at final judgment. The 'that the spirit may be saved' clause explicitly shows this is NOT final destruction — it is categorically different from 2 Thess 1:9.

    Context: aionios + result-noun pattern. 'Eternal judgment' (aiōniou krimatos) = a judgment whose RESULT is permanent, not an ongoing process of judging. The judgment happens once; the verdict endures forever.

    Notes: aiōniou krimatos = 'eternal judgment.' Judgment is an event; 'eternal' modifies the permanence of the result.

    Duration Pattern — 'Eternal Salvation'¶

    And being made perfect, he became the author of eternal salvation unto all them that obey him;

    Context: aionios + result-noun pattern. 'Eternal salvation' (sōtērias aiōniou) = salvation whose RESULT endures forever, not an ongoing process of saving. Three Hebrews passages establish the pattern: aionios + result-noun = permanent result.

    Notes: sōtērias aiōniou = 'eternal salvation.' The third instance of the pattern. Applied to 2 Thess 1:9: 'everlasting destruction' = destruction whose result is permanent.

    Duration Word Test — olam = 3 Days¶

    I went down to the bottoms of the mountains; the earth with her bars [was] about me for ever: yet hast thou brought up my life from corruption, O LORD my God.

    Context: olam (H5769) = 'for ever' — but Jonah was in the fish for 3 DAYS. This proves olam is context-dependent, not always 'endless.' When applied to things that end (Jonah's confinement, Edom's smoke, a slave's service), olam means 'for the duration of' or 'permanently within the relevant timeframe.'

    Notes: olam = '3 days' in context. Proof that olam/aionios duration words are context-dependent.

    Duration Word Test — olam = Until Death¶

    Then his master shall bring him unto the judges; he shall also bring him to the door, or unto the door post; and his master shall bore his ear through with an aul; and he shall serve him for ever.

    Context: olam = 'for ever' in the context of a slave serving his master — obviously meaning 'for the rest of his life,' not 'endlessly.' Another proof that olam is not inherently 'eternal' but context-dependent.

    Notes: olam = 'for the rest of his life.' Context determines duration, not the word itself.

    Counter-Evidence — Apoleia as 'Waste'¶

    But when his disciples saw [it], they had indignation, saying, To what purpose [is] this waste?

    Context: apoleia used for 'waste' of ointment. ECT proponents argue the ointment still exists in a 'wasted' state. Counter-evaluation: the ointment was USED UP, GONE, CONSUMED — it ceased to exist as a useful substance. This actually SUPPORTS cessation: apoleia = the state of being used up/gone.

    Notes: apoleia = 'waste.' The ointment was consumed/used up — it ceased to exist as ointment. Supports cessation rather than 'ruined but still existing.'

    God Able to Save AND Destroy¶

    There is one lawgiver, who is able to save and to destroy: who art thou that judgest another?

    Context: James pairs sōzō (save) and apollymi (destroy) as the two things God is able to do. Binary: save OR destroy. Not save-to-blessing OR save-to-torment. God's power extends to both preservation and annihilation.

    Notes: sōsai kai apolesai = 'to save and to destroy.' The apollymi-sōzō antonym pair: God's two options are preservation and destruction.

    The Soul That Sins Shall Die¶

    Behold, all souls are mine; as the soul of the father, so also the soul of the son is mine: the soul that sinneth, it shall die.

    Context: Foundation text from etc7-01: the nephesh (soul) CAN DIE. If the soul is destructible, then apollymi (destroy both soul and body in gehenna, Matt 10:28) is literal, not metaphorical. The soul is not inherently immortal.

    Notes: hannephesh hachote't hi' tamut = 'the soul that sins, it shall die.' nephesh = soul. The soul is mortal and destructible.

    God Alone Has Immortality¶

    Who only hath immortality, dwelling in the light which no man can approach unto; whom no man hath seen, nor can see: to whom [be] honour and power everlasting. Amen.

    Context: Foundation text from etc7-02: God ALONE has athanasia (immortality). If only God possesses immortality, the wicked do not have it — they cannot live forever in torment. This removes the theological prerequisite for ECT (inherent soul immortality).

    Notes: monos echōn athanasian = 'alone having immortality.' If only God has immortality, the wicked cannot have eternal conscious existence in torment.

    Two Groups — Perishing vs. Saved¶

    For the preaching of the cross is to them that perish foolishness; but unto us which are saved it is the power of God.

    Context: Paul's binary: tois apollymenois ('those perishing') vs. tois sōzomenois ('those being saved'). Present participles indicate ongoing states leading to final outcomes. The two destinies are perishing and salvation — not two forms of eternal existence.

    Notes: apollymenois (Pres Mid/Pass Ptcp) vs. sōzomenois (Pres Pass Ptcp). Present participles = ongoing process toward final destiny.

    Two Groups — Saved and Perishing¶

    For we are unto God a sweet savour of Christ, in them that are saved, and in them that perish:

    Context: Same binary as 1 Cor 1:18: sōzomenois ('being saved') vs. apollymenois ('perishing'). Paul consistently uses the apollymi/sōzō antonym pair to describe the two destinies.

    Notes: Same apollymenois/sōzomenois binary. Paul's consistent framework: perishing vs. salvation.

    Never Perish — Negated apollymi¶

    And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any [man] pluck them out of my hand.

    Context: Jesus gives eternal life and promises believers 'shall never perish' (ou me apolōntai, emphatic double negative). The assurance is that believers will NOT perish — implying that perishing is the fate of those who do not receive eternal life.

    Notes: ou me apolōntai = 'they shall by no means perish.' Emphatic double negative. The promise of not-perishing presupposes that perishing is the default fate.

    God's Will — None of These Little Ones Perish¶

    Even so it is not the will of your Father which is in heaven, that one of these little ones should perish.

    Context: God's will is that none perish (apollymi). Parallel to 2 Pet 3:9 ('not willing that any should perish'). The Father's desire is that people NOT be destroyed — implying destruction is the alternative to salvation.

    Notes: apoletai (apollymi Aor Mid Subj 3s, 'should perish'). The Father's will = preservation, not destruction.

    Son of Perdition — Judas¶

    While I was with them in the world, I kept them in thy name: those that thou gavest me I have kept, and none of them is lost, but the son of perdition; that the scripture might be fulfilled.

    Context: ho huios tēs apōleias = 'the son of perdition/destruction.' Semitic destiny-genitive: the one whose destiny IS apoleia. Judas is characterized by and destined for destruction. Same title applied to the Antichrist in 2 Thess 2:3.

    Notes: ho huios tēs apōleias = 'the son of destruction.' Destiny-genitive = the person whose end is destruction.

    Apoleia — Peter's Curse on Simon¶

    But Peter said unto him, Thy money perish with thee, because thou hast thought that the gift of God may be purchased with money.

    Context: eis apōleian = 'unto destruction.' Peter wishes destruction on Simon Magus's money and potentially on Simon himself. The curse invokes destruction, not preservation in suffering.

    Notes: eis apōleian = directional: heading toward destruction.

    Vessels of Wrath Fitted for Destruction¶

    [What] if God, willing to shew [his] wrath, and to make his power known, endured with much longsuffering the vessels of wrath fitted to destruction:

    Context: skeuē orgēs katērtismena eis apōleian = 'vessels of wrath fitted/prepared for destruction.' The vessels are FITTED FOR destruction — apoleia is their purpose/destination. Paul uses apoleia as the final end of vessels of wrath.

    Notes: katērtismena eis apōleian = 'having been prepared for destruction.' Perfect passive participle = already fitted for their end.

    Non-Existence Declaration — Wicked Shall Not Be¶

    For yet a little while, and the wicked [shall] not [be]: yea, thou shalt diligently consider his place, and it [shall] not [be].

    Context: Psalm 37 is a wisdom psalm contrasting the fate of the righteous and wicked. Verse 10 uses explicit non-existence language (ayin) to describe the future state of the wicked.

    Notes: Uses ayin (non-existence particle) twice — the wicked shall not be, his place shall not be. This is cessation language, not ruin-while-continuing. Part of Psalm 37 triple destruction vocabulary cluster (abad v.20, kalah v.20, shamad v.38).

    Olethros Aionios — Everlasting Destruction from the Presence of the Lord¶

    Who shall be punished with everlasting destruction from the presence of the Lord, and from the glory of his power;

    Context: Paul describes the punishment awaiting those who do not know God and do not obey the gospel. This is the key eschatological olethros passage.

    Notes: Key passage combining olethros (G3639) with aionios (G166). The construction 'olethros aionios' could mean destruction that is everlasting in its result (conditionalist) or destruction that is everlasting in its process (ECT). The phrase 'from the presence of the Lord' (apo prosopou) is debated.

    Counter-Evidence: Apollymi as 'Lost' (Recoverable) — Lost Sheep, Coin, Son¶

    What man of you, having an hundred sheep, if he lose one of them, doth not leave the ninety and nine in the wilderness, and go after that which is lost, until he find it? And when he hath found [it], he layeth [it] on his shoulders, rejoicing. And when he cometh home, he calleth together [his] friends and neighbours, saying unto them, Rejoice with me; for I have found my sheep which was lost. I say unto you, that likewise joy shall be in heaven over one sinner that repenteth, more than over ninety and nine just persons, which need no repentance. Either what woman having ten pieces of silver, if she lose one piece, doth not light a candle, and sweep the house, and seek diligently till she find [it]? And when she hath found [it], she calleth [her] friends and [her] neighbours together, saying, Rejoice with me; for I have found the piece which I had lost. Likewise, I say unto you, there is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner that repenteth. And he said, A certain man had two sons: And the younger of them said to [his] father, Father, give me the portion of goods that falleth [to me]. And he divided unto them [his] living. And not many days after the younger son gathered all together, and took his journey into a far country, and there wasted his substance with riotous living. And when he had spent all, there arose a mighty famine in that land; and he began to be in want. And he went and joined himself to a citizen of that country; and he sent him into his fields to feed swine. And he would fain have filled his belly with the husks that the swine did eat: and no man gave unto him. And when he came to himself, he said, How many hired servants of my father's have bread enough and to spare, and I perish with hunger! I will arise and go to my father, and will say unto him, Father, I have sinned against heaven, and before thee, And am no more worthy to be called thy son: make me as one of thy hired servants. And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him. And the son said unto him, Father, I have sinned against heaven, and in thy sight, and am no more worthy to be called thy son. But the father said to his servants, Bring forth the best robe, and put [it] on him; and put a ring on his hand, and shoes on [his] feet: And bring hither the fatted calf, and kill [it]; and let us eat, and be merry: For this my son was dead, and is alive again; he was lost, and is found. And they began to be merry. Now his elder son was in the field: and as he came and drew nigh to the house, he heard musick and dancing. And he called one of the servants, and asked what these things meant. And he said unto him, Thy brother is come; and thy father hath killed the fatted calf, because he hath received him safe and sound. And he was angry, and would not go in: therefore came his father out, and intreated him. And he answering said to [his] father, Lo, these many years do I serve thee, neither transgressed I at any time thy commandment: and yet thou never gavest me a kid, that I might make merry with my friends: But as soon as this thy son was come, which hath devoured thy living with harlots, thou hast killed for him the fatted calf. And he said unto him, Son, thou art ever with me, and all that I have is thine. It was meet that we should make merry, and be glad: for this thy brother was dead, and is alive again; and was lost, and is found.

    Context: Jesus tells three parables about lost things in response to Pharisees criticizing him for receiving sinners. All three use apollymi (G622) in the sense of 'lost' — something that can be recovered.

    Notes: Critical counter-evidence for conditionalist reading of apollymi. In these parables, apollymi means 'lost' not 'destroyed' — the sheep, coin, and son are all recovered. ECT proponents argue this shows apollymi does not necessarily mean annihilation. However, the recoverable sense of apollymi in parables about seeking the lost does not govern eschatological judgment passages where destruction is explicitly irreversible (Matt 10:28, John 3:16). The context of each usage determines meaning.
