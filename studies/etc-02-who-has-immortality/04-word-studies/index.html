    Word Studies¶

    Question¶

    Who possesses immortality? Is the soul inherently immortal or is immortality a divine gift? Examine athanasia (G110), aphtharsia (G861), thnetos (G2349).

    G110 — athanasia¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G110-athanasia.md

    Occurrences: 3

    Deathlessness. Alpha-privative of thanatos (death). 3 NT occurrences: 1 Tim 6:16 (God alone has it), 1 Cor 15:53-54 (mortals must put it on at resurrection). All accusative singular feminine. Exclusively Pauline. Never applied to the human soul. No Hebrew source via LXX.

    Semantic categories: exclusive divine attribute, eschatological transformation at resurrection

    Key verses: 1 Tim 6:16, 1 Cor 15:53, 1 Cor 15:54

    Relevance: Core term for immortality — only 3 occurrences, all teaching either God's exclusive possession or believers' future acquisition through resurrection.

    G861 — aphtharsia¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G861-aphtharsia.md

    Occurrences: 8

    Incorruptibility/un-decay. Alpha-privative of phtheiro (to corrupt). 8 occurrences across 5 books, exclusively Pauline. KJV: incorruption (4x, 50%), immortality (2x, 25%), sincerity (2x, 25%). Three semantic categories: resurrection transformation (4 occ.), object of aspiration (2 occ.), ethical purity (2 occ.).

    Semantic categories: resurrection transformation, object of aspiration, ethical purity

    Key verses: 1 Cor 15:42, 1 Cor 15:50, 1 Cor 15:53, 1 Cor 15:54, Rom 2:7, 2 Tim 1:10, Eph 6:24, Tit 2:7

    Relevance: Near-synonym of athanasia, paired in 1 Cor 15:53-54. Something put on at resurrection or sought — never predicated of the human soul as a present possession.

    G2349 — thnetos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2349-thnetos.md

    Occurrences: 6

    Mortal/liable to die. From thnesko (to die). 6 occurrences across 3 books, exclusively Pauline. KJV: mortal (5x, 83%), mortality (1x, 17%). Used with soma/sarx in 3 of 6; substantival in the other 3. In 1 Cor 15:53 'this mortal' is used absolutely. LXX maps to adam (H120).

    Semantic categories: mortal body under sin, mortal body awaiting resurrection, mortal-to-immortal transformation, life in mortal flesh

    Key verses: Rom 6:12, Rom 8:11, 1 Cor 15:53, 1 Cor 15:54, 2 Cor 4:11, 2 Cor 5:4

    Relevance: Core term describing the present human condition. Paired with athanasia as its antonym in 1 Cor 15:53. Paul never names an immortal counterpart to the mortal body.

    G862 — aphthartos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G862-aphthartos.md

    Occurrences: 7

    Incorruptible/imperishable (adjective). Alpha-privative of phtheiro. 7 occurrences across 4 books. Applied to: God (Rom 1:23, 1 Tim 1:17), resurrection body (1 Cor 15:52), believer's crown (1 Cor 9:25), inheritance (1 Pet 1:4), seed (1 Pet 1:23), inner person (1 Pet 3:4). Never applied to the human soul as a present possession.

    Semantic categories: divine nature, resurrection body, imperishable reward, imperishable source, imperishable character

    Key verses: Rom 1:23, 1 Tim 1:17, 1 Cor 15:52, 1 Cor 9:25, 1 Pet 1:4, 1 Pet 1:23, 1 Pet 3:4

    Relevance: Adjective form of aphtharsia. Pattern: God has it inherently; believers receive it at resurrection or as future reward; the human soul is never described with it.

    G5356 — phthora¶

    Occurrences: 9

    Corruption/decay/ruin. From phtheiro (to corrupt). 9 NT occurrences. KJV: corruption (4x, 44%), of corruption (2x, 22%), perish (1x), the corruption (1x), destroyed (1x). Direct antonym of aphtharsia. Key verses: Rom 8:21 (creation subject to bondage of corruption), 1 Cor 15:42,50 (sown in corruption, corruption cannot inherit incorruption), Gal 6:8 (sow to flesh, reap corruption), 2 Pet 1:4 (corruption in the world through lust), 2 Pet 2:12 (perish in own corruption).

    Semantic categories: physical decay/decomposition, moral corruption, eschatological ruin

    Key verses: Rom 8:21, 1 Cor 15:42, 1 Cor 15:50, Gal 6:8, 2 Pet 1:4, 2 Pet 2:12

    Relevance: The negative counterpart to aphtharsia. Defines what humans presently ARE (corruptible, subject to decay) vs. what they must BECOME (incorruptible). The corruption/incorruption word family is the mirror of the mortal/immortal vocabulary.

    G5349 — phthartos¶

    Occurrences: 6

    Corruptible/perishable (adjective). From phtheiro. 6 NT occurrences. KJV: corruptible (3x, 50%), others include 'a corruptible', 'to corruptible', 'with corruptible things'. Direct antonym of aphthartos. Key verses: Rom 1:23 (corruptible man vs. uncorruptible God), 1 Cor 9:25 (corruptible crown vs. incorruptible), 1 Cor 15:53-54 (this corruptible must put on incorruption), 1 Pet 1:18 (not redeemed with corruptible things), 1 Pet 1:23 (corruptible seed vs. incorruptible).

    Semantic categories: human nature (corruptible man), perishable rewards, mortal body requiring transformation

    Key verses: Rom 1:23, 1 Cor 9:25, 1 Cor 15:53, 1 Cor 15:54, 1 Pet 1:18, 1 Pet 1:23

    Relevance: Adjective describing the present human condition — corruptible/perishable. Paired directly with aphthartos in 1 Cor 15:53-54 and 1 Cor 9:25. The transformation from phthartos to aphthartos is the resurrection.

    G1312 — diaphthora¶

    Occurrences: 6

    Decay/corruption (thorough decay). From dia + phtheiro. 6 NT occurrences, all in Acts (2:27, 2:31, 13:34, 13:35, 13:36, 13:37). KJV: corruption (100%). All in context of messianic resurrection proof — Christ's body did not see corruption (decay), but David's did. Proves resurrection is bodily, not merely spiritual.

    Semantic categories: physical bodily decay, messianic resurrection proof

    Key verses: Acts 2:27, Acts 2:31, Acts 13:34, Acts 13:35, Acts 13:36, Acts 13:37

    Relevance: Concentrated usage in Peter's and Paul's sermons proving Christ's bodily resurrection. The argument is about physical decay — Christ was raised before decomposition occurred. This supports a physicalist anthropology where resurrection, not an immortal soul, is the hope.

    G1746 — endyo¶

    Occurrences: 31

    To put on/clothe oneself with. From en + dyo (to sink into). ~29-31 NT occurrences. KJV: put on, clothe, arrayed, etc. Used in 1 Cor 15:53-54 for putting on immortality. Every Pauline usage describes acquisition of something not previously possessed: put on Christ (Rom 13:14, Gal 3:27), put on new man (Eph 4:24, Col 3:10), put on armor (Eph 6:11), put on virtues (Col 3:12). Never used for unveiling/revealing something already present. The TR-labash-endyo translation relationship study confirms consistent acquisition meaning.

    Semantic categories: clothing metaphor for acquisition, spiritual transformation, moral virtue

    Key verses: 1 Cor 15:53, 1 Cor 15:54, Rom 13:14, Gal 3:27, Eph 4:24, Eph 6:11, Col 3:10, Col 3:12

    Relevance: The verb used for receiving immortality in 1 Cor 15:53-54. Its consistent meaning of acquisition (not unveiling) confirms that immortality is received at resurrection, not revealed as already present.

    G3441 — monos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G3441-mónos.md

    Occurrences: 47

    Only/alone. 47 NT occurrences. KJV: alone (44.7%), only (44.7%). Four semantic categories: physical isolation, exclusivity/restriction, 'not X alone but also Y', doxological/divine exclusivity. In 1 Tim 6:16: monos echon athanasiān — 'only having immortality.' In every doxological use (1 Tim 1:17, 6:15-16, Jude 25, Rom 16:27, Rev 15:4), monos marks unqualified divine exclusivity.

    Semantic categories: physical isolation, exclusivity, mere/without accompaniment, doxological divine exclusivity

    Key verses: 1 Tim 6:15-16, John 17:3, 1 Tim 1:17, Jude 1:25

    Relevance: The qualifier in 1 Tim 6:16 establishing God's exclusive possession of immortality. In NT doxological contexts, monos admits no qualification — 'the only true God' (John 17:3), 'the only wise God' (1 Tim 1:17). Adding 'self-existent vs. derived' to monos in 1 Tim 6:16 has no textual basis.

    G2222 — zoe¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2222-zoe.md

    Occurrences: 134

    Life. 126 unique verses (134 tokens), 22 books. Dominant usage: 'eternal life' (~43 of 126). Life as present possession in the Son (John/1 John), life vs. death contrast, symbolic objects (bread/book/tree/water of life), divine attribute. Co-occurs with aionios 45 times. Primarily Johannine (John + 1 John = 42 of 126).

    Semantic categories: eternal/eschatological life, present possession in the Son, life vs. death contrast, symbolic objects of life, divine attribute

    Key verses: John 3:16, Rom 6:23, 1 John 5:11-12, John 5:26, John 17:3

    Relevance: The life that believers receive — always described as a gift, promise, or possession through Christ. Never described as an inherent human attribute apart from God.

    G2288 — thanatos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2288-thanatos.md

    Occurrences: 119

    Death. 106 unique verses (119 tokens), 15 books. From thnesko (to die). Seven semantic categories: physical/biological death, consequence of sin (Rom 5:12, 6:23), reigning cosmic power (Rom 5:14,17), Christ's atoning death, spiritual death/passage, death personified/second death, shadow of death. Root noun negated by alpha-privative to form athanasia (G110).

    Semantic categories: physical death, consequence of sin, cosmic power, Christ's atoning death, spiritual death, personified/second death, shadow of death

    Key verses: Rom 5:12, Rom 6:23, 1 Cor 15:26, Rev 20:14, Rev 21:4

    Relevance: The reality that athanasia negates. Death entered through sin (Rom 5:12), is the wages of sin (Rom 6:23), is the last enemy to be destroyed (1 Cor 15:26). Death is abolished only at the resurrection/new creation — not at the moment of dying.

    G5590 — psyche¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G5590-psyche.md

    Occurrences: 104

    Soul/life. 105 NT occurrences (87 tracked). From psycho (to breathe). KJV: soul/souls (59.8%), life/lives (33.3%), mind/heart (4.6%). Five semantic categories: inner self/emotions, physical life, person/individual, living creature, mind/will. Hebrew source: nephesh (H5315, 599 LXX co-occurrences). Never modified by 'immortal' in any NT passage.

    Semantic categories: inner self/emotions, physical life, person/individual, living creature, mind/will

    Key verses: Matt 10:28, Matt 22:37, Rev 16:3, John 10:11

    Relevance: The word that would need to be modified by 'immortal' for the innate immortality position — but never is. Can be destroyed (Matt 10:28), can die (Rev 16:3), is used for corpses in the OT equivalent (nephesh met).

    H5315 — nephesh¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H5315-nephesh.md

    Occurrences: 788

    Soul/living being/person. 682 unique verses (788 tokens), 31 books. From naphash (to breathe, refresh). KJV: soul (64%), life (14%), person (4%), reflexive (2%), creature (1%), body/dead body (1%). Used for animals (Gen 1:20-21,24), the whole living person (Gen 2:7), corpses (Lev 21:11, Num 6:6). Can die (Ezek 18:4,20). Never modified by 'immortal.'

    Semantic categories: breathing creature, whole person, life/vitality, inner self/desire, devotion to God, reflexive pronoun, corpse

    Key verses: Gen 2:7, Ezek 18:4, Ezek 18:20, Lev 21:11, Num 6:6

    Relevance: The Hebrew word for 'soul' — used for animals and humans alike, used for corpses, subject to death. The 753 OT occurrences never once describe nephesh as immortal.

    G622 — apollymi¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G622-apollymi.md

    Occurrences: 102

    To destroy fully/perish/lose. 86 unique verses (102 tokens), 15 books. Active voice: destroy. Middle/passive: perish, be lost. Key soteriological antonym of sozo (save). Seven semantic categories including physical destruction, divine judgment/eschatological ruin, perishing in eternal judgment, losing one's life.

    Semantic categories: physical destruction, divine judgment, eschatological perishing, losing life, losing possession, perishability

    Key verses: John 3:16, Matt 10:28, 1 Cor 15:18, 2 Pet 3:9

    Relevance: The verb used for the fate of the unsaved (perish) and for God's ability to destroy the soul (Matt 10:28). In John 3:16, perishing is the alternative to everlasting life — consistent with conditional immortality.

    G166 — aionios¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G166-aionios.md

    Occurrences: 71

    Eternal/everlasting. 71 tokens in 69 verses, 19 books. From aion (age). KJV: eternal (46.4%), everlasting (30.4%). Dominant usage: modifies zoe (life) ~43 times (62%). Six semantic categories including eternal life as gift, eternal judgment, eternal divine attributes.

    Semantic categories: eternal life as gift, eternal judgment, divine attributes, eternal realities, eternal purpose, eternal kingdom

    Key verses: John 3:16, Rom 6:23, Matt 25:46, Tit 1:2

    Relevance: Modifies the life believers receive — 'eternal life' is something given, promised, sought, and received through Christ. The adjective qualifies the duration of the life that immortality consists of.

    H2416 — chay¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H2416-chay.md

    Occurrences: 531

    Alive/living (adjective and noun). 449 unique verses (531 tokens), 29 books. In nephesh chayyah (living soul, Gen 2:7) and applied equally to animals (Gen 1:20-21,24). Life is in the blood (Lev 17:11). Oath formula 'as YHWH lives.' LXX maps to zoe (G2222), zao (G2198), therion (G2342).

    Semantic categories: living creatures, life/vitality, being alive, oath formula, living water

    Key verses: Gen 1:20, Gen 2:7, Lev 17:11, Deut 30:15

    Relevance: In Gen 2:7, man became a nephesh chayyah (living soul) — the same term used for animals. Life (chayyim) is presented as a divine gift, not an inherent property.

    H5397 — nshamah¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H5397-nshamah.md

    Occurrences: 24

    Breath/spirit/inspiration. 24 unique verses (25 tokens), 10 books. From nasham (to blow). KJV: breath (42%), breathe (21%), blast (12.5%), spirit (8.3%). Five semantic categories: breath of life at creation (Gen 2:7), divine destructive blast, total annihilation formula, human inner life/illumination, every living being. LXX maps primarily to pnoe (G4157, 14x), then pneuma (G4151, 10x).

    Semantic categories: breath of life, divine blast, total annihilation, inner illumination, every living being

    Key verses: Gen 2:7, Job 33:4, Job 34:14, Ps 150:6

    Relevance: The divine breath that animates humans (Gen 2:7). When God withdraws neshamah, all flesh perishes (Job 34:14-15). Life depends on God's continuing gift of breath.

    H7307 — ruach¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H7307-ruach.md

    Occurrences: 434

    Spirit/breath/wind. 434 OT occurrences. KJV: spirit (53.7%), wind (21.4%), breath (7.6%). Seven semantic categories: Spirit of God, wind, breath/life-force, human spirit/disposition, spirit beings, vanity (Ecclesiastes), compass direction. LXX maps primarily to pneuma (G4151, 235x).

    Semantic categories: Spirit of God, wind, breath/life-force, human spirit, spirit beings, vanity, compass direction

    Key verses: Gen 1:2, Ecc 12:7, Ps 104:29-30, Job 34:14

    Relevance: The life-force that returns to God at death (Ecc 12:7). Its withdrawal causes death (Ps 104:29, Job 34:14). Ruach is the animating principle God gives and takes back — not a surviving conscious entity.

    G4151 — pneuma¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G4151-pneuma.md

    Occurrences: 385

    Spirit/breath/wind. 385 NT occurrences. From pneo (to blow). KJV: Spirit/spirit (62%), Ghost (25.1%), spirits (7.6%). Eight semantic domains spanning Holy Spirit, evil spirits, human spirit, life-force, disembodied spirits, wind, figurative power. Hebrew source: ruach (H7307, 235 LXX co-occurrences).

    Semantic categories: Holy Spirit, evil spirits, human spirit, life-force, disembodied spirits, wind, figurative power

    Key verses: Matt 27:50, Luke 23:46, James 2:26, Rev 11:11

    Relevance: NT counterpart to ruach. As life-force, its departure = death (Matt 27:50, James 2:26). The spirit is what God gives and what returns to God — not a self-sustaining immortal entity.

    H4194 — maveth¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H4194-maveth.md

    Occurrences: 170

    Death. 153 unique verses (170 tokens), 27 books. From muth (to die). KJV: death (92%). Seven semantic categories: biographical marker, legal penalty, personified cosmic power, moral contrast (life vs. death), deliverance from death, realm/destination, death defeated (Isa 25:8, Hos 13:14). LXX maps overwhelmingly to thanatos (G2288, 97 co-occ., PMI 5.98).

    Semantic categories: biographical marker, legal penalty, personified power, life vs. death contrast, deliverance from death, realm/destination, death defeated

    Key verses: Deut 30:15,19, Isa 25:8, Hos 13:14, Prov 18:21

    Relevance: OT death vocabulary. Death is presented as the opposite of life (Deut 30:15), something from which God delivers (Ps 33:19), and something ultimately swallowed up in victory (Isa 25:8 → 1 Cor 15:54).

    H6 — abad¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H6-abad.md

    Occurrences: 219

    To perish/be lost/destroy. 219 tokens in 174 verses. Qal (61%): intransitive — 'to perish, be lost.' Piel/Hiphil: causative — 'to destroy.' KJV: perish (55%), destroy (32%), lost (6%). Primary Hebrew equivalent of apollymi (G622) via LXX (141 co-occurrences, PMI 6.14).

    Semantic categories: perishing/being lost, causative destruction, ready to perish

    Key verses: Ps 146:4, Deut 30:18, Ps 37:20

    Relevance: Hebrew destruction verb, mapped to apollymi in the LXX. In Ps 146:4, thoughts 'perish' (abad) at death — consciousness ceases.
