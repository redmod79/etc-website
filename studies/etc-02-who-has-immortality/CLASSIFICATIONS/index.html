    CLASSIFICATIONS.md — Per-Item Audit Trail¶

    Study: Who Possesses Immortality?¶

    This document contains the full Tree 3/4 classification walkthrough for every E-item classified as Cond. or ECT, and every N-item. Parser tool output was used for Gate 2 (Grammar) verification.

    E-Item Classifications (Tree 3)¶

    E1 (1 Tim 6:16) -- "God alone has immortality" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- No destruction vocabulary applied to wicked in this verse. V2 -- No conscious-torment vocabulary. However, this verse uses immortality vocabulary to establish who possesses it (God alone), which by exclusion establishes that no creature has it. The statement has positional force: "only" (monos) excludes all others from possessing immortality. Candidate: COND. (establishes that immortality is not inherent in humans).
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): God is the subject — not a symbolic entity. This verse describes a literal divine attribute. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: monos (G3441, A-NSM Nom Sg M) + echon (G2192, V-PAP-NSM Pres Act Ptcp Nom Sg M) + athanasiān (G110, N-ASF Acc Sg F). The present active participle echon denotes ongoing possession. Monos is attributive — "the only one having." The grammar unambiguously supports exclusive attribution. No alternative parsing. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Pauline epistle — didactic doxological formula. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E24 (1 Tim 1:17 — God as aphthartos), E23 (Rom 1:23 — God aphthartos vs. man phthartos), E27 (John 5:26 — Father has life in himself). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E2 (1 Cor 15:42) -- "Sown in corruption, raised in incorruption" -- Classified: Neutral

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "Corruption" (phthora) describes the present condition, not destruction of the wicked specifically. V2 -- No conscious-torment vocabulary. Neither V1 nor V2 triggered for wicked's fate. This is a descriptive contrast between present and resurrection states.
    • Result: Neutral. Both sides accept this describes the resurrection transformation.

    E3 (1 Cor 15:50) -- "Corruption cannot inherit incorruption" -- Classified: Neutral

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- No destruction vocabulary applied to the wicked. V2 -- No torment vocabulary. This states the necessity of transformation — both sides agree.
    • Result: Neutral. Observational statement about incompatibility of corruption and incorruption.

    E4 (1 Cor 15:53-54) -- "This mortal must put on immortality" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "Mortal" (thnetos) describes present human condition as liable to die; "put on" (endyo) immortality implies current non-possession. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "This mortal" (touto to thneton) — substantival, referring to mortal humans. Literal human beings. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: thneton (G2349, A-NSN Nom Sg N) used substantivally; endysasthai (G1746, V-AMN Aor Mid Inf) — aorist middle infinitive, "to clothe oneself with"; athanasiān (G110, N-ASF Acc Sg F) — accusative direct object. The grammar unambiguously shows immortality as the object acquired by the mortal subject via a verb of clothing/acquisition. No alternative parsing. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Pauline epistle — didactic theological argument. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E1 (God alone has immortality — humans must acquire it), E5 (humans seek immortality), E6 (Christ brought immortality to light). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E5 (Rom 2:7) -- "Those who seek immortality receive eternal life" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- Immortality is something sought (non-possession implied). Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): Those who by patient well-doing seek — literal human beings. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: zetousin (G2212, V-PAP-DPM Pres Act Ptcp Dat Pl M — "seeking"); aphtharsian (G861, N-ASF Acc Sg F). Present active participle = ongoing seeking. The grammar shows aphtharsia as the object of active seeking. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Pauline epistle — didactic. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E1, E4 (immortality not presently possessed). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E6 (2 Tim 1:10) -- "Christ abolished death, brought life and immortality to light" -- Classified: Neutral

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "Abolished death" uses destruction vocabulary, but applied to death itself, not to the wicked. V2 -- No torment vocabulary. This describes Christ's salvific work — both sides accept it.
    • Result: Neutral. Observational statement about Christ's work that both sides accept.

    E7 (Ezek 18:4, 20) -- "The soul that sinneth, it shall die" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "Die" (muth, H4191) — death vocabulary applied to the nephesh (soul). Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): The nephesh (soul) — referring to literal human persons (the sinning individual). PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: hannephesh (H5315, Noun.fs.Abs with article — "the soul") + hachote't (chata, Verb.Qal.Ptcp.fs — "the sinning one") + hi' (PersPron.3fs — "she/it") + tamut (muth, Verb.Qal.Impf.3fs — "shall die"). The nephesh is the grammatical subject of the verb "die." The Qal imperfect tamut is the standard form for dying. No alternative parsing — the soul is the subject of dying. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Prophetic oracle — direct speech of God ("Thus says the Lord GOD"). Didactic direct-speech prophecy. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E8 (God can destroy soul), E21 (living soul died), E22 (nephesh met — dead soul). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E8 (Matt 10:28) -- "God is able to destroy both soul and body in gehenna" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "Destroy" (apollymi, G622) applied to the soul (psyche). Destruction vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): Jesus speaking to the twelve about literal human judgment. The souls/bodies to be destroyed are literal human persons. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: apolesai (G622, V-AAN Aor Act Inf — "to destroy") + kai psychen kai soma (both soul [G5590, N-ASF] and body [G4983, N-ASN]) + en geenne (in gehenna [G1067, N-DSF]). Active voice infinitive with God as the agent. The verb apollymi means "to destroy fully." Both soul and body are direct objects of destruction. No alternative parsing — the grammar is straightforward. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Gospel — didactic instruction from Jesus to his disciples. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E7 (soul can die), E21 (living soul died). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E9 (Gen 2:7 + Gen 1:20-21) -- "Man became a living soul; same term used for animals" -- Classified: Neutral

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- No destruction vocabulary. V2 -- No torment vocabulary. This is a creation-formula observation that both sides accept.
    • Result: Neutral. Observational statement about the creation formula.

    E10 (Gen 3:22-24) -- "Man barred from tree of life lest he live forever" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- The purpose clause "lest he live forever" implies man will NOT live forever without the tree — mortality/cessation implied. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): Adam — literal human being. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: pen (Conj — "lest") + yishlach (shalach, Verb.Qal.Impf.3ms — "he send forth") + wachay (chayah, Verb.Qal.Perf.3ms — "and live") + leolam (olam, Noun.ms.Abs — "to eternity"). The pen clause is a purpose clause of prevention — God acts to prevent Adam from living forever. The grammar unambiguously shows that without the tree of life, Adam would not live forever. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Genesis narrative — historical report of divine action. Didactic. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E1 (God alone has immortality), E4 (mortals must acquire immortality). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E11 (Rom 6:23) -- "Wages of sin is death; gift of God is eternal life" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "Death" (thanatos) as the wages/penalty of sin. Death vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): Universal human subjects — sinners receive death; believers receive life. Literal humans. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: opsonia (G3800, N-NPN — "wages") + hamartias (G266, N-GSF — "of sin") + thanatos (G2288, N-NSM — "death"); charisma (G5486, N-NSN — "gift") + theou (G2316, N-GSM — "of God") + zoe aionios (G2222 + G166 — "eternal life"). The binary is grammatically exhaustive: two subjects (sin/God), two wages (death/eternal life). No third option is stated. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Pauline epistle — didactic. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E12 (John 3:16 — perish vs. life), E13 (1 John 5:11-12 — no life apart from Christ). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E12 (John 3:16) -- "Perish vs. have everlasting life" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "Perish" (apollymi, G622) — destruction vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "Whoever believes" — literal human beings. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: apoletai (G622, V-2AMS-3S Aor Mid Subj — "should perish") + eche (G2192, V-PAS-3S Pres Act Subj — "may have") + zoen aionion (eternal life). The construction hina me apoletai all' eche presents two mutually exclusive alternatives: perishing or having eternal life. The grammar is straightforward. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Gospel — didactic discourse by Jesus. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E11 (death vs. life), E13 (no life apart from Christ). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E13 (1 John 5:11-12) -- "He that hath not the Son hath not life" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- Absolute negation of life apart from Christ — those without Christ "hath not life." Cessation/non-possession of life. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): Those who have or do not have the Son — literal human beings. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): ouk echei ten zoen — "does not have the life." Present active indicative with negative particle. The statement is absolute. No alternative parsing. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Johannine epistle — didactic. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E11, E12. No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E14 (Job 34:14-15) -- "If God gathers spirit and breath, all flesh perishes" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "Perish" (gawa, expire) + "return to dust" — death/cessation vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "All flesh" (kol-basar) and "man" (adam) — literal humans and all living creatures. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: yigwa' (gawa, Verb.Qal.Impf.3ms — "expire") + kol-basar (all flesh) + yashub (shub, Verb.Qal.Impf.3ms — "return") + al-aphar (unto dust). The conditional "if God gathers his spirit and breath" governs the result "all flesh expires and man returns to dust." Grammar straightforward. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Wisdom literature — Elihu's direct theological speech. Didactic discourse. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E29 (Ps 104:29 — take away breath = death), E30 (Acts 17:25 — God gives all life). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E15 (Ecc 9:5) -- "The dead know not anything" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- Unconsciousness in death — cessation vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "The dead" (hammetim) — literal dead humans. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): hammetim enam yod'im me'umah — "the dead know not anything." Standard Hebrew negation. Grammar unambiguous. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Wisdom literature — Qoheleth's observation. Didactic. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E16 (Ps 146:4 — thoughts perish), E32 (Ps 115:17 — dead don't praise), E33 (Ecc 9:10 — no work in sheol). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E16 (Ps 146:4) -- "His breath goeth forth; in that very day his thoughts perish" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "Thoughts perish" (abad) — cessation of consciousness. Destruction/cessation vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): A human ruler/person whose breath departs — literal human. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): tetse rucho (breath goes out) + abdu eshtonotayv (his thoughts perish, abad Qal Perfect). Abad = perish/be lost. Applied to thoughts at the moment of death. Grammar clear. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Psalm — didactic wisdom observation within a praise psalm. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E15, E32, E33. No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E17 (1 Cor 15:21-22) -- "By man came death; by man came resurrection" -- Classified: Neutral

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "Death" mentioned, but as a universal fact, not as punishment of the wicked specifically. V2 -- No torment. This is an observational statement about Adam/Christ typology both sides accept.
    • Result: Neutral.

    E18 (1 Cor 15:26) -- "The last enemy destroyed is death" -- Classified: Neutral

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "Destroyed" (katargeo) applied to death itself, not to the wicked. Both sides agree death is the last enemy to be abolished.
    • Result: Neutral. Both ECT and conditionalist scholars accept this statement.

    E19 (1 Cor 15:18) -- "If no resurrection, the dead in Christ are perished" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "Perished" (apollymi) applied to dead believers — destruction/cessation vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "Those fallen asleep in Christ" — literal dead believers. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: apolonto (G622, V-2AMI-3P Aor Mid Ind Pl 3rd — "perished"). Aorist middle indicative — they have perished. The grammar is clear: without resurrection, dead believers have been destroyed. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Pauline epistle — didactic argument (reductio ad absurdum). PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E11, E12 (death vs. life binary). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E20 (Luke 20:35-36) -- "Those who attain the resurrection can no longer die" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "Neither can they die any more" — immortality is a quality of the resurrection state, not the present state. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): Those accounted worthy of the resurrection — literal human beings. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): oute apothanein eti dynantai — "neither can they die anymore." Standard Greek construction. The inability to die is predicated of the resurrection state, not the present. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Gospel — Jesus' didactic response to the Sadducees. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E4 (mortal puts on immortality at resurrection), E1 (God alone currently has immortality). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E21 (Rev 16:3) -- "Every living soul died in the sea" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "Died" (apothnesko) applied to psyche (soul). Death vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "Every living soul" (pasa psyche zosa) in the sea — marine creatures. Not literal wicked humans in final judgment. FAIL — subject is sea creatures, not humans facing eschatological judgment.
    • Step 3 (Reclassification): Gate 1 fails because the subjects are marine creatures, not humans facing final judgment. However, the observation that psyche can die is a factual textual observation both sides must accept. Corrected observation: "The term psyche (soul) can be the subject of dying." Re-enter V1/V2: this is a factual vocabulary observation. Neither positional vocabulary applies to a factual observation about word usage.
    • Result: Reclassified to Neutral. The observation that psyche/soul can die is a textual fact both sides accept; the positional implications are drawn elsewhere (E7, E8).

    E22 (Lev 21:11, Num 6:6) -- "Nephesh met — dead soul used for corpses" -- Classified: Neutral

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "Dead" (met) applied to nephesh (soul). However, this is a lexical observation about word usage, not a direct statement about the fate of the wicked. Both sides accept that nephesh is used for corpses in these passages.
    • Result: Neutral. A textual/lexical observation both sides accept.

    E23 (Rom 1:23) -- "Glory of the incorruptible God changed into corruptible images" -- Classified: Neutral

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- No destruction vocabulary for the wicked. V2 -- No torment. Observational contrast between God (aphthartos) and creatures (phthartos).
    • Result: Neutral.

    E24 (1 Tim 1:17) -- "The King eternal, immortal, invisible, the only wise God" -- Classified: Neutral

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1/V2 -- Neither destruction nor torment vocabulary. Divine doxology.
    • Result: Neutral.

    E25 (1 Cor 15:52) -- "The dead raised incorruptible, we shall be changed" -- Classified: Neutral

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1/V2 -- No destruction or torment vocabulary for the wicked. Describes resurrection transformation.
    • Result: Neutral.

    E26 (Mal 4:1-3) -- "Wicked shall be stubble, ashes under feet, neither root nor branch" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "Burn up" (ba'ar), "stubble," "ashes," "neither root nor branch" — destruction/cessation vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "All the proud and all that do wickedly" — literal wicked humans. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): wehayah lahem qash (they shall be stubble) + welibat otam (and it shall burn them up) + lo' ya'azob lahem shoresh we'anaph (it shall leave them neither root nor branch). Grammar unambiguous — the wicked are the subject of burning and becoming ashes. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Prophetic oracle — direct-speech prophecy ("saith the LORD of hosts"). Didactic. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E34 (Ps 37:9-10 — wicked shall not be), E11 (wages of sin is death). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E27 (John 5:26) -- "The Father has life in himself; gave the Son to have life in himself" -- Classified: Neutral

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1/V2 -- Neither destruction nor torment. Describes divine self-existent life.
    • Result: Neutral. Both sides accept this describes divine self-existent life.

    E28 (Isa 25:8) -- "He will swallow up death in victory" -- Classified: Neutral

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "Swallow up death" — destruction of death itself, not of the wicked. Both sides agree death will be abolished.
    • Result: Neutral.

    E29 (Ps 104:29) -- "Thou takest away their breath, they die and return to dust" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "Die" (gawa) + "return to dust" — death/cessation vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): Creatures whose breath God takes away — living beings generally, including humans. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): toseph rucham (you take their spirit/breath) + yigva'un (they expire) + we'el-apharam yeshubun (to their dust they return). Grammar clear. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Creation psalm — didactic praise. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E14 (Job 34:14-15), E30 (Acts 17:25). No conflict. PASS.
