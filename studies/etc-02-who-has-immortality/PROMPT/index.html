    Bible Study: Who possesses immortality? Is the soul inherently immortal or is immortality a divine gift? Examine athanasia (G110), aphtharsia (G861), thnetos (G2349).¶

    Question¶

    Who possesses immortality? Is the soul inherently immortal or is immortality a divine gift? Examine athanasia (G110), aphtharsia (G861), thnetos (G2349).

    Prior Research Summary¶

    The etc6-02 study is the most comprehensive predecessor, providing 57 explicit statements, 6 necessary implications, and 9 classified inferences covering the complete NT immortality vocabulary. The standalone immortality study and soul-nephesh-psyche word study provide earlier foundational analysis. Convergent finding across all prior studies: Scripture attributes immortality exclusively to God as an inherent possession and offers it to believers as a conditional gift received at the resurrection. The human soul is never described as inherently immortal in any biblical text. The phrase 'immortal soul' does not appear in Scripture.

    Primary Verses (40 — full analysis required)¶

    Core immortality vocabulary passages: All passages containing athanasia (G110), aphtharsia (G861), or thnetos (G2349) - 1 Tim 6:15-16 — God ALONE (monos) has immortality (athanasia) — crux text for exclusive divine attribute - 1 Cor 15:42-44 — Sown in corruption, raised in incorruption — paired contrast of present mortal state vs. resurrection state - 1 Cor 15:50 — Flesh and blood cannot inherit the kingdom of God; corruption cannot inherit incorruption (aphtharsia) - 1 Cor 15:53-54 — Mortal (thnetos) must PUT ON (endyo) immortality (athanasia) and incorruption (aphtharsia) — resurrection transformation text - Rom 2:7 — Those who SEEK (zeteo) immortality (aphtharsia) — immortality as an object of aspiration, not a present possession - 2 Tim 1:10 — Christ brought life and immortality (aphtharsia) to light through the gospel — immortality revealed, not inherent - Rom 6:12 — Let not sin reign in your MORTAL (thnetos) body — present mortal condition described - Rom 8:11 — Spirit that raised Jesus shall quicken your MORTAL (thnetos) bodies — mortal body awaiting resurrection - 2 Cor 4:11 — Life of Jesus manifested in our MORTAL (thnetos) flesh — present mortality, divine life manifested - 2 Cor 5:4 — Mortal (thnetos) swallowed up by life — clothing metaphor, mortality consumed at transformation - Eph 6:24 — Grace with all who love the Lord in sincerity/incorruption (aphtharsia) — ethical metaphorical use - Tit 2:7 — In doctrine showing uncorruptness (aphtharsia) — ethical metaphorical use of aphtharsia

    Aphthartos (incorruptible adjective) passages: All passages containing aphthartos (G862) — God's nature and believers' future - Rom 1:23 — Changed glory of uncorruptible (aphthartos) God for corruptible images — God described as inherently aphthartos - 1 Tim 1:17 — King eternal, immortal (aphthartos), invisible, the only wise God — divine doxology, aphthartos applied to God - 1 Cor 9:25 — We an incorruptible (aphthartos) crown — believers' reward described as incorruptible, future acquisition - 1 Pet 1:4 — Inheritance incorruptible (aphthartos) and undefiled — believers' inheritance described as aphthartos - 1 Pet 1:23 — Born again of incorruptible (aphthartos) seed — the word of God as incorruptible source

    Soul mortality passages: Passages where nephesh/psyche is said to die or be destroyed - Ezek 18:4 — The soul (nephesh) that sinneth, it shall die — soul mortality explicitly stated - Ezek 18:20 — The soul (nephesh) that sinneth, it shall die — restatement confirming souls can die - Matt 10:28 — God is able to DESTROY both soul (psyche) and body in gehenna — soul can be destroyed

    Creation and tree of life: Genesis creation formula and tree of life access - Gen 2:7 — Man BECAME a living soul (nephesh chayyah) — creation formula, not soul implanted into body - Gen 3:22-24 — Man barred from tree of life lest he live forever — immortality was not inherent; required external source - Rev 2:7 — To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the tree of life — echoes Gen 3:22-24, life as conditional reward

    Eternal life as gift/promise: Passages presenting eternal life as conditional gift, not inherent possession - Rom 6:23 — Wages of sin is death; gift of God is eternal life — binary: death vs. life as gift - John 3:16 — Perish vs. have everlasting life — exhaustive binary alternatives - John 10:28 — I give unto them eternal life and they shall never perish — life as a gift from Christ - John 17:2-3 — Power to give eternal life — life given by divine authority, defined as knowing God and Christ - 1 John 5:11-12 — Life is in the Son; he that hath not the Son hath not life — absolute negation of life apart from Christ - 1 John 2:25 — This is the promise he hath promised us: eternal life — life as a promise, not inherent possession - Tit 1:2 — In hope of eternal life, which God promised before the world began — life as promise

    God as life-sustainer: Passages teaching that all life depends on God's sustaining power - Job 34:14-15 — If God gather to himself his spirit and breath, all flesh shall perish together — life depends on God's sustaining

    Death as sleep / unconsciousness: Passages describing death as sleep and the dead as unconscious - Dan 12:2 — Many that sleep in the dust shall awake — resurrection from death-sleep, not release from intermediate state - Ecc 9:5-6 — The dead know not anything — unconsciousness in death, no continuing immortal soul activity - Ecc 9:10 — No work, device, knowledge, wisdom in sheol — death as cessation, not transition - Ps 146:4 — His breath goeth forth, he returneth to earth; in that very day his thoughts perish — cessation of consciousness at death

    Additional Primary Verses: - 1 Cor 15:21-22 — By man came death; by man came also resurrection of the dead — Adam/Christ typology for death and life - 1 Cor 15:26 — Last enemy destroyed is death — death as enemy to be abolished, not natural state of souls leaving bodies - Ps 104:29 — Thou takest away their breath, they die and return to dust — life withdrawal = death - 1 Cor 15:51-52 — We shall all be changed, in a moment, at the last trump — transformation event, not gradual unveiling - Isa 25:8 — He will swallow up death in victory — OT promise of death's abolition, quoted in 1 Cor 15:54

    Focus Areas¶

    1. Athanasia (G110) — exclusive divine attribute or acquirable quality?: READ $WORD_STUDIES/G110-athanasia.md for the canonical entry. Parse 1 Tim 6:16 with greek_parser.py — note the accusative case (direct object of 'having') and the modifier monos (only). Parse 1 Cor 15:53 and 15:54 — note the accusative with endyo (put on), a verb of acquisition. Key question: in 1 Tim 6:16 does monos admit any qualification (self-existent vs. derived)? The text says God ALONE HAS athanasia — check whether any textual basis exists for adding 'self-existent' vs. 'derived' as a modifier. Check also whether athanasia ever appears with psyche (soul) in any NT passage. Run cross_testament_parallels_v2.py on 1 Tim 6:16 to find OT background for God's exclusive immortality. Note the total absence of athanasia from Gospels, General Epistles, and Revelation — confined to Pauline corpus. Verses: 1 Tim 6:15-16, 1 Cor 15:53, 1 Cor 15:54 Strong's: G110, G3441, G1746

    2. Aphtharsia (G861) — incorruptibility as resurrection vocabulary: READ $WORD_STUDIES/G861-aphtharsia.md for the canonical entry. Parse each of the 8 occurrences: 1 Cor 15:42, 15:50, 15:53, 15:54 (resurrection transformation), Rom 2:7 and 2 Tim 1:10 (aspiration/revelation), Eph 6:24 and Tit 2:7 (ethical purity). In 1 Cor 15:53-54, aphtharsia is paired with athanasia as parallel but distinct concepts — corruptible/incorruption vs. mortal/immortality. In Rom 2:7, immortality must be SOUGHT (zeteo) — parse the grammar to confirm whether this implies present non-possession. In 2 Tim 1:10, Christ BROUGHT (photizo) life and aphtharsia TO LIGHT through the gospel — the verb implies revelation of something previously hidden, not creation of something new. Note whether aphtharsia is ever predicated of the human soul. Verses: 1 Cor 15:42, 1 Cor 15:50, 1 Cor 15:53, 1 Cor 15:54, Rom 2:7, 2 Tim 1:10, Eph 6:24, Tit 2:7 Strong's: G861, G5356, G5351

    3. Thnetos (G2349) — the present human condition as mortal: READ $WORD_STUDIES/G2349-thnetos.md for the canonical entry. Parse all 6 occurrences: Rom 6:12 (mortal body under sin's dominion), Rom 8:11 (mortal body awaiting quickening), 1 Cor 15:53 (this mortal must put on immortality), 1 Cor 15:54 (this mortal is swallowed up by life), 2 Cor 4:11 (mortal flesh), 2 Cor 5:4 (mortal swallowed up by life). Key question: in 1 Cor 15:53, 'this mortal' (touto to thneton) is used absolutely — substantival, not restricted to 'this mortal body.' Does Paul ever name an immortal counterpart to the mortal body? Check the LXX mapping of thnetos to adam (H120, 'man/human being'). Run cross_testament_parallels_v2.py on key thnetos passages. Verses: Rom 6:12, Rom 8:11, 1 Cor 15:53, 1 Cor 15:54, 2 Cor 4:11, 2 Cor 5:4 Strong's: G2349, G110, G861

    4. Aphthartos (G862) — the incorruptible adjective across God, creation, and believers: READ $WORD_STUDIES/G862-aphthartos.md for the canonical entry. All 7 occurrences: Rom 1:23 (God as aphthartos — inherent nature), 1 Tim 1:17 (God as aphthartos — doxology), 1 Cor 15:52 (resurrection body raised aphthartos), 1 Cor 9:25 (incorruptible crown — future reward), 1 Pet 1:4 (incorruptible inheritance — reserved in heaven), 1 Pet 1:23 (incorruptible seed — the word of God), 1 Pet 3:4 (incorruptible hidden person of the heart — spiritual quality). Note the pattern: God has aphthartos as an inherent attribute; believers receive aphthartos as a future quality or spiritual gift; the human soul as such is never called aphthartos. Parse each occurrence to confirm grammatical construction. Verses: Rom 1:23, 1 Tim 1:17, 1 Cor 15:52, 1 Cor 9:25, 1 Pet 1:4, 1 Pet 1:23, 1 Pet 3:4 Strong's: G862, G861, G5349

    5. The corruption/decay word family — phthora (G5356), phthartos (G5349), diaphthora (G1312): The phtheiro word family forms the negative counterpart to the aphtharsia/aphthartos family. Phthora (G5356, 9 occ.) means decay/corruption/ruin — see Rom 8:21 (creation subject to bondage of corruption), Gal 6:8 (sow to flesh, reap corruption), 2 Pet 1:4 (corruption that is in the world through lust), 2 Pet 2:12 (shall perish in their own corruption). Phthartos (G5349, 6 occ.) means corruptible/perishable — 1 Cor 15:53-54 paired with aphtharsia, 1 Cor 9:25 (corruptible crown vs. incorruptible), 1 Pet 1:18 (not redeemed with corruptible things), 1 Pet 1:23 (corruptible seed vs. incorruptible). Diaphthora (G1312, 6 occ.) means decay, used in Acts 2:27,31; 13:34-37 for Christ's body not seeing corruption — messianic resurrection proof. Run word study searches to verify occurrence counts and check if any canonical entries exist for G5356, G5349, G1312. Verses: Rom 8:21, Gal 6:8, 2 Pet 1:4, 2 Pet 2:12, 1 Cor 15:53-54, 1 Cor 9:25, 1 Pet 1:18, 1 Pet 1:23, Acts 2:27, Acts 2:31, Acts 13:34-37 Strong's: G5356, G5349, G1312, G5351

    6. Genesis 2:7 and the creation formula — did man receive an immortal soul?: READ $WORD_STUDIES/H5315-nephesh.md for the canonical nephesh entry. Parse Gen 2:7 with hebrew_parser.py — the verb is hayah (became), not nathan (placed into). Man became a nephesh chayyah — the same term used for animals in Gen 1:20-21, 24. This means nephesh describes the whole living being, not an immortal component inserted into the body. Then examine Gen 3:22-24: God barred access to the tree of life 'lest he live forever' (pen...wachay leolam). If man already had an immortal soul, barring him from the tree of life would be pointless — he would live forever regardless. READ $WORD_STUDIES/H2416-chay.md for the canonical chay entry. Run cross_testament_parallels_v2.py on Gen 2:7 and Gen 3:22. Verses: Gen 2:7, Gen 3:22-24, Gen 1:20-21, Gen 1:24 Strong's: H5315, H2416, H5397, H7307

    7. The soul can die — nephesh mortality and psyche destruction: READ $WORD_STUDIES/H5315-nephesh.md and $WORD_STUDIES/G5590-psyche.md for canonical entries. Key passages: Ezek 18:4,20 — 'the soul (nephesh) that sinneth, it SHALL DIE' (muwth). Matt 10:28 — God is able to 'DESTROY (apollymi) both soul (psyche) and body in gehenna.' Rev 16:3 — 'every living soul (psyche) died.' Lev 21:11, Num 6:6 — nephesh met ('dead soul') used for corpses. READ $WORD_STUDIES/G622-apollymi.md for the canonical apollymi entry. The critical question: how many times is nephesh or psyche modified by 'immortal' in any biblical text? (Answer from prior studies: zero.) Parse the key destruction passages to confirm verb forms. Verses: Ezek 18:4, Ezek 18:20, Matt 10:28, Rev 16:3, Lev 21:11, Num 6:6 Strong's: H5315, G5590, G622, H4191

    8. Eternal life as gift, promise, and future acquisition — not inherent possession: The pattern is remarkably consistent across multiple independent authors: Rom 6:23 (gift — charisma), Tit 1:2 (promise — epangelia), 1 John 2:25 (promise), John 10:28 (I GIVE — didomi), John 17:2 (power to GIVE), Rom 2:7 (to those who SEEK — zeteo), 1 John 5:11-12 (life IN the Son; without the Son = no life). READ $WORD_STUDIES/G2222-zoe.md for the canonical zoe entry. Key question: how many NT passages describe eternal life as an inherent human possession vs. a divine gift? Check whether any passage says humans already have eternal life apart from Christ. The John 5:26 passage is particularly important — 'the Father hath life in himself' — self-existent life is a DIVINE attribute, then GIVEN to the Son. Run cross_testament_parallels_v2.py on Rom 6:23 and John 3:16. Verses: Rom 6:23, Tit 1:2, 1 John 2:25, John 10:28, John 17:2-3, 1 John 5:11-12, John 5:24-26, John 3:16, Gal 6:8 Strong's: G2222, G166, G5485

    9. The endyo (put on) metaphor — clothing metaphor for acquisition vs. unveiling: READ $WORD_STUDIES/TR-labash-endyo.md for the canonical translation relationship entry. The verb endyo (G1746) means to put on, clothe oneself with. In 1 Cor 15:53-54, the mortal must 'put on' (endyo) immortality. Is endyo ever used for unveiling something already present, or does it consistently mean acquiring/receiving something new? Check: Rom 13:14 (put on the Lord Jesus Christ), Gal 3:27 (put on Christ), Eph 4:24 (put on the new man), Eph 6:11 (put on the whole armor of God), Col 3:10,12 (put on the new man, put on compassion). In every case, endyo describes acquiring something not previously worn. Compare with ependyomai (G1902) in 2 Cor 5:2,4 — 'clothed upon,' the super-addition of resurrection glory. Also note the contrast with ekdyo (G1562) 'to unclothe' in 2 Cor 5:4 — Paul does NOT want to be unclothed (die) but clothed upon (transformed). Verses: 1 Cor 15:53-54, Rom 13:14, Gal 3:27, Eph 4:24, Eph 6:11, Col 3:10, Col 3:12, 2 Cor 5:2-4 Strong's: G1746, G1902, G1562

    10. The monos (G3441) qualifier in 1 Tim 6:16 — does 'only' admit qualification?: READ $WORD_STUDIES/G3441-mónos.md for the canonical entry. Parse 1 Tim 6:16 — monos echon athanasiān. The participle echon (having) with monos creates an exclusive attribution: only-having. Check whether any NT 'monos + divine attribute' construction admits qualification. Compare John 17:3 (the only true God — monos alethinos theos), 1 Tim 1:17 (the only wise God — mono sopho theo), Jude 25 (the only wise God — mono sopho theo). In each case, monos marks unqualified exclusivity. The ECT position adds 'self-existent vs. derived' as a distinction not in the text — examine whether any textual basis exists for this. READ also $WORD_STUDIES/TR-bad-monos.md for the Hebrew-Greek mapping. Verses: 1 Tim 6:15-16, John 17:3, 1 Tim 1:17, Jude 25 Strong's: G3441, G110, G2192

    11. Death as sleep — resurrection presupposes whole-person death, not disembodied survival: The death-as-sleep metaphor appears in both Testaments: Deut 31:16, Job 7:21, Job 14:12, Jer 51:39, Dan 12:2, John 11:11, Acts 7:60, Acts 13:36, 1 Cor 15:6,18,51, 1 Thess 4:14-15. Death is 'sleep' because (a) the dead are unconscious (Ecc 9:5-6, Ps 146:4, Ps 115:17, Ps 6:5) and (b) sleep implies eventual waking (resurrection). If humans have immortal souls that remain conscious after death, calling death 'sleep' would be misleading. READ $WORD_STUDIES/G2288-thanatos.md and $WORD_STUDIES/H4194-maveth.md for death vocabulary. 1 Cor 15:18 is particularly significant: 'Then they also which are fallen asleep in Christ are PERISHED (apollymi)' — if there were no resurrection, the dead are not in heaven; they are perished. This makes no sense if they have immortal souls already in God's presence. Verses: Dan 12:2, John 11:11, 1 Cor 15:6, 1 Cor 15:18, 1 Thess 4:13-16, Ecc 9:5-6, Ps 146:4, Ps 115:17, Acts 7:60, Acts 13:36 Strong's: G2288, H4194, G2837

    12. God as sustainer of life — Job 34:14-15, Acts 17:25-28, Ps 104:29-30: Multiple passages teach that life is not self-sustaining: Job 34:14-15 (if God gathers his spirit and breath, all flesh perishes), Ps 104:29-30 (take away breath = die and return to dust; send forth spirit = created/renewed), Acts 17:25-28 (God gives to all life and breath; in him we live and move and have our being), Heb 1:3 (upholding all things by the word of his power), Col 1:17 (by him all things consist). If human souls were inherently immortal, they would not depend on God's sustaining power — they would continue existing by their own nature. But Scripture consistently teaches that ALL created life depends on the Creator's active sustaining. The tree of life in Gen 2-3 and Rev 22 represents this external source of continued existence. Run cross_testament_parallels_v2.py on Job 34:14-15 and Acts 17:25. Verses: Job 34:14-15, Ps 104:29-30, Acts 17:25-28, Heb 1:3, Col 1:17, Gen 2:9, Rev 22:14 Strong's: H5397, H7307, G4157, G2222
