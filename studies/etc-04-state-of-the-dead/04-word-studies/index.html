    Word Studies¶

    Question¶

    Condition of the dead -- conscious or unconscious? What happens between death and resurrection? Examine ALL Old Testament death-state passages (the dead know nothing, thoughts perish, no praise, silence, forgetfulness, sleep), ALL New Testament death-as-sleep passages, and ALL alleged conscious intermediate state passages (2 Cor 5:1-9, Phil 1:23, Luke 23:43, Luke 16:19-31, Rev 6:9-11, 1 Sam 28:3-20, Matt 17:1-9, 1 Pet 3:18-20). Examine the Hebrew and Greek 'sleep' vocabulary (shakab H7901, yashen H3462, koimao G2837, katheudo G2518) and 'rest/waiting' vocabulary. Investigate sheol (H7585) and hades (G86) as the abode of the dead.

    H7585 — sheol¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H7585-showl.md

    Occurrences: 68

    Sheol (shᵉʼôwl) — feminine noun from uncertain root. The abode/world of the dead (subterranean retreat). 68 tokens across 64 unique verses in 17 books. KJV translates as 'hell' (48.4%), 'grave' (40.6%), 'pit' (4.7%). Receives both righteous (Jacob Gen 37:35, David Psa 16:10, Hezekiah Isa 38:10) and wicked (Korah Num 16:30-33). Characterized by silence (Psa 115:17), no work/knowledge/wisdom (Ecc 9:10), darkness (Job 10:21-22), no praise (Psa 6:5, Isa 38:18-19), forgetfulness (Psa 88:12).

    Semantic categories: the grave/abode of all dead, unconsciousness and silence, temporary holding until resurrection

    Key verses: Gen 37:35, Psa 16:10, Ecc 9:10, Psa 6:5, Psa 115:17, Isa 38:18-19

    Relevance: Central term for where the dead go. Every sheol characterization describes unconsciousness, silence, and cessation — not conscious existence. This is the E-item foundation for the conditionalist position.

    G86 — hades¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G86-háidēs.md

    Occurrences: 11

    Hades (háidēs) — from alpha-privative + eido = 'unseen.' NT equivalent of sheol. 11 occurrences across 5 books. LXX translates sheol as hades 58 times. Acts 2:27,31 confirm equivalence by quoting Psa 16:10. Three semantic categories: (1) general realm of the dead (Acts 2:27,31; 1 Cor 15:55), (2) judgment context (Matt 11:23, Luke 10:15, Luke 16:23), (3) defeated power (Rev 1:18, 6:8, 20:13-14). Hades is TEMPORARY — cast into lake of fire (Rev 20:14).

    Semantic categories: realm of the dead (= sheol), defeated power, temporary holding

    Key verses: Acts 2:27, Acts 2:31, Matt 16:18, Rev 1:18, Rev 20:13-14, Luke 16:23

    Relevance: NT equivalent of sheol. The sheol→hades equivalence (confirmed by Acts 2:27,31 quoting Psa 16:10) means all OT sheol characterizations (unconsciousness, silence) apply to hades. Hades is temporary — not the final state.

    H3462 — yashen¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H3462-yâshên.md

    Occurrences: 21

    Yashen (yâshên) — primitive root verb meaning to be slack/languid, to sleep; figuratively to die; also to grow old. 21 occurrences across 12+ books. Used for death-sleep in Dan 12:2 ('many of them that sleep in the dust'), Jer 51:39 ('sleep a perpetual sleep and not wake'), Psa 13:3 ('lest I sleep the sleep of death'). The death-sleep metaphor implies unconsciousness — sleep is a state without awareness.

    Semantic categories: literal sleep, death as sleep, perpetual sleep

    Key verses: Dan 12:2, Jer 51:39, Psa 13:3

    Relevance: One of four key sleep-death vocabulary words. Dan 12:2 uses yashen for the dead who await resurrection ('awake'). The metaphor of sleep for death is foundational — it implies unconsciousness between death and resurrection.

    G2837 — koimao¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2837-koimao.md

    Occurrences: 19

    Koimao (κοιμάω) — verb meaning to put to sleep, to fall asleep, to die. 19 occurrences across 7 books. The standard NT term for death as sleep. Used by Jesus (John 11:11), Luke (Acts 7:60, 13:36), Paul (1 Cor 15:6,18,20,51; 1 Thess 4:13-15), and Peter (2 Pet 3:4). 1 Cor 15:18: if no resurrection, those 'fallen asleep' (koimethentes) have PERISHED — proving sleep = unconscious state, not conscious heavenly existence.

    Semantic categories: literal sleep, death as sleep (euphemism), resurrection awaited

    Key verses: John 11:11, Acts 7:60, 1 Cor 15:6, 1 Cor 15:18, 1 Cor 15:51, 1 Thess 4:14

    Relevance: The NT standard death-sleep word. Used by 4+ authors including Jesus. 1 Cor 15:18 is the decisive test: if koimao meant conscious existence with Christ, Paul could not say the dead have 'perished' without resurrection.

    H5315 — nephesh¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H5315-nephesh.md

    Occurrences: 788

    Nephesh — feminine noun from naphash (to breathe). 788 tokens across 682 verses in 31 books. Extraordinary breadth: 214 unique KJV translations. Primary meanings: soul (64%), life (14%), person (4%), reflexive self (2%), desire/appetite (3%), creature (1%), dead body (1%). Man BECAME a nephesh chayyah (Gen 2:7) — did not receive one. Animals are also nephesh chayyah (Gen 1:24). Nephesh can DIE (Ezek 18:4, Num 31:19), be DESTROYED (Matt 10:28 psyche), and refer to CORPSES (Lev 21:11, Num 6:6).

    Semantic categories: living being/creature, life/vitality, person/self, desire/appetite, corpse/dead body

    Key verses: Gen 2:7, Gen 1:24, Ezek 18:4, Lev 21:11, Num 6:6

    Relevance: Foundational for understanding what 'dies' at death. Man IS a soul (holistic), not HAS a soul (dualistic). Nephesh can die (Ezek 18:4) and refers to corpses — it is not an inherently immortal entity.

    G5590 — psyche¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G5590-psyche.md

    Occurrences: 105

    Psyche (ψυχή) — feminine noun from psycho (to breathe/blow cool). 105 NT occurrences. NT equivalent of nephesh. Semantic domains: psychological faculties, life/death, person, creature, attitudes. Can be destroyed (Matt 10:28). Rev 6:9 'souls' (psychas) under the altar = lives/selves of martyrs, not disembodied spirits. The lexicon note on 'immortal soul' is a Greek-philosophical import, not a biblical doctrine.

    Semantic categories: life/vitality, person/self, psychological faculty, creature

    Key verses: Matt 2:20, Matt 10:28, Rev 6:9, Acts 2:41

    Relevance: NT equivalent of nephesh. Rev 6:9 uses psychas for the martyrs under the altar — understanding psyche as 'life/self' rather than 'immaterial soul' changes the interpretation of this passage entirely.

    H7307 — ruach¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H7307-ruach.md

    Occurrences: 434

    Ruach (rûwach) — feminine noun. 434 occurrences. Translated as spirit (53.7%), wind (21.4%), breath (7.6%), and other. The animating principle shared by humans and animals (Ecc 3:19). Returns to God at death (Ecc 12:7). Never used for corpses. When ruach departs, thoughts PERISH (Psa 146:4) — ruach is the life-force, not the conscious self.

    Semantic categories: wind/breath, divine Spirit, human spirit/disposition, life-force/animating principle

    Key verses: Gen 1:2, Ecc 12:7, Ecc 3:19, Psa 146:4, Psa 104:29-30

    Relevance: What returns to God at death. Shared with animals (Ecc 3:19), proving ruach is the animating principle, not a conscious immaterial soul. Combined with Psa 146:4 (thoughts perish when ruach departs), ruach's departure = cessation of consciousness.

    G4151 — pneuma¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G4151-pneuma.md

    Occurrences: 385

    Pneuma (πνεῦμα) — neuter noun from pneo (to blow/breathe). 385 occurrences. Extremely wide semantic range: Holy Spirit, demonic spirits, angels, wind, breath, life-force, inner disposition. Stephen commits his pneuma to Jesus (Acts 7:59) — paralleling Jesus (Luke 23:46) and fulfilling Ecc 12:7. The next verse says Stephen 'fell asleep' — pneuma commitment is not immediate conscious presence but trust in God's keeping.

    Semantic categories: Holy Spirit, human spirit/disposition, breath/wind, demonic spirits, life-force

    Key verses: Acts 7:59, Luke 23:46, 1 Pet 3:19, James 2:26

    Relevance: NT equivalent of ruach. Stephen's pneuma-commitment + sleep language (Acts 7:59-60) demonstrates that commending one's spirit to God is an act of trust, not immediate conscious transfer.

    H5397 — neshamah¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H5397-nshamah.md

    Occurrences: 25

    Neshamah (nᵉshâmâh) — feminine noun from nasham (to blow away). 25 tokens across 24 verses in 10 books. Translated as breath (42%), breathe (21%), blast (12.5%), spirit (8.3%), inspiration (4.2%). The breath God breathed into man at creation (Gen 2:7). Appears in destruction contexts (breath of God's nostrils = divine judgment). Psa 150:6: 'let everything that hath breath praise the LORD' — this ceases at death (Psa 115:17).

    Semantic categories: breath of life (creation), divine breath (judgment), all breathing things

    Key verses: Gen 2:7, Job 33:4, Isa 2:22, Psa 150:6

    Relevance: The specific term in Gen 2:7 — what God breathed into man. When neshamah ceases (Psa 146:4 uses ruach, the parallel term), the living being ceases. Not a conscious soul but the breath of life.

    H4194 — maveth¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H4194-maveth.md

    Occurrences: 170

    Maveth — masculine noun, primary Hebrew word for death. From muwth (H4191, to die). Paired frequently with sheol. Used for the death sentence (Gen 2:17), the state of death, and personified death. Isa 38:18: 'death (maveth) cannot celebrate thee.'

    Semantic categories: physical death, death personified, death as a state

    Key verses: Gen 2:17, Psa 6:5, Isa 38:18, Hos 13:14

    Relevance: Primary Hebrew death term. Every maveth context describes cessation, not conscious survival. Paired with sheol to describe the state of the dead.

    G2288 — thanatos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2288-thanatos.md

    Occurrences: 119

    Thanatos — masculine noun, primary Greek word for death. 119 occurrences. Includes 'second death' (Rev 20:14, 21:8). Defeated enemy that shall be destroyed (1 Cor 15:26). Death and hades cast into lake of fire (Rev 20:14) — death itself is abolished.

    Semantic categories: physical death, second death, death as enemy, spiritual death (metaphorical)

    Key verses: Rom 6:23, 1 Cor 15:26, Rev 20:14, Heb 2:14

    Relevance: NT death vocabulary. The 'second death' uses the same word, showing death means death, not eternal conscious existence.

    G110 — athanasia¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G110-athanasia.md

    Occurrences: 3

    Athanasia — feminine noun from alpha-privative + thanatos = 'deathlessness/immortality.' Only 3 occurrences: 1 Cor 15:53 (mortals must PUT ON immortality), 1 Cor 15:54 (mortal puts on immortality → death swallowed), 1 Tim 6:16 (God ALONE has immortality). The rarity and exclusivity are significant — immortality is not a human possession but a divine gift acquired at resurrection.

    Semantic categories: divine attribute, resurrection gift, deathlessness

    Key verses: 1 Tim 6:16, 1 Cor 15:53, 1 Cor 15:54

    Relevance: Only 3 occurrences, all establishing that immortality belongs to God alone and is acquired by believers at resurrection. The phrase 'immortal soul' never appears in Scripture.

    G861 — aphtharsia¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G861-aphtharsia.md

    Occurrences: 8

    Aphtharsia — incorruption/immortality. 8 occurrences. Something to SEEK (Rom 2:7), brought to light through the gospel (2 Tim 1:10), PUT ON at resurrection (1 Cor 15:53-54). Every usage presents incorruption as a future acquisition, not a present possession.

    Semantic categories: incorruption, immortality as gift, resurrection quality

    Key verses: Rom 2:7, 1 Cor 15:53-54, 2 Tim 1:10

    Relevance: Paired with athanasia. Both terms describe what mortals ACQUIRE at resurrection. If humans already had an incorruptible/immortal component, these passages would be meaningless.

    G386 — anastasis¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G386-anastasis.md

    Occurrences: 42

    Anastasis — feminine noun from anistemi (to stand up again). 42 occurrences. The consistent biblical hope for the dead: bodily resurrection. Used by Jesus (Matt 22:23-32, John 5:29, 6:39-40,44,54, 11:25), Paul (1 Cor 15, Phil 3:11, 1 Thess 4:16), Peter (Acts 2:31, 4:2), and Hebrews author (6:2, 11:35). Acts 4:2: the Sadducees were grieved that the apostles 'preached through Jesus the resurrection from the dead.'

    Semantic categories: bodily resurrection, the biblical hope, eschatological event

    Key verses: John 11:25, 1 Cor 15:42, Phil 3:11, Acts 2:31, Rev 20:5-6

    Relevance: The consistent biblical alternative to 'going to heaven at death.' Resurrection — not death — is when believers receive their reward, are transformed, and are 'with the Lord.'

    G373 — anapauo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G373-anapauo.md

    Occurrences: 13

    Anapauo — verb from ana + pauo (to cease). 13 tokens across 12 verses in 8 books. Means to cause to rest, to give rest, to take rest. Rev 6:11: souls told to 'rest' (anapausontai). Rev 14:13: dead in the Lord 'rest from their labours.' The death-rest vocabulary treats death as cessation of activity.

    Semantic categories: physical rest, spiritual rest, death as rest/cessation

    Key verses: Matt 11:28, Rev 6:11, Rev 14:13

    Relevance: Links Rev 6:11 (souls told to rest) with Rev 14:13 (dead rest from labours). Death is described as rest/cessation — not conscious activity.

    H5117 — nuwach¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H5117-nuwach.md

    Occurrences: 108

    Nuwach — verb meaning to rest, settle, be quiet. Dan 12:13: 'thou shalt rest (tanuwach), and stand in thy lot at the end of the days.' Death = rest, resurrection = standing in one's lot. The OT rest-death vocabulary.

    Semantic categories: physical rest, cessation from activity, death as rest

    Key verses: Dan 12:13, Isa 57:2, Job 3:13

    Relevance: Dan 12:13 explicitly connects rest (nuwach) with death and standing in one's lot (resurrection) 'at the end of the days.' Death is rest until the eschatological awakening.

    G622 — apollymi¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G622-apollymi.md

    Occurrences: 102

    Apollymi — to destroy utterly, to perish, to lose. Used in 1 Cor 15:18: if no resurrection, believers have 'perished' (apolonto, Aor Mid Ind 3P). This is the decisive cross-reference for conscious intermediate state claims — if the dead were already in conscious bliss, they could not 'perish' without resurrection.

    Semantic categories: destruction, perishing, loss

    Key verses: 1 Cor 15:18, Matt 10:28, John 3:16

    Relevance: 1 Cor 15:18 is the logical test for every conscious intermediate state claim. Paul's own argument proves that without resurrection, dead believers are destroyed — impossible if they are already consciously with Christ.

    G4983 — soma¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G4983-soma.md

    Occurrences: 146

    Soma — body. Used in 2 Cor 5:8 (absent from the body), Phil 1:20 (Christ magnified in my body), Phil 3:21 (shall change our vile body). The soma is what is transformed at resurrection, not discarded for disembodied existence.

    Semantic categories: physical body, resurrection body, embodied existence

    Key verses: 2 Cor 5:8, Phil 3:21, 1 Cor 15:44

    Relevance: Key to the 2 Cor 5 and Phil 1-3 passages. Paul's hope is a resurrection BODY, not disembodied existence. He explicitly does not want to be 'unclothed' (2 Cor 5:4).

    G3705 — horama¶

    Occurrences: 12

    Horama — vision, that which is seen. Jesus uses this word to describe the transfiguration in Matt 17:9: 'Tell the vision (horama) to no man.' Used elsewhere for supernatural visions (Acts 7:31, 9:10, 10:3, 10:17, 10:19, 11:5, 12:9, 16:9-10, 18:9). The transfiguration was a visionary experience, not proof of conscious intermediate state.

    Semantic categories: supernatural vision, that which is seen

    Key verses: Matt 17:9, Acts 10:3, Acts 16:9

    Relevance: Jesus himself classifies the transfiguration as a 'vision' (horama), not as evidence that Moses was consciously alive in an intermediate state. Moses was resurrected (Jude 1:9), Elijah was translated (2 Ki 2:11).

    H7901 — shakab¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H7901-shâkab.md

    Occurrences: 262

    Shakab (shâkab) — primitive root verb meaning to lie down for rest, sexual connection, decease, or any other purpose. 262 occurrences. The most theologically significant usage is the death formula 'slept with his fathers' (wayyishkab 'im-'abotayw) used for ~36 kings in 1-2 Kings and 2 Chronicles. Also used in Job 14:12 ('man lieth down and riseth not'), Isa 14:18, Ezek 32 (slain warriors in sheol). LXX maps primarily to koimaomai (G2837, 141x, PMI 6.6) and katheudo (G2518, 19x, PMI 6.61). Death formula presents death as lying down to sleep among ancestors — unconscious repose.

    Semantic categories: death formula: slept with his fathers, death and the grave (non-formulaic), physical rest and sleep, sexual relations (euphemistic)

    Key verses: 1 Ki 2:10, 1 Ki 11:43, Deu 31:16, Job 14:12, Job 3:13, Isa 14:18

    Relevance: OT 'lie down/sleep' used for death; 'sleep with thy fathers' formula appears ~36 times for kings. LXX translates with koimaomai (G2837), which becomes the NT death-as-sleep word. The semantic chain shakab → koimao establishes death-as-sleep as a unified biblical concept across Testaments.

    G2518 — katheudo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2518-katheudo.md

    Occurrences: 23

    Katheudo (καθεύδω) — verb from kata (G2596) + heudo (to sleep); to lie down to rest, fall asleep. 23 occurrences across 5 books (Matt 8, Mark 8, Luke 2, Eph 1, 1 Thess 4). Three semantic categories: (1) literal physical sleep, (2) sleep of death/euphemism — Jesus uses this for death in Matt 9:24, Mark 5:39, Luke 8:52, and Paul in 1 Thess 5:10, (3) spiritual lethargy/moral unconsciousness. Antonym: gregoreo (G1127, to watch/be vigilant).

    Semantic categories: literal physical sleep, sleep of death (euphemism), spiritual lethargy or moral unconsciousness

    Key verses: Matt 9:24, Mark 5:39, Luke 8:52, 1 Thess 5:10, 1 Thess 5:6

    Relevance: NT 'sleep' used for death by Jesus himself (Matt 9:24). Distinguished from koimao (G2837): katheudo is the common word for literal sleep occasionally extended to death, while koimao is the standard NT death-euphemism. Both words used by Jesus for death establish the sleep metaphor as authoritative.

    G3857 — paradeisos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G3857-paradeisos.md

    Occurrences: 3

    Paradeisos (παράδεισος) — masculine noun of Oriental origin (cf. H6508 pardes); a park, Eden, place of future happiness. Only 3 NT occurrences: Luke 23:43 (Jesus to the thief), 2 Cor 12:4 (Paul caught up to paradise = third heaven), Rev 2:7 (tree of life in the paradise of God). LXX uses paradeisos to translate Hebrew gan (garden, H1588) especially for the Garden of Eden. The eschatological paradise (Rev 2:7) connects to the restored Eden with the tree of life (Rev 22:2), identifying paradise as the future new creation rather than a present intermediate state.

    Semantic categories: present abode of the righteous dead (alleged), heavenly/third heaven realm, eschatological restored Eden

    Key verses: Luke 23:43, 2 Cor 12:4, Rev 2:7

    Relevance: Only 3 NT uses. Luke 23:43 is the key death-state passage — but Jesus was NOT in paradise on that day (John 20:17). 2 Cor 12:4 describes a living person's vision. Rev 2:7 is eschatological (tree of life in paradise of God). The word itself does not establish a conscious intermediate state.
