    Classification Audit Trail — etc7-04 State of the Dead¶

    This document contains the full Tree 3/4 walkthroughs for every E-item classified Cond. or ECT, and every N-item. Each walkthrough uses actual parser output for Gate 2 verification.

    E-Items Classified Cond.¶

    E1 (Psa 146:4) -- "His breath goeth forth, he returneth to his earth; in that very day his thoughts perish" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "perish" (abad) applied to thoughts of the dying person. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): The grammatical subject is a generic human being (context: "princes," v.3, i.e. mortal men). PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: abad (Verb.Qal.Perf.3p = "perish," completed action); eshtonoth (Noun.fp.Abs.+3ms = "his thoughts"). Grammar unambiguously states thoughts perish when breath departs. No alternative parsing. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Psalm -- direct didactic statement within a hymn of praise. Not parabolic or apocalyptic. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E2 (Ecc 9:5, dead know nothing), E3 (Ecc 9:6, love/hatred/envy perished), E4 (Ecc 9:10, no knowledge in sheol), E5 (Psa 6:5, no remembrance), E9 (Job 14:21, dead know not). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E2 (Ecc 9:5) -- "The dead know not any thing, neither have they any more a reward; for the memory of them is forgotten" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- negation of knowledge/awareness applied to the dead. Cessation vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "The dead" (hammetim, Qal ptcp of muwth) -- literal dead human beings. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser: hammetim (Verb.Qal.Ptcp.mp.Abs) einam (Noun.ms.Abs.+3mp = NEG) yodim (Verb.Qal.Ptcp.mp.Abs = knowing) meumah (Noun.fs.Abs = something/anything). Double negation: ein + meumah = absolute nothing. Grammar unambiguous. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Wisdom literature, direct didactic observation. Not parabolic or apocalyptic. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E1, E3-E12, E14. No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E3 (Ecc 9:6) -- "Their love, and their hatred, and their envy, is now perished" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "perished" (abad) applied to emotional/volitional capacities of the dead. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): The dead (continuing from v.5). PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser: ahavah (love), sinah (hatred), qinah (jealousy) -- avdah (Verb.Qal.Perf.3fs = perished, completed action). Grammar unambiguous. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Wisdom literature, didactic. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E1, E2, E4-E12. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E4 (Ecc 9:10) -- "There is no work, nor device, nor knowledge, nor wisdom, in the grave [sheol]" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- negation of all activity and cognition in sheol. Cessation vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): Generic human going to sheol ("whither thou goest"). PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser: ein (Noun.ms.Cst = NEG) + maaseh (deed) + cheshbon (account) + daath (knowledge) + chokmah (wisdom) + bisheol (in sheol). Four-fold existential negation. Grammar unambiguous. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Wisdom literature, didactic imperative. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E1-E3, E5-E12. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E5 (Psa 6:5) -- "In death there is no remembrance of thee: in the grave [sheol] who shall give thee thanks?" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- negation of remembrance and thanksgiving in death/sheol. Cessation vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): David speaking about the dead generically. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser: bammavet ein zikrekha (in death there is not remembrance of you). Bisheol mi yodeh lakh (in sheol who will praise you -- rhetorical question expecting "no one"). Grammar unambiguous. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Psalm of lament, direct didactic statement about death. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E1-E4, E6-E12. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E6 (Psa 115:17) -- "The dead praise not the LORD, neither any that go down into silence" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- negation of praise; descent into "silence" (dumah). Cessation vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "The dead" (hammetim) -- literal dead humans. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser: lo (Neg) hammetim (Verb.Qal.Ptcp.mp.Abs = the dead) yehallu (Verb.Piel.Impf.3mp = will praise) Yah (the LORD). Kol-yordey (Verb.Qal.Ptcp.mp.Cst = all descending into) dumah (Noun.fs.Abs = silence). Grammar unambiguous. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Psalm of praise, didactic statement. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E1-E5, E7-E12. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E7 (Psa 88:10-12) -- "Wilt thou shew wonders to the dead? shall the dead arise and praise thee?" etc. -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- series of rhetorical negations about the dead: no wonders, no praise, no lovingkindness, no faithfulness, no righteousness, "land of forgetfulness." Cessation vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "The dead" (hammetim), refaim (shades). PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser: ha-lammetim (interrogative + to-the-dead) taaseh-pele (will you do wonder)? im-refaim (if-shades) yaqumu (will arise) yodukha (praise you)? Interrogative construction expecting "no." Grammar unambiguous. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Psalm of lament, didactic questions. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E1-E6, E8-E12. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E8 (Job 14:12) -- "Man lieth down, and riseth not: till the heavens be no more, they shall not awake, nor be raised out of their sleep" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "riseth not," "shall not awake," "sleep" -- cessation/death-sleep vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "Man" (generic human being). PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Yishkab (from shakab H7901) velo-yaqum (and does not arise). Grammar unambiguous: death is lying down without rising. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Wisdom literature, direct speech in didactic poetry. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E1-E7, E9-E12. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E9 (Job 14:21) -- "His sons come to honour, and he knoweth it not; and they are brought low, but he perceiveth it not" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- double negation of perception: "knoweth it not," "perceiveth it not." Cessation of awareness. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): A dead man (continuing from the death described in vv.10-14). PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Velo-yeda (and he knows not) ... velo-yabin (and he perceives not). Two distinct verbs (yada, bin) both negated. Grammar unambiguous. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Wisdom literature, direct speech. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E1-E8, E10-E12. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E10 (Isa 38:18-19) -- "The grave cannot praise thee, death cannot celebrate thee: they that go down into the pit cannot hope for thy truth" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- triple negation: cannot praise, cannot celebrate, cannot hope. Cessation vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): The dead generically (sheol, mavet, yordey-bor). PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Sheol cannot todekha (praise you), mavet cannot yehalelekha (celebrate you), yordey-bor cannot yesabberu (hope for). Grammar unambiguous. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Hezekiah's prayer -- direct speech, didactic. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E1-E9, E11-E12. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E11 (Psa 30:9) -- "Shall the dust praise thee? shall it declare thy truth?" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- rhetorical questions expecting "no." The dead (dust) cannot praise or declare. Cessation. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "Dust" = the dead (metonymy for dead person). PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Hayodekha aphar (will dust praise you)? Hayaggid amittekha (will it declare your truth)? Interrogative expecting "no." Grammar unambiguous. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Psalm of thanksgiving, didactic rhetorical question. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E1-E10, E12. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E12 (Isa 26:14) -- "They are dead, they shall not live; they are deceased, they shall not rise: therefore hast thou ... made all their memory to perish" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "dead," "shall not live," "shall not rise," "memory to perish." Cessation vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): Oppressive rulers -- literal human beings. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Methim bal-yichyu (dead, shall not live). Refaim bal-yaqumu (deceased, shall not rise). Grammar unambiguous. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Prophetic song, direct statement. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E1-E11. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E13 (Job 3:17-18) -- "There the wicked cease from troubling; and there the weary be at rest. There the prisoners rest together; they hear not the voice of the oppressor" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "cease," "rest," "hear not." Cessation vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): All dead humans (wicked, weary, prisoners). PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Chadal (cease) from rogez (troubling). Lo shamu (hear not) the voice. Grammar unambiguous. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Wisdom literature, direct speech. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E1-E12. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E14 (Acts 2:34) -- "David is not ascended into the heavens" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- explicit denial of heavenly ascent for a dead righteous person. Cessation/unconsciousness direction. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): David -- literal, historical human being. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser: ou (PRT-N) gar (CONJ) Daueid (N-PRI, Nom Sg M) anebe (V-2AAI-3S, Aor Act Ind 3s of anabaino G305 = ascended) eis tous ouranous (into the heavens). Negation particle ou + aorist verb = "David did not ascend." Grammar unambiguous. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Apostolic sermon -- direct didactic teaching under Holy Spirit inspiration (Acts 2:4). PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E1-E13. If the righteous dead ascend to heaven, Peter's statement is false. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E15 (Heb 11:39-40) -- "These all, having obtained a good report through faith, received not the promise" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- OT saints "received NOT the promise." They are not yet in their reward. Death did not bring them fulfillment. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): OT heroes of faith (Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham, etc.) -- literal humans. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser: ouk (PRT-N) ekomisanto (V-AMI-3P, Aor Mid Ind Pl 3rd of komizo = received/carried off) ten epaggelian (the promise). Grammar unambiguous: they did not receive the promise. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Epistle -- direct didactic teaching. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E14 (David not ascended). If OT saints are already in heaven, the author's statement is false. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E16 (1 Cor 15:18) -- "Then they also which are fallen asleep in Christ are perished" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "perished" (apollymi) applied to dead believers if there is no resurrection. Destruction vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): Dead believers ("fallen asleep in Christ") -- literal humans. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser: koimethentes (V-APP-NPM, Aor Pass Ptcp of koimaomai G2837 = those having fallen asleep) en Christo (in Christ) apolonto (V-2AMI-3P, Aor Mid Ind 3P of apollymi G622 = perished). Grammar unambiguous: Paul states that without resurrection, the dead in Christ have perished. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Epistle -- direct didactic argument (reductio ad absurdum). PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E1-E15. If dead believers were already in conscious bliss, they could not "perish" without resurrection. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E17 (Ezek 18:4) -- "The soul that sinneth, it shall die" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "die" (muwth) applied to the soul (nephesh). Death vocabulary applied to the soul itself. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "The soul" (nephesh) = the person. Literal human. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Hannephesh hachote't hi tamut (the soul sinning, it shall die). Emphatic pronoun hi (it). Grammar unambiguous: the soul itself dies. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Prophetic oracle -- direct speech of God ("Thus says the Lord GOD"). PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E18 (Matt 10:28, soul can be destroyed). PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E18 (Matt 10:28) -- "Fear him which is able to destroy both soul and body in hell" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "destroy" (apollymi G622) applied to soul and body. Destruction vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): Soul and body of a human being. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Apollesai (Aor Act Inf of apollymi G622 = to destroy) kai psychen kai soma (both soul and body) en geenne (in gehenna). Grammar unambiguous: God is able to destroy (not merely torment) both soul and body. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Jesus's direct teaching -- didactic instruction. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E17 (soul that sins shall die). PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E19 (1 Tim 6:16) -- "Who only hath immortality" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- God ALONE has immortality; implies no human possesses it inherently. Supports conditional immortality. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): God. Statement is about divine exclusivity. Implication falls on humans. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Monos (G3440, alone/only) echon (having) athanasin (immortality G110). Grammar unambiguous: "alone having" admits no exceptions. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Epistle -- doxology within didactic letter. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E16 (dead perish without resurrection), E17-E18 (soul can die/be destroyed). PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E20 (Gen 3:19) -- "Dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return" -- Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- death defined as return to dust. No surviving consciousness mentioned. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): Adam / generic humanity. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Aphar attah veel-aphar tashuv (dust you are and to dust you shall return). Grammar unambiguous. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Narrative -- direct speech of God. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E1 (thoughts perish), E21 (Gen 2:7, creation formula). PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E-Items with Candidate ECT (Tree 3 Walkthrough)¶

    E41 (Luke 16:23) -- "In hell [hades] he lift up his eyes, being in torments" -- Candidate: ECT

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V2 -- "torments" (basanois G931), conscious experience in hades. Candidate: ECT.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "A certain rich man" (anthropos tis plousios) -- a character in a parabolic narrative. The opening formula matches Luke 12:16 (labeled parabole) and 16:1 (universally parabolic). Luke 15:3 provides a single parabole label for the discourse. FAIL. Subject is a parabolic character, not a literal human.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Parabolic discourse (Luke 15:3-16:31). FAIL. Genre is parabolic.
    • Step 3 Reclassification:
    • RC1: Gate 1 fails (parabolic character). Gate 3 fails (parabolic genre).
    • RC2: Corrected observation: "A parabolic character is depicted in torments in hades within a parable whose stated point is 'they have Moses and the prophets; let them hear them' (vv.29,31)."
    • RC3: Re-enter V1/V2: Parabolic depiction does not constitute didactic teaching about the literal state of the dead. Neither V1 nor V2 applies to a parabolic character as evidence about human fate.
    • Result: Gates 1 and 3 fail. Classification revised to: Neutral.

    E42 (Rev 6:9-11) -- "I saw under the altar the souls of them that were slain ... they cried with a loud voice" -- Candidate: ECT

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V2 -- "cried out" could suggest conscious activity. Candidate: ECT.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "Souls" (psychas G5590) under the altar in an apocalyptic vision. Every surrounding element is symbolic (seals, horsemen, stars falling, sky rolling up). Parallel: Abel's blood "cries" from the ground (Gen 4:10) -- forensic personification. Rev 16:7: the altar itself speaks. FAIL. Subjects are symbolic vision-figures, not literal humans.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Apocalyptic vision (Revelation's seal sequence). FAIL. Genre is apocalyptic.
    • Step 3 Reclassification:
    • RC1: Gate 1 fails (symbolic vision-figures). Gate 3 fails (apocalyptic genre).
    • RC2: Corrected observation: "In an apocalyptic vision, symbolic figures under a symbolic altar cry out in the manner of Abel's blood crying from the ground."
    • RC3: Re-enter V1/V2: Apocalyptic personification does not constitute didactic teaching about human consciousness in death. Rev 20:4-5 states these same souls "LIVED" at the first resurrection -- they were not alive before.
    • Result: Gates 1 and 3 fail. Classification revised to: Neutral.

    E43 (2 Cor 5:8) -- "Absent from the body, and to be present with the Lord" -- Candidate: ECT

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V2 -- "present with the Lord" could suggest conscious existence after death. Candidate: ECT.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): Paul speaking about believers generally. Literal humans. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser: ekdemesai (V-AAN, Aor Act Inf of ekdemeo G1553 = to be absent from) ek tou somatos (from the body) kai (CONJ) endemesai (V-AAN, Aor Act Inf of endemeo G1736 = to be at home with) pros ton Kurion (with the Lord). Two aorist infinitives connected by kai. The aorist DOES NOT specify temporal relationship or duration between the two events. The reading "absent from body THEREFORE IMMEDIATELY present with Lord" requires importing temporal simultaneity that the grammar does not contain. FAIL. Grammar allows alternative: death (absent from body) leads to resurrection (present with Lord) without specifying intervening duration.
    • Gate 2 additional: V.4 (same passage): Paul explicitly does NOT want to be ekdysasthai (unclothed/disembodied) but ependysasthai (clothed upon). Purpose: "that mortality (to thneton G2349) might be swallowed up by life (tes zoes G2222)" -- resurrection language from 1 Cor 15:54.
    • Step 3 Reclassification:
    • RC1: Gate 2 fails -- aorist infinitives do not specify temporal relationship; "immediately" is imported.
    • RC2: Corrected observation: "Paul expresses willingness to depart from the body and be at home with the Lord, using two aorist infinitives that state both events without specifying the temporal relationship between them."
    • RC3: Re-enter V1/V2: The corrected observation contains neither destruction nor conscious-torment vocabulary. It is a statement of Paul's preference and confidence.
    • Result: Gate 2 fails. Classification revised to: Neutral.

    E44 (Phil 1:23) -- "Having a desire to depart, and to be with Christ; which is far better" -- Candidate: ECT

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V2 -- "to be with Christ" could suggest conscious intermediate state. Candidate: ECT.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): Paul. Literal human. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser: analysai (V-AAN, Aor Act Inf of analyo G360 = to depart) kai (CONJ) syn Christo (with Christ) einai (V-PAN, Pres Act Inf of eimi = to be). The articular infinitive to analysai is paired with syn Christo einai via kai without specifying temporal duration between them. Same grammatical pattern as 2 Cor 5:8. FAIL. Grammar does not specify temporal immediacy between departing and being with Christ.
    • Gate 2 additional: Same letter -- Phil 3:11: "if by any means I might attain unto the resurrection of the dead" (ten exanastasin ten ek nekron). Phil 3:20-21: "we look for the Saviour...who shall change our vile body." Paul's hope is resurrection, not disembodied intermediate existence.
    • Step 3 Reclassification:
    • RC1: Gate 2 fails -- kai connecting two infinitives does not establish temporal immediacy.
    • RC2: Corrected observation: "Paul expresses desire to depart and be with Christ, using two infinitives connected by kai that state both events without specifying temporal relationship."
    • RC3: Re-enter V1/V2: Corrected observation contains neither destruction nor conscious-torment vocabulary.
    • Result: Gate 2 fails. Classification revised to: Neutral.

    E45 (Luke 23:43) -- "Verily I say unto thee, To day shalt thou be with me in paradise" -- Candidate: ECT

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V2 -- "paradise" with "today" could suggest immediate conscious presence. Candidate: ECT.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): Jesus and the thief. Literal humans. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser: semeron (G4594, ADV = today). The adverb semeron can grammatically modify either lego (V-PAI-1S = I say) or ese (V-FDI-2S = you will be). Greek manuscripts contain NO punctuation. The comma placement is entirely editorial (inserted by translators). FAIL. Reading depends on editorial punctuation absent from the original Greek.
    • Gate 2 additional: John 20:17 -- three days later, Jesus told Mary: oupo (G3768, ADV-N = not yet) anababeka (V-RAI-1S, Perf Act Ind 1s of anabaino G305 = I have ascended) pros ton Patera (to the Father). If paradise = God's presence, Jesus was not there on Friday. The proposed ECT reading creates a direct contradiction with John 20:17.
    • Step 3 Reclassification:
    • RC1: Gate 2 fails -- reading depends on editorial comma placement absent from original Greek. John 20:17 contradicts the proposed reading.
    • RC2: Corrected observation: "Jesus uses the formula 'Truly I say unto thee today' (semeron modifying lego, as in the Syriac Peshitta reading) followed by a promise of future paradise."
    • RC3: Re-enter V1/V2: Corrected observation contains neither destruction nor conscious-torment vocabulary. It is a promise with unspecified timing.
    • Result: Gate 2 fails. Classification revised to: Neutral.

    N1 -- "When ruach/breath departs, the entire person ceases conscious existence" -- Based on: E1, E2, E20, E21 -- Candidate: Cond.

If breath departs and dust returns, the living being ceases. Would an ECT scholar agree? An ECT scholar would argue that the "soul" continues. But that requires ADDING a concept (immortal soul) that E1-E2 explicitly negate (thoughts perish, know nothing). PASS -- the disagreement requires adding a concept the texts do not contain.
    • N-Test 2 (No interpretation): Is this the only possible conclusion from "thoughts perish" + "dead know nothing" + "dust returns"? Yes -- these statements together leave no room for continuing consciousness. PASS.
