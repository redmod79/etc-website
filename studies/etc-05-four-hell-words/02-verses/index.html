    Verses¶

    Question¶

    Semantic range of Sheol (H7585), Hades (G86), Gehenna (G1067), Tartaroo (G5020). Interchangeability? Does any inherently mean eternal torment? LXX translation patterns.

    Jacob expects to go to sheol mourning¶

    And all his sons and all his daughters rose up to comfort him; but he refused to be comforted; and he said, For I will go down into the grave unto my son mourning. Thus his father wept for him.

    Context: Jacob believes Joseph is dead. A righteous patriarch expects sheol.

    Notes: First sheol occurrence. Jacob is righteous, yet expects sheol. KJV: 'grave.'

    Korah swallowed alive into sheol¶

    Numbers 16:30: But if the LORD make a new thing, and the earth open her mouth, and swallow them up, with all that [appertain] unto them, and they go down quick into the pit; then ye shall understand that these men have provoked the LORD. [...] Numbers 16:33: They, and all that [appertained] to them, went down alive into the pit, and the earth closed upon them: and they perished from among the congregation.

    Context: Judgment on Korah for rebellion. KJV: 'pit' for sheol.

    Notes: Sheol receives the judged. 'Perished from among the congregation' — cessation language.

    Fire burns to lowest sheol¶

    For a fire is kindled in mine anger, and shall burn unto the lowest hell, and shall consume the earth with her increase, and set on fire the foundations of the mountains.

    Context: Song of Moses — divine judgment on unfaithful Israel.

    Notes: KJV: 'lowest hell.' Fire metaphor about extent of God's consuming anger. Verb 'consume' (akal) = destruction.

    The LORD brings down to sheol and raises up¶

    The LORD killeth, and maketh alive: he bringeth down to the grave, and bringeth up.

    Context: Hannah's prayer — celebrating God's sovereignty over life and death.

    Notes: Kill/make alive = bring down to sheol/bring up. Sheol = death, with resurrection hope. KJV: 'grave.'

    Hide me in sheol until wrath passes¶

    O that thou wouldest hide me in the grave, that thou wouldest keep me secret, until thy wrath be past, that thou wouldest appoint me a set time, and remember me!

    Context: Job's lament about human mortality.

    Notes: Sheol as temporary hiding place. 'Until thy wrath be past' + 'set time' = temporal. KJV: 'grave.'

    Rephaim tremble; sheol naked before God; Abaddon uncovered¶

    Job 26:5: Dead [things] are formed from under the waters, and the inhabitants thereof. [...] Job 26:6: Hell [is] naked before him, and destruction hath no covering.

    Context: Job describes God's power over all creation including the underworld.

    Notes: H7496 rephaim + H7585 sheol + H11 abaddon. Sheol-Abaddon pairing.

    No remembrance in death; no praise in sheol¶

    For in death [there is] no remembrance of thee: in the grave who shall give thee thanks?

    Context: David's prayer in distress — psalm of lament.

    Notes: E-tier: sheol characterized by 'no remembrance' and inability to praise. KJV: 'grave.' Didactic psalm.

    Wicked turned into sheol¶

    The wicked shall be turned into hell, [and] all the nations that forget God.

    Context: Psalm of praise for God's justice.

    Notes: KJV: 'hell.' Same word (sheol) as Ps 6:5 ('grave'). Wicked go to same place as Jacob/David/Job.

    God will not leave my soul in sheol¶

    For thou wilt not leave my soul in hell; neither wilt thou suffer thine Holy One to see corruption.

    Context: Messianic psalm — quoted by Peter in Acts 2:27,31.

    Notes: Pivotal: OT sheol (H7585), NT quotation uses hades (G86). Confirms sheol = hades. Also pairs sheol with shachath (H7845). KJV: 'hell.'

    Sheep laid in sheol; God redeems from sheol¶

    Psalm 49:14: Like sheep they are laid in the grave; death shall feed on them; and the upright shall have dominion over them in the morning; and their beauty shall consume in the grave from their dwelling. [...] Psalm 49:15: But God will redeem my soul from the power of the grave: for he shall receive me. Selah.

    Context: Wisdom psalm contrasting foolish rich and righteous.

    Notes: Three sheol refs, all 'grave.' Death 'feeds on' wicked (consumption). Righteous hope: God redeems from sheol's power.

    No wonders, lovingkindness, or praise in sheol¶

    Psalm 88:10: Wilt thou shew wonders to the dead? shall the dead arise [and] praise thee? Selah. [...] Psalm 88:11: Shall thy lovingkindness be declared in the grave? [or] thy faithfulness in destruction? [...] Psalm 88:12: Shall thy wonders be known in the dark? and thy righteousness in the land of forgetfulness?

    Context: Darkest psalm — lament with no resolution.

    Notes: E-tier: Comprehensive negation. Parallels: grave (sheol) = destruction (abaddon) = dark = land of forgetfulness.

    The dead praise not the LORD¶

    The dead praise not the LORD, neither any that go down into silence.

    Context: Psalm contrasting living who praise with dead who cannot.

    Notes: Sheol = 'silence.' Consistent with Ps 6:5, 88:10-12, Ecc 9:10.

    If I make my bed in sheol, thou art there¶

    If I ascend up into heaven, thou [art] there: if I make my bed in hell, behold, thou [art there].

    Context: David on God's omnipresence.

    Notes: Heaven-sheol merism. God's omnipresence, not sheol's nature. KJV: 'hell.'

    Way of life above; depart from sheol beneath¶

    The way of life [is] above to the wise, that he may depart from hell beneath.

    Context: Proverb contrasting wise (life) and foolish (sheol/death).

    Notes: Life = above; sheol = beneath. Wise avoids premature death. KJV: 'hell.'

    Sheol and Abaddon never satisfied¶

    Hell and destruction are never full; so the eyes of man are never satisfied.

    Context: Proverbial comparison: insatiability.

    Notes: Sheol-Abaddon pairing. KJV: 'Hell and destruction.' Hebrew pairs them as synonyms.

    The dead know nothing; no work in sheol¶

    Ecclesiastes 9:5: For the living know that they shall die: but the dead know not any thing, neither have they any more a reward; for the memory of them is forgotten. [...] Ecclesiastes 9:6: Also their love, and their hatred, and their envy, is now perished; neither have they any more a portion for ever in any [thing] that is done under the sun. [...] Ecclesiastes 9:10: Whatsoever thy hand findeth to do, do [it] with thy might; for [there is] no work, nor device, nor knowledge, nor wisdom, in the grave, whither thou goest.

    Context: Qoheleth's observation about death and the state of the dead.

    Notes: Comprehensive negation of consciousness: no knowledge, no reward, no work, no device, no wisdom in sheol. KJV: 'grave.'

    Sheol enlarges herself without measure¶

    Therefore hell hath enlarged herself, and opened her mouth without measure: and their glory, and their multitude, and their pomp, and he that rejoiceth, shall descend into it.

    Context: Woe oracle against Jerusalem's corruption.

    Notes: Personification of sheol as insatiable. Imagery is swallowing/consuming, not torturing. KJV: 'hell.'

    Sheol stirred up to meet king of Babylon¶

    Isaiah 14:9: Hell from beneath is moved for thee to meet [thee] at thy coming: it stirreth up the dead for thee, [even] all the chief ones of the earth; it hath raised up from their thrones all the kings of the nations. [...] Isaiah 14:10: All they shall speak and say unto thee, Art thou also become weak as we? art thou become like unto us? [...] Isaiah 14:11: Thy pomp is brought down to the grave, [and] the noise of thy viols: the worm is spread under thee, and the worms cover thee. [...] Isaiah 14:15: Yet thou shalt be brought down to hell, to the sides of the pit.

    Context: Mashal (taunt-poem, v.4) against king of Babylon. Genre: satirical prophecy.

    Notes: Genre critical: v.4 labels this mashal. Dead 'speaking' = poetic personification. v.10: 'weak as we.' v.11: worms = decomposition. KJV: 'hell/grave.'

    Sheol cannot praise; death cannot celebrate¶

    For the grave cannot praise thee, death can [not] celebrate thee: they that go down into the pit cannot hope for thy truth.

    Context: Hezekiah's thanksgiving for deliverance from death.

    Notes: E-tier: sheol = no praise; death = no celebration; pit = no hope. Three parallel terms. KJV: 'grave.'

    Carcasses — worm not die, fire not quenched¶

    And they shall go forth, and look upon the carcases of the men that have transgressed against me: for their worm shall not die, neither shall their fire be quenched; and they shall be an abhorring unto all flesh.

    Context: Final verse of Isaiah. Righteous look upon aftermath of judgment. Quoted in Mark 9:48.

    Notes: Subjects are 'carcases' (peger H6297) = dead bodies, NEVER used for living. Worm (tolaath H8438) = decomposition agent. 'Unquenchable fire' = fire completing its work.

    O sheol, I will be thy destruction¶

    I will ransom them from the power of the grave; I will redeem them from death: O death, I will be thy plagues; O grave, I will be thy destruction: repentance shall be hid from mine eyes.

    Context: God's promise of triumph over death — quoted by Paul in 1 Cor 15:55.

    Notes: Sheol-maveth parallelism. God DESTROYS sheol/death, not preserves them. Paul quotes with hades replacing sheol.

    Out of the belly of sheol cried I¶

    And said, I cried by reason of mine affliction unto the LORD, and he heard me; out of the belly of hell cried I, [and] thou heardest my voice.

    Context: Jonah's prayer from inside the fish — near-death experience.

    Notes: Sheol used metaphorically for near-death. Jonah was alive. KJV: 'hell.'

    Capernaum brought down to hades¶

    And thou, Capernaum, which art exalted unto heaven, shalt be brought down to hell: for if the mighty works, which have been done in thee, had been done in Sodom, it would have remained until this day.

    Context: Jesus pronounces woe on unrepentant cities.

    Notes: Hades (G86) = humiliation/judgment. Heaven-hades merism echoing Isa 14:13-15.

    Gates of hades shall not prevail¶

    And I say also unto thee, That thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my church; and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it.

    Context: Peter's confession at Caesarea Philippi.

    Notes: Hades as power of death. 'Gates of hades' = power of death won't overcome the church.

    Capernaum thrust down to hades¶

    And thou, Capernaum, which art exalted to heaven, shalt be thrust down to hell.

    Context: Parallel to Matt 11:23.

    Notes: Same hades usage. Humiliation/judgment imagery.

    Rich man in hades, in torments¶

    And in hell he lift up his eyes, being in torments, and seeth Abraham afar off, and Lazarus in his bosom.

    Context: Parable of Rich Man and Lazarus. Genre: parable (series in Luke 15-16).

    Notes: Hades (G86) + basanos (G931). Genre = parable — fails Tree 3 Gates 1,3. ONLY passage associating hades with consciousness. Word is basanos (touchstone), not basanizo.

    Peter quotes Ps 16:10: soul not left in hades¶

    Because thou wilt not leave my soul in hell, neither wilt thou suffer thine Holy One to see corruption.

    Context: Peter's Pentecost sermon quoting Psalm 16:10 (LXX).

    Notes: E-tier: Direct quotation with hades (G86) replacing sheol (H7585). Confirms sheol-hades equivalence.

    Christ's soul not left in hades¶

    He seeing this before spake of the resurrection of Christ, that his soul was not left in hell, neither his flesh did see corruption.

    Context: Peter's interpretation of Ps 16:10 as messianic prophecy.

    Notes: Second confirmation: hades = sheol. Christ in hades = general abode of ALL dead, not punishment.

    O death, thy sting; O grave, thy victory¶

    O death, where [is] thy sting? O grave, where [is] thy victory?

    Context: Paul's resurrection chapter climax, quoting Hosea 13:14.

    Notes: Hades = 'grave' in KJV. Third NT confirmation of sheol-hades equivalence. Hades = defeated enemy.

    Keys of hades and death¶

    [I am] he that liveth, and was dead; and, behold, I am alive for evermore, Amen; and have the keys of hell and of death.

    Context: Christ's self-identification to John.

    Notes: Hades-thanatos pairing (cf. sheol-maveth in OT). Christ holds authority over hades.

    Death and Hades follow the pale horse¶

    And I looked, and behold a pale horse: and his name that sat on him was Death, and Hell followed with him. And power was given unto them over the fourth part of the earth, to kill with sword, and with hunger, and with death, and with the beasts of the earth.

    Context: Fourth seal — apocalyptic vision of death.

    Notes: Hades personified alongside Death, as in OT sheol-maveth. Power is 'to kill' — death vocabulary.

    Death and hades give up dead; cast into lake of fire¶

    Revelation 20:13: And the sea gave up the dead which were in it; and death and hell delivered up the dead which were in them: and they were judged every man according to their works. [...] Revelation 20:14: And death and hell were cast into the lake of fire. This is the second death.

    Context: Great White Throne judgment.

    Notes: Hades is TEMPORARY — delivers up dead, then destroyed. DISTINCT from lake of fire. Lake of fire = 'second death.'

    In danger of gehenna of fire¶

    But I say unto you, That whosoever is angry with his brother without a cause shall be in danger of the judgment: and whosoever shall say to his brother, Raca, shall be in danger of the council: but whosoever shall say, Thou fool, shall be in danger of hell fire.

    Context: Sermon on the Mount — intensifying the law on murder/anger.
