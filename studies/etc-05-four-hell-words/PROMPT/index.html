    Focus Areas¶

    FA1. Semantic Range of Sheol (H7585): This area must establish what sheol actually means in its OT contexts before comparing it to Greek NT terms. The KJV's inconsistent translation of the same word as both 'grave' and 'hell' raises immediate questions about whether theological assumptions drove translation choices. Verses: Genesis 37:35, Numbers 16:30, Deuteronomy 32:22, 1 Samuel 2:6, Job 7:9, Job 14:13, Job 26:5-6, Psalm 6:5, Psalm 9:17, Psalm 16:10, Psalm 49:14-15, Psalm 86:13, Psalm 88:10-12, Psalm 115:17, Psalm 139:8, Proverbs 5:5, Proverbs 15:24, Proverbs 27:20, Ecclesiastes 9:5-6, Ecclesiastes 9:10, Song of Solomon 8:6, Isaiah 5:14, Isaiah 14:9-11, Isaiah 14:15, Isaiah 28:15, Isaiah 38:18, Ezekiel 32:21, Ezekiel 32:27, Hosea 13:14, Amos 9:2, Jonah 2:2, Habakkuk 2:5 Strong's: H7585, H7496, H11, H7845, H4194

    FA2. Semantic Range of Hades (G86): If hades is semantically equivalent to sheol (as Acts 2:27,31 confirms), then the NT concept of hades should carry the same characteristics: temporary, receives both righteous and wicked, characterized by unconsciousness in didactic passages. Luke 16:23 is the key test case. Verses: Matthew 11:23, Matthew 16:18, Luke 10:15, Luke 16:23, Acts 2:27, Acts 2:31, 1 Corinthians 15:55, Revelation 1:18, Revelation 6:8, Revelation 20:13-14 Strong's: G86, H7585, G2288

    FA3. Semantic Range of Gehenna (G1067): This area must carefully distinguish between what the text actually says about gehenna and what theological tradition has read into it. The vocabulary Jesus chooses (destroy vs. torment) and the OT background (valley of slaughter, corpse disposal) are the primary evidence. Verses: Matthew 5:22, Matthew 5:29-30, Matthew 10:28, Matthew 18:9, Matthew 23:15, Matthew 23:33, Mark 9:43-48, Luke 12:5, James 3:6, Joshua 15:8, 2 Kings 23:10, 2 Chronicles 28:3, 2 Chronicles 33:6, Jeremiah 7:31-32, Jeremiah 19:2-6, Jeremiah 32:35, Isaiah 30:33, Isaiah 66:24 Strong's: G1067, G622, H1516, H2011, H8612, H6297, G4663, H8438

    FA4. Tartaroo (G5020) — Single Occurrence Analysis: Tartaroo is the most specialized of the four words. Its restriction to a single verse about angelic imprisonment makes it the easiest to analyze but also the most important to correctly delimit — it should not be conflated with human eschatology. Verses: 2 Peter 2:4, Jude 1:6, Jude 1:7 Strong's: G5020, G126

    FA5. LXX Translation Patterns — Sheol to Greek: The LXX data is critical evidence. The overwhelming sheol→hades mapping (58 times) establishes the semantic equivalence. But the secondary mappings (thanatos 14x, katabaino 17x, katago 10x, apoleia 3x) show that translators sometimes chose context-sensitive renderings — all of which point to death/descent/destruction, never to torment. Verses: Psalm 16:10, Acts 2:27, Acts 2:31, Hosea 13:14, 1 Corinthians 15:55, Isaiah 38:18, Psalm 6:5 Strong's: H7585, G86, G2288, G2609, G2597, G684, G5590

    FA6. Interchangeability — Can These Words Substitute for Each Other?: If these four words designated a single concept, they should be interchangeable in context. The test is whether you can substitute 'gehenna' for 'sheol' in passages like Psalm 6:5 or Genesis 37:35 without absurdity. If Jacob expected to go to a place of fiery punishment to be with his dead son, the text becomes incoherent.
