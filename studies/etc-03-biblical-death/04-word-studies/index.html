    Word Studies¶

    Question¶

    What does 'death' mean in the Bible? Is death cessation of existence, separation of soul from body, or separation from God? Examine the Hebrew words maveth (H4194) and mut (H4191), and the Greek words thanatos (G2288) and apothnesko (G599). Trace the origin of death (Gen 2-3), the nature and meaning of physical death, spiritual death, and the second death (Rev 2:11; 20:6, 14; 21:8).

    H4194 — maveth¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H4194-maveth.md

    Occurrences: 170

    Primary Hebrew noun for death. 170 tokens across 27 books. Nearly all renderings are 'death' (~92%). LXX maps overwhelmingly to thanatos (G2288) with 97 co-occurrences (PMI 5.98). Semantic categories include physical death as biographical marker, death as legal penalty, death personified, death vs. life as moral contrast, deliverance from death, death as realm/destination, and death defeated/swallowed up (Isa 25:8; Hos 13:14).

    Semantic categories: physical death, legal penalty, death personified, death vs. life contrast, deliverance from death, death as realm, death defeated

    Key verses: Gen 2:17, Deut 30:15, Isa 25:8, Hos 13:14, Prov 18:21

    Relevance: Primary Hebrew death noun — core study target. The LXX bridge to thanatos (G2288) ensures cross-testament continuity.

    H4191 — muwth¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H4191-muwth.md

    Occurrences: 1028

    Primary Hebrew verb 'to die.' 1028 occurrences — the most frequent death word in the OT. Semantic categories: natural/physical death, judicial/capital death penalty (mot yumat formula), divine death decree and warning (Gen 2:17 mot tamut), violent/causative death (Hiphil), and ritual impurity from the dead. LXX maps primarily to apothnesko (G599, 366 co-occ) and thanatoo (G2289, 121 co-occ).

    Semantic categories: natural death, judicial death penalty, divine death warning, violent death, contact with the dead

    Key verses: Gen 2:17, Gen 3:3-4, Exo 21:12, Ezek 18:4, Ecc 9:5

    Relevance: Primary Hebrew death verb — the mot tamut construction in Gen 2:17 is foundational for understanding the Bible's first death warning.

    G2288 — thanatos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2288-thanatos.md

    Occurrences: 119

    Primary Greek noun for death. 119 tokens in 106 verses across 15 books. Semantic categories: physical/biological death, death as consequence of sin (Rom 5:12; 6:23), death as reigning cosmic power (Rom 5:14,17), Christ's atoning death, spiritual death (John 5:24; 1 John 3:14; Rom 8:6), death personified/second death (Rev 6:8; 20:14; 21:4), and shadow of death (OT allusion). Primary antonym: zoe (G2222, life). Hebrew source: maveth (H4194) with PMI 27.43.

    Semantic categories: physical death, consequence of sin, cosmic power, Christ's death, spiritual death, second death, shadow of death

    Key verses: Rom 5:12, Rom 6:23, 1 Cor 15:26, Rev 20:14, Rev 21:4

    Relevance: Primary Greek death noun — used for physical death, spiritual death, and the second death. Same word across all three categories.

    G599 — apothnesko¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G599-apothnēskō.md

    Occurrences: 122

    Primary Greek verb 'to die.' 122 occurrences across 14 books. Compound form (apo + thnesko) emphasizing completeness of dying. Semantic categories: physical death (narrative), Christ's substitutionary death, dying to sin/law/self (Paul's metaphor), death as consequence of sin (John 8:21,24; Rom 5:15), death overcome by resurrection, willingness to die. Hebrew source: muwth (H4191, 366 LXX co-occ, PMI 28.53).

    Semantic categories: physical death, Christ's death, dying to sin, consequence of sin, death overcome, grain metaphor

    Key verses: Rom 5:6, 1 Cor 15:3, 1 Cor 15:22, Rom 6:2, John 11:14

    Relevance: Primary Greek death verb — the compound form intensifies the simple verb thnesko, emphasizing the completeness of dying.

    G3498 — nekros¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G3498-nekros.md

    Occurrences: 132

    Greek adjective/noun 'dead.' 132 occurrences. Three semantic domains: (1) physical corpse/deceased, (2) spiritually dead (Eph 2:1,5; Col 2:13), (3) inoperative/powerless (James 2:17; Rom 7:8). The word evokes a corpse — utterly passive, unable to respond. Used in 'ek nekron' (from the dead) ~50x for resurrection.

    Semantic categories: physical corpse, spiritual death, inoperative

    Key verses: Eph 2:1, Col 2:13, Rev 20:12, James 2:26, Rom 6:11

    Relevance: The adjective describing the death-state. Its metaphorical use for spiritual death borrows from the literal meaning (cessation, inoperativeness).

    H5315 — nephesh¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H5315-nephesh.md

    Occurrences: 788

    Hebrew noun meaning soul/life/person/creature. 682 unique verses, 788 tokens across 31 books. Semantic categories: breathing creature (Gen 1:20,24; 2:7), whole person/legal individual (Exo 1:5), life/vitality at risk (Job 2:4), inner self/emotions (1 Sam 1:10), wholehearted devotion, reflexive pronoun, and corpse/ritual uncleanness (Num 6:6). LXX maps overwhelmingly to psyche (G5590, 599 co-occ). Critical: nephesh chayyah is used for both animals and humans; man BECAME a nephesh chayyah, not 'received' one.

    Semantic categories: breathing creature, whole person, life at risk, inner self, devotion, corpse

    Key verses: Gen 2:7, Ezek 18:4, Num 6:6, Deut 4:29, Ps 33:19

    Relevance: Central to the 'separation of soul from body' question. Nephesh can die (Ezek 18:4), is used for corpses (Num 6:6), and is shared with animals — arguing against an immortal-soul definition of death.

    G5590 — psyche¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G5590-psyche.md

    Occurrences: 105

    Greek noun meaning soul/life/person. 105 occurrences. Semantic categories: inner self/emotions (Matt 26:38), physical life/life-force (Matt 16:25; John 10:11), person/individual (Acts 2:41), living creature (Rev 16:3), mind/will (Eph 6:6). Hebrew source: nephesh (H5315, 599 LXX co-occ). Note: Thayer's lexicon entry importing 'immortal' to pneuma has been corrected — Scripture grants immortality to God alone (1 Tim 6:16).

    Semantic categories: inner self, physical life, person, living creature, mind/will

    Key verses: Matt 10:28, Matt 16:25, Rev 6:9, Acts 2:41, 1 Pet 3:20

    Relevance: The Greek soul word. Matt 10:28 says God can DESTROY (apollymi) the psyche — contradicting the immortal-soul doctrine.

    H7585 — sheol¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H7585-showl.md

    Occurrences: 68

    Hebrew noun meaning grave/realm of the dead. 64 unique verses across 17 books. KJV translates as 'hell' (31v, 48%), 'grave' (26v, 41%), 'pit' (3v). Semantic categories: realm of the dead (universal destination), descent language (yarad 26x), sheol-death paired terms (15 co-occ with maveth), fate of the wicked, rescue from sheol (Ps 16:10), sheol as insatiable (Prov 27:20). LXX maps overwhelmingly to hades (G86, 58 co-occ, PMI 8.31). Characterized by unconsciousness, silence, darkness (Ecc 9:10; Ps 6:5; 115:17).

    Semantic categories: realm of the dead, descent, death parallel, fate of wicked, rescue from, insatiable

    Key verses: Gen 37:35, Ps 16:10, Ecc 9:10, Hos 13:14, Ps 9:17

    Relevance: The OT grave/underworld word. Characterized as a place of unconsciousness and silence, not conscious torment.

    G622 — apollymi¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G622-apollymi.md

    Occurrences: 102

    Greek verb 'to destroy fully, perish, lose.' 102 tokens across 15 books. Active: to destroy/ruin (Matt 2:13; 10:28; 22:7). Middle/passive: to perish (John 3:16; Luke 13:3; 2 Pet 3:9). Middle with object: to lose (Matt 16:25; Luke 15). Louw-Nida domain 20.003 (Destroy) and 13.003 (state of non-existence). Primary Hebrew source: abad (H6, 141 co-occ, PMI 6.14). Primary antonym: sozo (G4982, save).

    Semantic categories: physical destruction, divine judgment, eschatological perishing, losing life/soul, losing possession, decay

    Key verses: Matt 10:28, John 3:16, 2 Pet 3:9, Luke 13:3, Luke 19:10

    Relevance: Primary NT destruction verb. The perish/eternal-life binary (John 3:16) depends on this word meaning actual destruction, not ongoing torment.

    G684 — apoleia¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G684-apoleia.md

    Occurrences: 20

    Greek noun 'destruction, perdition, waste.' 20 tokens across 11 books. Semantic categories: final eschatological ruin (Matt 7:13; Rom 9:22; Phil 3:19), 'son of perdition' (John 17:12; 2 Thess 2:3), beast going into perdition (Rev 17:8,11), waste/squandering (Matt 26:8), false teachers' ruin (2 Pet 2:1-3). Louw-Nida domain 20.031 (Destroy). Hebrew source: abad word-group (H6/H9/H11).

    Semantic categories: eschatological ruin, son of perdition, beast's end, waste, heresy-induced ruin

    Key verses: Matt 7:13, John 17:12, Phil 3:19, Rev 17:8, 2 Pet 3:7

    Relevance: The noun form of destruction — the destined state of the wicked. Contrasted with soteria (salvation).

    G110 — athanasia¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G110-athanasia.md

    Occurrences: 3

    Greek noun 'immortality/deathlessness.' Only 3 occurrences: 1 Cor 15:53,54 (mortal puts on immortality at resurrection); 1 Tim 6:16 (God alone has immortality). Alpha-privative of thanatos — the negation of death. All 3 in accusative singular feminine. Exclusively Pauline. No Hebrew source via LXX.

    Semantic categories: resurrection transformation, exclusive divine attribute

    Key verses: 1 Cor 15:53, 1 Cor 15:54, 1 Tim 6:16

    Relevance: Only 3 occurrences — God alone possesses it (1 Tim 6:16); believers put it on at resurrection (1 Cor 15:53-54). Not presently possessed by humans.

    G861 — aphtharsia¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G861-aphtharsia.md

    Occurrences: 8

    Greek noun 'incorruptibility.' 8 occurrences across 5 books. Dominant category: resurrection transformation (1 Cor 15:42,50,53,54 — 4 of 8). Also: object of aspiration (Rom 2:7 — sought; 2 Tim 1:10 — brought to light). Ethical purity (Eph 6:24; Tit 2:7). Exclusively Pauline. Paired with athanasia in 1 Cor 15:53-54 (corruptible/incorruption || mortal/immortality).

    Semantic categories: resurrection transformation, aspiration, ethical purity

    Key verses: 1 Cor 15:42, 1 Cor 15:53, Rom 2:7, 2 Tim 1:10

    Relevance: Something put on at resurrection, sought by believers, brought to light by Christ — not inherently possessed.

    G2673 — katargeo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2673-katargeo.md

    Occurrences: 32

    Greek verb 'to render entirely idle/useless, abolish.' 27 tokens across 9 books, overwhelmingly Pauline. Semantic categories: literal render useless (Luke 13:7), nullify law/faith/promise (Rom 3:3,31; 4:14), abolish powers/enemies including death (1 Cor 15:24,26; 2 Tim 1:10; Heb 2:14), old covenant glory passing away (2 Cor 3:7-14), cease/pass away (1 Cor 13:8-11). Root: kata + argeo (to be idle).

    Semantic categories: render useless, nullify, abolish enemies, old covenant passing, cease

    Key verses: 1 Cor 15:26, 2 Tim 1:10, Heb 2:14, 2 Thess 2:8

    Relevance: The verb used for death being 'destroyed/abolished' — death is rendered inoperative, not merely subdued.

    G2837 — koimao¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2837-koimao.md

    Occurrences: 19

    Greek verb 'to put to sleep.' 19 occurrences. Dominant NT usage: death as sleep (euphemism) — Acts 7:60; 13:36; 1 Cor 15:6,18,20,51; 1 Thess 4:13-15; 2 Pet 3:4. Also: literal sleep (Luke 22:45; Acts 12:6). Resurrection context (1 Cor 15:20,51; Matt 27:52). Sleep = temporary unconsciousness; death = temporary unconsciousness until resurrection.

    Semantic categories: literal sleep, death as sleep, resurrection context

    Key verses: Acts 7:60, 1 Cor 15:6, 1 Cor 15:18, 1 Thess 4:14, John 11:11

    Relevance: The primary NT metaphor for death. Implies unconsciousness during the death-state and expected awakening (resurrection).

    H7307 — ruach¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H7307-ruach.md

    Occurrences: 434

    Hebrew noun meaning wind/breath/spirit. 434 occurrences across 32 books. Semantic categories: Spirit of God/divine Spirit (Gen 1:2; Isa 11:2), wind/meteorological force (Gen 8:1), breath/life-force (Gen 6:17; Ecc 12:7), human spirit/disposition (Gen 41:8; Ps 51:10), spirit beings (1 Sam 16:14), vanity/futility in Ecclesiastes, compass direction. LXX maps primarily to pneuma (G4151, 235x).

    Semantic categories: divine Spirit, wind, breath/life-force, human disposition, spirit beings, vanity

    Key verses: Gen 1:2, Ecc 12:7, Ps 146:4, Ecc 3:21, Ps 104:29

    Relevance: The ruach returns to God at death (Ecc 12:7) — not as a conscious entity but as the animating principle. Removing ruach = death.

    H5397 — nshamah¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H5397-nshamah.md

    Occurrences: 24

    Hebrew noun meaning breath/blast/inspiration. 24 unique verses across 10 books. Semantic categories: breath of life at creation (Gen 2:7; 7:22; Job 33:4), divine blast in judgment (Job 4:9; Ps 18:15; Isa 30:33), total annihilation formula 'no nshamah left' (Deut 20:16; Josh 10:40), human inner life/illumination (Prov 20:27; Job 32:8), every living being (Ps 150:6). LXX maps primarily to pnoe (G4157, 14x, PMI 9.36).

    Semantic categories: breath of life, divine blast, total annihilation, illumination, every living being

    Key verses: Gen 2:7, Gen 7:22, Job 33:4, Ps 150:6, Isa 42:5

    Relevance: The specific breath God breathed into man (Gen 2:7). Its removal = death. It is the animating principle, not a conscious entity.

    G2222 — zoe¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2222-zoe.md

    Occurrences: 134

    Greek noun 'life.' 126 unique verses across 22 books. Dominant usage: eternal/eschatological life, typically qualified by aionios (45 shared verses). Also: life as present possession in the Son (John 5:26; 1 John 5:12), life contrasted with death (19 co-occ with thanatos), symbolic objects of life, earthly life, life as divine attribute (Eph 4:18). Primary antonym: thanatos (G2288). Hebrew source: chay (H2416, 136 co-occ).

    Semantic categories: eternal life, present possession, life vs. death, symbolic objects, divine attribute

    Key verses: John 3:16, Rom 6:23, John 5:24, 1 John 5:12, Rev 21:4

    Relevance: The antonym of thanatos. The life-death binary (death vs. eternal life, not death vs. eternal torment) is central to this study.

    G2349 — thnetos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2349-thnetos.md

    Occurrences: 6

    Greek adjective 'mortal, subject to death.' Only 6 occurrences, exclusively Pauline. Used: mortal body under sin's dominion (Rom 6:12), mortal body awaiting resurrection (Rom 8:11), mortal-to-immortal transformation (1 Cor 15:53,54), life in mortal flesh (2 Cor 4:11; 5:4). Paired with athanasia (G110) as direct antonym in 1 Cor 15:53-54.

    Semantic categories: mortal body, resurrection transformation, mortality

    Key verses: Rom 6:12, Rom 8:11, 1 Cor 15:53, 1 Cor 15:54, 2 Cor 5:4

    Relevance: Humans are mortal — thnetos, subject to death. Immortality must be 'put on' at resurrection.

    G1067 — geenna¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1067-geenna.md

    Occurrences: 12

    Greek noun 'Gehenna/hell.' 12 NT occurrences, 11 by Jesus. Transliteration of Hebrew Valley of Hinnom. Almost exclusively Synoptic + James 3:6. Associated with fire (6x with pyr), body-part removal (6x with skandalizo), and destruction of soul and body (Matt 10:28 with apollymi). Not found in John, Acts, Paul, or Revelation (which uses 'lake of fire'). All of Matthew's 7 gehenna passages pair gehenna with destruction vocabulary, never torment.

    Semantic categories: fiery destruction, self-denial, soul and body destroyed, judicial judgment, moral corruption

    Key verses: Matt 5:22, Matt 10:28, Matt 23:33, Mark 9:43, Luke 12:5

    Relevance: Jesus's term for the place of final judgment. Always paired with destruction vocabulary, not torment vocabulary.

    H6 — abad¶

    Occurrences: 219

    Hebrew verb meaning 'to wander away, lose oneself, perish, destroy.' 219 tokens in 174 OT verses across 25 books. Qal (119x): intransitive 'to perish, be lost.' Piel (41x): causative 'to cause to perish, destroy.' Hiphil (26x): stronger causative 'to make perish, destroy utterly.' Translation: ~55% 'perish,' ~32% 'destroy,' ~6% 'lost.' Used in Ps 146:4 for thoughts 'perishing' at death. Primary LXX bridge: apollymi (G622, 141 co-occ, PMI 6.14). UBS domain: non-existence.

    Semantic categories: perish, destroy, be lost, wander away

    Key verses: Ps 146:4, Deut 4:26, Job 4:9, Ezek 28:16, Ps 9:5

    Relevance: OT destruction vocabulary — the same verb used for the wicked's fate and for thoughts 'perishing' at death. UBS domain classification: non-existence.

    H8045 — shamad¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H8045-shamad.md

    Occurrences: 101

    Hebrew verb meaning 'to desolate, exterminate, destroy.' 92 tokens in 86 OT verses. Almost exclusively causative: Hiphil (69x, active extermination) and Niphal (21x, passive destruction). Total annihilation sense — not damage but extermination to the point of no remnant. ~94% translated 'destroy/destroyed.'

    Semantic categories: exterminate, annihilate, total destruction

    Key verses: Deut 7:24, Deut 28:48, 2 Ki 21:3, Amos 9:8

    Relevance: OT destruction vocabulary — carries the sense of total annihilation, no remnant remaining.

    G862 — aphthartos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G862-aphthartos.md

    Occurrences: 7

    Greek adjective 'incorruptible, undecaying.' 7 occurrences. Applied to God (Rom 1:23; 1 Tim 1:17), the resurrection body (1 Cor 15:52), the imperishable crown (1 Cor 9:25), and the believer's inheritance (1 Pet 1:4,23; 3:4). Cognate of aphtharsia (G861).

    Semantic categories: divine attribute, resurrection body, imperishable

    Key verses: 1 Cor 15:52, 1 Tim 1:17, 1 Pet 1:4

    Relevance: The adjective form of incorruptibility — the resurrection body is raised incorruptible.

    H2416 — chay¶

    G166 — aionios¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G166-aionios.md

    Occurrences: 71

    Greek adjective 'eternal, age-long.' 71 tokens in 69 verses across 19 books. ~92% translated 'eternal' or 'everlasting.' Most characteristic compound: 'eternal life' (zoe aionios, 45 co-occ with zoe). Also: eternal punishment (Matt 25:46), eternal fire (Matt 18:8), eternal glory (2 Tim 2:10). Root: aion (G165, age).

    Semantic categories: eternal duration, eschatological quality

    Key verses: Matt 25:46, John 3:16, Rom 6:23, 2 Tim 1:9

    Relevance: Modifies both 'life' and 'punishment/fire' — the question is whether aionios describes duration of process or permanence of result.
