    Word Studies¶

    Question¶

    Rev 20:10 -- named subjects (devil, beast, false prophet). Applied to humans or supernatural/symbolic entities? Basanizo (G928) usage. Apply Tree 3 subject gate.

    G928 — basanizo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G928-basanizo.md

    Occurrences: 12

    Verb from G931 basanos (touchstone for testing metals). 12 tokens in 12 verses across 6 books. Translation: 'torment' family 7/12 (58.3%); remaining 5 renderings (tossed, toiling, vexed, pained) reveal broader semantic range. Gospels: 6x (physical suffering, demonic encounters). Epistles: 1x (moral distress). Revelation: 5x (eschatological contexts). The 7 non-judgment uses (illness, waves, rowing, distress, childbirth) outnumber the 5 judgment uses. All 5 judgment uses are either in Revelation's apocalyptic framework or involve demons speaking of their own future. No epistle, no Gospel didactic section, and no OT passage uses basanizo for the final fate of generic human wicked.

    Semantic categories: physical illness/suffering, physical distress/battering, moral distress, demonic anticipation of judgment, eschatological judgment (apocalyptic)

    Key verses: Matt 8:29, Matt 14:24, 2 Pet 2:8, Rev 9:5, Rev 14:10, Rev 20:10, Rev 12:2

    Relevance: THE core verb in Rev 20:10. basanisthesontai (V-FPI-3P) is the torment formula. The complete NT distribution shows the word's judgment uses are confined to Revelation's apocalyptic framework and demonic speech. The non-judgment uses demonstrate the word's broader semantic range, preventing the interpretive error of reading only the 'eternal torment' sense.

    G929 — basanismos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G929-basanismos.md

    Occurrences: 6

    Noun from G928. 6 occurrences, ALL in Revelation. Three semantic categories: demonic/supernatural torment (Rev 9:5, time-bounded at 5 months), eschatological judgment torment (Rev 14:11), commercial/imperial torment (Rev 18:7, 10, 15 — Babylon's fall, time-bounded at 'one hour'). Time-bounded in 4 of 6 occurrences. The word family's exclusive concentration in Revelation's apocalyptic framework is a key distributional observation.

    Semantic categories: demonic/supernatural torment, eschatological judgment, commercial/imperial torment

    Key verses: Rev 9:5, Rev 14:11, Rev 18:7, Rev 18:10, Rev 18:15

    Relevance: Noun form of the basanizo word family. All 6 occurrences are in Revelation — the torment word family is genre-specific. Time-bounded in 4 of 6 uses (5 months in 9:5; 1 hour for Babylon in 18:7,10,15). The etc7-11 study documented this distribution.

    G931 — basanos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G931-basanos.md

    Occurrences: 3

    Root noun of the basanizo word family. Originally a touchstone (Lydian stone) used to test the purity of gold/silver. 3 occurrences: Matt 4:24 (physical diseases), Luke 16:23 (parabolic torment in Hades), Luke 16:28 (parabolic 'place of torment'). The etymological base reveals the word family's root meaning: testing/proving through pressure, extended to torture/torment.

    Semantic categories: physical suffering/disease, parabolic afterlife torment, testing/examination (etymological)

    Key verses: Matt 4:24, Luke 16:23, Luke 16:28

    Relevance: The root noun reveals the word family's etymology: touchstone for testing metals. The metaphorical extension from 'testing by stone' to 'torment' is secondary. The Luke 16 uses are in a parabolic context (rich man and Lazarus), not didactic teaching about eschatological fate.

    G930 — basanistes¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G930-basanistes.md

    Occurrences: 1

    Agent noun 'tormentor.' Single occurrence: Matt 18:34 in the parable of the unforgiving servant — 'delivered him to the tormentors.' Parabolic context, not eschatological fate.

    Semantic categories: parabolic tormentor

    Key verses: Matt 18:34

    Relevance: Completes the basanizo word family. The one occurrence is in a parable, not a description of eschatological fate. The 'tormentors' are jailers in a debtor's prison context — temporal, corrective, and parabolic.

    G2342 — therion¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2342-therion.md

    Occurrences: 46

    Neuter noun, wild/dangerous animal. 46 NT occurrences: 38 in Revelation (84%), 8 outside (all literal animal sense). In Revelation, therion is ALWAYS the apocalyptic beast-entity — a symbolic political-religious power with seven heads, ten horns, composite body from Daniel 7 beasts. Outside Revelation: literal wild animals (Mark 1:13, Acts 28:4-5, Heb 12:20), generic animal class (Acts 10:12, 11:6, James 3:7), or metaphorical (Titus 1:12).

    Semantic categories: literal wild animal, generic wild animals (class), metaphorical brutish character, apocalyptic beast (political-religious power), beast as object of false worship

    Key verses: Rev 13:1, Rev 13:11, Rev 17:8, Rev 19:20, Rev 20:10

    Relevance: Identifies one of the three subjects of Rev 20:10. The beast is NOT a human being but a symbolic entity: seven heads, ten horns, composite of Daniel 7 beasts, 'was and is not and shall ascend.' Tree 3 Gate 1 automatically FAILs: 'a symbolic entity (a figure in an apocalyptic vision).'

    G5578 — pseudoprophetes¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G5578-pseudoprophetes.md

    Occurrences: 11

    Masculine noun, compound: false + prophet. 11 occurrences in 7 books. CRITICAL DISTINCTION: SINGULAR in Revelation (3x: Rev 16:13, 19:20, 20:10) = one specific apocalyptic entity (identified as the earth beast of Rev 13:11-17 via Rev 19:20's matching vocabulary). PLURAL elsewhere (7x: Matt 7:15, 24:11, 24:24, Mark 13:22, Luke 6:26, 2 Pet 2:1, 1 John 4:1) = a class of human deceivers. Acts 13:6 is singular with a personal name (Bar-Jesus) — human individual, distinct from the apocalyptic entity.

    Semantic categories: warning against deceptive teachers, eschatological warning (signs and wonders), historical precedent, individual instance (Bar-Jesus), eschatological triad (dragon, beast, false prophet)

    Key verses: Rev 16:13, Rev 19:20, Rev 20:10, Matt 7:15, Matt 24:11, Acts 13:6

    Relevance: Identifies one of the three subjects of Rev 20:10. The singular/plural distinction is critical: the false prophet of Rev 20:10 is the specific apocalyptic entity (= earth beast of Rev 13:11-17), not a generic human false prophet. Rev 19:20 provides the identification key via matching vocabulary (poiesas ta semeia enopion autou). Tree 3 Gate 1 automatically FAILs: 'a symbolic entity.'

    G1228 — diabolos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1228-diabolos.md

    Occurrences: 38

    Adjective used substantively. 36 unique verses (38 tokens) across 14 books. Functions in two registers: (1) proper name/title of the chief adversary of God (always articular/singular), (2) adjective for human 'false accusers/slanderers' (1 Tim 3:11, 2 Tim 3:3, Tit 2:3). The personal-adversary uses establish the devil as: tempter (Matt 4:1-11), active adversary of believers (Eph 6:11, 1 Pet 5:8), origin of sin/murder (John 8:44, 1 John 3:8), subject of eschatological judgment (Rev 12:9, 20:2, 20:10, Matt 25:41).

    Semantic categories: Satan as tempter, Satan as active adversary, Satan as origin of sin/murder, Satan in eschatological judgment, human slanderers (adjectival use)

    Key verses: Rev 12:9, Rev 20:2, Rev 20:10, Matt 25:41, John 8:44, 1 John 3:8, Heb 2:14

    Relevance: Identifies the first named subject of Rev 20:10. The devil (ho diabolos) is explicitly identified as the dragon/old serpent (Rev 12:9, 20:2), a spirit being (Eph 2:2), with angelic forces (Matt 25:41), predating humanity (Gen 3:1). Tree 3 Gate 1 automatically FAILs: 'a non-human spirit being.'

    G4567 — satanas¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G4567-satanas.md

    Occurrences: 36

    Transliterated Hebrew title (from H7854 satan = adversary). 36 occurrences. Same referent as diabolos (G1228) but emphasizes different aspects: diabolos = slanderer/accuser (Greek semantic equivalent); satanas = adversary (Hebrew transliteration). Both refer to the same non-human supernatural being.

    Semantic categories: personal adversary, tempter, cosmic adversary

    Key verses: Rev 12:9, Rev 20:2, Rev 20:7

    Relevance: Parallel identification for the devil in Rev 20:10. The identification chain (Rev 12:9, 20:2) links dragon = serpent = diabolos = satanas — all four titles refer to one non-human supernatural spirit being.

    G1404 — drakon¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1404-drakon.md

    Occurrences: 13

    All 13 NT occurrences in Revelation. The dragon is the primary symbolic representation of Satan in Revelation, explicitly identified as 'that old serpent, called the Devil and Satan' (Rev 12:9, 20:2). The dragon has seven heads, ten horns (Rev 12:3), leads angelic forces (Rev 12:7), gives authority to the beast (Rev 13:2).

    Semantic categories: apocalyptic adversary figure

    Key verses: Rev 12:3, Rev 12:7, Rev 12:9, Rev 13:2, Rev 20:2

    Relevance: Part of the identification chain for the first subject of Rev 20:10: dragon = serpent = devil = Satan. All 13 occurrences are in Revelation's apocalyptic framework.

    G3041 — limne¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G3041-limne.md

    Occurrences: 10

    Feminine noun, lake/pond. 10 occurrences: 5 in Luke (Lake of Gennesaret), 5 in Revelation (lake of fire). In Revelation, limne appears exclusively as 'lake of fire' (limne tou pyros) or 'lake of fire and brimstone.' Self-interpreted in Rev 20:14 as 'the second death' — a death concept, not a torment concept.

    Semantic categories: natural body of water, eschatological place of punishment (lake of fire)

    Key verses: Rev 19:20, Rev 20:10, Rev 20:14, Rev 20:15, Rev 21:8

    Relevance: The destination of all three subjects in Rev 20:10. The lake of fire is self-defined as 'the second death' (Rev 20:14) via the X=Y symbol-decoding formula. Death and hades (personified entities) are also cast in (Rev 20:14), paralleling 1 Cor 15:26 (last enemy destroyed is death).

    G2303 — theion¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2303-theion.md

    Occurrences: 7

    Noun, brimstone/sulfur. 7 occurrences: 6 in Revelation, 1 in Luke (17:29 Sodom). Fire-and-brimstone is the standard judgment pair. The Sodom paradigm (Gen 19:24) establishes the pattern: brimstone + fire = total, irreversible destruction. In Revelation, theion appears in lake of fire imagery (Rev 19:20, 20:10, 21:8), judgment torment (Rev 14:10), and demonic warfare (Rev 9:17-18).

    Semantic categories: Sodom judgment archetype, eschatological torment, lake of fire imagery, demonic warfare imagery

    Key verses: Luke 17:29, Rev 14:10, Rev 19:20, Rev 20:10, Rev 21:8

    Relevance: Component of the 'lake of fire and brimstone' in Rev 20:10. The Sodom paradigm shows fire + brimstone produces total destruction (ashes, per 2 Pet 2:6), not ongoing torment. Every OT and NT use of fire-and-brimstone results in destruction.

    G2673 — katargeo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2673-katargeo.md

    Occurrences: 32

    27 NT occurrences. Semantic range includes: abolish, render powerless, nullify, destroy, do away with, make of no effect. Used in Heb 2:14 for Christ destroying the devil and in 1 Cor 15:26 for destroying death. The lexical ambiguity (render powerless vs. destroy utterly) means the exact outcome requires contextual determination.

    Semantic categories: abolish/nullify, render powerless, destroy, do away with

    Key verses: Heb 2:14, 1 Cor 15:26, 2 Tim 1:10

    Relevance: Applied to the destruction of the devil (Heb 2:14) and death (1 Cor 15:26). Even the supernatural entity (the devil) receives destruction vocabulary in a didactic epistle, not torment vocabulary.

    G2719 — katesthio¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2719-katesthio.md

    Occurrences: 20

    Compound verb: kata + esthio (eat down, devour, consume). Used in Rev 20:9 for fire 'devouring' the human nations — destruction vocabulary applied to human subjects at the same event where basanizo is applied to non-human/symbolic entities. The vocabulary differentiation is within the same literary unit.

    Semantic categories: consume/devour, destruction by fire

    Key verses: Rev 20:9

    Relevance: The vocabulary applied to HUMAN subjects in Rev 20:9 — fire 'devoured' (katephagen) the nations. Different from basanizo applied to devil/beast/false prophet in v.10. This vocabulary differentiation within the same narrative is a key neutral observation.

    G2288 — thanatos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2288-thanatos.md

    Occurrences: 119

    119 tokens across 15 books. Encompasses physical death, death as sin's consequence (Rom 6:23), death as cosmic power (Rom 5:14), Christ's atoning death, spiritual death, and personified/second death (Rev 6:8, 20:14, 21:4). The 'second death' (ho thanatos ho deuteros) in Rev 20:14 and 21:8 is the term applied to human fate at the lake — death vocabulary, not torment vocabulary.

    Semantic categories: physical/biological death, death as sin's consequence, death as cosmic power, Christ's atoning death, spiritual death, second death (personified/eschatological)

    Key verses: Rom 6:23, 1 Cor 15:26, Rev 6:8, Rev 20:14, Rev 21:4, Rev 21:8

    Relevance: The term used for HUMAN fate at the lake of fire: 'the second death.' When humans enter the lake (Rev 20:14-15, 21:8), the terminology is consistently thanatos (death), not basanizo (torment). This vocabulary differentiation is a key observation: non-human/symbolic entities receive torment language; humans receive death language.

    G165 — aion¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G165-aion.md

    Occurrences: 120

    120 tokens across 22 books. Semantic range: age, era, perpetuity, world, universe. The doubled formula 'eis tous aionas ton aionon' (unto the ages of the ages) appears ~13x in Revelation: 9x for God's existence/reign (genuinely endless), 1x for saints' reign, 3x for judgment (14:11, 19:3, 20:10). The formula's meaning appears subject-determined: applied to the eternal God = genuinely eternal; applied to judgment on non-eternal subjects = permanent result. OT parallel: Isa 34:10 uses identical 'for ever' language for Edom — not still burning.

    Semantic categories: duration/perpetuity, intensified perpetuity (ages of ages), this present age/world, the age to come, time-span/epochs, created order/universe, end of the age

    Key verses: Rev 4:9-10, Rev 14:11, Rev 19:3, Rev 20:10, Rev 1:6

    Relevance: Component of the 'for ever and ever' formula in Rev 20:10. The formula's meaning is demonstrably subject-determined: applied to God (genuinely eternal) vs. applied to judgment (Isa 34:10, Rev 19:3 — permanent result of completed destruction). The aion word study documents the full semantic range.

    G166 — aionios¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G166-aionios.md

    Occurrences: 71

    Adjective derived from aion (G165). Used in Matt 25:41 'everlasting fire prepared for the devil and his angels' and Matt 25:46 'everlasting punishment.' The Hebrews pattern (eternal redemption, eternal judgment, eternal salvation = single acts with permanent results) demonstrates aionios can describe the permanence of the result, not the duration of the process. Jude 1:7 + 2 Pet 2:6: Sodom suffered 'eternal fire' but is now 'ashes.'

    Semantic categories: eternal/everlasting duration, permanent result (Hebrews pattern)

    Key verses: Matt 25:41, Matt 25:46, Jude 1:7, Heb 6:2, Heb 9:12

    Relevance: Related adjective form to aion. The 'eternal fire' of Matt 25:41 and Jude 1:7 produces ashes (2 Pet 2:6) and is 'prepared for the devil and his angels' — primary designation for non-human entities.

    G622 — apollymi¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G622-apollymi.md

    Occurrences: 102

    Destroy/perish — the primary NT verb for destruction. Jesus uses apollymi for the Sodom paradigm: 'destroyed them all' (Luke 17:29). Human fate vocabulary across all genres: destroy, perish. This destruction vocabulary spans Gospels, epistles, OT — unlike basanizo which is confined to Revelation's apocalyptic framework for judgment uses.

    Semantic categories: physical destruction, eschatological destruction, spiritual perishing

    Key verses: Luke 17:29, Matt 10:28, John 3:16

    Relevance: Human fate vocabulary used by Jesus himself. The fire-and-brimstone paradigm is interpreted by Jesus as apollymi (destroy), not basanizo (torment). Cross-genre, cross-author destruction vocabulary for human wicked.

    G615 — apokteino¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G615-apokteino.md

    Occurrences: 83

    Slay/kill. Used in Rev 19:21 for human remnant 'slain' (apektanthesan, V-API-3P) with the sword. Different vocabulary from v.20 where beast/false prophet are cast 'alive' (zontes) into the lake. The vocabulary distinction is within the same verse pair.

    Semantic categories: slay/kill (destruction vocabulary)

    Key verses: Rev 19:21

    Relevance: Human vocabulary in Rev 19:21 — humans are 'slain' (apokteino) while symbolic entities are cast 'alive' (zao) into the lake in v.20. The text maintains vocabulary differentiation between human and non-human/symbolic subjects.

    G4969 — sphazo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G4969-sphazo.md

    Occurrences: 10

    Slay (violently). The beast counterfeits the Lamb through shared sphazo vocabulary: Rev 5:6 Lamb 'slain' (esphagmenon) and Rev 13:3 beast with deadly wound 'as it were wounded to death' (esphagmenen). The shared vocabulary establishes the counterfeit death-and-resurrection pattern — the beast mimics Christ.

    Semantic categories: violent slaying, counterfeit death-resurrection pattern

    Key verses: Rev 5:6, Rev 13:3

    Relevance: Documents the beast's counterfeit relationship to the Lamb. The beast is a symbolic entity that mimics Christ's death/resurrection through shared vocabulary — further confirming its non-literal nature.

    G4442 — pyr¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G4442-pyr.md

    Occurrences: 74

    74 tokens across 14 books. Primary word for fire. Revelation dominates (25/73 unique verses, 34%). Semantic categories: eschatological punishment (lake of fire, Gehenna), judgment parables, divine theophany, cosmic plagues, testing/refining, figurative/metaphorical. Fire collocates with ballo (cast, 19x), theion (brimstone, 7x), katakaiō (burn up, 7x). The fire-and-brimstone paradigm originates in Gen 19:24 (Sodom) and produces total destruction.

    Semantic categories: eschatological punishment, judgment parables, divine theophany, cosmic plagues, testing/refining, figurative

    Key verses: Matt 25:41, Rev 20:10, Rev 20:14-15, Luke 17:29, Jude 1:7

    Relevance: Component of 'lake of fire' in Rev 20:10. The fire-and-brimstone paradigm consistently produces destruction and ashes (Gen 19:24 → 2 Pet 2:6 → Luke 17:29), not ongoing torment.

    H1966 — heylel¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H1966-heylel.md

    Occurrences: 1

    Hapax legomenon in Isa 14:12. Noun from halal (H1984, to shine). Means 'shining one/morning star.' The full phrase 'heylel ben-shachar' (shining one, son of the dawn) uses astral imagery. Isaiah repurposes Canaanite mythic template as a taunt against the king of Babylon. The five 'I will' statements of self-exaltation (vv.13-14) followed by descent to sheol (v.15) became the template for understanding the origin of sin. The fate described is descent/humiliation, not ongoing torment.

    Semantic categories: celestial epithet, pride and fall archetype, translation trajectory (epithet to proper name)

    Key verses: Isa 14:12

    Relevance: OT background for the Satan-figure. The fate described in Isa 14:12-15 is descent to sheol and the pit — humiliation and destruction language, not ongoing torment. The same entity whose Rev 20:10 fate is described as 'tormented for ever' is described in the OT with descent/destruction language.

    H3742 — kerub¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H3742-kruwb.md

    Occurrences: 105

    105 tokens across 12 OT books. Uncertain derivation. Cherubim appear as: Eden boundary guardians (Gen 3:24), ark/mercy seat figures (Exo 25), temple imagery, throne/presence language, Ezekiel visionary cherubim. Ezek 28:14 identifies the subject as 'the anointed cherub that covereth' — a non-human angelic being in God's presence.

    Semantic categories: Eden guardians, ark/mercy seat figures, temple imagery, throne/presence language, Ezekiel visionary cherubim

    Key verses: Gen 3:24, Ezek 28:14, Ezek 28:16

    Relevance: Ezek 28:14 identifies the Satan-figure as a cherub — a non-human angelic being. This confirms the devil's ontological category as non-human for Tree 3 Gate 1 purposes. The same cherub becomes 'ashes upon the earth' (Ezek 28:18) and exists 'never any more' (Ezek 28:19).

    G4592 — semeion¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G4592-semeion.md

    Occurrences: 77

    Sign/miracle. Rev 19:20 uses 'ta semeia' (the signs/miracles) to identify the false prophet — 'the false prophet that wrought miracles before him.' This vocabulary matches Rev 13:13-14 exactly, confirming the false prophet IS the earth beast of Rev 13:11-17.

    Semantic categories: miraculous signs, identification link

    Key verses: Rev 19:20, Rev 13:13-14

    Relevance: The identification link between the false prophet (Rev 19:20) and the earth beast (Rev 13:11-17). The matching vocabulary (semeia + enopion autou) confirms these are the same entity.
