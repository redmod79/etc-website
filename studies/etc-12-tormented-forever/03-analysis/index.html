    Detailed Analysis — Rev 20:10 Named Subjects and Basanizo Usage¶

    Question¶

    Rev 20:10 — named subjects (devil, beast, false prophet). Applied to humans or supernatural/symbolic entities? Basanizo (G928) usage. Apply Tree 3 subject gate.

    Verse-by-Verse Analysis¶

    Rev 20:7-8 — Satan released, deceives nations¶

    Context: After the millennium, Satan is released from his prison and goes out to deceive the nations (Gog and Magog) for battle. The subject (Satanas) is the same entity identified in Rev 20:2 and 12:9 by the four-fold chain: dragon = old serpent = devil = Satan. Direct statement: Satan is loosed and deceives nations globally. Original language: Satanas (G4567) is a transliteration of Hebrew satan (H7854, "adversary"). The four-fold identification chain in Rev 20:2 confirms the subject is a non-human supernatural spirit being. Cross-references: Rev 12:9 provides the fullest identification: "the great dragon... that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan, which deceiveth the whole world." The deception activity in 20:8 matches 12:9's "deceiveth the whole world." Gen 3:1 identifies the serpent as pre-human. Relationship to other evidence: Establishes the narrative context for Rev 20:10. The subject who is cast into the lake (v.10) is the same non-human entity released here.

    Rev 20:9 — Humans devoured by fire from heaven¶

    Context: The deceived nations surround the camp of the saints and the beloved city. Fire from God out of heaven devours them. Direct statement: Fire from God devours the human nations. Original language: katephagen (V-2AAI-3S from katesthio G2719) — aorist active indicative, "devoured." This is consumption/destruction vocabulary applied to human subjects. Cross-references: Ezek 38:22 describes fire and brimstone against Gog — the same Gog/Magog referenced here. The destruction vocabulary parallels the Sodom paradigm (Gen 19:24, Luke 17:29). Relationship to other evidence: The vocabulary distinction is critical. In v.9, human nations receive "devoured" (katephagen, destruction). In v.10, the non-human/symbolic trio receives "tormented" (basanisthesontai). Different vocabulary for different subjects in the same literary unit.

    Rev 20:10 — Devil, beast, false prophet tormented¶

    Context: The devil is cast into the lake of fire and brimstone where the beast and false prophet already are. All three are tormented day and night for ever and ever. Direct statement: The three named subjects — the devil, the beast, and the false prophet — are tormented (basanisthesontai) day and night unto the ages of the ages. Original language: basanisthesontai (V-FPI-3P from basanizo G928) — future passive indicative 3rd plural. The singular subject ho diabolos shifts to a plural verb to include the beast (to therion, G2342, neuter) and the false prophet (ho pseudoprophetes, G5578, masculine). hemeras kai nuktos (genitive of time) — "day and night." eis tous aionas ton aionon — "unto the ages of the ages." Cross-references: Rev 12:9 and 20:2 identify the devil as non-human. Rev 13:1-2 identifies the beast as a symbolic composite entity. Rev 19:20 identifies the false prophet as the earth beast of Rev 13:11-17. The torment formula (basanisthesontai eis tous aionas ton aionon) occurs only here in Scripture. Relationship to other evidence: This verse is the only place in the Bible that applies the "tormented for ever and ever" formula to any subject. All three subjects fail Tree 3 Gate 1 (Subject Gate): the devil is a non-human spirit being, the beast is a symbolic entity, and the false prophet is a symbolic entity.

    Rev 20:14 — Death and hades cast into lake; "this is the second death"¶

    Context: After the great white throne judgment (vv.11-13), death and hades are cast into the lake of fire. The text self-interprets: "This is the second death." Direct statement: Death and hades are cast into the lake of fire. The lake of fire is the second death. Original language: eblethēsan (V-API-3P from ballo G906) — aorist passive indicative 3rd plural. The predicate nominative: "houtos ho thanatos ho deuteros estin he limne tou pyros" — the X=Y symbol-decoding formula identifies the lake as "the second death." Cross-references: 1 Cor 15:26 — "The last enemy that shall be destroyed is death." Rev 21:4 — "there shall be no more death." Death personified as an enemy is destroyed when cast into the lake, resulting in the cessation of death itself. Relationship to other evidence: No torment vocabulary is applied to death, hades, or to humans in this verse. The self-interpreting formula defines the lake as a death concept, not a torment concept. The vocabulary distinction from v.10 (basanizo for non-human/symbolic entities) vs. v.14 (thanatos for the human outcome) is maintained.

    Rev 20:15 — Humans cast into the lake of fire¶

    Context: Those not found in the book of life are cast into the lake of fire. Direct statement: Humans not in the book of life are cast into the lake. Original language: eblethe (V-API-3S from ballo G906) — aorist passive indicative 3rd singular. Cross-references: Rev 21:8 lists eight categories of human wicked and assigns them "the second death." No torment formula is applied to any human category at the lake. Relationship to other evidence: When humans enter the lake, the text uses "cast into" and "second death" — not basanizo, not "day and night," not "for ever and ever." The vocabulary distinction between human and non-human/symbolic subjects is consistent throughout Rev 19-21.

    Rev 19:20 — Beast and false prophet cast alive into the lake¶

    Context: Battle of Armageddon conclusion. The beast is captured along with the false prophet (identified by matching vocabulary from Rev 13:13-14). Both are cast alive into the lake of fire. Direct statement: The beast and the false prophet are cast alive (zontes) into the lake of fire burning with brimstone. Original language: zontes (V-PAP-NPM from zao G2198) — present active participle, nominative plural masculine, "living/alive." eblethēsan (V-API-3P from ballo G906) — aorist passive indicative. The false prophet is identified by "ho poiesas ta semeia enopion autou" ("the one who wrought signs before him"), matching Rev 13:13-14. Cross-references: Rev 13:13-14 describes the earth beast performing signs (semeia) in the sight of the first beast. Rev 19:20 uses the same vocabulary to identify the false prophet as this entity. The beast and false prophet are cast "alive" — a different verb than what is applied to humans. Relationship to other evidence: In Rev 19:21, the human remnant is "slain" (apektanthesan, aorist passive from apokteino G615). The text uses different vocabulary for symbolic entities (cast alive) and humans (slain) in the same narrative.

    Rev 19:21 — Human remnant slain¶

    Context: Following the capture of beast and false prophet, the remaining human followers are slain by the sword proceeding from Christ's mouth. Direct statement: The remnant (hoi loipoi, the remaining humans) were slain (apektanthesan). Original language: apektanthesan (V-API-3P from apokteino G615) — aorist passive indicative 3rd plural, "were slain." Cross-references: This parallels Rev 20:9 where human followers of Satan are "devoured" (katephagen). In both cases, humans receive destruction vocabulary while the symbolic entities receive different treatment. Relationship to other evidence: The vocabulary differentiation within a single verse-pair (19:20 cast alive vs. 19:21 slain) is the clearest internal evidence that Revelation deliberately distinguishes what happens to symbolic entities from what happens to humans.

    Rev 12:9 — Four-fold identification: dragon = serpent = devil = Satan¶

    Context: War in heaven. Michael and his angels defeat the dragon, who is cast to earth. Direct statement: The great dragon is identified as "that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan, which deceiveth the whole world." His angels are cast out with him. Original language: The four-fold articular apposition: ho drakon ho megas (the great dragon), ho ophis ho archaios (that old serpent), ho kaloumenos Diabolos kai ho Satanas (called the Devil and Satan). Each title carries non-human semantic content. "His angels" (hoi aggeloi autou) confirms command of angelic forces. Cross-references: Rev 20:2 restates this identification in the immediate context of Rev 20:10. Gen 3:1 identifies the serpent as pre-human. Eph 2:2 identifies the devil as "the spirit" (to pneuma) — a spirit being. Relationship to other evidence: This verse conclusively establishes that the devil of Rev 20:10 is not a human being but a supernatural spirit entity with angelic forces.

    Rev 20:2 — Restated identification in immediate context¶

    Context: Opening of the millennium. An angel binds the dragon for 1000 years. Direct statement: The angel lays hold on "the dragon, that old serpent, which is the Devil, and Satan," and binds him for a thousand years. Original language: Same four-fold identification as 12:9, restated in the immediate chapter context of the crux verse. Cross-references: The binding of a supernatural being for 1000 years confirms non-human identity. Angel-on-spirit-being interaction is consistent throughout Scripture (cf. Jude 1:9, Michael and the devil). Relationship to other evidence: Confirms the subject of v.10 (ho diabolos) is the same non-human entity bound in v.2.

    Rev 13:1-2 — Sea beast: composite symbolic entity¶

    Context: John sees a beast rise from the sea with seven heads, ten horns, composite body parts from four Daniel 7 beasts (leopard, bear, lion in reverse order). The dragon gives the beast its authority. Direct statement: The beast has seven heads, ten horns, and body parts from multiple animals. The dragon gives it power, seat, and authority. Original language: therion (G2342, N-ASN) — neuter noun. The beast has kephalas hepta (seven heads) and kerata deka (ten horns). The composite body incorporates pardalis (leopard), arktos (bear), leon (lion) — Daniel 7's four beasts in reverse order. Cross-references: Dan 7:3-7 describes four separate beasts; Rev 13 combines all four into one. Rev 17:9-11 decodes the heads: seven heads = seven mountains = seven kings. Rev 17:8 describes the beast with a temporal existence pattern ("was, and is not, and shall ascend") that no single human can fulfill. Relationship to other evidence: The composite nature, multi-headed form, and centuries-spanning existence pattern make it impossible for the beast to be a literal human individual. This is the second subject of Rev 20:10.

    Rev 13:11-14 — Earth beast (= false prophet): symbolic characteristics¶

    Context: A second beast rises from the earth with two horns like a lamb and speech like a dragon. It exercises the first beast's authority, performs signs, deceives earth-dwellers, and commands image-worship. Direct statement: The earth beast has entirely symbolic characteristics: horns like a lamb, speaks as a dragon, exercises delegated authority, performs signs (semeia), makes fire come down from heaven. Original language: The vocabulary match with Rev 19:20 is exact: "poiei semeia megala" (13:13) corresponds to "poiesas ta semeia enopion autou" (19:20). Cross-references: Rev 19:20 explicitly identifies the false prophet by this matching vocabulary, confirming the earth beast IS the false prophet. Rev 16:13 places the false prophet in the counterfeit trinity alongside the dragon and beast. Relationship to other evidence: The false prophet of Rev 20:10 is this symbolic entity — not a generic human false prophet. No literal human has two horns like a lamb, speaks like a dragon, or gives breath to an image.

    Rev 17:8 — Beast "was, and is not, and shall ascend"¶

    Context: An angel explains the mystery of the beast to John. Direct statement: The beast "was, and is not; and shall ascend out of the bottomless pit, and go into perdition (apoleia)." Original language: en kai ouk estin kai mellei anabainein — a temporal existence pattern spanning multiple eras. Cross-references: Rev 17:9-11 further decodes: seven heads = seven mountains = seven kings, with the beast itself being "the eighth" and "of the seven." This multi-century, multi-kingdom existence is impossible for a single human individual. Relationship to other evidence: The beast goes into "perdition" (apoleia, G684) — destruction vocabulary. Even within Revelation's own description, the beast's destination is expressed with destruction terminology.

    Rev 16:13 — Counterfeit trinity as a unit¶

    Context: Sixth bowl plague. Three unclean spirits like frogs proceed from the dragon, beast, and false prophet. Direct statement: Three unclean spirits (pneumata tria akatharta) come from the mouths of the dragon, beast, and false prophet. Original language: pseudoprophetes (G5578) appears here as singular without the article — one of only three singular Revelation occurrences (16:13, 19:20, 20:10). Cross-references: The counterfeit trinity mirrors the divine Trinity: dragon (counterfeiting the Father), beast (counterfeiting the Son via the sphazo death-resurrection counterfeit), false prophet (counterfeiting the Spirit). Relationship to other evidence: All three members of the unholy trinity are non-human/symbolic entities. They function as a unit, and they receive the same fate as a unit in Rev 20:10.

    Rev 21:8 — Eight human categories: lake = second death¶

    Context: The new heaven and new earth. After describing the absence of death, sorrow, and pain (v.4), the text lists eight categories of wicked humans. Direct statement: The fearful, unbelieving, abominable, murderers, whoremongers, sorcerers, idolaters, and all liars shall have their part in the lake burning with fire and brimstone, "which is the second death." Original language: Eight human categories, each a substantival adjective or noun describing human moral failures. The outcome: ho thanatos ho deuteros — "the second death." Cross-references: Rev 20:14-15 uses the same "second death" identification. Rom 6:23: "the wages of sin is death (thanatos)." The consistent term for human fate at the lake is death, not torment. Relationship to other evidence: This is the most comprehensive list of human categories at the lake of fire. For every human category, the outcome is "the second death." The basanizo/basanisthesontai formula is never applied to any human category.

    Rev 1:1 — Revelation "signified"¶

    Context: Opening verse of Revelation establishing the genre. Direct statement: God gave the revelation to Christ to show his servants things to come, and "he sent and signified it by his angel." Original language: esemanen (V-AAI-3S from semaino G4591) — "he communicated by signs/symbols." Cross-references: The verb semaino establishes the literary genre: apocalyptic vision communicated through symbolic imagery. This genre declaration applies to every passage in Revelation. Relationship to other evidence: For Tree 3 Gate 3 (Genre Gate), Revelation is classified as apocalyptic vision. Every positional candidate from Revelation passes through the Genre Gate, which requires symbolic/apocalyptic genre recognition.

    Heb 2:14 — Christ destroys the one having the power of death — the devil¶

    Context: Didactic epistle discussing Christ's incarnation. Christ took on flesh and blood so that through death he might destroy the devil. Direct statement: Christ became human "that through death he might destroy (katargese) him that had the power of death, that is, the devil." Original language: katargese (V-AAS-3S from katargeo G2673) — aorist active subjunctive, "might destroy/render powerless." The purpose clause (hina + subjunctive) states the goal of Christ's incarnation. ton diabolon (A-ASM from diabolos G1228) — the devil explicitly identified by the touto estin formula. Cross-references: 1 Cor 15:26 uses the same verb (katargeo) for the destruction of death. 1 John 3:8: "to destroy (luo) the works of the devil." Relationship to other evidence: Even the supernatural entity (the devil) receives destruction vocabulary in a didactic epistle — not torment vocabulary. The genre is didactic (higher weight under SIS than apocalyptic).

    Matt 25:41 — Eternal fire prepared for the devil and his angels¶

    Context: Sheep and goats judgment. Jesus addresses those on the left. Direct statement: "Depart from me, ye cursed, into everlasting fire, prepared for the devil and his angels." Original language: to pur to aionion to hetoimasmenon to diabolo kai tois aggelois autou — the fire was "prepared" (hetoimasmenon, perfect passive participle from hetoimazo) for the devil and his angels. The primary designation is for non-human entities. Cross-references: Rev 20:10 describes the devil being cast into this same fire/lake. Matt 25:46 uses kolasis (G2851, judicial punishment), NOT basanizo (G928) — different vocabulary from Rev 20:10. Relationship to other evidence: The fire was originally designated for non-human entities (the devil and his angels). Humans are sent there secondarily. The vocabulary here is kolasis, not basanizo.

    1 John 3:8 — Son of God manifested to destroy the devil's works¶

    Context: Johannine didactic epistle. Direct statement: The devil sinneth from the beginning. The Son of God was manifested "that he might destroy (luse) the works of the devil." Original language: luse (V-AAS-3S from luo G3089) — "might destroy/dissolve." ap arches ("from the beginning") — the devil's activity predates human history. Cross-references: Heb 2:14 uses katargeo; 1 John uses luo. Both are destruction verbs applied to the devil or his works. Gen 3:15 — the proto-evangelium promises the serpent's head will be crushed. Relationship to other evidence: Destruction vocabulary for the devil's works appears in multiple didactic contexts — a genre with higher interpretive weight than apocalyptic.

    Matt 8:29 — Demons anticipate basanizo¶

    Context: Gadarene demoniacs. The demons cry out to Jesus. Direct statement: "Art thou come hither to torment (basanisai) us before the time?" Original language: basanisai (V-AAN from basanizo G928) — aorist active infinitive. The demons use basanizo for what they expect from Jesus. "Before the time" (pro kairou) — a time-bounded expectation. Cross-references: Mark 5:7 and Luke 8:28 are Synoptic parallels. All three record demons using basanizo for their anticipated fate. Relationship to other evidence: This is the only Gospel occurrence of basanizo in a judgment context, and the speakers are demons — non-human spirit beings — describing their own expected fate. Basanizo in judgment contexts originates in demonic speech, not in didactic teaching about human destiny.

    Matt 14:24 — Ship "tossed" (basanizo) by waves¶

    Context: Jesus walks on water. The ship is battered by waves. Direct statement: The ship was "tossed" (basanizomenon) with waves. Original language: basanizomenon (V-PPP-NSN from basanizo G928) — present passive participle, neuter singular. Applied to a ship (ploion, neuter) — an inanimate object. Cross-references: Mark 6:48 uses basanizo for the disciples "toiling" in rowing. Relationship to other evidence: Basanizo applied to an inanimate object proves the word does not inherently require consciousness or punishment. The KJV translates it "tossed" — recognizing the broader semantic range.

    Mark 5:7 — Demon begs "torment me not"¶

    Context: Gerasene demoniac, Markan parallel. Direct statement: The demon adjures Jesus "by God, that thou torment me not." Original language: basanises (V-AAS-2S from basanizo G928) — aorist active subjunctive. Parallel to Matt 8:29 and Luke 8:28. Cross-references: All three Synoptics record this encounter with basanizo. The pattern is consistent: demons use basanizo for their anticipated judgment. Relationship to other evidence: Confirms the pattern that basanizo in judgment contexts appears in demonic speech about their own fate.

    Mark 6:48 — Disciples "toiling" (basanizo) in rowing¶

    Context: Disciples struggle against the wind on the sea. Direct statement: Jesus saw them "toiling" (basanizomenous) in rowing. Original language: basanizomenous (V-PPP-APM from basanizo G928) — present passive participle, accusative plural masculine. Applied to physical exertion. Cross-references: Parallel to Matt 14:24 (ship tossed). Both non-judgment uses of basanizo. Relationship to other evidence: Another non-judgment occurrence confirming basanizo's broad semantic range: physical strain, not punishment.

    Luke 8:28 — Demon begs "torment me not" (Lukan parallel)¶

    Context: Lukan parallel to the Gerasene demoniac. Direct statement: The demon cries out, "I beseech thee, torment me not." Original language: basanises (from basanizo G928). Third Synoptic parallel. Cross-references: Matt 8:29, Mark 5:7. Relationship to other evidence: Completes the Synoptic triad of demonic basanizo usage.

    2 Pet 2:8 — Lot "vexed" (basanizo) his soul¶

    Context: Peter's epistle uses Lot as an example. Lot's righteous soul was vexed by Sodom's wickedness. Direct statement: The righteous man "vexed (ebasanizen) his righteous soul from day to day with their unlawful deeds." Original language: ebasanizen (V-IAI-3S from basanizo G928) — imperfect active indicative, "was vexing/tormenting." Applied to moral distress experienced by a righteous man. Cross-references: This is the only basanizo occurrence in any epistle. The context is moral distress, not divine punishment. "From day to day" (hemeras ex hemeras) uses a day-based temporal formula similar to Rev 20:10's "day and night." Relationship to other evidence: The sole epistle occurrence of basanizo describes moral distress, not eschatological torment. This confirms that basanizo's NT semantic range extends well beyond punishment.

    Rev 9:5 — Basanizo with explicit five-month time limit¶

    Context: Fifth trumpet. Locust-like beings from the abyss are given permission to torment for five months. Direct statement: "They should be tormented (basanisthesontai) five months." Original language: basanisthesontai (V-FPI-3P from basanizo G928) — same morphological form as Rev 20:10. menas pente (accusative of duration) — five months. edothe (divine passive from didomi) — God's sovereign permission. Cross-references: Rev 20:10 uses the same verb form but with "day and night for ever and ever" instead of "five months." The explicit time limit here demonstrates that basanizo does not carry inherent duration — it requires modifiers. Relationship to other evidence: The word itself is neutral on duration. In Rev 9:5 it is explicitly time-bounded; in Rev 20:10 it has the ages-of-ages formula. The duration depends on the modifier, not on the word.

    Rev 12:2 — Woman "pained" (basanizo) in childbirth¶

    Context: The woman clothed with the sun in labor. Direct statement: She was "pained (basanizomene) to be delivered." Original language: basanizomene (V-PPP-NSF from basanizo G928) — present passive participle, feminine singular. Applied to a positive symbolic figure (the woman representing God's people). Cross-references: Within Revelation itself, basanizo is used for childbirth pain — a non-judgment, non-punitive context. Relationship to other evidence: This occurrence proves that basanizo in Revelation does not always mean eschatological torment. The word appears in Revelation for childbirth pain, prophetic witness (11:10), time-limited scorpion torment (9:5), and eschatological judgment (14:10, 20:10).

    Rev 11:10 — Two prophets "tormented" earth-dwellers¶

    Context: The two witnesses' ministry distressed those dwelling on earth. Direct statement: "These two prophets tormented (ebasanisan) them that dwelt on the earth." Original language: ebasanisan (V-AAI-3P from basanizo G928) — aorist active indicative. The prophets' message caused distress to earth-dwellers — prophetic ministry, not eschatological punishment. Cross-references: This non-judgment Revelation use of basanizo further broadens the word's semantic range within the book. Relationship to other evidence: Another non-judgment use of basanizo within Revelation itself.

    Rev 14:10-11 — Beast-worshippers: torment with fire, smoke ascending¶

    Context: Third angel's message. The fate of those who worship the beast. Direct statement: The beast-worshipper "shall be tormented (basanisthesetai) with fire and brimstone in the presence of the holy angels, and in the presence of the Lamb." The smoke of their basanismos "ascendeth up for ever and ever." Original language: basanisthesetai (V-FPI-3S from basanizo G928) — future passive indicative 3rd singular. kapnos tou basanismou auton (the smoke of their torment) — the "for ever" formula applies to the smoke, not directly to the torment. The etc7-11 study analyzed this passage in depth. Cross-references: Rev 19:3 uses the identical formula for Babylon's smoke, and Rev 18:21-23 describes Babylon as "found no more at all." Isa 34:9-10 uses identical language for Edom's ended fire. Relationship to other evidence: (Examined in depth in etc7-11-smoke-ascending-forever.) The smoke formula draws on Isa 34:10 and can describe a permanent result rather than an ongoing process.

    Rev 19:3 — Babylon's smoke rises "for ever and ever"¶

    Context: After Babylon's fall. Direct statement: "Her smoke rose up for ever and ever (eis tous aionas ton aionon)." Original language: Same formula as Rev 20:10 and 14:11. Cross-references: Rev 18:21-23 describes Babylon with fivefold "no more at all" cessation language. The entity whose smoke rises "for ever" is explicitly described as having ceased to exist. Relationship to other evidence: Demonstrates that the "for ever and ever" formula can describe a permanent, irreversible result of destruction, not an ongoing process. The formula's meaning is subject-determined.

    Isa 14:12-15 — Morning star brought down to sheol¶

    Context: Isaiah's taunt against the king of Babylon, with language transcending any human king. Direct statement: "How art thou fallen from heaven, O Lucifer (heylel)... thou shalt be brought down to hell (sheol), to the sides of the pit." Original language: heylel (H1966) — hapax legomenon, "morning star/shining one." napalta (Qal perfect 2ms from naphal) — "you have fallen." nigdatta (Niphal perfect 2ms from gada) — "you are cut off." Cross-references: Luke 10:18 — Jesus: "I beheld Satan as lightning fall from heaven." The NT applies this pattern to Satan. Relationship to other evidence: The OT describes the Satan-figure's fate as descent and humiliation, not ongoing torment. The vocabulary is fall/cut off/descent to sheol and pit.

    Ezek 28:14-19 — Anointed cherub becomes ashes, "never any more"¶

    Context: Oracle against the king of Tyre, with language transcending any human king — "anointed cherub," "upon the holy mountain of God," "perfect from the day created." Direct statement: "I will bring forth a fire from the midst of thee, it shall devour thee, and I will bring thee to ashes upon the earth... thou shalt be a terror, and never shalt thou be any more." Original language: kerub (H3742) — cherub, an angelic being. akal (Qal perfect 3fs from akal) — "devour/consume." epher (H665) — "ashes/dust." eneka (ain + 2ms, "you are not") plus ad olam (H5769, "unto eternity") — absolute cessation: "you are not unto eternity." Cross-references: This is the same entity described in Rev 20:10 as "tormented for ever and ever." In Ezek 28, that entity becomes ashes and exists no more — absolute destruction language. Relationship to other evidence: This is the most striking cross-testament parallel. The OT describes the Satan-figure with destruction/cessation vocabulary (ashes, devour, never any more), while Rev 20:10 uses torment vocabulary for the same being. Under SIS principles, the didactic/prophetic Ezekiel passage (direct divine speech: "I will bring forth fire") has higher genre weight than the apocalyptic vision of Revelation.

    Isa 34:9-10 — Edom's smoke "shall go up for ever"¶

    Context: Oracle against Edom. Direct statement: "It shall not be quenched night nor day; the smoke thereof shall go up for ever (le-olam)." Yet Isa 34:11-17 describes animals inhabiting the ruins. Original language: le-olam (H5769) — "for ever." laylah va-yomam — "night nor day," paralleling Rev 20:10's "day and night." Cross-references: The identical language ("for ever," "night nor day," "not quenched") appears in Rev 20:10. Edom is demonstrably not still burning — animals dwell in the ruins (Isa 34:11-17). Relationship to other evidence: Establishes the OT precedent: "for ever" smoke language describes a permanent result (total devastation), not an ongoing process (still burning). This directly informs the meaning of the same formula in Rev 20:10.

    Dan 7:11 — Fourth beast slain, body destroyed, given to burning flame¶

    Context: Daniel's vision of four beasts. The fourth beast receives judgment. Direct statement: "I beheld even till the beast was slain, and his body destroyed, and given to the burning flame." Original language: Aramaic: qetilat (from qetal, "to kill/slay"). geshmah hoobad (body was destroyed). yehibat liqedat esha (given to the burning of fire). Cross-references: Revelation's beast is the conceptual successor to Daniel 7's fourth beast. The OT uses destruction vocabulary (slain, body destroyed, given to burning) for symbolic beast-entities. Relationship to other evidence: The OT paradigm for symbolic beast-powers is destruction, not eternal torment. Daniel 7's beast is slain and destroyed; Revelation's beast is cast into the lake. The vocabulary shift from destruction to torment occurs only in Revelation's apocalyptic framework.

    Rev 18:21-23 — Babylon "found no more at all"¶

    Context: Fall of Babylon. Direct statement: Babylon shall be "found no more at all," with fivefold cessation (found, heard, found, shine, heard — each "no more at all"). Original language: ou me heurethe eti — emphatic double negative with eti (anymore). Repeated five times for absolute cessation. Cross-references: Rev 19:3 says Babylon's smoke rises "for ever and ever." The coexistence of total cessation language and "forever" smoke language proves the formula describes a permanent result of destruction. Relationship to other evidence: The same formula applied to Babylon (Rev 19:3) is applied to the devil/beast/false prophet (Rev 20:10). Babylon's case demonstrates the formula describes permanent destruction.

    Gen 3:1 — The serpent pre-human¶

    Context: Garden of Eden. The serpent exists before any human sin. Direct statement: "The serpent was more subtil than any beast of the field which the LORD God had made." Original language: nachash (H5175) — serpent. A created being distinct from humanity, existing before human sin. Cross-references: Rev 12:9 and 20:2 identify "that old serpent" as the Devil and Satan. The serpent's pre-human existence confirms the devil is not and never has been a human being. Relationship to other evidence: Establishes the pre-human origin of the entity named in Rev 20:10 as "the devil."

    Eph 2:2 — Devil as "the spirit" — spirit being¶

    Context: Paul describes the believer's former walk. Direct statement: They walked "according to the prince of the power of the air, the spirit (to pneuma) that now worketh in the children of disobedience." Original language: to pneuma — "the spirit." The devil is explicitly called a spirit being. Cross-references: Combined with Rev 12:9 (dragon/serpent), Gen 3:1 (pre-human serpent), Ezek 28:14 (cherub), this establishes the devil's ontological category: non-human spirit being. Relationship to other evidence: Confirms the devil is not a human for Tree 3 Gate 1 purposes.

    1 Cor 15:26 — Last enemy destroyed is death¶

    Context: Paul's resurrection chapter. Direct statement: "The last enemy that shall be destroyed is death." Original language: katargeitai (V-PPI-3S from katargeo G2673) — present passive indicative, "is being destroyed/abolished." Same verb as Heb 2:14. Cross-references: Rev 20:14 — death cast into lake of fire. Rev 21:4 — "no more death." The lake destroys death. Relationship to other evidence: The result of casting death into the lake is its destruction and cessation. The lake functions as an instrument of destruction.

    Rom 6:23 — Wages of sin is death¶

    Context: Pauline didactic epistle. Direct statement: "The wages of sin is death (thanatos); but the gift of God is eternal life." Original language: thanatos (G2288) — same noun as "second death" in Rev 20:14 and 21:8. The contrast is death vs. eternal life. Cross-references: Rev 20:14, 21:8. The didactic contrast is death vs. life, not torment vs. life. Relationship to other evidence: The cross-genre, didactic statement defines sin's consequence as death, using the same word that defines the human outcome at the lake.

    Luke 17:29 — Jesus interprets Sodom fire: "destroyed them all"¶

    Context: Jesus' eschatological discourse. Direct statement: "It rained fire and brimstone from heaven, and destroyed (apolesen) them all." Original language: apolesen (V-AAI-3S from apollymi G622) — "destroyed." Jesus uses destruction vocabulary for the fire-and-brimstone paradigm. Cross-references: Gen 19:24 — the original event. 2 Pet 2:6 — "turned to ashes." Jude 1:7 — "eternal fire." Relationship to other evidence: Jesus himself interprets fire and brimstone as destruction, not ongoing torment. This is a didactic saying with higher genre weight than apocalyptic vision.

    2 Pet 2:4, 6 — Angels cast to Tartarus; Sodom turned to ashes¶

    Context: Peter's examples of divine judgment. Direct statement: Angels that sinned are cast to Tartarus, reserved for judgment. Sodom is turned to ashes, "an ensample unto those that after should live ungodly." Original language: tephrosas (V-AAP-NSM from tephroo G5077) — "having turned to ashes." hupodeigma (G5262) — "example." Cross-references: Peter explicitly calls Sodom's destruction by fire an example for future ungodly judgment. The result was ashes, not ongoing torment. Relationship to other evidence: The inspired interpretation of "eternal fire" (Jude 1:7) producing "ashes" (2 Pet 2:6) informs the meaning of fire-and-brimstone language throughout Scripture.

    Jude 1:6-7 — Angels in chains; Sodom's "eternal fire"¶

    Context: Jude parallels 2 Peter 2. Direct statement: Angels kept in "everlasting chains under darkness unto the judgment of the great day." Sodom suffers "the vengeance of eternal fire (puros aioniou)." Original language: puros aioniou — "eternal fire." Yet the fire produced ashes (2 Pet 2:6), not ongoing burning. Cross-references: The "eternal" quality of the fire describes the permanence of its result, not the duration of the process. Relationship to other evidence: Demonstrates that "eternal fire" in biblical usage produces permanent destruction, not ongoing torment.

    Mal 4:1 — Wicked become stubble, burned up completely¶

    Context: OT prophetic eschatology. Direct statement: "All that do wickedly shall be stubble: and the day that cometh shall burn them up... it shall leave them neither root nor branch." Original language: qash (H7179) — stubble (quickly consumed). "Neither root nor branch" — nothing remains. Cross-references: This OT eschatological language describes total consumption of human wicked — not ongoing torment. Relationship to other evidence: OT prophetic vocabulary for human eschatological fate is destruction/consumption, consistent across genres.

    Obad 1:16 — Wicked "as though they had not been"¶

    Context: Obadiah's prophecy against Edom and the nations. Direct statement: "They shall be as though they had not been." Original language: kelo hayu — "as though they had not been." Absolute cessation language. Cross-references: Psa 37:10 — "the wicked shall not be." Relationship to other evidence: Cessation language for human wicked in non-apocalyptic prophecy.

    Psa 37:10 — Wicked "shall not be"¶

    Context: Wisdom psalm. Direct statement: "Yet a little while, and the wicked shall not be: yea, thou shalt diligently consider his place, and it shall not be." Original language: enenu (ain + 3ms suffix) — "he is not." Same negative existential construction as Ezek 28:19 for the Satan-figure. Cross-references: Obad 1:16.     Matt 7:15 — Generic false prophets (plural)¶

    Context: Sermon on the Mount. Direct statement: "Beware of false prophets (pseudoprophetai), which come to you in sheep's clothing."     Matt 24:11 — Many false prophets (plural)¶

    Patterns Identified¶

    Pattern 1: Consistent vocabulary differentiation between human and non-human/symbolic subjects¶

    Throughout Rev 19-21, the text uses distinct vocabulary for distinct subjects: - Humans: devoured (katephagen, 20:9), slain (apektanthesan, 19:21), second death (20:14-15, 21:8) - Non-human/symbolic entities: cast alive (zontes, 19:20), tormented (basanisthesontai, 20:10) - Personified abstractions (death/hades): cast into (eblethēsan, 20:14)

    This vocabulary differentiation is consistent across multiple passages and is a neutral observation both sides must acknowledge.

    Pattern 2: Basanizo judgment uses confined to apocalyptic framework and demonic speech¶

    Of the 12 NT occurrences of basanizo: 7 are non-judgment (illness, waves, rowing, moral distress, childbirth, prophetic witness). The 5 judgment uses are all either in Revelation's apocalyptic framework (Rev 9:5, 14:10, 20:10) or involve demons speaking of their own future (Matt 8:29, Mark 5:7, Luke 8:28). No epistle, Gospel didactic section, or OT passage uses basanizo for human eschatological fate.

    Pattern 3: OT destruction vocabulary for the same entities¶

    The OT describes the Satan-figure with destruction/cessation vocabulary: - Isa 14:12-15: fallen, cut off, brought down to sheol - Ezek 28:18-19: fire devours, becomes ashes, "never any more" - Dan 7:11: beast slain, body destroyed, given to burning flame

    Pattern 4: The "for ever" formula is subject-determined¶

    The eis tous aionas ton aionon formula in Revelation: - Applied to God: genuinely eternal (9x) - Applied to saints' reign: genuinely eternal (1x) - Applied to judgment: parallels OT usage where identical language describes ended events (Isa 34:9-10, Edom; Rev 19:3, Babylon "found no more")

    Pattern 5: Destruction vocabulary spans all genres; torment vocabulary is genre-specific¶

    Destruction/death vocabulary for the fate of the wicked appears in OT prophets (Mal 4:1, Obad 1:16), wisdom literature (Psa 37:10), Pauline epistles (Rom 6:23, 1 Cor 15:26), Johannine epistles (1 John 3:8), Petrine epistles (2 Pet 2:6), and Jesus' own words (Luke 17:29). Torment vocabulary for judgment appears only in Revelation's apocalyptic framework and in demonic speech.

    Word Study Integration¶

    Basanizo (G928) — 12 occurrences¶

    The word derives from basanos (G931), a touchstone for testing the purity of metals. The metaphorical extension to "torment" is secondary. The KJV translators recognized the semantic breadth, using five different English words: tormented, tossed, toiling, vexed, pained.

    The non-judgment uses (7 of 12) reveal the word's broader range: physical illness (Matt 4:24, 8:6), ship battered by waves (Matt 14:24), physical exertion in rowing (Mark 6:48), moral distress (2 Pet 2:8), childbirth pain (Rev 12:2), prophetic distress (Rev 11:10). The application to an inanimate ship (Matt 14:24) demonstrates the word does not inherently require conscious experience of punishment.

    Basanismos (G929) — 6 occurrences¶

    All 6 occurrences are in Revelation. Time-bounded in 4 of 6: five months (Rev 9:5) and one hour for Babylon (Rev 18:7, 10, 15). The word family is genre-specific.

    Basanos (G931) — 3 occurrences¶

    The root noun. Physical diseases (Matt 4:24) and parabolic context (Luke 16:23, 28). The etymological base confirms the testing/examination root meaning.

    Basanistes (G930) — 1 occurrence¶

    The agent noun. One occurrence in a parable (Matt 18:34). No occurrences in didactic teaching.

    Therion (G2342) — 46 occurrences¶

    38 of 46 in Revelation. In Revelation, therion is always the apocalyptic beast-entity. The concentration confirms "the beast" is a technical term in Revelation for the symbolic political-religious power.

    Pseudoprophetes (G5578) — 11 occurrences¶

    Singular in Revelation (3x: 16:13, 19:20, 20:10) = one specific apocalyptic entity. Plural elsewhere (7x) = class of human deceivers. Acts 13:6 is singular outside Revelation but with a personal name (Bar-Jesus). The distinction is clear and consistent.

    Diabolos (G1228) — 38 occurrences¶

    The proper-name uses (always articular/singular) refer to the supernatural adversary. Identified as: dragon, old serpent, spirit being (Eph 2:2), pre-human (Gen 3:1), commander of angels (Matt 25:41), subject of destruction (Heb 2:14, katargeo; 1 John 3:8, luo).

    Katargeo (G2673) — 32 occurrences¶

    Applied to the devil (Heb 2:14) and to death (1 Cor 15:26). The semantic range includes abolish, render powerless, destroy. Regardless of the exact nuance, the verb is destruction-class vocabulary, not torment-class.

    Cross-Testament Connections¶

    OT → NT: Satan-figure's fate¶

    Ezek 28:18-19 (ashes, never any more) → Rev 20:10 (tormented for ever). The same entity receives destruction vocabulary in the OT prophetic oracle and torment vocabulary in the NT apocalyptic vision. Under SIS principles, the prophetic direct divine speech ("I will bring forth fire... I will bring thee to ashes") has higher genre weight than apocalyptic symbolism.

    OT → NT: "For ever" smoke formula¶

    Isa 34:9-10 (Edom, smoke ascending forever, not quenched day nor night) → Rev 14:11 (smoke ascending for ever), Rev 19:3 (Babylon's smoke for ever), Rev 20:10 (tormented for ever). The OT paradigm shows this formula describes permanent results of completed destruction.

    OT → NT: Fire-and-brimstone paradigm¶

    Gen 19:24 (Sodom destroyed) → Luke 17:29 (Jesus: "destroyed them all") → 2 Pet 2:6 (turned to ashes) → Jude 1:7 (eternal fire, yet ashes) → Rev 19:20, 20:10 (lake of fire and brimstone). The paradigm consistently produces destruction and ashes.

    OT → NT: Symbolic beast destruction¶

    Dan 7:11 (fourth beast slain, body destroyed, given to burning) → Rev 13:1-2 (composite beast from Daniel 7) → Rev 19:20 (beast cast into lake) → Rev 20:10 (beast "tormented"). The OT paradigm for symbolic beast-powers is destruction.

    Difficult or Complicating Passages¶

    Rev 14:10-11 — Beast-worshippers and the torment formula¶

    This is the closest the text comes to applying torment vocabulary to human subjects. The beast-worshippers are human individuals who "shall be tormented with fire and brimstone." Two points complicate a straightforward reading: (1) the passage is in Revelation's apocalyptic framework (Genre Gate), and (2) the "for ever and ever" formula applies to the smoke of their torment, not directly to the torment itself. The etc7-11 study analyzed this passage in depth and classified all Revelation-sourced positional candidates as Neutral via the Genre Gate. The passage remains a key I-B item requiring SIS resolution.

    The "same lake" argument¶

    All three non-human/symbolic entities (devil, beast, false prophet), the personified death/hades, and unsaved humans end up in the same lake of fire. The argument: if the lake is the same destination, the experience must be the same for all. Against this: the text itself differentiates vocabulary (devoured, slain, second death for humans vs. tormented for non-human/symbolic entities). Different subjects receive different descriptions at the same destination. The text's own vocabulary argues against automatic transfer.

    The "beast-as-representative" counter-argument¶

    The beast represents a system composed of human adherents. Does the torment of the beast-symbol transfer to the humans within that system? The text addresses this directly: Rev 19:20-21 has the beast and false prophet cast alive into the lake, while the human adherents are slain. Rev 20:9 has human followers of Satan devoured while Satan himself receives the torment formula. Rev 21:8 assigns the human wicked "the second death." The text consistently applies different vocabulary to the system and to the humans within it.

    Katargeo ambiguity (Heb 2:14)¶

    Katargeo has a broad semantic range (abolish, render powerless, destroy). Whether Christ "destroys" the devil or "renders him powerless" is a genuine lexical question. However, regardless of the exact nuance, the verb is in the destruction/cessation category, not the torment category. The didactic epistle describes the devil's fate with destruction-class vocabulary.

    Preliminary Synthesis¶

    The evidence consistently distinguishes between the subjects of Rev 20:10 and human beings at three levels:

    1. Subject identification: All three subjects of the "tormented for ever and ever" formula are non-human or symbolic: the devil (supernatural spirit being), the beast (symbolic political-religious system), the false prophet (symbolic entity = earth beast of Rev 13:11-17). None is a literal human being.

    2. Vocabulary differentiation: The text uses distinct vocabulary for distinct subjects — humans receive destruction/death vocabulary (devoured, slain, second death); non-human/symbolic entities receive torment vocabulary. This distinction is maintained across Rev 19-21.

    3. Cross-genre distribution: Destruction/death vocabulary for the fate of the wicked spans all genres and all authors (OT prophets, wisdom, Gospels, epistles). Torment vocabulary for judgment appears only in Revelation's apocalyptic framework and in demonic speech.

    Under the Tree 3 Subject Gate, all three subjects of Rev 20:10 automatically FAIL Gate 1 (not literal human beings). Under the Genre Gate, the passage automatically FAILS Gate 3 (apocalyptic vision per Rev 1:1 semaino). These gate failures do not automatically make the verse Neutral — the reclassification process (Tree 3 Step 3) determines the corrected positional classification.
