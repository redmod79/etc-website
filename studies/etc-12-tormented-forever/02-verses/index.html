    Question¶

    Rev 20:10 -- named subjects (devil, beast, false prophet). Applied to humans or supernatural/symbolic entities? Basanizo (G928) usage. Apply Tree 3 subject gate.

    Crux text: Devil cast into lake, tormented with beast and false prophet¶

    Rev 20:7 And when the thousand years are expired, Satan shall be loosed out of his prison, Rev 20:8 And shall go out to deceive the nations which are in the four quarters of the earth, Gog and Magog, to gather them together to battle: the number of whom [is] as the sand of the sea. Rev 20:9 And they went up on the breadth of the earth, and compassed the camp of the saints about, and the beloved city: and fire came down from God out of heaven, and devoured them. Rev 20:10 And the devil that deceived them was cast into the lake of fire and brimstone, where the beast and the false prophet [are], and shall be tormented day and night for ever and ever.

    Context: Rev 20:7-10 is a continuous narrative unit. After the millennium (vv.1-6), Satan is released, deceives nations (Gog/Magog), they surround the saints' camp, fire devours the human followers (v.9, katephagen), and the devil is cast into the lake where beast/false prophet already are (v.10). The verb 'devoured' (katephagen) is used for human nations; 'tormented' (basanisthesontai) is used only for the non-human/symbolic trio.

    Notes: Key grammatical observations: (1) basanisthesontai (V-FPI-3P) is future passive indicative 3rd plural — the plural matches the compound subject (devil + beast + false prophet), confirming all three are subject to the torment formula. (2) katephagen (V-2AAI-3S) in v.9 is aorist active, applied to the human nations — different vocabulary for different subjects. (3) hemeras kai nuktos (genitive of time) describes 'day and night' — genitive expressing duration of activity period. (4) eis tous aionas ton aionon — the doubled ages formula. The immediate subject (ho diabolos) is grammatically singular but the verb shifts to plural to include beast and false prophet.

    Death/Hades and humans cast into lake — 'this is the second death'¶

    Rev 20:14 And death and hell were cast into the lake of fire. This is the second death. Rev 20:15 And whosoever was not found written in the book of life was cast into the lake of fire.

    Context: Immediately follows the great white throne judgment (vv.11-13). Death and Hades (personified entities) are cast into the lake. Humans not in the book of life are cast into the lake. The self-interpreting formula 'this is the second death' (houtos ho thanatos ho deuteros estin he limne tou pyros) defines the lake as death — not torment. No torment vocabulary is applied to either death/hades or humans here.

The verse provides the most comprehensive identification chain in Scripture: ho drakon ho megas (the great dragon), ho ophis ho archaios (that old serpent), ho kaloumenos Diabolos kai ho Satanas (called the Devil and Satan). This chain establishes the devil as a non-human supernatural spirit being — a dragon/serpent with angelic forces under him.

    Restated identification: dragon = serpent = devil = Satan (immediate context)¶

    Rev 20:2 And he laid hold on the dragon, that old serpent, which is the Devil, and Satan, and bound him a thousand years,

    Context: Opening of the millennium narrative (Rev 20:1-3). An angel binds the dragon/devil/Satan for 1000 years. This verse restates the identification chain of Rev 12:9 in the immediate context of Rev 20:10, confirming the subject of v.10 (ho diabolos) is the same non-human supernatural spirit being.

The binding for 1000 years (temporal control of a supernatural being) further confirms non-human identity. An angel (angelos) performs the binding — angel-on-spirit-being interaction, not angel-on-human.

    Earth beast (= false prophet): symbolic characteristics, identified in Rev 19:20¶

    Rev 13:11 And I beheld another beast coming up out of the earth; and he had two horns like a lamb, and he spake as a dragon. Rev 13:12 And he exerciseth all the power of the first beast before him, and causeth the earth and them which dwell therein to worship the first beast, whose deadly wound was healed. Rev 13:13 And he doeth great wonders, so that he maketh fire come down from heaven on the earth in the sight of men, Rev 13:14 And deceiveth them that dwell on the earth by [the means of] those miracles which he had power to do in the sight of the beast; saying to them that dwell on the earth, that they should make an image to the beast, which had the wound by a sword, and did live.

    Context: Second beast rises from the earth with entirely symbolic characteristics: two horns like a lamb (counterfeiting Christ), speaks as a dragon (channeling Satan's authority), exercises delegated authority from the first beast, performs signs (semeia), makes fire come down from heaven, deceives earth-dwellers.     Notes: to pneuma to nun energoun — 'the spirit that now worketh.' The devil is explicitly called 'the spirit' (pneuma) — a spirit being.     Last enemy destroyed is death — parallels death cast into lake in Rev 20:14¶

The lake destroys death itself.

    Notes: katargeitai (V-PPI-3S from katargeo G2673) — present passive indicative, 'is being destroyed/abolished.' The same verb (katargeo) used in Heb 2:14 for the destruction of the devil. Death is personified as an enemy to be destroyed — when cast into the lake of fire (Rev 20:14), the result is 'no more death' (Rev 21:4).     Wages of sin is death — same word as 'second death'¶

    Context: Pauline didactic epistle. The wages of sin is thanatos (death) — the same word used in 'second death' (ho thanatos ho deuteros) in Rev 20:14 and 21:8. The contrast is death vs. eternal life, not torment vs. eternal life.

    Notes: thanatos (G2288) — the same noun used in Rev 20:14 'this is the second death' and 21:8 'which is the second death.' Paul contrasts death (thanatos) with eternal life (zoe aionios) — the antithesis is death vs. life, not torment vs. life. This didactic, cross-genre statement informs the meaning of 'second death' in Revelation.

    Angels in chains + Sodom's 'eternal fire' — yet reduced to ashes¶

    Notes: puros aioniou (fire eternal) — genitive construction. aionios (G166) modifies the fire, yet the fire produced ashes (2 Pet 2:6).
