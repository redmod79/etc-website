    CONCLUSION — Rev 20:10 Named Subjects, Basanizo Usage, and Tree 3 Subject Gate¶

    Question¶

    Rev 20:10 names three subjects for the "tormented day and night for ever and ever" formula: the devil, the beast, and the false prophet. Are these literal human beings, or supernatural/symbolic entities? What does basanizo (G928) mean across its NT usage? What happens when Tree 3's Subject Gate is applied?

    Summary Answer¶

    All three named subjects of the "tormented for ever and ever" formula in Rev 20:10 are non-human or symbolic entities. The devil is a supernatural spirit being (Rev 12:9, 20:2, Eph 2:2). The beast is a symbolic political-religious system (Rev 13:1-2, 17:8-11). The false prophet is a symbolic entity identified as the earth beast of Rev 13:11-17 (Rev 19:20). Tree 3 Gate 1 (Subject Gate) fails for all three. The torment formula (basanisthesontai eis tous aionas ton aionon) appears once in Scripture, applied only to these three non-human/symbolic entities — never to any human being. Revelation consistently uses different vocabulary for humans at the lake of fire: devoured (Rev 20:9), slain (Rev 19:21), and the second death (Rev 20:14-15, 21:8).

    Key Verses¶

    Four-Fold Identification Chain (Rev 12:9) And the great dragon was cast out, that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan, which deceiveth the whole world: he was cast out into the earth, and his angels were cast out with him.

    Three Subjects Tormented (Rev 20:10) And the devil that deceived them was cast into the lake of fire and brimstone, where the beast and the false prophet are, and shall be tormented day and night for ever and ever.

    Humans Devoured (Rev 20:9) And they went up on the breadth of the earth, and compassed the camp of the saints about, and the beloved city: and fire came down from God out of heaven, and devoured them.

    The Lake Is the Second Death (Rev 20:14) And death and hell were cast into the lake of fire. This is the second death.

    Eight Human Categories Receive the Second Death (Rev 21:8) But the fearful, and unbelieving, and the abominable, and murderers, and whoremongers, and sorcerers, and idolaters, and all liars, shall have their part in the lake which burneth with fire and brimstone: which is the second death.

    Beast and False Prophet Cast Alive; Humans Slain (Rev 19:20-21) And the beast was taken, and with him the false prophet that wrought miracles before him... These both were cast alive into a lake of fire burning with brimstone. And the remnant were slain with the sword of him that sat upon the horse.

    Christ Destroys the Devil (Heb 2:14) Forasmuch then as the children are partakers of flesh and blood, he also himself likewise took part of the same; that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death, that is, the devil.

    Satan-Figure Becomes Ashes (Ezek 28:18-19) Therefore will I bring forth a fire from the midst of thee, it shall devour thee, and I will bring thee to ashes upon the earth in the sight of all them that behold thee... thou shalt be a terror, and never shalt thou be any more.

    Jesus Interprets Fire and Brimstone as Destruction (Luke 17:29) But the same day that Lot went out of Sodom it rained fire and brimstone from heaven, and destroyed them all.

    Wages of Sin Is Death (Rom 6:23) For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.

    Analysis¶

    1. The Three Named Subjects — None Is a Literal Human Being¶

    Rev 20:10 names three subjects for the torment formula: the devil (ho diabolos, G1228), the beast (to therion, G2342), and the false prophet (ho pseudoprophetes, G5578). The text itself identifies each as non-human or symbolic.

    The Devil. Rev 12:9 provides the most comprehensive identification chain in Scripture: "the great dragon... that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan, which deceiveth the whole world." Rev 20:2 restates this identification in the immediate context: "the dragon, that old serpent, which is the Devil, and Satan." The four-fold articular apposition uses the naming-copula participle (ho kaloumenos) to link dragon, serpent, Devil, and Satan as one entity. Each title carries non-human content: drakon (G1404) appears exclusively in Revelation (all 13 occurrences); ophis (serpent) traces to Gen 3:1 (pre-human); Diabolos and Satanas are titles for a supernatural adversary. "His angels" (hoi aggeloi autou, Rev 12:9) confirms command over angelic forces — impossible for a human. Eph 2:2 identifies the devil as "the spirit" (to pneuma) — explicitly a spirit being. Gen 3:1 places the serpent before any human sin. Ezek 28:14 identifies the Satan-figure as "the anointed cherub that covereth" (kerub, H3742) — a non-human angelic being. The devil is a supernatural spirit entity, not a human.

    The Beast. Rev 13:1-2 describes the beast rising from the sea with seven heads (kephalas hepta), ten horns (kerata deka), and a composite body from four Daniel 7 beasts in reverse order — leopard, bear, lion. No literal creature or human being has seven heads and ten horns. Rev 17:9-11 decodes the symbolism: the seven heads are seven mountains and seven kings. The beast's temporal description in Rev 17:8 — "was, and is not, and shall ascend out of the bottomless pit" — describes a multi-century political-religious system whose existence pattern no single human can fulfill. The beast counterfeits Christ through shared sphazo (G4969) vocabulary: Rev 13:3's "deadly wound" (esphagmenen) mirrors the Lamb "slain" (esphagmenon) in Rev 5:6. The word study for therion (G2342) confirms that 38 of its 46 NT occurrences are in Revelation, where it is always the apocalyptic beast-entity. The beast is a symbolic entity.

    The False Prophet. Rev 19:20 identifies the false prophet by matching vocabulary from Rev 13:13-14: "the false prophet that wrought miracles (ta semeia) before him (enopion autou), with which he deceived them that had received the mark." This vocabulary match — poiesas ta semeia enopion autou — identifies the false prophet as the earth beast of Rev 13:11-17. The earth beast has entirely symbolic characteristics: two horns like a lamb, speaks as a dragon, exercises the first beast's delegated authority, gives breath (pneuma) to an image. No literal human has these features. The word study for pseudoprophetes (G5578) documents the critical singular/plural distinction: the singular form in Revelation (3 occurrences: Rev 16:13, 19:20, 20:10) always refers to one specific apocalyptic entity, while the plural form elsewhere (7 occurrences: Matt 7:15, 24:11, 24:24, Mark 13:22, Luke 6:26, 2 Pet 2:1, 1 John 4:1) refers to a class of human deceivers. Acts 13:6 is singular outside Revelation but with a personal name (Bar-Jesus). The false prophet of Rev 20:10 is a symbolic entity.

    2. Tree 3 Subject Gate Application¶

    The ETC methodology's Tree 3 requires every E-item with a positional candidate (Cond. or ECT) to pass four validation gates before the classification stands. Gate 1 is the Subject Gate: "Is the grammatical subject a literal human being?" Automatic FAIL conditions include "a non-human spirit being" and "a symbolic entity (a figure in an apocalyptic vision)."

    For Rev 20:10's torment formula: - The devil: Non-human spirit being (Rev 12:9, Eph 2:2, Gen 3:1, Ezek 28:14). Gate 1 FAILS. - The beast: Symbolic entity in an apocalyptic vision (Rev 13:1-2, 17:8-11). Gate 1 FAILS. - The false prophet: Symbolic entity in an apocalyptic vision (Rev 19:20 = Rev 13:11-17). Gate 1 FAILS.

    All three subjects fail the Subject Gate. The proposed ECT classification for E9 ("shall be tormented day and night for ever and ever") cannot stand because no literal human being is the subject of the torment formula. The reclassification check (Tree 3 Step 3) produces a corrected observation: "Three non-human/symbolic entities are described as tormented in Revelation's apocalyptic vision." This corrected observation does not pass V1 (destruction vocabulary) or V2 (human-torment vocabulary) as evidence about human fate. The item is reclassified as Neutral.

    Additionally, Gate 3 (Genre Gate) would independently fail: Revelation is an apocalyptic vision per its own opening declaration (Rev 1:1, semaino G4591 — "communicated by signs/symbols").

    3. Vocabulary Differentiation — The Text's Own Distinctions¶

    The most striking internal evidence is that Revelation itself uses different vocabulary for different subjects at the lake of fire. This vocabulary differentiation appears in multiple passages:

    Rev 20:9 vs. 20:10. In v.9, fire from heaven "devoured" (katephagen, V-2AAI-3S from katesthio G2719) the human nations. In v.10, the devil is cast into the lake and the three non-human/symbolic entities are "tormented" (basanisthesontai, V-FPI-3P from basanizo G928). Different verbs for different subjects in consecutive verses.

    Rev 19:20 vs. 19:21. The beast and false prophet are cast "alive" (zontes, V-PAP-NPM from zao G2198) into the lake. The human remnant (hoi loipoi) is "slain" (apektanthesan, V-API-3P from apokteino G615). Different verbs for symbolic entities and humans in consecutive verses.

    Rev 20:14-15 and 21:8. When humans enter the lake, the designation is "the second death" (ho thanatos ho deuteros). Rev 20:14 provides the self-interpreting formula: "This is the second death" — using the X=Y predicate nominative construction that functions throughout Daniel and Revelation as symbolic interpretation (cf. Rev 1:20, 17:9, 17:15, 17:18). Eight categories of human wicked in Rev 21:8 all receive "the second death" — not the torment formula.

    The pattern is consistent: humans receive destruction/death vocabulary (devoured, slain, second death); non-human/symbolic entities receive torment vocabulary (basanisthesontai). The text maintains this distinction even when describing the same event.

    4. Basanizo (G928) — Complete NT Semantic Range¶

    The verb basanizo occurs 12 times in the NT across 6 books. The word derives from basanos (G931), a touchstone used for testing the purity of metals. The metaphorical extension to "torment/distress" is secondary. The KJV translators recognized the semantic breadth by using five different English words: tormented, tossed, toiling, vexed, pained.

    Non-judgment uses (7 of 12): - Matt 4:24, Matt 8:6 — physical illness/paralysis - Matt 14:24 — a ship "tossed" by waves (inanimate object — the word does not require a conscious subject) - Mark 6:48 — disciples "toiling" in rowing (physical exertion) - 2 Pet 2:8 — Lot "vexed" his righteous soul at Sodom's wickedness (moral distress, the only epistle occurrence) - Rev 12:2 — woman "pained" in childbirth (within Revelation itself, a non-judgment use) - Rev 11:10 — two witnesses "tormented" earth-dwellers (prophetic ministry, not punishment)

    Judgment uses (5 of 12): - Matt 8:29, Mark 5:7, Luke 8:28 — demons use basanizo for their own anticipated fate ("torment us/me not") - Rev 9:5 — basanizo with explicit five-month time limit (the word does not inherently carry eternal duration) - Rev 14:10 — beast-worshippers "tormented" in apocalyptic framework - Rev 20:10 — devil, beast, false prophet "tormented" in apocalyptic framework

    The distribution is revealing. All 5 judgment uses are either in Revelation's apocalyptic framework or involve demons (non-human spirit beings) speaking of their own anticipated fate. No epistle, no Gospel didactic section, and no OT passage uses basanizo for the final fate of generic human wicked. Destruction/death vocabulary (apollymi, apokteino, thanatos, katesthio) for human fate spans all genres and all authors. Torment vocabulary for judgment is genre-specific to apocalyptic and demonic speech.

    The word family reinforces this pattern. Basanismos (G929) has 6 occurrences, all in Revelation, time-bounded in 4 of 6 (five months in Rev 9:5; one hour for Babylon in Rev 18:7, 10, 15). Basanos (G931) has 3 occurrences: physical diseases (Matt 4:24) and parabolic context (Luke 16:23, 28). Basanistes (G930) has 1 occurrence in a parable (Matt 18:34).

    5. OT Background — Destruction Language for the Same Entities¶

    The OT describes the Satan-figure and symbolic beast-entities with destruction/cessation vocabulary — not torment.

    Ezek 28:18-19. The "anointed cherub that covereth" (the Satan-figure behind the king of Tyre) receives this fate: "I will bring forth a fire from the midst of thee, it shall devour thee, and I will bring thee to ashes upon the earth... thou shalt be a terror, and never shalt thou be any more." The Hebrew is absolute: akal (devour), epher (ashes), eneka ad olam (you are not unto eternity). This is the same entity described in Rev 20:10 with the torment formula. In the OT prophetic oracle — direct divine speech — the fate is ashes and cessation.

    Isa 14:12-15. The "morning star" (heylel, H1966, hapax legomenon) is "fallen from heaven," "cut down to the ground," and "brought down to sheol, to the sides of the pit." Luke 10:18 applies this pattern to Satan. The fate is descent and humiliation, not ongoing torment.

    Dan 7:11. The fourth beast (the conceptual predecessor of Revelation's beast) is "slain, and his body destroyed, and given to the burning flame." Aramaic destruction vocabulary (qetilat, slain; geshmah hoobad, body destroyed) for a symbolic beast-entity.

    Under SIS principles, these passages carry significant weight. Ezek 28 is direct divine speech ("I will bring forth fire... I will bring thee to ashes") — a genre with higher interpretive clarity than apocalyptic vision. The OT consistently uses destruction, not torment, for these entities.

    6. The "For Ever and Ever" Formula — Subject-Determined Meaning¶

    The formula eis tous aionas ton aionon appears approximately 13 times in Revelation. Applied to God's existence and reign (9 occurrences: Rev 1:6, 4:9-10, 5:13, 7:12, 10:6, 11:15, 15:7, 22:5), it is genuinely eternal. Applied to saints' reign (Rev 22:5), it is genuinely eternal.

    In judgment contexts (Rev 14:11, 19:3, 20:10), the OT parallel is decisive. Isa 34:9-10 uses identical language for Edom: smoke "shall go up for ever" (le-olam), "not quenched night nor day" — yet Isa 34:11-17 describes animals dwelling in the ruins. Edom is not still burning. Rev 19:3 says Babylon's smoke rises "for ever and ever" — yet Rev 18:21-23 describes Babylon with fivefold "found no more at all" cessation language. The same formula applied to Babylon describes a permanent result of completed destruction, not an ongoing process.

    The formula's meaning is demonstrably subject-determined: applied to the eternal God, it is genuinely eternal; applied to judgment on non-eternal subjects, it communicates the total completeness and permanence of the judgment. Biblical "ascending" language consistently uses physical scale to communicate magnitude — smoke ascending to heaven means total destruction (Josh 8:20, Judg 20:40), sin reaching heaven means maximum accumulated magnitude (Rev 18:5, Gen 18:20). "Forever" applied to ascending smoke is the temporal equivalent: maximum magnitude of destruction. The escalation from le-olam (Isa 34:10, regional Edom) to eis tous aionas ton aionon (Rev 14:11, 20:10, final judgment) communicates that the final judgment is more total than any prior one.

    In Rev 20:10, the formula "tormented day and night for ever and ever" does not use the smoke-ascending idiom — because the verse focuses on the torment process rather than the destruction result. The destruction is already stated on both sides: v.9 says fire "devoured" the human armies (katephagen, aorist — completed), and v.14 defines the lake of fire as "the second death." The torment of v.10 sits within this destruction framework. "Forever and ever" applied to the torment communicates its total completeness — the torment runs to full completion, and completed torment produces destruction, as the OT process→outcome pattern consistently shows (Isa 13:8-9, Nah 1:9-10, Zeph 1:15,18). (Examined in depth in etc7-11-smoke-ascending-forever and etc7-22-vengeance-and-justice.)

    7. The "Beast-as-Representative" Counter-Argument¶

    The strongest counter-argument holds that even if the beast is a symbolic system, it represents human adherents, and the torment of the symbol transfers to those humans. The text addresses this directly.

    Rev 19:20-21 provides the clearest evidence: the beast and false prophet are cast "alive" (zontes) into the lake, while the human remnant is "slain" (apektanthesan). In the same two-verse narrative, the text deliberately uses different verbs for the symbolic entities and for their human followers. Rev 20:9 describes the post-millennium human followers of Satan as "devoured" (katephagen) by fire — destruction vocabulary. Rev 21:8 lists eight categories of human sinners and assigns them "the second death."

    The text maintains the vocabulary distinction between what happens to the symbolic entities and what happens to the humans within those systems. The torment formula is not transferred from the symbol to the humans. The humans receive death vocabulary.

    8. Cross-Genre Evidence Pattern¶

    Destruction/death vocabulary for the fate of the wicked spans every genre and every major biblical author: - OT prophets: Mal 4:1 (burn up as stubble, neither root nor branch), Obad 1:16 (as though they had not been), Isa 34:9-10 (smoke ascending from ended fire) - Wisdom literature: Psa 37:10 (the wicked shall not be) - Pauline epistles: Rom 6:23 (wages of sin is death), 1 Cor 15:26 (death destroyed) - Johannine epistles: 1 John 3:8 (destroy the works of the devil) - Petrine epistles: 2 Pet 2:6 (turned to ashes, an ensample) - Jesus' own words: Luke 17:29 (destroyed them all), Matt 10:28 (destroy soul and body)

    Torment vocabulary for judgment appears only in Revelation's apocalyptic framework and in demonic speech. This cross-genre distribution is a significant datum under SIS principles, where didactic passages have higher interpretive weight than apocalyptic vision.

    9. The Sodom Paradigm — Fire and Brimstone Produces Ashes¶

    The fire-and-brimstone imagery in Rev 20:10 traces directly to Gen 19:24 — the destruction of Sodom. Jesus interprets this paradigm: "destroyed them all" (Luke 17:29, apollymi G622). Peter interprets it: "turning the cities of Sodom and Gomorrha into ashes... an ensample unto those that after should live ungodly" (2 Pet 2:6). Jude calls it "the vengeance of eternal fire" (Jude 1:7) — yet the fire produced ashes, not ongoing burning. Peter explicitly calls this destruction paradigm an "ensample" (hupodeigma, G5262) for future judgment of the ungodly.

    The inspired interpretation of the fire-and-brimstone paradigm is destruction producing ashes. "Eternal fire" describes the permanence of the result, not the duration of the process.

    10. The Lake of Fire Self-Interpreted as the Second Death¶

    Rev 20:14 provides an explicit self-interpretation: "This is the second death" (houtos ho thanatos ho deuteros estin he limne tou pyros). The predicate nominative construction uses the X=Y symbol-decoding formula attested throughout Daniel and Revelation. The lake of fire IS the second death — a death concept, not a torment concept.

    When death and hades are cast into the lake (Rev 20:14), the result is the destruction of death itself — "there shall be no more death" (Rev 21:4), paralleling 1 Cor 15:26 ("The last enemy that shall be destroyed is death"). The lake functions as an instrument of destruction. (Examined in depth in etc6-13-lake-of-fire-second-death.)

    Rom 6:23 states the wages of sin is thanatos (death) — the same word used for "second death." The didactic contrast is death vs. eternal life, not torment vs. eternal life.

    I-B Resolution¶

    I-B Resolution: I1 — Same lake = same experience¶

    Step 1 — Tension: - FOR: E9 (all three in lake + tormented), E10 (humans also in lake), E16 (fire prepared for devil and his angels — humans sent there too) - AGAINST: E12 (different vocabulary: cast alive vs. slain in Rev 19:20-21), E8 (devoured for humans in 20:9), E10-E11 (second death for humans in 20:14-15, 21:8), N1 (vocabulary differentiation is a necessary implication)

    Step 2 — Clarity Assessment:

    Item Level Rationale
    E12 Plain Rev 19:20-21 uses different verbs in consecutive verses for symbolic entities (cast alive) and humans (slain) — directly addresses the question of whether different subjects receive the same treatment
    N1 Plain Observable vocabulary fact verified from the Greek text across Rev 19-21
    E8 Plain katephagen (devoured) for human nations in Rev 20:9 is unambiguous destruction vocabulary
    E10-E11 Contextually Clear "Second death" designation for humans requires awareness that this is a death concept per the X=Y formula, but the death vocabulary is clear
    E9 Contextually Clear The torment formula is stated plainly but applied to non-human/symbolic subjects in apocalyptic genre
    E16 Ambiguous Matt 25:41 says fire was prepared for the devil and his angels; humans are sent there — but does not state they experience the same thing as the devil

    Step 3 — Weight: The AGAINST side has multiple Plain items (E12, N1, E8) plus Contextually Clear items (E10-E11). The FOR side has one Contextually Clear item (E9, but subject is non-human) and one Ambiguous item (E16). The weight favors the AGAINST side.

    Step 4 — SIS Application: The Plain items (E12, N1, E8) govern the reading of Ambiguous items. The text's own vocabulary differentiation — humans slain/devoured/second death vs. non-human entities tormented — determines the reading of the "same lake" observation. Being in the same location does not require the same experience when the text explicitly describes different outcomes.

    Step 5 — Resolution: Strong Plain statements on the AGAINST side (vocabulary differentiation in same narrative units) with only Ambiguous statements on the FOR side. The text itself distinguishes what happens to different subjects at the same destination.

    I-B Resolution: I5 — "For ever and ever" always genuinely eternal¶

    Step 1 — Tension: - FOR: Rev 4:9-10 (formula applied to God — genuinely eternal), E9 (formula applied to judgment in Rev 20:10) - AGAINST: E20 (Isa 34:9-10 uses identical "for ever" language for Edom — animals inhabit ruins), E21 (Rev 19:3 + 18:21-23: Babylon's smoke "for ever" yet "found no more at all")

    Step 2 — Clarity Assessment:

    Item Level Rationale
    E20 Plain Isa 34:9-10 uses identical formula; Isa 34:11-17 describes animals in ruins — the OT demonstrates the formula does not always mean ongoing process. Observable fact.
    E21 Plain Rev 18:21-23 explicitly says "found no more at all" five times; Rev 19:3 uses "for ever" for the same entity. Coexistence of cessation and "forever" language in the same literary context is observable.
    Rev 4:9-10 Plain Applied to God, the formula is genuinely eternal.
    E9 Contextually Clear The formula in Rev 20:10 requires determining whether it follows the God-usage (genuinely eternal) or the judgment-usage (permanent result).

    Step 3 — Weight: Both sides have Plain items. However, the AGAINST side demonstrates from OT and NT evidence that the identical formula is used for entities whose cessation is explicitly described. The FOR side shows the formula is genuinely eternal when applied to God, but this does not prove it is univocal across all subjects.

    Step 4 — SIS Application: The OT passage (Isa 34:9-10) provides the interpretive key for the judgment usage of the formula. The OT is the source material; Revelation draws on its imagery. The Edom and Babylon parallels demonstrate the formula can describe permanent results of completed destruction. Applied to God (genuinely eternal being), it is genuinely eternal. Applied to judgment on non-eternal subjects, it describes permanence of result.

    Step 5 — Resolution: Strong The Isa 34:9-10 and Babylon evidence (Plain on the AGAINST side) demonstrates the formula is not univocal. The formula's meaning is subject-determined: genuinely eternal for God, permanent result for judgment on non-eternal subjects. The claim that it is always genuinely eternal requires ignoring the OT usage and the Babylon evidence within Revelation itself.

    Word Studies¶

    Basanizo (G928) — 12 NT occurrences¶

    From basanos (G931, touchstone for testing metals). 7 non-judgment uses (illness, waves, rowing, moral distress, childbirth, prophetic witness). 5 judgment uses (all in Revelation's apocalyptic framework or demonic speech). No epistle, Gospel didactic section, or OT passage uses this word for human eschatological fate. The KJV translates it with five different English words.

    Basanismos (G929) — 6 occurrences¶

    All in Revelation. Time-bounded in 4 of 6 (five months, Rev 9:5; one hour for Babylon, Rev 18:7, 10, 15).

    Therion (G2342) — 46 occurrences¶

    38 of 46 in Revelation, where it is always the apocalyptic beast-entity. A symbolic political-religious system, not a literal individual.

    Pseudoprophetes (G5578) — 11 occurrences¶

    3 singular in Revelation = one specific apocalyptic entity (= earth beast per Rev 19:20). 7 plural elsewhere = class of human deceivers. 1 singular outside Revelation with personal name (Acts 13:6, Bar-Jesus).

    Diabolos (G1228) — 38 occurrences¶

    Supernatural adversary when articular/singular. Identified as dragon/serpent (Rev 12:9), spirit being (Eph 2:2), pre-human (Gen 3:1), commander of angels (Matt 25:41). Subject of destruction vocabulary (Heb 2:14, katargeo; 1 John 3:8, luo).

    Katargeo (G2673) — 32 occurrences¶

    Applied to the devil (Heb 2:14) and death (1 Cor 15:26). Semantic range includes abolish, render powerless, destroy. Destruction-class vocabulary regardless of exact nuance.

    Difficult Passages¶

    Rev 14:10-11 — Torment vocabulary applied to beast-worshippers¶

    This passage comes closest to applying torment vocabulary to human subjects. Two factors complicate a straightforward ECT reading: (1) the passage is in Revelation's apocalyptic framework (Genre Gate fails), and (2) the "for ever and ever" formula applies to the smoke (kapnos) of their torment, not directly to the torment itself — and Rev 19:3 shows the identical smoke formula describes Babylon, which is "found no more at all." (Examined in depth in etc7-11-smoke-ascending-forever.)

    The "same lake" argument¶

    The devil, beast, false prophet, death, hades, and unsaved humans all end up in the same lake. The inference that same destination = same experience is resolved I-B Strong against (see I-B Resolution above): the text itself uses different vocabulary for different subjects at the same destination.

    The "beast-as-representative" argument¶

    The beast represents a system that includes human adherents. The inference that the beast's torment transfers to those humans is classified I-C (compatible external) — an interpretive framework not stated in the text. The text applies different vocabulary to the symbol and to the humans within the system (Rev 19:20-21, 20:9, 21:8).

    Katargeo ambiguity (Heb 2:14)¶

    Whether katargeo means "destroy" or "render powerless" is a genuine lexical question. The word study documents the semantic range. Regardless of exact nuance, the verb is destruction-class vocabulary applied to the devil in a didactic epistle. E15 is classified Neutral because of this ambiguity — both sides can accept that the text uses this verb for the devil.

    What CAN Be Said (Scripture explicitly states or necessarily implies)¶

    • The three subjects of the "tormented for ever and ever" formula in Rev 20:10 are the devil (non-human spirit being), the beast (symbolic entity), and the false prophet (symbolic entity). None is a literal human being.
    • The torment formula (basanisthesontai eis tous aionas ton aionon) appears once in Scripture and is applied only to these three non-human/symbolic entities.
    • Revelation uses different vocabulary for human subjects at the lake: devoured (Rev 20:9), slain (Rev 19:21), the second death (Rev 20:14-15, 21:8). The vocabulary differentiation is a textual fact.
    • The lake of fire is self-interpreted as "the second death" (Rev 20:14).
    • Basanizo in judgment contexts appears only in Revelation's apocalyptic framework or in demonic speech. No epistle, Gospel didactic section, or OT passage applies it to human eschatological fate.
    • The OT describes the Satan-figure with destruction/cessation vocabulary: fire devours, becomes ashes, exists never any more (Ezek 28:18-19).
    • The "for ever" formula is used in the OT for Edom (Isa 34:9-10), which is not still burning.
    • Destruction/death vocabulary for human fate spans all genres (OT prophets, wisdom, Gospels, epistles). Torment vocabulary for judgment is genre-specific to Revelation and demonic speech.
    • The wages of sin is death (Rom 6:23) — the same word as "second death."
    • Jesus interprets fire and brimstone as "destroyed them all" (Luke 17:29). Peter calls Sodom's reduction to ashes "an ensample" (2 Pet 2:6).

    What CANNOT Be Said (not explicitly stated or necessarily implied by Scripture)¶

    • It cannot be said from the text that the "tormented for ever and ever" formula applies to human beings. No verse applies this formula to any human individual or class.
    • It cannot be said that being in the same lake necessarily means experiencing the same outcome. The text uses different vocabulary for different subjects at the same destination.
    • It cannot be said that the torment of a symbolic entity automatically transfers to the humans represented by that entity. The text applies different vocabulary to the symbol and to the humans within the system.
    • It cannot be said that basanizo is the standard biblical term for the final fate of human wicked. The word appears in judgment contexts only in Revelation's apocalyptic framework and in demonic speech.
    • It cannot be said that the "for ever and ever" formula is univocal across all subjects. The OT uses identical language for Edom (ended fire), and Revelation uses it for Babylon (found no more at all).
    • It cannot be said that the OT teaches ongoing torment for the Satan-figure. Ezekiel 28 describes that entity becoming ashes and existing never any more.

    Conclusion¶

    Rev 20:10 names three subjects for the "tormented day and night for ever and ever" formula: the devil, the beast, and the false prophet. The text identifies all three as non-human or symbolic entities. The devil is a supernatural spirit being identified by a four-fold chain (Rev 12:9, 20:2) and called "the spirit" (Eph 2:2). The beast is a symbolic composite entity with seven heads, ten horns, and a multi-century temporal existence pattern (Rev 13:1-2, 17:8). The false prophet is the earth beast of Rev 13:11-17, identified by matching vocabulary in Rev 19:20.

    Tree 3's Subject Gate fails for all three subjects. None is a literal human being. The Genre Gate independently fails because Revelation is an apocalyptic vision (Rev 1:1, semaino). The proposed ECT classification for Rev 20:10 does not survive the mechanical gate process.

    The torment formula (basanisthesontai eis tous aionas ton aionon) appears once in Scripture — Rev 20:10 — and is applied only to these three non-human/symbolic entities. It is never applied to any human being anywhere in the Bible. When humans enter the lake of fire, the text consistently uses different vocabulary: devoured (Rev 20:9), slain (Rev 19:21), and the second death (Rev 20:14-15, 21:8).

    Basanizo (G928) in judgment contexts appears only in Revelation's apocalyptic framework or in demonic speech. No epistle, Gospel didactic section, or OT passage uses it for the final fate of human wicked. Destruction/death vocabulary for human fate spans all genres and all authors.

    The OT describes the Satan-figure (the same entity as Rev 20:10's devil) with destruction/cessation vocabulary: fire devours, becomes ashes, exists never any more (Ezek 28:18-19). The "for ever" formula is used for Edom (Isa 34:9-10), which is not still burning, and for Babylon (Rev 19:3), which is "found no more at all" (Rev 18:21-23).

    Seven explicit statements at the conditionalist direction passed all four Tree 3 gates — all from didactic genres (epistles, Jesus' sayings, OT prophets, wisdom). Zero explicit statements at the ECT direction survived the gate process. The two I-B inferences (same lake = same experience; formula always means genuinely eternal) were both resolved Strong against the ECT reading based on the text's own vocabulary differentiation and the OT/NT evidence for subject-determined formula meaning.

    The evidence at the E and N tiers is entirely Neutral (33 items documenting textual facts) or Conditionalist (7 items from didactic genres). The ECT case for Rev 20:10 rests entirely at the inference level (3 items: 2 I-B resolved Strong against, 1 I-C).
