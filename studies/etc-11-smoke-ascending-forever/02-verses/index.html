    Verses¶

    Question¶

    Rev 14:9-11, 19:3 -- OT source imagery (Isa 34:9-10, Gen 19:28, Mal 4:1-3). Ongoing torment vs. permanent destruction? Apply Tree 3 genre gate.

    Primary crux text: the third angel's warning — smoke of torment ascending forever¶

    And the third angel followed them, saying with a loud voice, If any man worship the beast and his image, and receive [his] mark in his forehead, or in his hand, The same shall drink of the wine of the wrath of God, which is poured out without mixture into the cup of his indignation; and he shall be tormented with fire and brimstone in the presence of the holy angels, and in the presence of the Lamb: And the smoke of their torment ascendeth up for ever and ever: and they have no rest day nor night, who worship the beast and his image, and whosoever receiveth the mark of his name.

    Context: Part of the three angels' messages in Revelation 14. The first angel proclaims the everlasting gospel (v.6-7), the second announces Babylon's fall (v.8), and the third warns against worshipping the beast. This is within an apocalyptic vision sequence — the Lamb on Mount Zion with 144,000 (vv.1-5), the three angels (vv.6-12), the harvest of the earth (vv.14-20). Every element is symbolic: Lamb, Mount Zion, harps, angels, sickle, winepress.

    Notes: Rev 14:10 — basanisthesetai (G928) is future passive indicative 3S, matching the conditional 'if any man' in v.9. The torment occurs enopion (G1799) the holy angels and the Lamb — throne-room/judgment language. Rev 14:11 — kapnos (G2586) is nominative singular, anabainei (G305) is present active indicative — the smoke 'ascends' using the same present tense as the living creatures' ceaseless praise in 4:8. The 'ages of ages' formula (eis aionas aionon) uses accusative plural + genitive plural of aion (G165). The anapausis (G372) construction 'ouk echousin anapausin hemeras kai nuktos' exactly parallels Rev 4:8.

    Second crux text: Babylon's smoke rises forever — identical formula applied to completed destruction¶

    And again they said, Alleluia. And her smoke rose up for ever and ever.

    Context: Follows the exhaustive destruction of Babylon in Rev 18. After 'utterly burned with fire' (18:8), 'in one hour' (18:10,17,19), and fivefold 'found no more at all' (18:21-23), heaven celebrates Babylon's destruction. The identical 'smoke ascending for ever and ever' (eis tous aionas ton aionon) formula is applied to an entity whose destruction is explicitly complete and total.

    Notes: The Greek construction is nearly identical to Rev 14:11: kapnos (G2586) nominative + anabainei (G305) present active + eis tous aionas ton aionon. Key difference: Rev 19:3 has the full article (tous) before aionas. Both use the same 'ages of ages' formula. Applied to Babylon — a symbolic entity 'found no more at all' — this formula cannot mean ongoing burning, since the thing is gone. It means permanent memorial/evidence of completed destruction.

    Primary OT source: Edom's smoke ascending forever — the formula Rev 14:11 draws upon¶

    And the streams thereof shall be turned into pitch, and the dust thereof into brimstone, and the land thereof shall become burning pitch. It shall not be quenched night nor day; the smoke thereof shall go up for ever: from generation to generation it shall lie waste; none shall pass through it for ever and ever.

    Context: Isaiah 34 is a judgment oracle against Edom (Idumea). The chapter opens with universal judgment language (vv.1-4), then focuses on Edom (vv.5-17). The fire-and-brimstone imagery (v.9) and the smoke-ascending-forever formula (v.10) are the primary OT source for Rev 14:11's language. Critically, vv.11-17 describe animals DWELLING in the territory — pelicans, hedgehogs, owls, ravens, wild goats. The 'forever' fire has ended.

    Notes: Hebrew parsing of v.10: le-olam (H5769, 'to eternity') governs ya'aleh (Qal imperfect 3ms of alah H5927, 'ascend') ashanah (H6227+3fs, 'its smoke'). The le-netsach netsachim (H5331, 'to perpetuity of perpetuities') is the STRONGEST Hebrew 'forever' form — construct + plural, intensifying. Yet animals dwell there in vv.11-17. The olam fire terminated. This is THE interpretive key for Rev 14:11.

    The continuation: animals dwelling in Edom's ruins — the 'forever' fire has ended¶

    But the cormorant and the bittern shall possess it; the owl also and the raven shall dwell in it: and he shall stretch out upon it the line of confusion, and the stones of emptiness. They shall call the nobles thereof to the kingdom, but none [shall be] there, and all her princes shall be nothing. And thorns shall come up in her palaces, nettles and brambles in the fortresses thereof: and it shall be an habitation of dragons, [and] a court for owls. The wild beasts of the desert shall also meet with the wild beasts of the island, and the satyr shall cry to his fellow; the screech owl also shall rest there, and find for herself a place of rest. There shall the great owl make her nest, and lay, and hatch, and gather under her shadow: there shall the vultures also be gathered, every one with her mate. Seek ye out of the book of the LORD, and read: no one of these shall fail, none shall want her mate: for my mouth it hath commanded, and his spirit it hath gathered them. And he hath cast the lot for them, and his hand hath divided it unto them by line: they shall possess it for ever, from generation to generation shall they dwell therein.

    Context: Immediately following the 'smoke ascending forever' declaration in v.10. The same prophet, in the same oracle, describes the RESULT of the judgment: not ongoing fire but permanent desolation inhabited by wild animals. The animals 'rest' (v.14) and 'dwell' (v.17) — activities impossible if the fire were still burning. The 'for ever' in v.17 (le-olam) describes the animals' permanent habitation of the ruins.

    Notes: V.14 uses 'rest' (manoach H4494) for the screech owl — the same concept (cessation, settling) that anapausis expresses in Greek. The territory has become habitable. V.17 says 'they shall possess it for ever (le-olam), from generation to generation shall they dwell therein.' The same olam applied to the animals' habitation as was applied to the smoke's ascent in v.10. This proves olam-fire = permanent result, not endless process.

    Foundational fire-and-brimstone paradigm: Sodom destroyed, Abraham sees smoke aftermath¶

    Then the LORD rained upon Sodom and upon Gomorrah brimstone and fire from the LORD out of heaven; And he overthrew those cities, and all the plain, and all the inhabitants of the cities, and that which grew upon the ground. But his wife looked back from behind him, and she became a pillar of salt. And Abraham gat up early in the morning to the place where he stood before the LORD: And he looked toward Sodom and Gomorrah, and toward all the land of the plain, and beheld, and, lo, the smoke of the country went up as the smoke of a furnace.

    Context: The foundational narrative establishing the fire-and-brimstone judgment paradigm. Every subsequent fire-and-brimstone passage references this event. Key: the fire 'destroyed' (overthrew), and Abraham sees smoke as AFTERMATH — visible evidence of completed destruction. The smoke is not evidence of ongoing torment but of the destruction that already happened.

    Notes: Hebrew parsing: v.28 uses qitor (H7008, 'dense smoke/vapor') not ashan (H6227). Qitor emphasizes thick, furnace-like quality. The smoke 'went up' (alah H5927, Qal perfect 3ms) — completed action. Abraham sees the aftermath the morning AFTER the destruction. NT interpretation: Luke 17:29 says fire 'destroyed them all' (apollymi G622); 2 Pet 2:6 says Sodom became 'ashes' (tephroo); Jude 1:7 calls it 'eternal fire' yet the result is ashes.

    Eschatological fire producing ashes: the wicked become stubble, burned up, leaving ashes¶

    For, behold, the day cometh, that shall burn as an oven; and all the proud, yea, and all that do wickedly, shall be stubble: and the day that cometh shall burn them up, saith the LORD of hosts, that it shall leave them neither root nor branch. But unto you that fear my name shall the Sun of righteousness arise with healing in his wings; and ye shall go forth, and grow up as calves of the stall. And ye shall tread down the wicked; for they shall be ashes under the soles of your feet in the day that I shall do [this], saith the LORD of hosts.

    Context: The OT's most explicit eschatological fire passage. Malachi uses total consumption language: wicked = stubble (qash H7179), burned up (lahat H3857 Piel), leaving neither root nor branch. The RESULT is ashes (epher H665) under the soles of the righteous' feet. No torment vocabulary — only destruction and cessation vocabulary.

    Notes: Hebrew parsing (Mal 3:19 in Hebrew = 4:1 in English): bo'er (H1197 Qal participle) 'burning'; qash (H7179) 'stubble'; lihat (H3857 Piel perfect 3ms) 'devour'. V.3 (Heb 3:21): epher (H665) 'ashes/dust' — the stated RESULT. The wicked are ashes, not tormented beings. The stubble simile is decisive: stubble ceases to exist when burned. Corroborated by 2 Pet 2:6 where Sodom's fire-and-brimstone result is also 'ashes'.

    Babylon's destruction: torment and utter burning with fire — time-limited basanismos¶

    How much she hath glorified herself, and lived deliciously, so much torment and sorrow give her: for she saith in her heart, I sit a queen, and am no widow, and shall see no sorrow. Therefore shall her plagues come in one day, death, and mourning, and famine; and she shall be utterly burned with fire: for strong [is] the Lord God who judgeth her.

    Context: Babylon's judgment described as basanismos (G929) — the same word used in Rev 14:11 for the beast-worshippers' torment. Yet the result is 'utterly burned with fire' and plagues come 'in one day.' Three of six basanismos occurrences describe Babylon's judgment (18:7,10,15).

    Notes: Basanismos (G929) occurrence 4 of 6. The basanismos of Babylon is time-limited ('one day,' 'one hour'), followed by complete destruction ('utterly burned'). This same basanismos whose smoke rises 'for ever and ever' in 19:3 is explicitly finite in duration and total in result.

    Basanismos occurrence: Babylon's torment comes 'in one hour'¶

    Standing afar off for the fear of her torment, saying, Alas, alas, that great city Babylon, that mighty city! for in one hour is thy judgment come.

    Context: Kings of the earth witness Babylon's basanismos and note it comes 'in one hour' — explicitly time-bounded torment.

    Notes: Basanismos (G929) occurrence 5 of 6. Time-limited: 'in one hour.' The merchants (v.15) also stand afar off 'for the fear of her basanismos' — occurrence 6 of 6.

    Basanismos occurrence: merchants fear Babylon's completed torment¶

    The merchants of these things, which were made rich by her, shall stand afar off for the fear of her torment, weeping and wailing,

    Context: The third Babylon-basanismos occurrence. Merchants weep at the completed destruction of their source of wealth.

    Notes: Basanismos (G929) occurrence 6 of 6. Total distribution: Rev 9:5 (twice, five-month limit), Rev 14:11 (crux text), Rev 18:7, 18:10, 18:15 (all Babylon's completed destruction). 3 of 6 = Babylon's time-limited, completed destruction. 1 of 6 = five-month limit. Only 1 (14:11) is the crux text.

    Fivefold 'found no more at all': the most total destruction language in Scripture¶

    And a mighty angel took up a stone like a great millstone, and cast [it] into the sea, saying, Thus with violence shall that great city Babylon be thrown down, and shall be found no more at all. And the voice of harpers, and musicians, and of pipers, and trumpeters, shall be heard no more at all in thee; and no craftsman, of whatsoever craft [he be], shall be found any more in thee; and the sound of a millstone shall be heard no more at all in thee; And the light of a candle shall shine no more at all in thee; and the voice of the bridegroom and of the bride shall be heard no more at all in thee: for thy merchants were the great men of the earth; for by thy sorceries were all nations deceived.

    Context: Five 'no more at all' declarations for Babylon: (1) city found no more, (2) music heard no more, (3) craftsmen found no more, (4) light shines no more, (5) wedding voices heard no more. Total, exhaustive cessation language. Yet the smoke of this same Babylon 'rises for ever and ever' in 19:3.

    Notes: The entity whose smoke rises 'for ever and ever' in 19:3 is described here with the most comprehensive cessation language in Scripture. Every aspect of life — music, industry, light, marriage — ceases completely. The smoke formula therefore CANNOT mean the entity is still burning; it means permanent memorial of completed destruction.

    Same-book same-author anapausis parallel: living creatures 'rest not day and night'¶

    And the four beasts had each of them six wings about [him]; and [they were] full of eyes within: and they rest not day and night, saying, Holy, holy, holy, Lord God Almighty, which was, and is, and is to come.

    Context: The four living creatures before God's throne. Same author (John), same book (Revelation), same word (anapausis G372), same grammatical construction (ouk echousin anapausin hemeras kai nuktos). In 4:8, 'rest not day and night' characterizes uninterrupted activity during its occurrence — not eternal duration. No one interprets this as the creatures being eternally conscious without rest in a torment-like sense.

    Notes: Greek parsing confirms identical construction to Rev 14:11: anapausin (G372, accusative singular feminine) + ouk echousin (G2192, present active indicative 3P) + hemeras kai nuktos (genitive of time). The only difference: in 4:8, the activity is praise (positive); in 14:11, the activity is torment (negative). The phrase characterizes the NATURE of the activity (uninterrupted, without respite), not its eternal duration.

    Genre marker: Revelation communicated through 'signs' (semaino G4591)¶

    The Revelation of Jesus Christ, which God gave unto him, to shew unto his servants things which must shortly come to pass; and he sent and signified [it] by his angel unto his servant John:

    Context: The programmatic opening verse of Revelation. The verb esemainen (G4591, aorist active indicative 3S) — 'signified' — establishes that the entire book communicates through signs and symbols. This is the foundation for the Tree 3 genre gate: Revelation is apocalyptic/symbolic, not didactic/plain.

    Notes: Semaino (G4591) means to communicate through signs rather than direct statement. All 4 Johannine uses (John 12:33; 18:32; 21:19; Rev 1:1) involve indirect, symbolic communication of future events. The word establishes the book's hermeneutical framework: its imagery is sign-language, not literal description.

    Basanismos with five-month time limit: torment can be temporally bounded¶

    And to them it was given that they should not kill them, but that they should be tormented five months: and their torment [was] as the torment of a scorpion, when he striketh a man.

    Through the wrath of the LORD of hosts is the land darkened, and the people shall be as the fuel of the fire: no man shall spare his brother.

    Context: Isaiah describes divine judgment on Israel. The people become fuel (ma'akholeth, H3980) for fire — consumed by it, not perpetually burning in it. The fire consumes its fuel; the fuel does not endure forever.

    Notes: People as fuel of fire — fuel is consumed by fire, not preserved in it. Cessation/consumption imagery for divine wrath.
