    Verses¶

    Question¶

    Does Hebrew olam (H5769) always mean eternal/endless? All major usage categories including clearly limited uses. Duration determined by nature of subject?

    Root meaning of olam: From everlasting to everlasting applied to God¶

    Lord, thou hast been our dwelling place in all generations. Before the mountains were brought forth, or ever thou hadst formed the earth and the world, even from everlasting to everlasting, thou [art] God.

    Context: Prayer of Moses. Olam appears in the maximal doxological doubling me-olam ad-olam ('from everlasting to everlasting'), applied to God's being. This is the baseline genuinely-eternal use.

    Notes: Parser shows: me-olam (Prep + Noun.ms.Abs 'eternity') ad-olam (Prep + Noun.ms.Abs 'eternity'). The construct me-olam ad-olam = 'from concealed-time to concealed-time.' When applied to God (El, Noun.ms.Abs), it conveys genuine eternality because God is without beginning or end. Category A.

    El Olam: The Everlasting God — divine title¶

    And [Abraham] planted a grove in Beersheba, and called there on the name of the LORD, the everlasting God.

    Context: Abraham names God 'El Olam' — a construct chain (Noun.ms.Cst + Noun.ms.Abs) meaning 'God of eternity.' Applied to God's nature.

    Notes: Parser confirms El (Noun.ms.Cst) + olam (Noun.ms.Abs) = 'God of eternity.' A divine title — olam describes God, so duration is genuinely eternal. Category A.

    The everlasting God: Creator who does not faint¶

    Hast thou not known? hast thou not heard, [that] the everlasting God, the LORD, the Creator of the ends of the earth, fainteth not, neither is weary? [there is] no searching of his understanding.

    Context: Isaiah's comfort to Israel. God is called 'the everlasting God' (elohei olam). Applied to God as Creator — genuinely eternal.

    Notes: Another divine-attribute use of olam. Category A.

    God inhabits eternity¶

    For thus saith the high and lofty One that inhabiteth eternity, whose name [is] Holy; I dwell in the high and holy [place], with him also [that is] of a contrite and humble spirit, to revive the spirit of the humble, and to revive the heart of the contrite ones.

    Context: God described as 'inhabiting eternity' — uses ad (H5703) rather than olam, but the concept is equivalent. Category A.

    Notes: Uses ad (H5703) not olam (H5769), but demonstrates the same concept of God inhabiting endless duration. Category A.

    The eternal God is thy refuge¶

    The eternal God [is thy] refuge, and underneath [are] the everlasting arms: and he shall thrust out the enemy from before thee; and shall say, Destroy [them].

    Context: Moses' blessing on Israel. God called 'the eternal God' with 'everlasting arms.' Uses qedem (H6924, 'antiquity/ancient') for 'eternal' and olam for 'everlasting.' Category A.

    Notes: KJV 'eternal' here is qedem (H6924, antiquity), 'everlasting arms' is olam (H5769). Both temporal words applied to God. Category A.

    The LORD shall reign for ever and ever¶

    The LORD shall reign for ever and ever.

    Context: Song of Moses after the Red Sea crossing. Uses the intensified form le-olam va-ed ('for ever and ever'). The existence of this combined form implies olam alone is not maximal.

    Notes: Le-olam va-ed: olam (H5769) + ad (H5703). If olam already meant 'infinite,' adding ad ('and further') would be redundant. Applied to God's reign — genuinely eternal. Category A / Construct form evidence.

    Context: Law of the Hebrew slave who chooses to remain. 'For ever' (le-olam) = for the slave's lifetime, not literally for eternity. Universally understood as lifetime service.

    Parallel slave law: servant 'for ever'¶

    Then thou shalt take an aul, and thrust [it] through his ear unto the door, and he shall be thy servant for ever. And also unto thy maidservant thou shalt do likewise.

    Context: Deuteronomic parallel to Exo 21:6. Same le-olam, same limited-by-mortality meaning. Confirms Category C.

    Notes: Same construct as Exo 21:6. Le-olam in slave-law context = lifetime. Category C.

    Olam = three days: Jonah in the fish¶

    I went down to the bottoms of the mountains; the earth with her bars [was] about me for ever: yet hast thou brought up my life from corruption, O LORD my God.

    Context: Jonah's prayer from inside the fish. He describes the confinement as olam ('for ever'), yet it lasted only three days and three nights (Jon 1:17). The word 'yet' (Heb. waw-adversative) shows the olam was terminated by God's rescue.

The most extreme example of olam's context-dependent duration. Category C.

    Samuel dedicated 'for ever' — meaning his lifetime¶

    But Hannah went not up; for she said unto her husband, [I will not go up] until the child be weaned, and [then] I will bring him, that he may appear before the LORD, and there abide for ever. ... Therefore also I have lent him to the LORD; as long as he liveth he shall be lent to the LORD. And he worshipped the LORD there.

    Context: Hannah dedicates Samuel to tabernacle service 'for ever' (ad-olam, v.22). Verse 28 defines this olam: 'as long as he liveth.' The text itself equates olam with a human lifespan.

    Notes: V.22 uses ad-olam; v.28 explicitly defines it as 'as long as he liveth' (kol ha-yamim asher hayah). The Bible itself interprets olam as 'for his lifetime.' Category C.

    Context: God explicitly revokes an olam promise. He said ad-olam ('for ever') but now reverses it with chalilah li ('be it far from me'). This is Category D — the most direct evidence that olam does not mean 'unconditionally eternal.'

    God revokes Saul's olam kingdom¶

    And Samuel said to Saul, Thou hast done foolishly: thou hast not kept the commandment of the LORD thy God, which he commanded thee: for now would the LORD have established thy kingdom upon Israel for ever. But now thy kingdom shall not continue: the LORD hath sought him a man after his own heart, and the LORD hath commanded him [to be] captain over his people, because thou hast not kept [that] which the LORD commanded thee.

    Context: The LORD would have established Saul's kingdom ad-olam ('for ever'), but 'now thy kingdom shall not continue.' Conditional olam revoked due to disobedience. Category D.

    Notes: Second explicit revocation. God intended olam but withdrew it because of human failure. Category D.
