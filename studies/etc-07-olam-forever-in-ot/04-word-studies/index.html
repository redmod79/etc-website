    Word Studies¶

    Question¶

    Does Hebrew olam (H5769) always mean eternal/endless? All major usage categories including clearly limited uses. Duration determined by nature of subject?

    H5769 — olam¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H5769-owlam.md

    Occurrences: 432

    Masculine noun from root meaning 'to conceal' (H5956). Core image: a horizon that disappears from sight — time beyond perception in either direction. 406 unique verses (432 tokens), 34 books. Translated 'for ever' (195x, 49%), 'everlasting' (54x, 13%), 'perpetual' (22x, 5%), 'of old' (13x, 3%), 'ancient' (4x, 1%). Six semantic categories: (1) eternal duration of God, (2) everlasting covenant/promises, (3) perpetual cultic ordinances, (4) immemorial past ('of old'), (5) eschatological age to come, (6) permanent judgment/desolation. LXX renders as aion (G165) 287x and aionios (G166) 100x. The extraordinary range of translations (40+ in KJV) confirms context-dependent duration.

    Semantic categories: eternal duration of God, everlasting covenant/promises, perpetual cultic ordinances, immemorial past (of old), eschatological/age to come, permanent judgment/desolation

    Key verses: Psa 90:2, Gen 21:33, Gen 17:7, Exo 12:14, Deu 32:7, Dan 12:2, Isa 34:10, Exo 21:6, Jon 2:6

    Relevance: Primary target of the study. The word's semantic range — from 3 days (Jonah) to genuinely eternal (God) — demonstrates that olam's duration is determined by the nature of its subject.

    H5956 — alam (root verb)¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H5956-alam.md

    Occurrences: 33

    Primitive root verb meaning 'to veil from sight, conceal (literally or figuratively).' 33 occurrences across 15 books. Five semantic categories: (1) unwitting concealment in legal/ritual contexts (Lev 4:13; 5:2-4), (2) deliberate avoidance/looking away (Deu 22:1,3,4), (3) divine or cosmic hiddenness (Job 28:21; Psa 90:8), (4) interpersonal concealment/deception (1Sa 12:3; Psa 26:4), (5) God hiding/withdrawing (Psa 10:1; Lam 3:56). The derivative olam (H5769) inherits this 'concealment' meaning: olam = 'the concealed/vanishing point' in time. Word family: olam (H5769, 439x), elem (H5958, young man = 'one not yet prominent'), almah (H5959, young woman = 'a veiled/private one'), taalummah (H8587, 'a hidden thing').

    Semantic categories: unwitting concealment, deliberate avoidance, divine hiddenness, interpersonal concealment, God hiding

    Key verses: Lev 4:13, Job 28:21, Psa 90:8, Deu 22:1

    Relevance: Establishes the etymological foundation: olam fundamentally means 'concealed/hidden [time],' not 'infinite duration.' The word denotes time whose endpoint is not visible, not time without an endpoint.

    H5957 — alam (Aramaic)¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/A5957-alam.md

    Occurrences: 20

    Aramaic cognate of Hebrew olam (H5769). 15 unique verses (20 tokens) in Daniel (13) and Ezra (2). Four semantic categories: (1) eternal duration of God and His kingdom (Dan 2:20,44; 6:26; 7:18), (2) everlasting kingdom/dominion (Dan 4:3,34; 7:14,27), (3) court formula 'O king, live for ever' (Dan 2:4; 3:9; 6:6,21; Neh 2:3), (4) ancient/past time (Ezr 4:15,19). LXX renders as aion (G165) 12x and aionios (G166) 4x.

    Semantic categories: eternal duration, everlasting kingdom, court formula (non-literal), ancient past

    Key verses: Dan 2:4, Dan 3:9, Dan 6:6, Dan 6:21, Neh 2:3, Dan 7:14

    Relevance: The court formula 'O king, live for ever' (Dan 2:4; 3:9; 5:10; 6:6,21; Neh 2:3) is universally acknowledged as non-literal — no one believes the speakers meant the king would literally live forever. This provides a clear example of olam/alam used in a non-literal, formulaic way.

    H5703 — ad¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H5703-ad.md

    Occurrences: 51

    Masculine noun from H5710 (adah, 'to advance'). 48 unique verses (51 tokens), 12 books. Means 'perpetuity, advance, terminus.' Over 95% rendered 'for ever' or 'and ever.' Dominantly paired with olam in le-olam va-ed ('for ever and ever'). Six categories: (1) doxological formula (Exo 15:18; Psa 45:6), (2) independent duration (Psa 111:3,8), (3) God's nature (Isa 57:15), (4) messianic (Isa 9:6), (5) earthly durability (Prov 12:19), (6) primordial (Hab 3:6). LXX renders as aion (G165) 33x.

    Semantic categories: doxological formula, independent perpetuity, God's nature, messianic, earthly durability, primordial

    Key verses: Exo 15:18, Psa 45:6, Isa 57:15, Hab 3:6

    Relevance: The existence of the combined form le-olam va-ed implies that olam alone is not maximal — you would not add 'and further' to a word that already means 'infinite.' If olam inherently meant 'absolutely eternal,' the intensifier ad would be redundant.

    H5331 — netsach¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H5331-netsach.md

    Occurrences: 44

    Masculine noun from H5329 (natsach, 'to be eminent'). 44 occurrences across 11 books. Translated 'for ever' (21x, 48%), 'perpetual' (3x), 'victory' (1x), 'strength' (1x). Four semantic categories: (1) perpetual duration (dominant, 31 uses), (2) denial of perpetual divine wrath — used in synonymous parallelism with olam (Psa 103:9; Isa 57:16; Jer 3:5; Psa 77:7-8), (3) victory/strength/eminence (1Sa 15:29; 1Ch 29:11), (4) finality/completeness. Co-occurs with olam (H5769) 6x. LXX renders primarily as telos (G5056, 'end') 19x and aion (G165) 10x.

    Semantic categories: perpetual duration, denial of perpetual wrath, victory/strength, finality

    Key verses: Psa 103:9, Isa 57:16, Jer 3:5, Psa 77:7-8, Isa 34:10, 1Sa 15:29

    Relevance: Critical for the netsach-olam synonymous parallelism that DENIES perpetual divine wrath. In Psa 103:9 and Isa 57:16, both 'forever' words are used to say what God will NOT do — He will not maintain anger endlessly. This is theologically decisive for judgment contexts.

    H1860 — deraon¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H1860-draown.md

    Occurrences: 2

    Masculine noun from unused root meaning 'to repulse.' Only 2 OT occurrences: Isa 66:24 ('abhorring') and Dan 12:2 ('contempt'). Denotes an object so repulsive it provokes active recoil. In Isa 66:24, deraon describes the onlookers' reaction to peger (corpses/dead bodies). In Dan 12:2, it forms the construct chain deraon olam ('everlasting contempt').

    Semantic categories: eschatological corpse horror, everlasting contempt

    Key verses: Dan 12:2, Isa 66:24

    Relevance: Critical for Dan 12:2 interpretation. Deraon in its only other occurrence (Isa 66:24) describes the reaction to DEAD BODIES (peger), not living beings suffering. This defines what 'everlasting contempt' means: permanent disgust at the result of destruction, not duration of suffering.

    H8548 — tamid¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H8548-tamid.md

    Occurrences: 105

    Masculine noun from root meaning 'to stretch.' 104 occurrences. Means 'continuance as indefinite extension.' Translated 'continually' (53x, 51%), 'perpetual' (2x, 2%). Primarily a temple/worship term: the continual burnt offering (olat tamid), the showbread, the lamp, the incense. Key distinction from olam: tamid emphasizes regular, uninterrupted recurrence; olam emphasizes limitless duration. The daily sacrifice (tamid) is not eternal (olam) but regular and uninterrupted (tamid).

    Semantic categories: continual burnt offering, sanctuary elements, Daniel's 'daily', continual devotion, persistent states

    Key verses: Exo 29:42, Num 28:3, Dan 8:11, Psa 16:8

    Relevance: Semantic comparison helps define olam's distinctiveness. Tamid = unbroken recurrence (the twice-daily sacrifice). Olam = limitless duration. The daily sacrifice was called tamid (continual) but not olam (everlasting) — they are semantically distinct. Both describe something that eventually ceased.

    H1285 — berith¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H1285-beriyth.md

    Occurrences: 317

    Feminine noun meaning 'covenant, compact' (from root meaning 'to cut'). 284 occurrences in 26 books. The dominant OT word for covenant. Co-occurs with olam (H5769) 29x in the formula berith olam ('everlasting covenant'). Seven semantic categories: patriarchal, Sinai/Mosaic, new/everlasting, treaty/league, special types (salt, peace), covenant breaking, marriage. LXX renders as diatheke (G1242) 246x.

    Semantic categories: patriarchal covenant, Sinai/Mosaic covenant, new/everlasting covenant, treaty/league, covenant breaking

    Key verses: Gen 17:7, Gen 17:13, Exo 24:7-8, Jer 31:31, Jer 32:40

    Relevance: Berith olam ('everlasting covenant') appears with multiple subjects, some of which have ended (circumcision covenant, Gen 17:13 → Gal 5:2-6). Demonstrates that olam in covenantal contexts does not always mean 'unconditionally eternal.'

    G165 — aion¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G165-aion.md

    Occurrences: 120

    Masculine noun meaning 'an age, perpetuity, the world.' 95 unique verses (120 tokens), 22 books. From G104 aei ('always'). Seven semantic categories: (1) duration/perpetuity (eis ton aiona, 'for ever'), (2) intensified perpetuity (eis tous aionas ton aionon, 'for ever and ever'), (3) this present age/world, (4) age to come, (5) time-span/epochs ('since the world began'), (6) created order/universe, (7) end of the age. LXX renders Hebrew olam (H5769) as aion 287x, establishing a near one-to-one bridge.

    Semantic categories: duration/perpetuity, intensified perpetuity, this present age, age to come, time-span/epochs, created order, end of the age

    Key verses: Jhn 6:51, Heb 5:6, Rev 14:11, Mat 13:22, Luk 1:70, Heb 1:2

    Relevance: Primary LXX equivalent of olam (287x). The NT inherits olam's semantic range through aion. The word 'ages' (plural) and 'this age' demonstrate that aion means a period of time, not inherently 'eternity.'

    G166 — aionios¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G166-aionios.md

    Occurrences: 71

    Adjective derived from aion (G165). 71 tokens in 69 unique verses, 19 books. Means 'pertaining to an age, age-lasting.' Translated 'eternal' (32x, 46%) and 'everlasting' (21x, 30%). Dominant collocation: zoe aionios ('eternal life,' ~43x, 62%). Applied to judgment ~7 times (~10%). Past-time uses: Rom 16:25 ('since the world began'), 2Ti 1:9 ('before the world began'), Tit 1:2 (both past and future in same verse). Hebrew source: olam (H5769) 100x with PMI 26.16.

    Semantic categories: eternal life, eternal judgment/punishment, eternal attributes of God, eternal heavenly realities, eternal purpose/pre-temporal, eternal kingdom

    Key verses: Jhn 3:16, Mat 25:46, 2Th 1:9, Jude 1:7, Rom 16:25, 2Ti 1:9, Tit 1:2

    Relevance: LXX translates olam as aionios 100x. Aionios inherits olam's full semantic range including backward-looking uses. If aionios inherently meant 'infinite future,' its past-time uses would be impossible. The existence of aidios (G126, 'strictly everduring') as a separate word shows aionios is not inherently 'eternal.'

    G126 — aidios¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G126-aidios.md

    Occurrences: 2

    Adjective from G104 aei ('always'). Only 2 NT occurrences: Rom 1:20 ('eternal power') and Jude 1:6 ('everlasting chains'). Means 'everduring, without beginning or end, inherently eternal.' Lexicon explicitly distinguishes it from aionios (G166): aidios denotes intrinsic, absolute eternality; aionios can denote age-long duration.

    Semantic categories: divine attributes, eschatological judgment

    Key verses: Rom 1:20, Jude 1:6

    Relevance: The existence of aidios as a separate 'truly eternal' word alongside aionios shows that aionios (olam's heir) does not inherently mean 'eternal without end.' In Jude 6-7, the same author uses aidios for angelic chains (v.6) but aionios for Sodom's fire (v.7) — two different words in adjacent verses, suggesting different semantic force.

    H6783 — tsemithuth¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H6783-tsemîythuth.md

    Occurrences: 2

    Feminine noun from H6789 (tsamath, 'to extirpate'). Only 2 occurrences, both in Lev 25 (Jubilee legislation): Lev 25:23 ('the land shall not be sold for ever') and Lev 25:30 (house in walled city 'established for ever'). Unlike olam (hidden/concealed time), tsemithuth derives from 'cutting off' — permanence through irreversible severance. Translated 'for ever' 100% of the time.

    Semantic categories: permanent land alienation, extinction/severance permanence

    Key verses: Lev 25:23, Lev 25:30

    Relevance: Shows Hebrew has multiple 'forever' words with different nuances. Olam = time beyond perception; tsemithuth = permanence through irreversible cutting. The existence of this specialized word further demonstrates that olam is not a simple synonym for 'eternal.'

    H6297 — peger¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H6297-peger.md

    Occurrences: 24

    Masculine noun meaning 'corpse, carcass, dead body.' 24 occurrences across 11 books. Four semantic categories: battlefield dead, divine judgment on Israel, prophetic judgment imagery, idolatrous contamination. Co-occurs with deraon (H1860) in Isa 66:24 — the objects of deraon are dead bodies.

    Semantic categories: battlefield dead, divine judgment, prophetic imagery, idolatrous contamination

    Key verses: Isa 66:24, Isa 34:3, Num 14:29

    Relevance: Critical for Dan 12:2 interpretation. In Isa 66:24, deraon describes the reaction to peger (corpses). The objects of 'everlasting contempt' (deraon olam) are dead bodies, not living conscious beings.

    H6 — abad¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H6-abad.md

    Occurrences: 219

    Primitive root verb meaning 'to wander away, lose oneself; to perish, destroy.' 174 unique OT verses (219 tokens), 25 books. Qal (intransitive, 'perish') 61%, Piel/Hiphil (causative, 'destroy') 39%. LXX renders as apollymi (G622) 141x — the dominant OT-NT bridge for 'perish' language (John 3:16). UBS domain: Non-Exist (002002002006).

    Semantic categories: physical loss, individual death, covenant-curse, divine destruction, fate of the wicked, loss of abstract qualities

    Key verses: Psa 37:20, Psa 146:4, Deu 4:26, Jhn 3:16

    Relevance: Destruction vocabulary establishing what olam-modified punishment produces: cessation/perishing, not ongoing torment. Core semantic is loss/non-existence.

    H3615 — kalah¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H3615-kalah.md

    Occurrences: 258

    Primitive root verb meaning 'to end, consume, finish.' 199 unique verses (258 tokens). Piel (transitive, 'consume/destroy') dominates. Used for divine judgment consumption (Exo 32:10; Lam 3:22) and completion of wrath (Eze 5:13). LXX renders as synteleo (G4931) 85x.

    Semantic categories: completion of task, finishing speaking, divine judgment/consumption, wrath accomplished, eyes failing, resources spent

    Key verses: Exo 32:10, Lam 3:22, Eze 5:13

    Relevance: Destruction vocabulary: divine wrath is 'accomplished/completed' (kalah), not ongoing. Wrath has a terminus.

    H7843 — shachath¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H7843-shachath.md

    Occurrences: 164

    Primitive root verb meaning 'to decay, ruin, corrupt, destroy.' 136 unique verses, 25 books. Hiphil (causative, 'destroy') 98 tokens, Piel ('corrupt') 39 tokens. Used for moral corruption (Gen 6:11-12), physical destruction (Gen 19:13-14), ruining objects (Jer 13:7), and divine restraint from destruction (Deu 4:31). LXX renders as diaphtheiro (G1311) 48x.

    Semantic categories: moral corruption, physical destruction, ruining objects, divine restraint, destroyer as agent, self-destruction

    Key verses: Gen 6:11-12, Gen 19:13-14

    Relevance: Destruction vocabulary: ruin/corruption. Not torment vocabulary.

    H8045 — shamad¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H8045-shamad.md

    Occurrences: 101

    Primitive root verb meaning 'to desolate, exterminate.' 86 unique OT verses (92 tokens). Nearly exclusively Hiphil (causative, 'destroy utterly') and Niphal (passive, 'be destroyed'). Total annihilation — not damage but extermination to the point of no remnant. Concentrated in Deuteronomic corpus (56%). LXX renders as apollymi (G622) 23x. UBS domain: Non-Exist (002002002006).

    Semantic categories: exterminating Canaanites, covenant curse, dynastic destruction, destroying idolatry, extermination plots, eschatological destruction

    Key verses: Deu 4:26, Psa 145:20, Deu 28:63

    Relevance: Destruction vocabulary: total annihilation/extermination. 'All the wicked will he destroy' (Psa 145:20). Same UBS domain as abad (Non-Exist).
