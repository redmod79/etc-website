    Conclusion: Judgment Parables and Associated Imagery¶

    Master ID¶

    (blank — to be assigned post-study)

    Question¶

    What do Jesus' judgment parables and associated imagery teach about the fate of the wicked? Focus on: Matt 13:24-30,36-43 (wheat & tares / furnace of fire), Matt 13:47-50 (dragnet), 'outer darkness' (Matt 8:12; 22:13; 25:30), 'weeping and gnashing of teeth' (7 occurrences), and the fire/furnace vocabulary.

    Summary Answer¶

    Jesus' judgment parables consistently use destruction, consumption, and exclusion vocabulary for the fate of the wicked. The decisive verb in the tares parable is katakaio (G2618, "burn completely"), which describes total consumption in all 12 NT occurrences. The furnace image (kaminos G2575) inherits three OT furnace traditions, all of which describe furnaces that destroy their contents. The gnashing formula (brygmos + klauthmos) describes grief and rage at the moment of exclusion, not ongoing torment — the OT background (charaq H2786) is hostile rage in all 5 uses, and Luke 13:28 identifies seeing exclusion as the trigger. The basanizo word family (torment) is entirely absent from every passage in this study.

    Key Verses¶

    Tares Parable Interpretation (Matt 13:40-42) As therefore the tares are gathered and burned in the fire; so shall it be in the end of this world. The Son of man shall send forth his angels, and they shall gather out of his kingdom all things that offend, and them which do iniquity; And shall cast them into a furnace of fire: there shall be wailing and gnashing of teeth.

    Gnashing Trigger Identified (Luke 13:28) There shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth, when ye shall see Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob, and all the prophets, in the kingdom of God, and you yourselves thrust out.

    Destroy Soul and Body (Matt 10:28) And fear not them which kill the body, but are not able to kill the soul: but rather fear him which is able to destroy both soul and body in hell.

    Furnace Burns to Ashes (Mal 4:1,3) For, behold, the day cometh, that shall burn as an oven; and all the proud, yea, and all that do wickedly, shall be stubble: and the day that cometh shall burn them up, saith the LORD of hosts, that it shall leave them neither root nor branch... And ye shall tread down the wicked; for they shall be ashes under the soles of your feet.

    Gnashing Then Cessation (Psa 112:10) The wicked shall see it, and be grieved; he shall gnash with his teeth, and melt away: the desire of the wicked shall perish.

    OT Gnashing = Rage (Acts 7:54) When they heard these things, they were cut to the heart, and they gnashed on him with their teeth.

    Outer Darkness = Exclusion (Matt 8:11-12) And I say unto you, That many shall come from the east and west, and shall sit down with Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob, in the kingdom of heaven. But the children of the kingdom shall be cast out into outer darkness: there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth.

    Sodom Paradigm (2 Pet 2:6) And turning the cities of Sodom and Gomorrha into ashes condemned them with an overthrow, making them an ensample unto those that after should live ungodly.

    Everlasting Punishment (Matt 25:46) And these shall go away into everlasting punishment: but the righteous into life eternal.

    Vocabulary Differentiation (Rev 20:9-10) And fire came down from God out of heaven, and devoured them. And the devil that deceived them was cast into the lake of fire and brimstone, where the beast and the false prophet are, and shall be tormented day and night for ever and ever.

    Analysis¶

    1. katakaio (G2618) — Jesus' Interpretive Verb for the Fate of the Tares¶

    The parable of the wheat and tares (Matt 13:24-30) and Jesus' self-interpretation (Matt 13:36-43) form the primary passage in this study. The householder sows good seed; an enemy sows tares. At harvest, the tares are gathered and burned (katakaio, v.30). Jesus then decodes the parable with six copular identification formulas (vv.37-39): the sower = the Son of Man, the field = the world, the good seed = sons of the kingdom, the tares = sons of the wicked one, the enemy = the devil, the harvest = the end of the age, the reapers = the angels. After this decoding, v.40 states: "As therefore the tares are gathered and burned (katakaio) in the fire, so shall it be in the end of this age."

    katakaio (G2618) is a compound verb (kata + kaio) meaning "burn down completely, consume wholly." The kata- prefix intensifies the base verb kaio ("burn") to completion — the same prefix pattern found in katesthio ("devour completely," kata + esthio), katapino ("swallow up," kata + pino), and katalyo ("destroy utterly," kata + lyo). All 12 NT occurrences of katakaio describe total consumption: chaff burned up (Matt 3:12), tares burned (Matt 13:30,40), magic books burned (Acts 19:19), works burned (1 Cor 3:15), animal carcasses burned outside the camp (Heb 13:11), earth burned up (2 Pet 3:10), trees burned up (Rev 8:7), and Babylon utterly burned (Rev 18:8). Not one occurrence describes ongoing burning without consumption.

    The self-interpretation pattern (per the grammar reference on self-interpretation-literal-correction) elevates the passage from purely parabolic to semi-didactic. Jesus decoded the symbols but retained the simile structure in v.40 ("as...so"). The verb katakaio carries its full semantic weight — total consumption — even within the simile. John the Baptist uses the same verb for the same image in Matt 3:12 ("he will burn up the chaff with unquenchable fire"), establishing cross-speaker consistency within the same Gospel.

    The primary Hebrew source for katakaio is saraph (H8313, "to burn"), with 63 LXX co-occurrences. The OT-to-NT translation continuity means that when Jesus uses katakaio, the word carries the Hebrew concept of fire-as-consumption.

    2. kaminos (G2575) — The Furnace and Its OT Background¶

    The phrase "the furnace of fire" (ten kaminon tou pyros) appears only in Matt 13:42 and 13:50. kaminos (G2575, "furnace") has 4 NT occurrences total: the two judgment passages plus Rev 1:15 (simile for intense heat) and Rev 9:2 (simile for smoke volume).

    Three OT furnace traditions feed into the NT kaminos via the LXX:

    attuwn (H861, Aramaic): Nebuchadnezzar's execution furnace in Daniel 3. The LXX maps attuwn univocally to kaminos. The narrative reports that the furnace killed the soldiers who merely approached it (Dan 3:22). The three Hebrews survived only through divine intervention (the fourth figure in the fire, 3:25). Default result = death. The furnace is an instrument of execution, not a confinement facility.

    kuwr (H3564): The smelting furnace of Ezekiel 22:18-22. Israel is compared to dross (sig H5509) in God's smelting furnace. The verb nathak (H5413, "melt/pour") appears four times in vv.20-22. The smelting furnace consumes the dross and extracts the metal. The worthless material is destroyed, not preserved.

    tannuwr (H8574): The oven/furnace of Malachi 4:1. "The day cometh, that shall burn as an oven; and all the proud, yea, and all that do wickedly, shall be stubble: and the day that cometh shall burn them up, saith the LORD of hosts, that it shall leave them neither root nor branch." The result: "they shall be ashes under the soles of your feet" (Mal 4:3). Total consumption to ashes.

    Every OT furnace tradition describes a device that destroys its contents. No OT furnace tradition describes a device that preserves its contents in ongoing suffering.

    3. The Gnashing Formula — Grief and Rage at the Moment of Judgment¶

    "Weeping and gnashing of teeth" (klauthmos kai brygmos ton odonton) appears in 7 NT verses (Matt 8:12; 13:42; 13:50; 22:13; 24:51; 25:30; Luke 13:28). The formula pairs with three different judgment images: furnace of fire (13:42, 13:50), outer darkness (8:12; 22:13; 25:30), and cutting asunder (24:51). This variety shows the formula describes the RESPONSE to judgment, not a specific punishment.

    brygmos (G1030, "gnashing") derives from brycho (G1031), which maps 1:1 to Hebrew charaq (H2786) in the LXX. All 5 OT charaq uses express hostile rage directed at a target: - Job 16:9 — enemy gnashes in wrath at Job - Psa 35:16 — mockers gnash at David in malice - Psa 37:12 — wicked gnashes at the righteous (habitual action, participial form) - Psa 112:10 — wicked gnashes and melts away; desire perishes - Lam 2:16 — enemies gnash in triumph over Jerusalem

    Acts 7:54 provides the sole NT use of brycho: the Sanhedrin "gnashed on [Stephen] with their teeth" in murderous fury (imperfect tense = sustained rage). They proceeded to stone him. This is not pain — it is hostile fury directed at a target.

    klauthmos (G2805, "weeping/wailing") has 9 NT occurrences: 7 in the judgment formula, 2 outside it. The non-formulaic uses confirm its semantic range: Matt 2:18 (Rachel weeping for her dead children — grief at irreversible loss) and Acts 20:37 (Ephesian elders weeping at Paul's departure — grief at parting). klauthmos expresses grief.

    Combined: weeping (grief at loss) + gnashing (rage at exclusion) = the emotional response at the moment of judgment.

    Psa 112:10 is the critical OT verse linking gnashing to cessation. The wicked sees the righteous prosper, is angry (kaas), gnashes teeth (charaq), and then MELTS AWAY (masas H4549, Niphal). The sequence — rage THEN cessation — maps directly onto the NT judgment formula: gnashing at the moment of judgment, then destruction.

    Luke 13:28 is the interpretive key. "There shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth, WHEN YE SHALL SEE Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and all the prophets, in the kingdom of God, and you yourselves thrust out." The temporal clause hotan opsesthe ("when ye shall see") identifies the gnashing as a RESPONSE to a specific trigger: seeing others inside the kingdom while being expelled outside. This is a one-time judgment-event description.

    4. Outer Darkness — Exclusion from the Kingdom Feast¶

    The phrase "outer darkness" (to skotos to exoteron) appears in 3 verses (Matt 8:12; 22:13; 25:30), all in Matthew. The word exoteros (G1857) occurs ONLY in these three verses in the entire NT. The double-article construction (to skotos to exoteron) with the comparative adjective marks "THE outer darkness" as a definite, particular destination.

    All three outer-darkness passages share a common pattern: 1. A feast/celebration context — sitting with Abraham (8:11), wedding feast (22:2), entering the joy of the lord (25:21,23) 2. An exclusion verb — ekballo (G1544, "cast out") 3. The definite destination — to skotos to exoteron 4. The consequence formula — klauthmos kai brygmos ton odonton

    The outer-darkness image contains NO fire vocabulary, NO torment vocabulary, and NO duration language. It describes exclusion from the lit feast — being outside the celebration, in the dark.

    Matt 8:12 is the only non-parabolic outer-darkness passage. Jesus makes a direct prophetic statement (no "the kingdom of heaven is like" formula) about unbelieving Israel being cast out while Gentiles sit with the patriarchs. Matt 22:13 and 25:30 are parabolic.

    The outer-darkness passages do not specify what happens to the excluded person after the exclusion. The image describes the ACT of exclusion and the EMOTIONAL response, but not the subsequent state or duration. This makes the outer-darkness passages functionally neutral on the question of whether the excluded person continues to exist or ceases to exist.

    5. The Fire/Darkness Tension — Both Images Are Figurative¶

    Matthew uses two image sets for the same eschatological fate: - FURNACE SET: Matt 13:42,50 — "cast into the furnace of fire" (bright light, consuming heat) - DARKNESS SET: Matt 8:12; 22:13; 25:30 — "cast into outer darkness" (no light)

    Both sets include "weeping and gnashing of teeth." Both appear in the same Gospel, same author, same eschatological framework. A literal furnace of fire produces brilliant light. Literal outer darkness has no light. The same fate cannot be simultaneously a blazing furnace and total darkness.

    The tension has three possible resolutions: 1. They describe different fates — but both describe the fate of those excluded from the kingdom, and the gnashing formula links them. 2. They describe different aspects of one literal place — but a place cannot be simultaneously a blazing furnace and total darkness. 3. Both are figurative — furnace representing destruction/consumption (consistent with katakaio and all OT furnace traditions), darkness representing exclusion from God's light/kingdom (consistent with the feast-exclusion context).

    The third option is the only reading that harmonizes both image sets within the same author. The figurative reading is further supported by Matt 22:13, which adds "bind him hand and foot" — binding imagery that makes sense for preventing re-entry after exclusion but would be redundant if the person were being consumed by fire.

    6. Matthew's Same-Author Judgment Vocabulary¶

    Matthew's judgment vocabulary across all eschatological passages forms a consistent pattern:

    Destruction/consumption verbs: katakaio (G2618, Matt 3:12; 13:30,40), apollymi (G622, Matt 10:28; 21:41; 22:7), empretho (G1714, Matt 22:7), dichotomeo (G1371, Matt 24:51)

    Exclusion verbs: ekballo (G1544, Matt 8:12; 22:13; 25:30), ballo (G906, Matt 13:42,50; 5:29-30)

    Absent from Matthew's eschatological judgment vocabulary: basanizo (G928, 0 uses for human eschatological subjects), basanismos (G929, 0 uses), basanos (G931, 0 eschatological uses), basanistes (G930, only Matt 18:34 in a debt parable with an implied endpoint "until he should pay")

    The same author who writes the furnace and darkness parables also writes Matt 10:28 ("destroy both soul and body in hell"), 21:41 ("miserably destroy those wicked men"), and 22:7 ("destroyed those murderers and burned up their city"). The verb apollymi in judgment contexts with divine/authority subject consistently means actual destruction throughout Matthew's Gospel.

    7. Matt 25:46 — Everlasting Punishment and the Hebrews Pattern¶

    The grammatical parallel between kolasin aionion ("everlasting punishment") and zoen aionion ("everlasting life") in Matt 25:46 is real. Both use aionios (G166) in accusative singular feminine modifying comparable abstract nouns. Both are governed by the same verb (aperchomai G565).

    Three observations bear on this passage:

    First, Jesus chose kolasis (G2851, "punishment," from kolazo G2849 "to curtail, dock, prune") — NOT basanismos (G929, "torment"). kolasis has only 2 NT uses (Matt 25:46; 1 John 4:18). This is punishment vocabulary, not torment vocabulary.

    Second, the Hebrews pattern shows that aionios + result-noun = permanent result of completed act: "eternal redemption" (Heb 9:12, obtained once, result permanent), "eternal judgment" (Heb 6:2, rendered once, verdict permanent), "eternal destruction" (2 Thess 1:9, destroyed once, result permanent). If "eternal punishment" follows this pattern, it means punishment inflicted once with permanent result.

    Third, Tit 1:2 uses aionios twice in one verse with different temporal scopes ("eternal life" and "before age-long times"), demonstrating that the same adjective does not require the same duration in every occurrence.

    8. The Sodom Paradigm — "Eternal Fire" That Produced Ashes¶

    Jude 1:7 says Sodom and Gomorrah suffer "the vengeance of eternal fire" (pur aionion). 2 Pet 2:6 says the same cities were "turned to ashes" (tephroo G5077) and set as an "example" (hupodeigma G5262) for the ungodly. Jesus in Luke 17:29 says the fire "destroyed them all" (apollymi G622, aorist).

    The paradigm case of "eternal fire" produced ashes. The fire ended; the results are permanent. If the example/paradigm for "eternal fire" is cities reduced to ashes, then "everlasting fire" (Matt 18:8; 25:41) describes fire whose results are permanent, not fire that burns endlessly.

    9. OT Cessation Similes — The Consistent Biblical Pattern¶

    The OT compares the fate of the wicked to chaff (Psa 1:4; 35:5; Isa 5:24; 17:13; Hos 13:3), stubble (Mal 4:1; Isa 5:24; 47:14; Psa 83:13), smoke (Psa 37:20; 68:2; Hos 13:3), wax (Psa 68:2; 112:10), ashes (Mal 4:3; 2 Pet 2:6), morning cloud (Hos 13:3), early dew (Hos 13:3), dust (Isa 5:24), and fat on the altar (Psa 37:20). Every one of these substances ceases to exist. No biblical cessation simile depicts something continuing in a ruined or damaged state. The simile inventory is unanimously cessation-oriented.

    10. Vocabulary Differentiation — Destruction Words vs. Torment Words¶

    Rev 20:9-10 provides the most concentrated example: humans are devoured (katesthio G2719, v.9); the devil, beast, and false prophet are tormented (basanizo G928, v.10). Different verbs for different subjects in consecutive verses by the same author.

    This differentiation extends across the entire biblical corpus studied. The judgment parables (tares, dragnet, sheep/goats, talents, wedding feast) use destruction/exclusion vocabulary for human subjects (katakaio, apollymi, ballo, ekballo, dichotomeo). basanizo/basanismos for human eschatological judgment appears only in Revelation's apocalyptic framework. Jesus never uses basanizo or basanismos in his teaching about the fate of the wicked.

    Word Studies¶

    katakaio (G2618): 12 NT occurrences, all total consumption. Kata- prefix = completion. Primary Hebrew source: saraph (H8313). Jesus' chosen verb for the tares' fate.

    kaminos (G2575): 4 NT occurrences. Three OT furnace traditions all describe destruction. LXX maps from attuwn (execution), kuwr (smelting), tannuwr (baking/burning).

    brygmos (G1030): 7 NT occurrences, all in the judgment formula. Derives from brycho (G1031), which maps to charaq (H2786). All OT uses = hostile rage.

    klauthmos (G2805): 9 NT occurrences. Non-formulaic = grief at loss (Matt 2:18, Acts 20:37).

    exoteros (G1857): 3 NT occurrences, all "outer darkness" in Matthew. Exclusion from kingdom feast.

    kolasis (G2851): 2 NT occurrences (Matt 25:46; 1 John 4:18). From kolazo ("to curtail"). Punishment vocabulary, not torment vocabulary.

    basanizo (G928): 12 NT occurrences. Zero in any judgment parable, outer-darkness passage, gnashing-formula passage, or Matthean gehenna passage. Genre-restricted to Revelation's apocalyptic framework for eschatological judgment.

    charaq (H2786): 5 OT occurrences, all hostile rage. Maps to brycho (G1031) in LXX.

    Difficult Passages¶

    Matt 25:46 — The Symmetry Argument¶

    The kolasin aionion // zoen aionion parallel is real. If "everlasting life" is life without end, then "everlasting punishment" appears to require punishment without end, which implies ongoing conscious existence.

    Three textual factors complicate this reading: (1) kolasis is punishment vocabulary, not torment vocabulary; (2) the Hebrews pattern shows aionios + result-noun = permanent result of completed act; (3) Tit 1:2 uses aionios twice with different temporal scopes in one verse. The symmetry is grammatical (both use the same adjective) but the Hebrews pattern and the kolasis etymology suggest the punishment is permanent in result, not perpetual in process.

    Outer Darkness — Does It Imply Ongoing Existence?¶

    The outer-darkness passages describe exclusion from the kingdom feast with an emotional response (weeping and gnashing). They do not specify what happens after the exclusion. The image is compatible with both ongoing existence in darkness and cessation after exclusion. Taken alone, the outer-darkness passages are neutral on duration.

    Matt 22:13 — Binding Imagery¶

    "Bind him hand and foot" adds a detail absent from the other outer-darkness passages. Binding implies ongoing existence (you cannot bind something that has ceased to exist). However, the binding occurs within a parabolic narrative (Gate 1 and Gate 3 both fail), reducing its evidential weight for establishing doctrine about the duration of post-judgment existence.

    The Gnashing Formula — Momentary or Ongoing?¶

    The gnashing formula appears 7 times. Luke 13:28 identifies the trigger as a one-time event (seeing exclusion). The OT background supports a momentary rage response. However, the formula uses ekei estai ("there shall be") which describes what characterizes a place, not just what happens once. The question is whether "there shall be weeping and gnashing" means "at that place, weeping and gnashing will occur" (momentary) or "at that place, weeping and gnashing are the ongoing condition" (perpetual). The text does not resolve this. The OT parallel in Psa 112:10 (gnashing then melting away) supports the momentary reading.

    I-B Resolution: I4 — "Everlasting Punishment" as Permanent Result vs. Ongoing Process¶

    Step 1 — Tension: - FOR (permanent result): E28 uses kolasis (punishment from kolazo "to curtail"), not basanismos (torment). Tit 1:2 uses aionios twice with different scopes in one verse. - AGAINST (ongoing process): E28 parallel structure — kolasin aionion // zoen aionion — same adjective, same form, same grammatical position. If "eternal life" means life without end, "eternal punishment" appears to mean punishment without end.

    What CAN Be Said (Scripture explicitly states or necessarily implies)¶

    • katakaio (G2618) means "burn completely/consume" in all 12 NT occurrences. This is the verb Jesus uses for the fate of the tares in both the parable and his interpretation (Matt 13:30,40).
    • All three OT furnace traditions (attuwn, kuwr, tannuwr) describe furnaces that destroy their contents (Dan 3:22; Ezk 22:20-22; Mal 4:1-3).
    • All 5 OT gnashing passages (charaq H2786) express hostile rage directed at a target. The sole NT use of the cognate verb brycho (Acts 7:54) confirms murderous fury.
    • Luke 13:28 identifies the trigger of gnashing as seeing exclusion from the kingdom ("when ye shall see Abraham, Isaac, Jacob... in the kingdom, and you yourselves thrust out").
    • The outer-darkness image appears only in feast/banquet contexts and describes exclusion from a lit celebration.
    • A literal furnace of fire and literal outer darkness contradict each other in the same author — one produces light, the other is absence of light.
    • Matthew's judgment parables use zero basanizo-family words for human eschatological subjects.
    • kolasis (G2851, Matt 25:46) is punishment vocabulary (from kolazo "to curtail"), not torment vocabulary (which would be basanismos G929).
    • Rev 20:9 uses katesthio (devour) for humans; Rev 20:10 uses basanizo (torment) for the devil/beast/false prophet. Different verbs for different subjects.
    • Malachi 4:1-3 states the wicked are burned up with neither root nor branch remaining; their final state is ashes under the righteous' feet.
    • Multiple OT passages state the wicked "shall not be" (ayin, Psa 37:10), "be no more" (ayin, Psa 104:35), "as though they had not been" (Obad 1:16).
    • The subjects of the unquenchable fire and undying worm in Isa 66:24 are peger (corpses/dead bodies), not living persons.
    • The paradigm case of "eternal fire" (Jude 1:7) produced ashes (2 Pet 2:6, tephroo). Jesus interprets Sodom's fire: "destroyed them all" (apollymi, Luke 17:29).
    • Every biblical cessation simile for the wicked (chaff, stubble, smoke, wax, ashes, dust, morning cloud, dew) describes a substance that ceases to exist.

    What CANNOT Be Said (not explicitly stated or necessarily implied by Scripture)¶

    • Scripture does not explicitly state that the gnashing formula describes an ongoing eternal state. The text describes an emotional response; the duration is not specified.
    • Scripture does not explicitly state what happens to the excluded person after being cast into outer darkness. The image describes the exclusion event and the emotional response, not the subsequent duration or state.
    • Scripture does not explicitly state that the furnace of fire preserves its contents. All OT furnace traditions describe destruction, and the verb katakaio means total consumption.
    • Scripture does not explicitly state that the torment of the devil/beast/false prophet (Rev 20:10) applies to human beings. The same passage uses devour (katesthio) for humans and torment (basanizo) for non-human entities.
    • Scripture does not explicitly state that the wicked possess inherent immortality enabling them to survive eternally in fire. No passage in this study asserts that human souls are naturally indestructible.
    • Scripture does not explicitly state that "everlasting punishment" (Matt 25:46) means ongoing conscious punishing. The word is kolasis (punishment/curtailment), and the Hebrews pattern of aionios + result-noun yields "permanent result of completed act."
    • Scripture does not explicitly state that "everlasting punishment" (Matt 25:46) means permanent cessation. The grammatical parallel with "everlasting life" is real, and the nature of the punishment (process vs. result) requires interpretation.

    Conclusion¶

    The judgment parables and associated imagery in this study yield 39 explicit statements, 9 necessary implications, and 8 inferences.

    At the E-tier: 13 items use destruction, cessation, or non-existence vocabulary applied to the wicked in didactic, prophetic, or hymnic genres with human subjects that passed all four validation gates. These include apollymi for soul and body in gehenna (Matt 10:28), fire judgment leaving neither root nor branch with ashes as the result (Mal 4:1,3), non-existence language (ayin — Psa 37:10; 104:35; Obad 1:16), cessation similes with destruction verbs (Psa 37:20; 68:2; 112:10), corpse-subjects for the unquenchable fire (Isa 66:24), fire devouring adversaries (Heb 10:27), and everlasting destruction (2 Thess 1:9). Zero E-tier items support the ECT position — the Tree 3 validation gates (subject, grammar, genre, harmony) reclassified every ECT-candidate E-item to Neutral.

    At the N-tier: 9 items document lexical facts, vocabulary patterns, and textual observations that both sides accept. These include the universal consumption meaning of katakaio, the hostile-rage meaning of OT gnashing, the destruction function of all OT furnaces, the absence of basanizo from Matthew's judgment vocabulary, and the vocabulary differentiation at Rev 20:9-10.

    At the I-tier: The conditionalist reading is classified I-A (evidence-extending, derived from E/N items, aligns with E/N, requires only systematization). The ECT reading is classified I-D (counter-evidence external) on three counts: (1) extending Rev 20:10 torment from non-human to human subjects overrides the text's vocabulary differentiation; (2) positing ongoing conscious existence in outer darkness requires adding duration and consciousness concepts the text does not state; (3) reading the furnace as preserving contents overrides all OT furnace backgrounds and the katakaio verb meaning. The one I-B item (Matt 25:46 symmetry) resolves Moderate toward Cond. based on the Hebrews aionios pattern, the kolasis vocabulary, and the Tit 1:2 datum.
