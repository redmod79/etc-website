    Bible Study: What do Jesus' judgment parables and associated imagery teach about the fate of the wicked? Focus on: Matt 13:24-30,36-43 (wheat & tares / furnace of fire), Matt 13:47-50 (dragnet), 'outer darkness' (Matt 8:12; 22:13; 25:30), 'weeping and gnashing of teeth' (7 occurrences), and the fire/furnace vocabulary.¶

    Question¶

    What do Jesus' judgment parables and associated imagery teach about the fate of the wicked? Focus on: Matt 13:24-30,36-43 (wheat & tares / furnace of fire), Matt 13:47-50 (dragnet), 'outer darkness' (Matt 8:12; 22:13; 25:30), 'weeping and gnashing of teeth' (7 occurrences), and the fire/furnace vocabulary.

    Prior Research Summary¶

    Nine prior etc6/etc7 studies directly relevant. etc6-20 studied the same question under earlier methodology: katakaio (G2618) = 'burn completely' (all 12 NT uses = total consumption); kaminos (G2575) traces to three OT furnace traditions all describing destruction; outer darkness = exclusion from kingdom feast (never equated with hell/gehenna/lake of fire); gnashing = OT rage/hostility (5 passages + Acts 7:54); all judgment parables use destruction/exclusion vocabulary with zero basanizo/basanismos. etc7-20 (Matt 10:28) confirmed same-author apollymi = actual destruction in judgment contexts. etc7-05 (four hell words) showed gehenna never collocates with basanizo. etc7-06 (destruction vocabulary) cataloged 7 destruction words with shared cessation core and cessation similes (chaff, wax, smoke, ashes, stubble). etc7-14 (judgment passages) found 0 of 8 major judgment passages use basanizo for humans; kolasis (G2851) = punishment not torment. etc7-08 (aionios) showed aionios does not inherently mean 'eternal/endless' and the Hebrews pattern governs 'eternal punishment.' etc7-13 (lake of fire) found vocabulary differentiation: humans = devoured/death; non-human = tormented. etc7-11 (smoke ascending) established smoke formula = permanent memorial of completed destruction via Isa 34:10 source. Gaps: katakaio needs etc7 classifier treatment; outer darkness needs I-B resolution; darkness/fire tension unresolved; Matt 13:36-43 genre implications for Tree 3; 7 gnashing occurrences need systematic per-occurrence classification; Dan 3 furnace parallel needs deeper analysis.

    Focus Areas¶

    1. katakaio (G2618) in the Tares Parable — Jesus' Interpretive Verb: This is THE decisive linguistic evidence in the study. READ $WORD_STUDIES/G2618-katakaio.md for the canonical word study — it should cover all 12 NT occurrences. The research agent must run greek_parser.py --verse on Matt 13:30 and Matt 13:40 to verify morphology. The kata- prefix pattern (kata + base verb = intensification to completion) parallels katesthio (kata+esthio = devour completely), katapino (kata+pino = swallow up), katalyo (kata+lyo = destroy utterly). Every other NT katakaio occurrence (Matt 3:12 chaff; 1 Cor 3:15 works burned; Heb 13:11 animal carcasses; 2 Pet 3:10 earth; Rev 8:7 trees; Rev 18:8 Babylon) describes total consumption with nothing remaining. Check the LXX translation relationship via TR-saraph-katakaio.md. Also run cross_testament_parallels_v2.py --hybrid-nt on Matt 13:40 to find NT parallels to Jesus' interpretation. Verses: Matt 13:30, Matt 13:40, Matt 3:12, 1 Cor 3:15, Heb 13:11, 2 Pet 3:10, Rev 8:7, Rev 18:8 Strong's: G2618, G2545

    2. kaminos (G2575) — OT Furnace Traditions and Their Implications: The furnace image has three OT sources, each with distinct implications. (1) kuwr H3564 (Ezk 22:18-22; Isa 48:10; Deut 4:20) — the smelting/refining furnace that consumes dross and leaves purified metal. Run hebrew_parser.py --verse on Ezk 22:20-22. The dross is CONSUMED, not preserved. (2) attuwn H861 (Dan 3:6-26) — the execution furnace. The text reports soldiers died from proximity (3:22); only divine intervention preserved the three Hebrews. Default result = death. Run hebrew_parser.py on Dan 3:22. (3) tannuwr H8574 (Mal 4:1) — the baking oven. 'Shall burn them up...shall leave them neither root nor branch.' Run hebrew_parser.py on Mal 4:1. Parse kaminos G2575 in all 4 NT uses (Matt 13:42,50; Rev 1:15; Rev 9:2). Also read the WG-burning.md word group for broader fire/burning vocabulary connections. Verses: Matt 13:42, Matt 13:50, Dan 3:6-26, Dan 3:22, Ezk 22:18-22, Mal 4:1-3, Rev 1:15, Rev 9:2, Deut 4:20, Isa 48:10 Strong's: G2575, H3564, H861, H8574, H3536

    8. Darkness vs. Fire Tension — Evidence of Figurative Usage: The research agent should lay out the two image sets side by side. FURNACE SET: Matt 13:42,50 — 'cast them into the furnace of fire' (ballo eis ten kaminon tou pyros). DARKNESS SET: Matt 8:12; 22:13; 25:30 — 'cast into outer darkness' (ekballo/ballo eis to skotos to exoteron). Both sets include 'weeping and gnashing of teeth.' Both appear in the same Gospel, same eschatological framework, same author. The darkness set has NO fire vocabulary; the furnace set has NO darkness vocabulary. A literal furnace of fire would produce brilliant light, not darkness. This tension has three possible resolutions: (1) they describe different fates/locations — but both describe the fate of the wicked excluded from the kingdom; (2) they describe different aspects of one literal place — but a place cannot be simultaneously a blazing furnace and total darkness; (3) both are figurative — furnace representing destruction/consumption, darkness representing exclusion from God's light/kingdom.
