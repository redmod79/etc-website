    CLASSIFICATIONS.md — Evidence Classification Audit Trail¶

    Study: etc7-21-judgment-parables-imagery¶

    Question: What do Jesus' judgment parables and associated imagery teach about the fate of the wicked?¶

    This document contains the full Tree 3/4 walkthroughs for every E-item classified as Cond. or ECT and every N-item. Each walkthrough documents the mechanical gate-by-gate classification process.

    E-Item Classifications (Tree 3 Walkthroughs)¶

    E1 (Matt 13:30) — "Gather ye together first the tares, and bind them in bundles to burn them [katakausai]" — Candidate: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — katakaio (G2618, "burn completely/consume") applied to the tares (representing the wicked per Jesus' decoding in v.38). Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): The grammatical subject of katakausai is "the tares" (ta zizania). In the parable itself, tares are plants, not literal human beings. However, Jesus decodes tares = "sons of the wicked one" (huioi tou ponerou, v.38). The verb katakaio is applied to parabolic elements (tares), not directly to decoded human referents in this verse. Subject is an allegorical element in a parable. FAIL — subject is a parabolic/allegorical element.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): katakausai = katakaio G2618, Aorist Active Infinitive. The articular infinitive construction (pros to katakausai auta) states the purpose: "for the burning of them." Grammar unambiguously supports total consumption (kata- prefix = completion). PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): This verse is within the parable itself (Matt 13:24-30), not within Jesus' interpretation (13:36-43). Genre = parabolic narrative. FAIL — parable.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Not reached (Gate 1 and Gate 3 failed).
    • Step 3 Reclassification Check:
    • RC1: Gate 1: Subject is tares (parabolic element). Gate 3: Genre is parabolic.
    • RC2: Corrected observation: "In a parable, plants representing the wicked are gathered and burned completely (katakaio)." The parabolic imagery uses cessation/consumption vocabulary for the fate of the wicked's symbolic representatives.
    • RC3: Re-enter V1: The corrected observation describes parabolic imagery of consumption. Parabolic depiction does not constitute didactic teaching about human fate at E-tier. Neither V1 nor V2 applies at the didactic level.
    • Result: Gate 1 and Gate 3 fail. Reclassified to Neutral. The text uses consumption vocabulary in parabolic imagery — both sides can accept this as a textual observation about what the parable depicts.

    E2 (Matt 13:40) — "As therefore the tares are gathered and burned [katakaietal] in the fire; so shall it be in the end of this world" — Candidate: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — katakaio (G2618, Present Passive Indicative 3S, "is consumed") applied to tares with explicit eschatological application ("so shall it be"). Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): The grammatical subject is "the tares" (ta zizania). Jesus has decoded tares = "sons of the wicked one" (v.38). V.40 uses the hosper...houtos ("as...so") comparative construction: "AS the tares are burned, SO shall it be at the end of the age." The subject of the burning is still the parabolic element (tares), applied via simile to the eschatological event. The decoded human referents (sons of the wicked one) are the implied ultimate subject, but the grammatical subject of katakaietai remains "the tares." FAIL — grammatical subject is the parabolic element, applied via simile.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): katakaietal = katakaio G2618, Present Passive Indicative 3S. Grammar is clear: the tares "are being consumed." The kata- prefix confirms completion. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): This verse is within Jesus' self-interpretation of the parable (13:36-43). The self-interpretation pattern (per grammar reference: self-interpretation-literal-correction.md) elevates the passage from purely parabolic to semi-didactic. However, v.40 retains the simile structure (hosper...houtos = "as...so"), maintaining the parabolic comparison rather than making a direct propositional statement. The decoded propositional statements are in vv.37-39 (X eisin Y). V.40 reverts to simile. Genre classification: semi-didactic with retained simile. This is a borderline case. The genre is NOT purely parabolic (Jesus has decoded the symbols) but NOT purely didactic (the simile structure is retained). BORDERLINE — treated as semi-didactic with qualification. The verb katakaio carries its full semantic weight even in simile form, but the simile structure means the application is via comparison, not direct statement.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): No conflicting E-items. Same-author katakaio usage in Matt 3:12 (chaff burned) supports the consumption reading. PASS.
    • Step 3 Reclassification Check:
    • RC1: Gate 1: Subject is tares (parabolic element applied via simile). Genre is semi-didactic with simile retention.
    • RC2: Corrected observation: "In Jesus' self-interpretation of his parable, the burning of the tares (katakaio = consume completely) is used as a simile for what will happen at the end of the age. The verb katakaio means total consumption in all 12 NT occurrences."
    • RC3: Re-enter V1: The corrected observation contains consumption vocabulary (katakaio) applied via decoded simile. The verb's meaning (total consumption) is a textual fact. But the application to human eschatological fate requires traversing the simile ("as...so"). V1 applies (cessation vocabulary present), but the parabolic-simile mediation reduces the directness.
    • Result: Gate 1 fails (parabolic subject). Reclassified to Neutral. The text says katakaio means total consumption (lexical fact both sides accept). The application of that consumption to human fate occurs through a simile in a semi-didactic context — the verb's meaning is not disputed, but whether the simile teaches literal consumption of humans requires interpretation. Both sides accept the textual observation: "Jesus uses a simile comparing the end-of-age judgment to tares being consumed by fire."

    E3 (Matt 13:42) — "And shall cast them into a furnace of fire: there shall be wailing and gnashing of teeth" — Classified: Neutral

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — kaminos tou pyros ("furnace of fire") — fire/furnace imagery. V2 — klauthmos kai brygmos ("wailing and gnashing") — conscious-state vocabulary (weeping, gnashing). Both V1 and V2 triggered.
    • Both V1 and V2 YES: Note both. Proceed to Step 2.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "them" (autous) refers to "all things that offend and them which do iniquity" (v.41) — decoded human referents. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): balousin = ballo G906, Future Active Indicative 3P ("shall cast"). kaminon = kaminos G2575, Accusative Singular Feminine. klauthmos = G2805, Nominative Singular Masculine. brygmos = G1030, Nominative Singular Masculine. Grammar is straightforward. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): This verse is within Jesus' self-interpretation (13:36-43). The decoded referents (v.41 = humans doing iniquity; v.39 = angels as reapers) make this semi-didactic. However, the "furnace of fire" itself is not decoded — Jesus decoded the sower, field, seed, tares, enemy, harvest, and reapers (vv.37-39) but did NOT decode the furnace. The furnace remains an uninterpreted image within the semi-didactic framework. BORDERLINE — semi-didactic context, but the furnace itself is not decoded.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): The furnace image conflicts with the outer-darkness image in the same author (Matt 8:12; 22:13; 25:30) — fire vs. darkness for the same fate. This tension confirms both are figurative. No direct E-item conflict on the positional question (the furnace text does not directly state either ECT or cessation — it states casting + furnace + emotional response).
    • Result: The verse states the judgment event (casting into furnace) and the emotional response (wailing and gnashing). It does not specify whether the furnace preserves or consumes its contents. The furnace image is not decoded by Jesus. Classified: Neutral. Both sides accept: "Jesus describes the wicked being cast into a furnace of fire, with wailing and gnashing of teeth." They disagree about what the furnace does to its contents.

    E4 (Matt 13:50) — Verbatim identical to E3 (Matt 13:42). Classified: Neutral. Same analysis.

    E5 (Matt 8:12) — "But the children of the kingdom shall be cast out into outer darkness: there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth" — Classified: Neutral

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — ekballo ("cast out") — exclusion, not destruction vocabulary per se. V2 — klauthmos kai brygmos ("weeping and gnashing") — conscious-state vocabulary. V2 triggered.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "children of the kingdom" (huioi tes basileias) — literal human beings (unbelieving Jews excluded from the messianic feast). PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): ekblethesontai = ekballo G1544, Future Passive Indicative 3P ("shall be cast out"). to skotos to exoteron = double article + comparative adjective. Grammar unambiguous. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): This is NOT within a parable. It is Jesus' direct prophetic statement in response to the centurion's faith. No "the kingdom of heaven is like" formula. Genre = direct speech/prophecy. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): No E-item conflict. The outer-darkness image is consistent across all three occurrences (8:12; 22:13; 25:30).
    • Result: All gates pass for V2 candidate (ECT direction — conscious-state vocabulary). However, the conscious-state vocabulary here (weeping, gnashing) describes the RESPONSE at the moment of exclusion, not an ongoing state. The text says weeping and gnashing occur "there" (ekei) but does not specify duration. The text describes exclusion from the kingdom feast and an emotional response. It does not state ongoing conscious torment. Classified: Neutral. Both sides accept: "Jesus says the children of the kingdom will be cast into outer darkness where there is weeping and gnashing of teeth." They disagree about what outer darkness is and whether the response is temporary or permanent.

    E6 (Matt 22:13) — "Bind him hand and foot, and take him away, and cast him into outer darkness; there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth" — Classified: Neutral

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V2 — conscious-state vocabulary (weeping, gnashing). Also binding imagery.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "him" = the guest without a wedding garment — an allegorical character within a parable (the wedding feast parable, 22:1-14). FAIL — allegorical character in a parable.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Parabolic. FAIL.
    • Step 3 Reclassification Check:
    • RC1: Subject is allegorical character; genre is parabolic.
    • RC2: Corrected observation: "In a parable, a character without proper garments is bound and expelled into outer darkness, with weeping and gnashing."
    • RC3: Parabolic depiction does not constitute didactic teaching about literal human fate. Neither V1 nor V2 applies at the didactic level.
    • Result: Gates 1 and 3 fail. Reclassified to Neutral. The text depicts parabolic exclusion imagery.

    E7 (Matt 25:30) — "Cast ye the unprofitable servant into outer darkness: there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth" — Classified: Neutral

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V2 — conscious-state vocabulary (weeping, gnashing).
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "the unprofitable servant" — an allegorical character in the talents parable (25:14-30). FAIL — allegorical character in a parable.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Parabolic. FAIL.
    • Step 3 Reclassification Check: Same as E6. Parabolic character, parabolic genre.
    • Result: Gates 1 and 3 fail. Reclassified to Neutral. Parabolic exclusion imagery.

    E8 (Matt 24:51) — "Shall cut him asunder, and appoint him his portion with the hypocrites: there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth" — Candidate: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — dichotomeo (G1371, "cut asunder") — destruction/execution vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "that evil servant" — an allegorical character in the unfaithful servant parable (24:45-51). FAIL — allegorical character.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Parabolic. FAIL.
    • Step 3 Reclassification Check:
    • RC1: Subject is allegorical; genre is parabolic.
    • RC2: Corrected: "In a parable, a faithless servant is cut asunder and assigned a portion with hypocrites."
    • RC3: Parabolic depiction. Neither V1 nor V2 applies at the didactic level.
    • Result: Gates 1 and 3 fail. Reclassified to Neutral. Parabolic imagery using destruction vocabulary.

    E9 (Luke 13:28) — "There shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth, when ye shall see Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob, and all the prophets, in the kingdom of God, and you yourselves thrust out" — Classified: Neutral

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V2 — conscious-state vocabulary (weeping, gnashing). V1 — ekballo ("thrust out") — exclusion vocabulary. V2 triggered.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "ye" / "you yourselves" — direct address to literal human audience. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): hotan opsesthe (G3752 + G3708, "when ye shall see") — temporal clause identifying the trigger. ekballomenous (G1544, present passive participle) + exo (G1854, "outside"). Grammar unambiguous. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Direct teaching (not within a parable formula). Response to a question about salvation. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with Matt 8:12 (same exclusion + gnashing pattern). No conflict.
    • Result: All gates pass for V2 candidate (ECT direction — conscious-state vocabulary). However, the verse identifies the gnashing as a RESPONSE to seeing exclusion ("when ye shall see... and you yourselves thrust out"). The temporal clause (hotan) makes it a response to a triggering event, not a description of an ongoing state. The text describes: exclusion from the kingdom + an emotional response (weeping and gnashing) triggered by seeing others inside. It does not state ongoing conscious existence. Classified: Neutral. Both sides accept: "Jesus says weeping and gnashing will occur when the excluded see the patriarchs in the kingdom." They disagree about whether this describes a momentary response or an ongoing state.

    E10 (Job 16:9) — "He gnasheth upon me with his teeth; mine enemy" — Classified: Neutral

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V2 — gnashing vocabulary. But the subject is an earthly enemy, not eschatological judgment. Neither V1 nor V2 in eschatological context. Neutral. This is OT background data about the meaning of gnashing — both sides accept it as a textual observation.

    E11 (Psa 37:12) — "The wicked gnasheth upon him with his teeth" — Classified: Neutral

    • Same analysis as E10. OT background for gnashing = hostile rage. Both sides accept the textual observation.

    E12 (Psa 112:10) — "He shall gnash with his teeth, and melt away: the desire of the wicked shall perish" — Candidate: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — masas (H4549, Niphal, "melt away") + abad (H6, "perish") — cessation/destruction vocabulary applied to the wicked. V2 — charaq (H2786, "gnash") — gnashing vocabulary (but gnashing here is rage, not pain). V1 takes priority (cessation is the outcome described). Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "the wicked" (rasha, H7563) — a category of human persons. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): charaq = H2786, Qal Imperfect 3ms ("shall gnash"). masas = H4549, Niphal Perfect 3ms ("melt away"). abad = H6, Qal Imperfect 3fs ("shall perish"). The subject of abad is "the desire" (taavah) of the wicked, not the wicked person directly. The subject of masas is the wicked person. Grammar supports: the wicked gnashes, melts away; the desire of the wicked perishes. PASS — masas (cessation verb) applies to the wicked person; abad applies to their desire.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Wisdom psalm — acrostic didactic poetry. Didactic. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with Psa 37:10 ("the wicked shall not be"), 37:20 ("wicked perish, consume into smoke"), 68:2 ("wicked perish like wax"). No conflicting E-items. PASS.
    • Result: All four gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands. The text states the wicked gnashes (rage), melts away (cessation), and their desire perishes (destruction). Both cessation and destruction vocabulary applied to a human category in didactic genre.

    E13 (Lam 2:16) — "They hiss and gnash the teeth" — Classified: Neutral

    • OT background for gnashing = hostile rage. Both sides accept. Neutral.

    E14 (Acts 7:54) — "They gnashed on him with their teeth" — Classified: Neutral

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V2 — gnashing vocabulary. But context is earthly historical event (Sanhedrin's rage at Stephen), not eschatological judgment. This is background data on what gnashing means. Neutral. Both sides accept: "brycho in Acts 7:54 describes murderous rage."

    E15 (Matt 10:28) — "Fear him which is able to destroy both soul and body in hell" — Candidate: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — apollymi (G622, "destroy") applied to both soul and body. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "soul and body" of human beings. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): apolesai = apollymi G622, Aorist Active Infinitive ("to destroy"). kai psychen kai soma = kai...kai binding two objects as one compound target. gehenna G1067, Dative of place. Grammar unambiguous. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Didactic — Jesus' commissioning discourse, direct teaching to disciples. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with Matt 21:41, 22:7 (same author, same verb apollymi for judgment). No conflicting E-items. PASS.
    • Result: All four gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands. (Examined in depth in etc7-20-matthew-10-28.)

    E16 (Matt 22:7) — "Destroyed those murderers, and burned up their city" — Candidate: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — apollymi (G622, "destroyed") + empretho (G1714, "burned up") — destruction vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "those murderers" — within the wedding feast parable, these are allegorical characters representing those who rejected and killed God's messengers. FAIL — allegorical characters in a parable.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Parabolic. FAIL.
    • Step 3 Reclassification Check:
    • RC1: Subject is allegorical; genre is parabolic.
    • RC2: Corrected: "In a parable, characters representing those who rejected the king's invitation are destroyed (apollymi) and their city burned up (empretho)."
    • RC3: Parabolic depiction using destruction vocabulary. Both sides accept the text uses apollymi and empretho here. Neither V1 nor V2 applies at the didactic level.
    • Result: Gates 1 and 3 fail. Reclassified to Neutral. Parabolic imagery using destruction vocabulary.

    E17 (Matt 21:41) — "He will miserably destroy those wicked men" — Candidate: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — apollymi (G622, "destroy") applied to wicked men. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "those wicked men" — within the parable of the wicked tenants (21:33-46). The audience speaks this line, but the tenants are allegorical characters. FAIL — allegorical characters.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Parabolic. FAIL.
    • Step 3 Reclassification Check:
    • RC1: Subject is allegorical; genre is parabolic.
    • RC2: Corrected: "In a parable, wicked tenants are destroyed (apollymi) by the vineyard lord."
    • RC3: Parabolic depiction using destruction vocabulary.
    • Result: Gates 1 and 3 fail. Reclassified to Neutral. Parabolic imagery.

    E18 (Matt 3:12) — "He will burn up the chaff with unquenchable fire" — Candidate: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — katakaio (G2618, "burn up") — cessation/destruction vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "the chaff" — a simile for the wicked (winnowing imagery). The subject is figurative (chaff representing people). FAIL — figurative/metaphorical subject.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): katakausei = G2618, Future Active Indicative 3S. Grammar unambiguous: "he will consume completely." PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Direct prophetic speech (John the Baptist's preaching). Not parabolic formula, but the chaff/wheat imagery is figurative. Genre = prophetic speech with figurative imagery. PASS (direct speech prophecy).
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with Matt 13:30,40 (same verb katakaio). PASS.
    • Step 3 Reclassification Check:
    • RC1: Gate 1: Subject is figurative (chaff = people, not literal chaff).
    • RC2: Corrected observation: "John the Baptist says the Messiah will burn up (katakaio) the chaff (representing the wicked) with unquenchable fire, using a winnowing metaphor."
    • RC3: Re-enter V1: The corrected observation retains cessation vocabulary (katakaio) applied to a figurative subject in prophetic speech. The verb's meaning (total consumption) is a lexical fact. But the subject is figurative.
    • Result: Gate 1 fails (figurative subject). The observation that katakaio means total consumption is a lexical fact (Neutral). The application to human fate operates through metaphor. Reclassified to Neutral. Both sides accept: "John the Baptist uses katakaio (total consumption) in a winnowing metaphor for judgment."

    E19 (Mal 4:1) — "Shall burn them up... leave them neither root nor branch" — Candidate: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — lahat (H3857, Piel, "devour/burn up") + "leave neither root nor branch" — destruction/cessation vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
