    Word Studies¶

    Question¶

    What do Jesus' judgment parables and associated imagery teach about the fate of the wicked? Focus on: Matt 13:24-30,36-43 (wheat & tares / furnace of fire), Matt 13:47-50 (dragnet), 'outer darkness' (Matt 8:12; 22:13; 25:30), 'weeping and gnashing of teeth' (7 occurrences), and the fire/furnace vocabulary.

    G2618 — katakaio¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2618-katakaio.md

    Occurrences: 12

    Compound verb (kata + kaio): to burn down completely, consume wholly. 11 unique verses (12 tokens) across 7 books. All occurrences describe total consumption — chaff burned up (Matt 3:12), tares burned (Matt 13:30,40), earth burned up (2 Pet 3:10), trees burned up (Rev 8:7), Babylon utterly burned (Rev 18:8), sin-offering bodies burned outside camp (Heb 13:11), works burned (1 Cor 3:15), magic books burned (Acts 19:19). The kata- prefix transforms kaio ('burn') into 'burn down/consume completely.' The passive dominance (58%) reflects judgment contexts where the focus is on the object being destroyed. Primary Hebrew source: saraph (H8313) with 63 LXX co-occurrences and PMI 7.37

    Semantic categories: judgment parable — winnowing/harvest, apocalyptic/cosmic destruction, judgment on Babylon, sacrificial/ritual burning, testing by fire, voluntary destruction

    Key verses: Matt 3:12, Matt 13:30, Matt 13:40, 2 Pet 3:10, Rev 8:7, Rev 18:8, Heb 13:11, 1 Cor 3:15

    Relevance: THE decisive verb in the tares parable. Jesus uses katakaio for the fate of the tares in both the parable (13:30) and his own interpretation (13:40). Every NT occurrence describes total consumption with nothing remaining. The kata- prefix pattern (kata+base = intensification to completion) parallels katesthio (devour completely) and katapino (swallow up). This is Jesus' chosen verb for what happens to the wicked at the end of the age.

    G2575 — kaminos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2575-kaminos.md

    Occurrences: 4

    Feminine noun: furnace, specifically an industrial/smelting furnace (distinguished from klibanos, domestic oven). 4 NT occurrences in 2 books: Matt 13:42,50 (judgment destination), Rev 1:15 (simile for intense heat), Rev 9:2 (simile for smoke volume). The LXX connection is overwhelmingly to Daniel 3 Aramaic vocabulary — kaminos is the standard LXX translation for attuwn (H861, Nebuchadnezzar's execution furnace). Also maps to kuwr (H3564, smelting furnace of Ezk 22). Three OT furnace traditions feed into kaminos: (1) attuwn — execution furnace that kills (Dan 3), (2) kuwr — smelting furnace that consumes dross (Ezk 22), (3) tannuwr — oven that reduces to ashes (Mal 4:1).

    Semantic categories: judgment destination — fiery destruction, simile for intense heat/refining brilliance, simile for smoke

    Key verses: Matt 13:42, Matt 13:50, Rev 1:15, Rev 9:2

    Relevance: Jesus' chosen image for the eschatological judgment destination. The phrase 'ten kaminon tou pyros' (the furnace of fire) appears only in Matt 13:42 and 13:50. Every OT furnace tradition that feeds kaminos describes destruction of contents: attuwn kills (Dan 3:22), kuwr consumes dross (Ezk 22:20-22), tannuwr burns to ashes (Mal 4:1). The furnace's function is consumption/execution, not preservation-in-suffering.

    G1030 — brygmos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1030-brygmos.md

    Occurrences: 7

    Masculine noun: gnashing (of teeth). 7 occurrences in 7 unique verses across 2 books (Matthew 6, Luke 1). All 7 appear in the identical fixed formula: 'there shall be the weeping and the gnashing of teeth' (ekei estai ho klauthmos kai ho brygmos ton odonton). All are nominative singular masculine. 100% collocation with klauthmos (weeping). The noun derives from brycho (G1031), which maps 1:1 to Hebrew charaq (H2786) — hostile rage gnashing in all 5 OT uses.

    Semantic categories: judgment formula — exclusion from the kingdom

    Key verses: Matt 8:12, Matt 13:42, Matt 13:50, Matt 22:13, Matt 24:51, Matt 25:30, Luke 13:28

    Relevance: Appears in every judgment parable and outer-darkness passage in this study. The formula 'weeping and gnashing of teeth' links furnace passages (13:42,50), outer-darkness passages (8:12; 22:13; 25:30), the dichotomeo passage (24:51), and Luke's interpretive passage (13:28). The OT background (charaq = rage/hostility in all 5 uses) and Acts 7:54 (brycho = murderous fury) establish gnashing as rage at exclusion, not pain from torment.

    G2805 — klauthmos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2805-klauthmos.md

    Occurrences: 9

    Masculine noun: weeping, wailing, lamentation. 9 occurrences across 3 books (Matthew 7, Luke 1, Acts 1). Seven of 9 uses are in the fixed judgment formula paired with brygmos. The two non-formulaic uses are Matt 2:18 (Rachel weeping for children — quoting Jer 31:15) and Acts 20:37 (Ephesian elders weeping at Paul's departure). Primary Hebrew source: bekiy (H1065 'weeping') with 20 LXX co-occurrences, PMI 9.23

    Semantic categories: judgment formula — weeping and gnashing, prophetic quotation — grief at loss, human grief — mourning at parting

    Key verses: Matt 8:12, Matt 13:42, Matt 13:50, Matt 22:13, Matt 24:51, Matt 25:30, Luke 13:28, Matt 2:18, Acts 20:37

    Relevance: The 'weeping' half of the judgment formula. The non-formulaic uses (Matt 2:18 = grief at loss of children; Acts 20:37 = grief at parting) confirm klauthmos expresses GRIEF at irreversible loss. In the judgment formula, klauthmos (grief at loss) + brygmos (rage at exclusion) describe the emotional response at the moment of judgment — not an ongoing state.

    G1857 — exoteros¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1857-exoteros.md

    Occurrences: 3

    Comparative adjective: outer, outermost. From exo (G1854 'outside') with -teros comparative suffix. 3 NT occurrences, ALL in Matthew (8:12; 22:13; 25:30), ALL in the fixed formula 'to skotos to exoteron' (the outer darkness). All parse as A-ASN-C (accusative singular neuter comparative). The double article construction (to skotos to exoteron) marks a definite, particular destination. Primary LXX source: chiytsown (H2435 'outer') used in Ezekiel's temple for 'outer court/gate.' Jesus takes this architectural/spatial term and applies it eschatologically.

    Semantic categories: exclusion from the messianic kingdom

    Key verses: Matt 8:12, Matt 22:13, Matt 25:30

    Relevance: Defines the 'outer darkness' — the destination of those excluded from the kingdom feast. All three contexts are feast/banquet settings (sitting with Abraham 8:11; wedding feast 22:2; entering joy 25:21). The outer darkness is OUTSIDE the lit celebration — exclusion imagery. The tension between darkness (no light) and furnace (bright fire) in the same author confirms both are figurative representations of the same judgment.

    G622 — apollymi¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G622-apollymi.md

    Occurrences: 102

    Compound verb (apo + ollymi): to destroy fully. 86 unique verses (102 tokens) across 15 NT books. Active voice = destroy (transitive); middle/passive = perish/be lost. Matthew uses apollymi 18x; judgment-context uses with divine/authority subject consistently = actual destruction: Matt 10:28 (destroy soul+body in gehenna), 21:41 (lord destroys wicked tenants), 22:7 (king destroyed murderers). The sozo-apollymi antithesis (14 NT verses) sets save and destroy in direct opposition.

    Semantic categories: violence/harm/destroy/kill, religious perish/save antonym, loss of possession, state of non-existence

    Key verses: Matt 10:28, Matt 21:41, Matt 22:7, Luke 17:29, John 3:16, James 4:12

    Relevance: Matthew's primary judgment destruction verb. The same author who writes the furnace/darkness parables uses apollymi for divine judgment in three other passages (10:28, 21:41, 22:7). 22:7 appears IN the wedding-feast parable that ends with outer darkness — apollymi (destroyed murderers) + empretho (burned city) + outer darkness (v.13) in one parable. The verb consistently means actual destruction, not preservation in suffering.

    G4442 — pyr¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G4442-pyr.md

    Occurrences: 74

    Primary neuter noun: fire. 73 unique verses (74 tokens) across 14 NT books. Revelation dominates (25/73). Matthew has 12 uses spanning judgment fire throughout the Gospel. Semantic categories: eschatological punishment (lake of fire, gehenna), judgment parables (trees/chaff burned), divine theophany, cosmic plagues, testing/refining. Key collocations: ballo (cast, 19x/26%), katakaio (burn up, 7x/10%), gehenna (6x/8%), theion (brimstone, 7x/10%). Primary Hebrew source: esh (H784) with 316 LXX co-occurrences.

    Semantic categories: eschatological punishment, judgment parables, divine theophany, cosmic plagues, testing/refining, figurative/metaphorical

    Key verses: Matt 3:10, Matt 13:40, Matt 13:42, Matt 25:41, Rev 20:14-15, Heb 12:29

    Relevance: Base noun in 'furnace of fire' (kaminos tou pyros) and 'everlasting fire' (pur aionion). The fire vocabulary is consistent across Matthew: fire consumes trees (3:10; 7:19), chaff (3:12), tares (13:40), and is the element in the furnace (13:42,50) and gehenna (5:22; 18:8-9). Matthew never uses fire in combination with torment vocabulary for human subjects.

    G4655 — skotos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G4655-skotos.md

    Occurrences: 32

    Neuter noun: darkness, obscurity. 32 tokens in 31 unique verses across 16 books. Semantic domains: physical darkness, moral/spiritual state, domain of evil, judgment. Matthew has 6 uses: 3 in the outer-darkness formula (8:12; 22:13; 25:30), 1 physical darkness at crucifixion (27:45), 2 spiritual darkness (4:16; 6:23). LXX source: choshek (H2822 'darkness').

    Semantic categories: physical darkness, moral/spiritual state, domain/power of evil, eschatological judgment

    Key verses: Matt 8:12, Matt 22:13, Matt 25:30, Matt 27:45, Col 1:13

    Relevance: Component of the 'outer darkness' formula. The tension between skotos (darkness = no light) and kaminos/pyr (furnace/fire = bright light) in the same author and same eschatological framework is a critical datum. A literal furnace produces light, not darkness. Both images in the same author describing the same fate confirms figurative usage — darkness = exclusion from God's light/kingdom; fire = destruction/consumption.

    G1067 — gehenna¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1067-geenna.md

    Occurrences: 12

    Feminine noun: gehenna (Valley of Hinnom). 12 NT occurrences: 7 in Matthew, 3 in Mark, 1 in Luke, 1 in James. 11 of 12 are Jesus' direct speech. Collocates: fire (6x), apollymi (3x), ballo (5x), zoe as antithesis (3x). basanizo NEVER co-occurs with gehenna. Life/gehenna contrast (Matt 18:9; Mark 9:43,45,47): 'enter life' vs. 'cast into gehenna fire' — life vs. destruction, not life vs. alternative-life.

    Semantic categories: fiery place of destruction, self-denial to escape it, destruction of soul and body, God's judicial judgment, moral corruption

    Key verses: Matt 5:22, Matt 5:29-30, Matt 10:28, Matt 18:9, Matt 23:33, Mark 9:43-48, Luke 12:5, James 3:6

    Relevance: The broader eschatological judgment location in Jesus' teaching. The furnace (kaminos) and gehenna share fire imagery and destruction vocabulary. gehenna collocates with apollymi (destroy) and pyr (fire) but NEVER with basanizo (torment). The life/gehenna contrast shows gehenna as the opposite of life — death/destruction, not ongoing existence in torment.

    G166 — aionios¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G166-aionios.md

    Occurrences: 71

    Adjective: pertaining to the age(s), age-long, everlasting. 71 NT occurrences. Duration determined by nature of subject. Three past-time uses (Rom 16:25; 2 Tim 1:9; Tit 1:2) demonstrate non-eternal meaning. The Hebrews pattern: aionios + result-noun = permanent result of completed act (eternal redemption Heb 9:12; eternal judgment Heb 6:2). Tit 1:2 uses aionios twice with different temporal scopes in one verse, falsifying 'same adjective = same duration.' The aidios/aionios distinction: aidios (G126) = strictly everduring; Jude uses aidios (v.6) then aionios (v.7) in adjacent verses.

    Semantic categories: divine/spiritual duration, eschatological result, pre-temporal duration

    Key verses: Matt 25:46, Matt 18:8, Matt 25:41, John 3:16, Heb 9:12, Heb 6:2, Tit 1:2, 2 Thess 1:9

    Relevance: Qualifies both 'punishment' (Matt 25:46) and 'fire' (Matt 18:8; 25:41) in the judgment parables framework. The Hebrews pattern governs interpretation: 'eternal punishment' = punishment rendered once with permanent result; 'eternal fire' = fire whose destructive result is permanent. The Sodom test case: Jude 1:7's 'eternal fire' produced 2 Pet 2:6's 'ashes' — eternal fire that ended, leaving permanent results.

    G2851 — kolasis¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2851-kolasis.md

    Occurrences: 2

    Feminine noun: punishment, correction. From kolazo (G2849 'to curtail, dock, prune'). Only 2 NT occurrences: Matt 25:46 ('everlasting punishment') and 1 John 4:18 ('fear hath torment/punishment'). This is PUNISHMENT vocabulary, not TORMENT vocabulary. The distinction matters: basanismos (G929) = torment; kolasis = punishment/correction from kolazo 'to curtail.' Aristotle distinguished kolasis (corrective, for the benefit of the sufferer) from timoria (retributive, for the satisfaction of the inflictor).

    Semantic categories: punishment, corrective discipline

    Key verses: Matt 25:46, 1 John 4:18

    Relevance: The word Jesus chose in Matt 25:46 for the fate of the goats is kolasis (punishment), NOT basanismos (torment). The kolazo root ('to curtail/prune') carries a cutting-off/ending sense. Combined with aionios in the Hebrews pattern: eternal punishment = punishment whose result is permanent cutting-off.

    G928 — basanizo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G928-basanizo.md

    Occurrences: 12

    Verb: to torment, put to the test. 12 NT occurrences. Judgment uses exclusively in Revelation (5 judgment uses, all Revelation). ZERO occurrences in any judgment parable, any outer-darkness passage, any gnashing-formula passage, or any Matthean gehenna passage. Genre-restricted to apocalyptic/visionary literature.

    Semantic categories: physical suffering/disease, demonic torment, apocalyptic judgment

    Key verses: Matt 8:6, Matt 8:29, Rev 14:10, Rev 20:10

    Relevance: THE critical absence. basanizo (torment) NEVER appears in any passage in this study. The judgment parables use katakaio (consume), apollymi (destroy), ballo (cast), ekballo (cast out), dichotomeo (cut asunder). basanizo for human eschatological judgment appears ONLY in Revelation's apocalyptic framework (Rev 14:10-11; 20:10 for non-human entities). This is a systematic vocabulary differentiation.

    G929 — basanismos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G929-basanismos.md

    Occurrences: 6

    Masculine noun: torment, torture. 6 NT occurrences, ALL in Revelation. 3 describe Babylon's completed destruction. 1 has five-month time limit (Rev 9:5). Exclusively apocalyptic vocabulary. Never in parables, never in Gospels, never in epistles.

    Semantic categories: apocalyptic judgment torment

    Key verses: Rev 9:5, Rev 14:11, Rev 18:7, Rev 18:10, Rev 18:15

    Relevance: Zero occurrences in any judgment parable or any passage in this study. The torment noun is exclusively Revelation vocabulary. Jesus NEVER uses basanismos in his teaching.

    G684 — apoleia¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G684-apoleia.md

    Occurrences: 20

    Feminine noun: destruction, perdition, waste. Cognate of apollymi (G622). Used for eschatological destruction in multiple passages. The noun form of the primary destruction verb.

    Semantic categories: destruction/perdition, waste/ruin

    Key verses: Matt 7:13, Phil 3:19, 2 Pet 3:7, Rev 17:8

    Relevance: The noun form of apollymi. Matt 7:13 contrasts the 'broad way that leads to destruction (apoleia)' with the 'narrow way that leads to life (zoe).' Destruction vs. life — not ongoing torment vs. life.

    G5077 — tephroo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G5077-tephroo.md

    Occurrences: 1

    Verb: to turn to ashes (from tephra 'ashes'). Hapax legomenon — only 1 NT use (2 Pet 2:6). Describes what happened to Sodom and Gomorrah. The cities were 'turned to ashes' (tephrosas, aorist active participle) and set as an 'example' (hupodeigma) for the ungodly.

    Semantic categories: destruction to ashes

    Key verses: 2 Pet 2:6

    Relevance: Defines the result of 'eternal fire' (Jude 1:7). The same cities that 'suffer the vengeance of eternal fire' were 'turned to ashes.' This establishes the paradigm: eternal fire = fire that produces permanent destruction (ashes), not eternal burning.

    G906 — ballo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G906-ballo.md

    Occurrences: 127

    Verb: to cast, throw. Common verb with wide usage. In judgment contexts: 'cast into the furnace of fire' (Matt 13:42,50), 'cast into fire/gehenna' (Matt 5:29-30; 18:8-9). The verb of directed judgment action.

    Semantic categories: casting/throwing, judgment casting

    Key verses: Matt 13:42, Matt 13:50, Matt 5:29, Matt 18:8

    Relevance: The verb for casting the wicked INTO the furnace of fire. Appears in Matt 13:42,50 as balousin (Future Active Indicative 3P) — 'they shall cast.' The casting is a one-time directed action, not an ongoing state.

    G1544 — ekballo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1544-ekballo.md

    Occurrences: 96

    Compound verb (ek + ballo): to cast out, expel. The verb of exclusion/expulsion. Used in the outer-darkness formula: 'cast out into the outer darkness' (Matt 8:12; 22:13; 25:30). Also in Luke 13:28: 'you yourselves thrust out' (ekballomenous exo).

    Semantic categories: expulsion/exclusion, exorcism, judgment casting out

    Key verses: Matt 8:12, Matt 22:13, Matt 25:30, Luke 13:28

    Relevance: The verb for exclusion from the kingdom feast. Appears in all three outer-darkness passages and Luke's gnashing passage. The ek- prefix emphasizes OUT-casting — removal from inside to outside. This is EXCLUSION vocabulary, not confinement-in-torment vocabulary.

    H2786 — charaq¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H2786-charaq.md

    Occurrences: 5

    Primitive root verb: to grate/gnash the teeth. 5 OT occurrences across 3 books (Psalms 3, Job 1, Lamentations 1). ALL 5 express hostile rage: Job 16:9 (enemy gnashes in wrath), Psa 35:16 (mockers gnash at David), Psa 37:12 (wicked gnashes at righteous), Psa 112:10 (wicked gnashes and melts away), Lam 2:16 (enemies gnash in triumph). Always Qal stem. Always collocates with shen (H8127 'teeth') — 100% of uses. LXX maps charaq 1:1 to brycho (G1031) with perfect bidirectional equivalence.

    Semantic categories: hostile gnashing of teeth (rage/animosity)

    Key verses: Job 16:9, Psa 35:16, Psa 37:12, Psa 112:10, Lam 2:16

    Relevance: THE OT background for brygmos (gnashing) in the judgment formula. All 5 OT uses are HOSTILE RAGE directed at the righteous or God's people. The subject is always an enemy/wicked person. The emotion is anger/malice, NOT pain/suffering. This OT background governs interpretation of the NT judgment formula: gnashing = rage at exclusion.

    G1031 — brycho¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1031-brycho.md

    Occurrences: 1

    Primary verb: to grate/gnash the teeth. Hapax legomenon — only Acts 7:54 (Sanhedrin gnashing at Stephen). Imperfect tense (ebruchon) indicates continuous/repeated action — sustained rage. Dual-domain classification: Louw-Nida 23.41 (mouth processes) AND 88.181 (anger/indignation). Root verb for brygmos (G1030). Maps 1:1 to charaq (H2786) in LXX.

    Semantic categories: teeth-gnashing as expression of rage

    Key verses: Acts 7:54

    Relevance: The sole NT use of the verb confirms the OT rage meaning. Acts 7:54 context: the Sanhedrin hears Stephen's accusation, is 'cut to the heart' (diaprio — enraged), and gnashes teeth AT (ep' auton) him. They then stone him. This is murderous fury, not pain or suffering. The cognate noun brygmos in the judgment formula carries this same rage connotation.

    G5149 — trizo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G5149-trizo.md

    Occurrences: 1

    Primary verb: to creak, squeak; by analogy to grate/grind teeth. Hapax legomenon — only Mark 9:18 (boy's demonic seizure). Present tense (trizei) depicts ongoing/habitual action during episodes. Distinguished from brycho (deliberate hostile gnashing) and brygmos (eschatological formula). Trizo is involuntary physical grinding during convulsions.

    Semantic categories: involuntary physical teeth-grinding

    Key verses: Mark 9:18

    Relevance: Shows that teeth-grinding CAN describe a temporary physical event. The boy's grinding stopped when Jesus cast out the demon. This is evidence against interpreting the judgment gnashing as an ongoing eternal state — it can describe a momentary response.

    G1371 — dichotomeo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1371-dichotomeo.md

    Occurrences: 2

    Compound verb (dicha 'in two' + temno 'to cut'): to cut in two, cut asunder. 2 NT occurrences (Matt 24:51; Luke 12:46) in the unfaithful servant parable. Both use identical form dichotomosei (Future Active Indicative 3S). Dual Louw-Nida domains: 19.002 (pierce/cut) AND 38.001 (punish). LXX background: butchering sacrificial animals (Exod 29:17, 1 Sam 15:33).

    Semantic categories: judicial punishment — severe consequence, physical cutting

    Key verses: Matt 24:51, Luke 12:46

    Relevance: Unique judgment verb in Matt 24:51 — the only gnashing occurrence with neither furnace nor outer-darkness imagery. The verb is violent and decisive: 'cut asunder.' Combined with 'appoint his portion with the hypocrites' and the gnashing formula. This is destruction/execution vocabulary.

    G1714 — empretho¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1714-empretho.md

    Occurrences: 1

    Compound verb (en + pretho 'to blow a flame'): to set on fire, burn up. Hapax legomenon — only Matt 22:7 ('burned up their city'). Aorist active indicative (enepresen). Same Louw-Nida domain as katakaio (14.008 Burning). Primary Hebrew source: saraph (H8313) with PMI 7.31

    Semantic categories: military destruction of a city

    Key verses: Matt 22:7

    Relevance: Same-author fire vocabulary in the wedding feast parable. Matt 22:7 pairs apollymi (destroyed murderers) with empretho (burned city) — two destruction verbs in one verse. This is in the SAME parable that ends with outer darkness (v.13). Matthew uses distinct fire verbs for distinct functions: empretho for city-burning, katakaio for eschatological consumption.

    H3564 — kuwr¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H3564-kuwr.md

    Occurrences: 11

    Masculine noun: furnace, specifically smelting/refining furnace. 9 unique verses across 6 books. Four semantic categories: Egypt as iron furnace (Deut 4:20; 1 Ki 8:51; Jer 11:4), metallurgical testing (Prov 17:3; 27:21), furnace of affliction (Isa 48:10), smelting furnace of judgment where dross is consumed (Ezk 22:18-22). LXX maps kuwr primarily to kaminos (G2575) with 6 co-occurrences and PMI 9.49

    Semantic categories: national deliverance metaphor, metallurgical testing, divine refining, judgment — dross consumed

    Key verses: Deut 4:20, Prov 17:3, Isa 48:10, Ezk 22:18-22

    Relevance: Second OT furnace tradition feeding into kaminos. The Ezk 22 usage is critical: Israel as dross in the kuwr — the smelting furnace CONSUMES the dross. The verb nathak (melt/pour) appears 4x in Ezk 22:20-22. The furnace's function here is consuming the worthless (dross), not preserving it.

    H8574 — tannuwr¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H8574-tannuwr.md

    Occurrences: 19

    Masculine noun: oven, fire-pot, furnace. 15 unique verses across 9 books. Seven semantic categories ranging from domestic baking oven (Lev 2:4) to divine judgment (Mal 4:1; Psa 21:9) to human wickedness as internal heat (Hos 7:4,6,7). LXX maps primarily to klibanos (G2823 'oven') rather than kaminos. The judgment usage in Mal 4:1 is critical: 'the day burns as a tannuwr' — the wicked become stubble and are burned up, leaving neither root nor branch.

    Semantic categories: domestic baking oven, ritual purity object, theophanic fire-pot, landmark, divine judgment/wrath, human wickedness as heat, famine/suffering

    Key verses: Gen 15:17, Mal 4:1, Psa 21:9, Hos 7:4

    Relevance: Third OT furnace tradition. Mal 4:1 is the critical verse: tannuwr as the image for eschatological judgment fire. The result: wicked = stubble, burned up, neither root nor branch, ashes under feet (4:3). This is the most explicit OT passage defining what the furnace does to the wicked: total consumption to ashes.

    H861 — attuwn¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/A861-attuwn.md

    Occurrences: 18

    Aramaic masculine noun: furnace (fire-place). 10 unique verses (18 tokens), ALL in Daniel 3:6-26. The execution furnace of Nebuchadnezzar. LXX maps univocally to kaminos (G2575) — perfect 1:1 alignment. The furnace killed soldiers from proximity (3:22); only divine intervention preserved the three Hebrews. Default result = death.

    Semantic categories: instrument of royal execution, arena of divine deliverance, execution carried out, theophanic encounter

    Key verses: Dan 3:6, Dan 3:17, Dan 3:20-23, Dan 3:22, Dan 3:25-26

    Relevance: Primary OT background for kaminos via LXX. The Daniel 3 furnace is the narrative image behind Jesus' 'furnace of fire.' The furnace's function is EXECUTION — it kills by default. Survival required direct divine intervention (the fourth figure). When Jesus says 'cast them into the furnace of fire,' the OT referent is an execution furnace, not a confinement facility.

    G930 — basanistes¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G930-basanistēs.md

    Occurrences: 1

    Masculine noun: torturer, tormentor, jailer. Only 1 NT occurrence: Matt 18:34 (unforgiving servant parable). The servant is delivered to the 'tormentors' (basanistais) until he pays all owed. This is a DEBT parable, not an eschatological judgment parable. The 'until he should pay' implies a finite, measurable process.

    Semantic categories: debt imprisonment/torture

    Key verses: Matt 18:34

    Relevance: The ONLY basanizo-family word in any Matthean parable. It appears in a non-eschatological debt parable, not in the judgment parables (tares, dragnet, sheep/goats, talents, wedding feast). The 'until he should pay' qualification further distinguishes it from the judgment parables' definitive language.

    G931 — basanos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G931-basanos.md

    Occurrences: 3

    Feminine noun: touchstone (for testing metals), hence torture, torment, pain. 3 NT occurrences: Matt 4:24 (physical suffering), Luke 16:23,28 (rich man in hades). ALL 3 are pre-judgment state, NOT eschatological. Zero uses in judgment parable contexts.

    Semantic categories: physical suffering, parabolic/pre-judgment context

    Key verses: Matt 4:24, Luke 16:23, Luke 16:28

    Relevance: Zero eschatological judgment uses. The only parabolic use is Luke 16 (rich man and Lazarus), which operates in hades (pre-judgment), not gehenna or furnace (post-judgment). The parabolic genre classification of Luke 16 (per etc7-09) further limits its evidential weight.
