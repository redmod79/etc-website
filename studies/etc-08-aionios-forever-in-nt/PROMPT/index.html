    Bible Study: Greek aionios (G166) -- all 71 NT occurrences. Compare aidios (G126). 'Eis tous aionas ton aionon' in ~20 passages. LXX usage. Does aionios inherently mean 'eternal/endless' or does it mean 'pertaining to the age' with duration determined by the nature of the subject?¶

    Question¶

    Greek aionios (G166) -- all 71 NT occurrences. Compare aidios (G126). 'Eis tous aionas ton aionon' in ~20 passages. LXX usage. Does aionios inherently mean 'eternal/endless' or does it mean 'pertaining to the age' with duration determined by the nature of the subject?

    Prior Research Summary¶

    This study is the NT Greek counterpart to etc7-07 (olam/forever in OT). The direct predecessor etc6-08 examined the same question and found: aionios derives from aion ('an age'), not from aei ('always') like aidios; the LXX translates olam as aionios 100 times (PMI 5.67); aidios (G126, 2 NT uses) has no Hebrew source in the LXX; aionios is used for PAST TIME in three passages (Rom 16:25, 2 Tim 1:9, Tit 1:2); aionios modifies 'life' ~42 times (~59%) but punishment/judgment only ~7 times (~10%); 'ages of ages' formula appears ~19 times for God/Christ and only 3 times in judgment contexts (Rev 14:11, 19:3, 20:10); Hebrews uses aionios for permanent results of completed acts (eternal redemption, salvation, inheritance, judgment); Mark 3:29 says 'eternal sin' (adjective modifies the sin itself). The etc6-08 tally: E=37 (4 Cond., 0 ECT, 33 Neutral), N=5 (2 Cond., 0 ECT, 3 Neutral), I-B=3 (all resolved Strong toward Cond.). The olam studies (etc6-07, etc7-07) established the nature-of-subject principle across 50+ OT cases. Prior Revelation studies (etc6-11, etc6-12, for-ever-and-ever-revelation) established that 'ages of ages' in judgment echoes OT olam smoke (Isa 34:10) = completed judgments, basanismos appears only in Revelation, and 'tormented for ever and ever' applies only to non-human/symbolic entities (Rev 20:10). 17 prior study CONCLUSION.md files were reviewed.

    Focus Areas¶

    1. Etymology and Derivation of Aionios (G166) from Aion (G165): Strong's defines aionios (G166) as 'from G165 (aion); perpetual (also used of past time, or past and future as well).' The root noun aion (G165) is defined as 'properly, an age; by extension, perpetuity.' Aion appears 120 times in the NT, translated 'ever' (51x), 'world' (30x), 'and ever' (21x), 'ages' (2x), etc. This translation spread confirms aion's basic meaning as 'an age/era' rather than 'infinity.' The KJV translates aion as 'world' 30 times (e.g., 'end of the world' = 'end of the age,' Mat 24:3; 'the god of this world,' 2Co 4:4), demonstrating that aion does not inherently mean 'eternity.' In contrast, aidios (G126) derives from aei (G104, 'always') and means 'everduring.' Greek has two words available: aionios (age-pertaining) and aidios (everduring). The existence of both as distinct words is significant. Verses: Mat 24:3, Mat 12:32, 2Co 4:4, Heb 1:2, Heb 9:26, Heb 11:3, Mar 10:30 Strong's: G166, G165, G126, G104

    2. Aidios (G126) vs. Aionios (G166): Two Distinct Words for Eternity: Paul uses aidios once (Rom 1:20) for God's 'eternal power and Godhead' -- describing attributes intrinsic to God's nature that have always existed. Jude uses aidios once (Jude 1:6) for 'everlasting chains' binding fallen angels. Critically, Jude then uses aionios in the NEXT verse (Jude 1:7) for Sodom's 'eternal fire.' The same author, in consecutive verses, chooses different words: aidios for the chains binding angels (absolute, unbreakable permanence) and aionios for fire that historically reduced cities to ashes (2Pe 2:6). If the two words were synonymous, Jude would not switch between them in adjacent sentences. The aidios chains genuinely never end (the angels remain bound until judgment). The aionios fire's 'eternity' is qualified by the historical evidence: Sodom is not still burning. Aidios derives from aei ('always') -- inherent, absolute eternality. Aionios derives from aion ('an age') -- duration pertaining to an age, with actual extent determined by the subject. Verses: Rom 1:20, Jud 1:6, Jud 1:7, 2Pe 2:6 Strong's: G126, G166, G104, G165

    3. Aionios for Past Time: The Decisive Counter-Evidence: Rom 16:25 uses the dative plural chronois aioniois ('in times eternal/age-long') for a mystery that was kept secret during past ages -- a period that HAS ENDED with the revelation of the gospel. 2 Tim 1:9 uses pro chronon aionion ('before eternal/age-long times') for God's purpose given before creation. Tit 1:2 is especially significant: it uses aionios for BOTH 'eternal life' (future hope) and 'before the world began' (past reference) in the same verse: 'In hope of eternal life [zoes aioniou], which God, that cannot lie, promised before the world began [pro chronon aionion].' If aionios inherently meant 'endless,' 'before endless times' would be incoherent -- you cannot have a beginning-point before endlessness. These past-time uses demonstrate that aionios means 'pertaining to the ages' and can refer to any age-spanning period, whether past, future, or both. Verses: Rom 16:25, 2Ti 1:9, Tit 1:2 Strong's: G166, G5550

    4. Aionios Modifying 'Life' (~43 of 71 Occurrences): John's Gospel alone contains 17 of the 71 occurrences, nearly all in the phrase 'eternal life.' The consistent NT teaching is that eternal life is: (1) a GIFT, not inherent (Rom 6:23; 1Jn 5:11), (2) received through FAITH in Christ (Jhn 3:15-16), (3) something believers currently HAVE (Jhn 5:24; 1Jn 5:13) but will fully experience at resurrection (1Co 15:53-54), (4) defined as KNOWING God (Jhn 17:3), and (5) contrasted with PERISHING (Jhn 3:16; 10:28) and DEATH (Rom 6:23). The life-vs-perish/death contrast is critical: if the wicked also have eternal conscious existence (in torment), the contrast between eternal life and perishing collapses. The wicked would not truly 'perish' -- they would merely have a different form of eternal existence. Verses: Jhn 3:15, Jhn 3:16, Jhn 3:36, Jhn 5:24, Jhn 10:28, Jhn 17:2, Jhn 17:3, Rom 2:7, Rom 6:23, Gal 6:8, 1Jn 5:11, 1Jn 3:15 Strong's: G166, G2222, G622, G2288

    5. Aionios Modifying Judgment: Fire, Punishment, Destruction, Sin, Damnation: Key observations: (1) Mat 25:46 uses kolasis (G2851, 'punishment/correction'), not basanizo (G928, 'torment'). Kolasis occurs only twice in the NT. (2) 2Th 1:9 uses olethros (G3639, 'destruction'), a word uniformly meaning ruin/death in all 4 NT occurrences. 'Everlasting destruction' = permanent destruction, not endless destroying. (3) Mar 3:29 reads 'eternal sin' (hamartias aioniou) in the critical text -- the adjective modifies the SIN, not a process of punishment. (4) Jud 1:7 calls Sodom's fire 'eternal' (pyros aioniou), yet Sodom became ashes (2Pe 2:6). The fire's eternality is in its divine source and irreversible effect, not in endless burning. (5) Heb 6:2 lists 'eternal judgment' (krimatos aioniou) among foundational doctrines -- judgment rendered once with permanent validity. (6) Mat 25:41 specifies the fire was 'prepared for the devil and his angels,' not originally for humans. Verses: Mat 25:46, Mat 25:41, Mat 18:8, Mar 3:29, 2Th 1:9, Jud 1:7, Heb 6:2, 2Pe 2:6, Mat 10:28 Strong's: G166, G2851, G3639, G928, G266

    6. The Hebrews Pattern: Aionios as Permanent Results of Completed Acts: Heb 9:12: Christ 'obtained eternal redemption' -- the redemption was OBTAINED (past tense, completed action), and its validity is aionios. The act of redemption is not ongoing; it happened once. Heb 5:9: Christ became 'the author of eternal salvation' -- salvation was authored/accomplished, and its effect is permanent. Heb 9:15: believers 'receive the promise of eternal inheritance' -- an inheritance received, not an endless process of inheriting. Heb 6:2: 'eternal judgment' is listed with baptisms and laying on of hands as foundational, once-delivered doctrines. The judgment is rendered; its verdict stands permanently. Heb 13:20: 'the blood of the everlasting covenant' -- the covenant is established; it does not expire. This pattern is directly relevant to Mat 25:46: 'eternal punishment' follows the same pattern -- punishment executed, its result permanent. Not 'eternal punishing' (ongoing process) but 'eternal punishment' (permanent result). Verses: Heb 9:12, Heb 5:9, Heb 9:15, Heb 6:2, Heb 13:20, Heb 9:14 Strong's: G166, G3085, G4991, G2817, G2917, G1242

    7. The 'Eis Tous Aionas Ton Aionon' Formula (~22 NT Passages): DOXOLOGICAL uses: Gal 1:5; Php 4:20; 1Ti 1:17; 2Ti 4:18; Heb 1:8; 13:21; 1Pe 4:11; 5:11; Rev 1:6,18; 4:9,10; 5:13,14; 7:12; 10:6; 11:15; 15:7; 22:5. JUDGMENT uses: Rev 14:11; 19:3; 20:10. When applied to God (Rev 4:9-10; 10:6; 15:7), the formula reflects God's genuinely endless nature. When applied to Christ (Rev 1:18), it reflects His post-resurrection immortality. When applied to saints' reign (Rev 22:5), it reflects their received immortality. When applied to judgment: Rev 14:11 directly echoes Isa 34:10 (Edom's 'forever' smoke from a judgment that ended). Rev 19:3 describes symbolic Babylon. Rev 20:10 names devil, beast, false prophet -- non-human/symbolic entities. Not a single 'ages of ages' passage in the NT applies this formula to literal, generic human wicked. The nature-of-subject principle applies here too: the formula's duration is determined by the nature of what it modifies. Verses: Gal 1:5, Php 4:20, 1Ti 1:17, Heb 1:8, Rev 1:6, Rev 1:18, Rev 4:9, Rev 4:10, Rev 5:13, Rev 7:12, Rev 10:6, Rev 11:15, Rev 14:11, Rev 15:7, Rev 19:3, Rev 20:10, Rev 22:5, Isa 34:10 Strong's: G165, G2316, G281

    8. LXX Usage: Aionios Translating Olam (H5769): The LXX data (H5769 -> G166, 100 co-occurrences, PMI 26.16) establishes a direct vocabulary bridge. Aionios also translates: dor (H1755, 'generation,' 23x), chuqqah (H2708, 'statute,' 20x), berith (H1285, 'covenant,' 20x), choq (H2706, 'enactment,' 14x). These translations show aionios was used by the LXX translators for 'everlasting statutes' (ceremonies that ended), 'everlasting covenants' (covenants that were superseded), and 'generational' contexts. Aidios (G126) has NO Hebrew source in the LXX -- it is a distinctly Greek word with no equivalent in the Hebrew OT. This is remarkable: the LXX translators, when rendering olam, consistently chose aion/aionios (the age-related word) rather than aidios (the strictly-everduring word). If olam inherently meant 'eternal,' aidios would have been the natural translation choice. The choice of aionios over aidios confirms that the translators understood olam (and therefore aionios) as 'age-pertaining' rather than 'strictly eternal.' Verses: Gen 19:24, Gen 19:28, Isa 34:10, Dan 12:2, Isa 66:24 Strong's: G166, G165, H5769, G126

    9. Matt 25:46 -- The Symmetry Argument and Its Resolution: The symmetry argument appears compelling but has multiple counter-evidence points: (1) TIT 1:2 PRECEDENT: In Tit 1:2, aionios appears twice in one verse referring to different temporal scopes (future life and past time). Same adjective, same verse, different durations. This directly falsifies 'same adjective = same duration.' (2) OLAM PRECEDENT: Dan 12:2 uses olam for both 'everlasting life' and 'everlasting contempt' -- identical grammar. The etc7-07 study demonstrated that olam's duration depends on its subject. Olam applied to God = genuinely eternal. Olam applied to Jonah's confinement = 3 days. (3) KOLASIS SEMANTICS: The punishment word is kolasis (G2851), not basanizo (G928). Kolasis occurs only twice in the NT. Its root kolazo (G2849) comes from kolos ('dwarf, cut short') -- correction by cutting/pruning, not ongoing torment. (4) HEBREWS PATTERN: 'Eternal punishment' follows the same pattern as 'eternal redemption' and 'eternal judgment' -- a completed act with a permanent result. (5) THE NOUN DETERMINES DURATION: Aionios modifies the NOUN. The life is genuinely endless because those who receive it are given immortality (1Co 15:53). The punishment is permanent because those who receive it are destroyed and do not return. The adjective describes permanence; the noun determines what kind of permanence. Verses: Mat 25:46, Tit 1:2, Dan 12:2, Heb 9:12, Heb 6:2, 1Co 15:53 Strong's: G166, G2851, G2849, G2222

    10. 2 Thess 1:9 -- 'Everlasting Destruction' (Olethron Aionion): Olethros (G3639) occurs 4 times in the NT: 1Co 5:5 (destruction of the flesh), 1Th 5:3 (sudden destruction), 2Th 1:9 (everlasting destruction), 1Ti 6:9 (destruction and perdition). In every occurrence, olethros means ruin, destruction, or death -- never 'torment' or 'ongoing conscious suffering.' Standard lexicons define olethros as 'ruin, death, destruction.' Paul wrote 'everlasting destruction,' not 'everlasting torment' or 'everlasting suffering.' If Paul meant endless conscious torment, he had vocabulary for it (basanizo/basanismos) and did not use it. The choice of olethros is deliberate: the penalty is destruction, and aionios modifies that destruction as permanent and irreversible. 'From the presence of the Lord' indicates separation from the source of life -- which produces death/cessation, not ongoing tormented existence. Verses: 2Th 1:9, 1Co 5:5, 1Th 5:3, 1Ti 6:9 Strong's: G166, G3639, G684

    11. Contested 'Ages of Ages' Passages: Rev 14:11, 19:3, 20:10: Rev 14:11 echoes Isa 34:10 verbatim: both describe ascending smoke that goes up 'for ever.' In Isaiah, this smoke comes from Edom's judgment -- a judgment that historically ended (Isa 34:11-17 describes animals inhabiting the territory). Rev 19:3 describes symbolic Babylon (Rev 17:5 says 'MYSTERY, BABYLON THE GREAT'). Rev 20:10 names three subjects: the devil (spiritual being), the beast (symbolic political entity), the false prophet (symbolic religious entity). NOT ONE of these is a literal, generic human being. When humans enter the lake of fire (Rev 20:14-15; 21:8), the text's term is 'the second death' -- not 'torment.' Rev 20:9 uses 'devoured' (kataphago) for human enemies -- destruction vocabulary. Rev 19:20-21 makes a sharp distinction: beast and false prophet are cast 'alive' into the lake, but the human 'remnant were slain' -- different verbs for symbolic vs. human entities. All three passages are within Revelation's apocalyptic framework (Tree 3 Gate 3 FAIL for literal ECT reading). No epistle, Gospel, or OT passage uses basanizo/basanismos for the final fate of generic human wicked. Verses: Rev 14:11, Rev 19:3, Rev 20:10, Rev 20:9, Rev 20:14, Rev 21:8, Rev 19:20, Rev 19:21, Isa 34:10, Gen 19:28 Strong's: G165, G928, G929, G2586, G2288

    12. Aionios for Divine Attributes and Heavenly Realities: Rom 16:26: 'the everlasting [aionios] God' -- God IS eternal, so aionios carries full eternal force. Heb 9:14: 'the eternal [aionios] Spirit' -- the Holy Spirit is divine, hence genuinely eternal. Heb 13:20: 'the everlasting [aionios] covenant' -- the new covenant established through Christ's blood, superseding the old. 2Pe 1:11: 'the everlasting [aionios] kingdom' -- Christ's kingdom has no end (Luk 1:33). 2Co 4:17-18: 'eternal [aionios] weight of glory' and 'things which are not seen are eternal [aionios]' -- the unseen heavenly realities contrasted with temporary visible things. Rev 14:6: 'the everlasting [aionios] gospel' -- God's message that spans all ages. 1Ti 1:17: 'the King eternal [ton aionon]' -- God as sovereign over the ages. These uses establish the maximal range of aionios. But they also confirm the principle: aionios describes what IS genuinely eternal because the subject IS genuinely eternal. The word reflects the subject's nature. Verses: Rom 16:26, Heb 9:14, Heb 13:20, 2Pe 1:11, 2Co 4:17, 2Co 4:18, 2Co 5:1, Rev 14:6, 1Ti 1:17, 2Th 2:16, 1Pe 5:10, 2Ti 2:10 Strong's: G166, G2316, G4151, G1242, G932

    13. Aionios in Non-Standard Contexts: Philemon, Gospel of Mark: Phm 1:15: Paul tells Philemon that Onesimus departed 'for a season [pros horan], that thou shouldest receive him for ever [aionion].' The contrast is between 'for a brief time' and 'permanently.' Aionios here clearly means 'for the rest of your life/permanently' -- not 'for literal infinity.' This is the same usage pattern as the Hebrew olam slave (Exo 21:6; Deu 15:17) where le-olam means 'for life.' Mar 3:29: 'But he that shall blaspheme against the Holy Ghost hath never forgiveness, but is in danger of eternal damnation [or: guilty of an eternal sin -- hamartias aioniou].' The critical text reads 'eternal sin' (hamartias aioniou), where aionios modifies the sin. The sin is irrevocable -- its guilt stands permanently. This is the same 'permanent result' pattern as the Hebrews uses. The sinner is not being punished forever; the sin itself has permanent, irrevocable standing. Verses: Phm 1:15, Mar 3:29, Exo 21:6, Deu 15:17 Strong's: G166, G266, H5769

    14. The Perish-vs-Life Contrast and the Immortality Question: John 3:16: 'should not PERISH [apollymi], but have everlasting life' -- two mutually exclusive outcomes. Rom 6:23: 'the wages of sin is DEATH [thanatos]; but the gift of God is eternal life' -- death as wages, life as gift. Gal 6:8: 'soweth to the flesh shall of the flesh reap CORRUPTION [phthora]; but he that soweth to the Spirit shall of the Spirit reap life everlasting' -- corruption/decay vs. life. Mat 7:13-14: 'broad is the way, that leadeth to DESTRUCTION [apoleia]... narrow is the way, which leadeth unto LIFE.' Php 3:19: 'whose end is DESTRUCTION [apoleia].' 1Ti 6:16: God ALONE [monos] has immortality. 1Co 15:53: mortal must PUT ON [enduo] immortality -- it is not inherent. Rom 2:7: believers SEEK immortality -- seeking what they do not yet possess. ECT requires God to grant the wicked eternal existence for the purpose of suffering -- but Scripture never says this. The wicked are never said to receive immortality, incorruptibility, or eternal existence. Verses: Jhn 3:16, Rom 6:23, Gal 6:8, Mat 7:13, Php 3:19, 1Ti 6:16, 1Co 15:53, Rom 2:7, Mat 10:28, Eze 28:18, Eze 28:19 Strong's: G166, G622, G2288, G684, G110, G861, G862

    15. OT Passages Where Olam Clearly Does NOT Mean Eternal (LXX/Aionios Background): From the etc7-07 study: olam modifies slave service (= lifetime), Jonah's confinement (= 3 days), Samuel's temple service (= his years), the Aaronic priesthood (= until Christ changed it), 20+ Mosaic ceremonies (= until the cross), Solomon's temple (= until destroyed), and fire/smoke judgments on Edom and Jerusalem (= until completed). In EVERY verifiable case where olam modifies something with a known endpoint, it means 'for the duration of that thing's existence' -- not 'for literal infinity.' The LXX translates these same passages using aion/aionios, giving the Greek words identical context-dependent semantics. This is the foundation of the nature-of-subject principle: olam/aionios takes its actual duration from the nature of its subject. When the subject is God (eternal by nature), the duration is genuinely eternal. When the subject is a mortal institution, creature, or judgment, the duration matches the subject's inherent limits. Verses: Isa 34:10, Jer 17:27, Dan 12:2, Isa 66:24, Gen 19:24, 2Pe 2:6, Mal 4:1, Mal 4:3, Psa 37:10, Oba 1:16 Strong's: H5769, G166, G165
