    Verses¶

    Question¶

    Greek aionios (G166) -- all 71 NT occurrences. Compare aidios (G126). 'Eis tous aionas ton aionon' in ~20 passages. LXX usage. Does aionios inherently mean 'eternal/endless' or does it mean 'pertaining to the age' with duration determined by the nature of the subject?

    Aionios modifying fire -- 'everlasting fire'; parallel with 18:9 'gehenna fire'¶

    Wherefore if thy hand or thy foot offend thee, cut them off, and cast [them] from thee: it is better for thee to enter into life halt or maimed, rather than having two hands or two feet to be cast into everlasting fire.

    Context: Aionios modifying fire. Parallel verse Mat 18:9 uses 'hell fire' (gehenna). Same fire described two ways: 'everlasting fire' = 'gehenna fire'.

    Notes: Focus Area 5: Aionios modifying judgment. The parallel with gehenna in v.9 shows 'everlasting fire' and 'gehenna fire' are synonymous.

    Aionios fire -- 'everlasting fire, prepared for the devil and his angels'¶

    Then shall he say also unto them on the left hand, Depart from me, ye cursed, into everlasting fire, prepared for the devil and his angels:

    Context: The fire is 'prepared for the devil and his angels' -- not originally designed for humans. Greek: to pyr to aionion to hetoimasmenon to diabolo kai tois angelois autou.

    Notes: Focus Area 5. Greek shows double article construction: to pyr to aionion -- 'the fire, the eternal one.' Fire was prepared (hetoimasmenon, perfect passive) for the devil, not for humans.

    CRITICAL: Symmetry passage -- 'everlasting punishment... life eternal'¶

    And these shall go away into everlasting punishment: but the righteous into life eternal.

    Context: The central symmetry passage. Same adjective aionios modifies both kolasin (punishment) and zoen (life). Focus Areas 5, 6, 9.

    Notes: Focus Area 9: The symmetry argument. Parsing shows kolasin aionion (Acc Sg F + Acc Sg F) and zoen aionion (Acc Sg F + Acc Sg F) -- identical grammatical construction. But Tit 1:2 uses same adjective for different temporal scopes in one verse. Kolasis (G2851) from kolazo (to curtail/cut short), not basanizo (to torment). Hebrews pattern: 'eternal punishment' = permanent result of completed act.

    Aionios sin -- 'eternal sin/damnation' -- adjective modifies the sin, not a process¶

    But he that shall blaspheme against the Holy Ghost hath never forgiveness, but is in danger of eternal damnation:

    Context: Critical text reads aioniou hamartematos -- 'eternal sin.' The adjective modifies the SIN itself, not a process of punishment. The sin's guilt stands permanently.

    Notes: Focus Area 13. Parsing confirms: aioniou (G166, Gen Sg F) modifies hamartematos (G265, Gen Sg N). The word is hamartema (a specific sinful act), not hamartia (sin as a condition). The sin is irrevocable -- permanent result pattern.

    Aionios life -- 'in the world [aion] to come eternal [aionios] life'¶

    But he shall receive an hundredfold now in this time, houses, and brethren, and sisters, and mothers, and children, and lands, with persecutions; and in the world to come eternal life.

    Context: Both aion (age/world) and aionios (eternal) appear together. 'In the aion to come, aionios life.' The root noun and its adjective in one sentence.

    Notes: Focus Area 1. The co-occurrence of aion ('age/world') and aionios ('eternal') in the same sentence highlights the etymological connection.

    Aionios life -- 'should not perish, but have eternal life'¶

    That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life.
