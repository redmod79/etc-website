    Word Studies¶

    Question¶

    Greek aionios (G166) -- all 71 NT occurrences. Compare aidios (G126). 'Eis tous aionas ton aionon' in ~20 passages. LXX usage. Does aionios inherently mean 'eternal/endless' or does it mean 'pertaining to the age' with duration determined by the nature of the subject?

    G166 — aionios¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G166-aionios.md

    Occurrences: 71

    Adjective from G165 aion ('an age'). 71 tokens in 69 unique verses across 19 books. Strong's: 'from G165; perpetual (also used of past time, or past and future as well).' KJV: 'eternal' (46.4%), 'everlasting' (30.4%). Dominant collocation: zoe (life) ~43x. Semantic categories: (1) Eternal life as gift, (2) Judgment/punishment, (3) Divine attributes, (4) Heavenly realities, (5) Pre-temporal reference, (6) Eschatological kingdom. Hebrew source: H5769 olam (100 co-occurrences, PMI 26.16), dor (23x), chuqqah (20x), beriyth (20x).

    Semantic categories: Eternal life as gift, Judgment/punishment, Divine attributes, Heavenly realities, Pre-temporal reference, Eschatological kingdom

    Key verses: Jhn 3:16, Mat 25:46, Rom 6:23, 2Th 1:9, Tit 1:2, Rom 16:25, Heb 9:12, Jud 1:7

    Relevance: PRIMARY TARGET. The central word of the study. All 71 occurrences must be categorized. Etymology from aion ('age') vs. aidios from aei ('always'). Past-time uses (Rom 16:25, 2Ti 1:9, Tit 1:2) disprove inherent 'endless' meaning. LXX translation of olam carries context-dependent semantics into the NT.

    G165 — aion¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G165-aion.md

    Occurrences: 120

    Masculine noun. 95 unique verses (120 tokens) across 22 books. Root noun of aionios. Strong's: 'properly, an age; by extension, perpetuity.' KJV: 'ever' (52.6%), 'world' (30.5%), 'and ever' (22.1%). 7 semantic categories: duration, intensified perpetuity ('ages of ages'), present age, age to come, time-span/epochs, created order, end of the age. Hebrew source: H5769 owlam (287x, PMI 31.41).

    Semantic categories: Duration/perpetuity, Ages of ages formula, This present age, Age to come, Time-span/epochs, Created order/universe, End of the age

    Key verses: Mat 24:3, Mat 12:32, 2Co 4:4, Heb 1:2, Rev 14:11, Rev 20:10

    Relevance: Root noun of aionios. The KJV translation 'world' 30x (meaning 'age/era') confirms the basic meaning as an age, not infinity. The 'eis tous aionas ton aionon' formula (~22 NT passages) derives from this word.

    G126 — aidios¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G126-aidios.md

    Occurrences: 2

    Adjective from G104 aei ('always'). Only 2 NT occurrences: Rom 1:20 (God's eternal power) and Jude 1:6 (everlasting chains). Strong's: 'everduring -- eternal, everlasting.' Denotes inherent, absolute eternality without reference to ages. Distinct from aionios which derives from aion ('age').

    Semantic categories: Divine attributes, Eschatological judgment

    Key verses: Rom 1:20, Jud 1:6

    Relevance: The comparison word. Greek has TWO words for eternity: aidios (from aei, 'always' -- inherent, absolute) and aionios (from aion, 'age' -- age-pertaining). Their coexistence as distinct words is significant. Jude uses BOTH in adjacent verses (aidios v.6, aionios v.7), choosing different words for different subjects.

    G104 — aei¶

    Occurrences: 8

    Adverb meaning 'always, ever, continually.' 8 NT occurrences. Root of aidios (G126). KJV: 'alway' (50%), 'always' (37.5%). Used for habitual/continual patterns: 'ye do always resist the Holy Ghost' (Act 7:51), 'alway delivered unto death' (2Co 4:11), 'the Cretians are alway liars' (Tit 1:12). Denotes habitual pattern rather than infinite duration. Establishes aidios's etymology as 'from always' -- inherent, absolute eternality.

    Semantic categories: Habitual/continual pattern, Persistent spiritual reality

    Key verses: Act 7:51, 2Co 4:11, Tit 1:12, Heb 3:10, 1Pe 3:15

    Relevance: Root adverb of aidios (G126). The etymology chain: aei ('always') -> aidios ('everduring') contrasts with aion ('age') -> aionios ('age-pertaining'). Different roots yield different semantic ranges.

    H5769 — owlam¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H5769-owlam.md

    Occurrences: 432

    Masculine noun. 406 unique verses in OT (432 tokens). From unused root related to H5956 alam (concealment). Core image: 'the vanishing point' -- time receding beyond sight. KJV: 'for ever' (48.8%), 'everlasting' (13.3%), 'perpetual' (5.4%), 'of old' (3.2%). 6 semantic categories: divine attributes, covenants, cultic ordinances, immemorial past, eschatological, permanent judgment. LXX: aion (287x, PMI 5.55), aionios (100x, PMI 5.67).

    Semantic categories: Divine eternity, Covenants, Cultic ordinances, Immemorial past, Eschatological, Permanent judgment

    Key verses: Psa 90:2, Gen 17:7, Dan 12:2, Isa 34:10, Exo 21:6

    Relevance: Hebrew source of aionios in LXX (100x). The nature-of-subject principle: olam applied to God = genuinely eternal; olam applied to slave service = lifetime; olam applied to Jonah = 3 days; olam applied to fire/smoke judgments = until completed. Aionios inherits this same context-dependent semantics.

    G2851 — kolasis¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2851-kolasis.md

    Occurrences: 2

    Feminine noun. Only 2 NT occurrences: Mat 25:46 ('everlasting punishment') and 1Jn 4:18 ('fear hath torment'). Root kolazo (G2849) from kolos ('dwarf, cut short') -- corrective pruning/curtailing. Distinguished from basanizo (torment) and timoria (retributive penalty). Louw-Nida domain: 38 (Punish).

    Semantic categories: Eschatological punishment, Tormenting quality of fear

    Key verses: Mat 25:46, 1Jn 4:18

    Relevance: The punishment word in Mat 25:46. Critical: Jesus chose kolasis (correction/curtailing), NOT basanizo (torment). Root kolazo means 'to cut short' -- penal infliction with corrective/judicial sense, not ongoing torture.

    G3639 — olethros¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G3639-olethros.md

    Occurrences: 4

    Masculine noun. 4 NT occurrences, all in Pauline epistles: 1Co 5:5 (destruction of flesh), 1Th 5:3 (sudden destruction), 2Th 1:9 (everlasting destruction), 1Ti 6:9 (destruction and perdition). From ollymi (to destroy). KJV: uniformly 'destruction.' Louw-Nida domain: 20.3 (Destroy). Every occurrence means ruin/destruction/death, never torment.

    Semantic categories: Disciplinary destruction, Eschatological destruction, Paired with perdition

    Key verses: 2Th 1:9, 1Th 5:3, 1Co 5:5, 1Ti 6:9

    Relevance: The destruction word in 2Th 1:9. Paul wrote 'everlasting DESTRUCTION' (olethron aionion), not 'everlasting torment.' If Paul meant endless conscious torment, he had vocabulary for it (basanizo) and did not use it.

    G928 — basanizo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G928-basanizo.md

    Occurrences: 12

    Verb. 12 tokens in 12 unique verses across 6 books. From basanos ('touchstone for testing metals'). KJV: 'tormented' and variants (58.3%), plus 'tossed', 'toiling', 'vexed', 'pained' (42%). 3 categories: Gospels (physical suffering, demonic encounters, 6x), Epistles (moral distress, 1x = 2Pe 2:8), Revelation (eschatological torment, 5x). All 5 judgment-context uses are in Revelation or demons' speech.

    Semantic categories: Physical suffering, Demonic encounters, Moral distress, Eschatological torment

    Key verses: Mat 8:29, Rev 14:10, Rev 20:10, 2Pe 2:8

    Relevance: The torment verb. Key distinction: Mat 25:46 uses kolasis, NOT basanizo. No epistle or Gospel uses basanizo for human final fate. All 5 judgment uses are in Revelation's apocalyptic framework or involve demons.

    G929 — basanismos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G929-basanismos.md

    Occurrences: 6

    Masculine noun. 6 NT occurrences, ALL in Revelation: Rev 9:5 (demonic torment), Rev 14:11 (smoke of torment), Rev 18:7, 18:10, 18:15 (Babylon's torment). 3 of 6 describe Babylon's completed destruction. Antonym: anapausis (rest, G372) -- explicitly contrasted in Rev 14:11.

    Semantic categories: Demonic torment, Eschatological judgment, Commercial/imperial torment

    Key verses: Rev 14:11, Rev 9:5, Rev 18:7, Rev 18:10, Rev 18:15

    Relevance: The torment noun. ALL 6 occurrences are in Revelation. 3 of 6 describe Babylon's COMPLETED destruction. Zero occurrences outside Revelation. No epistle or Gospel uses basanismos for human final fate.

    G622 — apollymi¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G622-apollymi.md

    Occurrences: 102

    Verb. 86 unique verses (102 tokens) across 15 books. From apo + ollymi ('to destroy fully'). KJV: 'perish' (36%), 'destroy' (30%), 'lose/lost' (27%). Active = destroy; Middle/Passive = perish. Primary antonym: sozo (save, G4982). 7 semantic categories including divine judgment and eschatological perishing. Hebrew source: abad (H6, 141x, PMI 6.14).

    Semantic categories: Physical destruction, Divine judgment, Eschatological perishing, Lost/found, Decay

    Key verses: Jhn 3:16, Jhn 10:28, Mat 10:28, Luk 17:27, 2Pe 3:9

    Relevance: Primary perishing/destruction verb. The antithesis of eternal life in John 3:16, 10:28. If the wicked have eternal conscious existence, they have not truly 'perished.'

    G2222 — zoe¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2222-zoe.md

    Occurrences: 134

    Feminine noun. 126 unique verses (134 tokens), 22 books. From zao ('to live'). KJV: 'life' (73.2%), 'of life' (25.4%). The primary collocate of aionios (~43x 'eternal life'). 6 semantic categories: eternal/eschatological life, present possession in the Son, life contrasted with death, symbolic objects of life, earthly life, divine attribute. Antonym: thanatos (death, G2288).

    Semantic categories: Eternal/eschatological life, Present possession, Life vs death, Symbolic objects, Divine attribute

    Key verses: Jhn 3:16, Rom 6:23, Jhn 17:3, 1Jn 5:11-12

    Relevance: The primary collocate of aionios. ~43 of 71 aionios occurrences modify zoe. The perish-vs-life and death-vs-life contrasts define the soteriological dichotomy.

    G110 — athanasia¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G110-athanasia.md

    Occurrences: 3

    Feminine noun. Only 3 NT occurrences: 1Co 15:53, 1Co 15:54, 1Ti 6:16. Alpha-privative + thanatos = 'deathlessness.' All 3 in accusative (direct object of 'put on' or 'has'). 1Ti 6:16: God ALONE (monos) has immortality. 1Co 15:53-54: mortal must PUT ON immortality at resurrection.

    Semantic categories: Eschatological transformation, Exclusive divine attribute

    Key verses: 1Ti 6:16, 1Co 15:53, 1Co 15:54

    Relevance: If God alone has immortality, the wicked cannot have inherent eternal existence. Immortality must be received (put on) at resurrection; it is not inherent in humans.

    G862 — aphthartos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G862-aphthartos.md

    Occurrences: 7

    Adjective. 7 unique verses across 4 books. Alpha-privative + phtheiro ('corrupt/decay'). KJV: 'incorruptible' (4x), 'immortal' (1x), 'not corruptible' (1x). Applied to God (Rom 1:23, 1Ti 1:17), resurrection body (1Co 15:52), heavenly inheritance (1Pe 1:4), imperishable seed (1Pe 1:23), inner character (1Pe 3:4).

    Semantic categories: Divine nature, Resurrection body, Imperishable reward, Imperishable source, Inner character

    Key verses: Rom 1:23, 1Co 15:52, 1Pe 1:4, 1Ti 1:17

    Relevance: Related to the immortality question. God is inherently incorruptible; believers BECOME incorruptible at resurrection. The wicked are never said to receive incorruptibility.

    G2586 — kapnos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2586-kapnos.md

    Occurrences: 13

    Masculine noun. 13 tokens in 11 verses. 91% in Revelation (10 of 11). 7 semantic categories: prophetic sign, incense, abyss smoke, judgment instrument, eternal consequence, divine glory, Babylon's destruction. Hebrew source: ashan (H6227, 20x co-occ, PMI 30.28). Key NT passages: Rev 14:11 ('smoke of their torment'), Rev 19:3 ('her smoke rose up').

    Semantic categories: Prophetic sign, Incense, Abyss smoke, Judgment instrument, Eternal consequence, Divine glory, Babylon's destruction

    Key verses: Rev 14:11, Rev 19:3, Rev 9:2, Acts 2:19

    Relevance: The smoke imagery in Rev 14:11 and 19:3 echoes Isa 34:10 (Edom's smoke). In Scripture, ascending smoke = evidence of COMPLETED destruction (Gen 19:28), not ongoing burning.

    H5331 — netsach¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H5331-netsach.md

    Occurrences: 44

    Masculine noun. 44 occurrences. From natsach ('to glitter from afar'). KJV: 'for ever' (47.7%), 'perpetual' (6.8%), 'never' (6.8%), 'victory' (2.3%). Collocates with olam (H5769) 6x in synonymous parallelism. Used in denials of perpetual divine wrath: 'neither will he keep his anger for ever' (Psa 103:9), 'I will not contend for ever' (Isa 57:16).

    Semantic categories: Perpetual duration, Denial of perpetual wrath, Victory/strength, Finality

    Key verses: Psa 103:9, Isa 57:16, Isa 34:10, Psa 77:8

    Relevance: Used in parallelism with olam to DENY perpetual divine wrath. Isa 34:10 uses le-netsach netsachim ('to perpetuity of perpetuities') -- the STRONGEST Hebrew 'forever' form -- for a judgment that ended.

    H1860 — deraon¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H1860-draown.md

    Occurrences: 2

    Masculine noun. Only 2 OT occurrences: Dan 12:2 ('everlasting contempt') and Isa 66:24 ('an abhorring unto all flesh'). From unused root meaning 'to repulse.' Denotes visceral revulsion -- the reaction of onlookers to something repulsive. In Isa 66:24, the object of deraon is CORPSES (peger), not living beings.

    Semantic categories: Object of horror, Everlasting contempt

    Key verses: Dan 12:2, Isa 66:24

    Relevance: The 'everlasting contempt' of Dan 12:2. Isa 66:24 shows what deraon describes: the revulsion of onlookers viewing dead bodies. Not everlasting conscious shame but everlasting abhorrence at the sight of the wicked's remains.

    G2304 — theios¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2304-theios.md

    Occurrences: 3

    Adjective. 3 NT occurrences: Acts 17:29 ('the Godhead'), 2Pe 1:3 ('divine power'), 2Pe 1:4 ('divine nature'). From theos. Context word for Rom 1:20 where aidios appears alongside theiotes (G2305, 'divinity').

    Semantic categories: Divine being, Divine power, Divine nature

    Key verses: Acts 17:29, 2Pe 1:3, 2Pe 1:4

    Relevance: Context for Rom 1:20. Paul uses aidios (G126) alongside theiotes (G2305, derived from theios) to describe God's eternal, divine attributes.

    H5703 — ad¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H5703-ad.md

    Occurrences: 51

    Masculine noun. 48 unique verses (51 tokens). From adah ('to advance'). KJV: 'for ever' (52.1%), 'and ever' (29.2%). Paired with olam in le-olam va-ed ('for ever and ever') 20x. The endpoint intensifier -- adds the idea of perpetuity advancing to a terminus. Combined with olam, implies olam alone is not maximal.

    Semantic categories: Paired with olam in doxology, Independent duration, God's nature, Messianic, Earthly permanence, Primordial

    Key verses: Exo 15:18, Psa 45:6, Isa 57:15

    Relevance: The le-olam va-ed formula implies olam alone is not maximal -- it needs intensification. LXX renders both olam and ad as aion (G165), collapsing the Hebrew distinction.
