    Word Studies¶

    Question¶

    Major judgment passages: Rev 20:11-15, Matt 25:31-46, Dan 12:2, Isa 66:22-24, John 5:28-29, 2 Thess 1:7-10, Heb 10:26-31, Rom 2:5-11. Each analyzed for E/N/I tier.

    G2851 — kolasis¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2851-kolasis.md

    Occurrences: 2

    Feminine noun from kolazo (G2849, 'to curtail/prune'). Only 2 NT uses: Matt 25:46 ('everlasting punishment') and 1 John 4:18 ('fear hath torment'). Louw-Nida Domain 38 (Punish). The root meaning is penal infliction with judicial/deterrent sense, distinct from basanismos (experiential torment process). KJV translates as 'punishment' (1x) and 'torment' (1x).

    Semantic categories: eschatological judicial punishment, tormenting quality of fear

    Key verses: Matt 25:46, 1 John 4:18

    Relevance: Core debate word in Matt 25:46. With only 2 NT uses, kolasis is too rare for extensive distributional analysis. Root kolazo means 'to curtail/prune' -- distinct from basanismos. Louw-Nida places it in the Punish domain alongside krisis, not in the Torture domain.

    G166 — aionios¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G166-aionios.md

    Occurrences: 71

    Adjective from aion (G165, 'an age'). 71 tokens in 69 unique verses across 19 books. Means 'pertaining to the age(s)' with actual duration determined by the nature of the subject. ~60% modify 'life'; ~10% modify judgment subjects. Three past-time uses prove it does not inherently mean 'endless' (Rom 16:25, 2 Tim 1:9, Tit 1:2). LXX translates olam (H5769) as aionios 100 times -- inheriting olam's context-dependent semantics.

    Semantic categories: eternal life as gift, eternal judgment/punishment, eternal attributes of God, eternal realities, eternal purpose/pre-temporal

    Key verses: Matt 25:46, 2 Thess 1:9, Jude 1:7, Heb 5:9, Heb 6:2, Heb 9:12, Tit 1:2

    Relevance: Modifies 'punishment' (Matt 25:46), 'destruction' (2 Thess 1:9), 'fire' (Matt 25:41, Jude 1:7). The etc7-08 study falsified the symmetry argument (same adjective = same duration) via Tit 1:2. The Hebrews pattern (aionios + result-noun = permanent result of completed act) governs interpretation across judgment passages.

    G622 — apollymi¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G622-apollymi.md

    Occurrences: 102

    Verb from apo + ollymi ('to destroy fully'). 102 tokens in 86 unique verses across 15 books. Active voice: to destroy/kill. Middle/passive: to perish/be lost. Louw-Nida classifies in BOTH Violence/Destroy (20.003) AND Exist/Non-exist (13.003). The apollymi-sozo binary spans 14 verses. Primary LXX bridge: abad (H6) -> apollymi (141 co-occurrences, PMI 6.14).

    Semantic categories: physical destruction/killing, divine judgment/eschatological ruin, perishing in eternal judgment, losing one's life/soul, losing a possession, decay/perishability

    Key verses: John 3:16, Matt 10:28, Rom 2:12, Luke 17:29, 2 Pet 3:9

    Relevance: The standard NT word for the wicked's final fate in the perish-vs-life binary. Used in Rom 2:12 for sinners 'perishing.' No lexicon defines apollymi as 'torment' -- the semantic core is ending/cessation. The etc7-06 study established this at E-tier.

    G684 — apoleia¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G684-apoleia.md

    Occurrences: 20

    Feminine noun from apollymi (G622). 20 tokens in 19 unique verses across 11 books. Louw-Nida 20.031 (Violence/Destroy). Denotes ruin, loss, destruction. Binary with zoe in Matt 7:13 ('way to destruction' vs 'way to life'). Used in Heb 10:39 ('draw back unto perdition').

    Semantic categories: final eschatological ruin, son of perdition destiny-genitive, beast goes into perdition, waste/squandering, heresy-induced ruin, apostasy drawing back

    Key verses: Matt 7:13, Heb 10:39, Phil 3:19, 2 Pet 3:7, Rev 17:8

    Relevance: Used in Heb 10:39 as the alternative to 'saving of the soul.' Appears in Matt 7:13 as the structurally decisive binary with zoe. The etc7-06 study confirmed: no lexicon defines apoleia as 'torment.'

    G3639 — olethros¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G3639-olethros.md

    Occurrences: 4

    Masculine noun from ollymi ('to destroy'). ONLY 4 NT uses -- ALL mean destruction/ruin. (1) 1 Cor 5:5 disciplinary destruction (with explicit salvation-purpose clause); (2) 1 Thess 5:3 sudden destruction; (3) 2 Thess 1:9 everlasting destruction; (4) 1 Tim 6:9 destruction and perdition. All Pauline. KJV renders uniformly as 'destruction.'

    Semantic categories: disciplinary destruction, sudden eschatological destruction, everlasting destruction from God's presence, destruction paired with perdition

    Key verses: 2 Thess 1:9, 1 Thess 5:3, 1 Cor 5:5, 1 Tim 6:9

    Relevance: Paul's word for final judgment in 2 Thess 1:9. With only 4 uses, all meaning destruction, Paul deliberately chose olethros over basanizo. The only 'remedial' use (1 Cor 5:5) has an explicit salvation clause absent from eschatological uses.

    G2920 — krisis¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2920-krisis.md

    Occurrences: 47

    Feminine noun from krino (G2919, 'to judge'). 47 NT uses across 13 books. Means decision, tribunal, justice. Louw-Nida domains: Court of Justice (056.001), Judge/Condemn/Acquit (056.005), Punish (038.001). Primary Hebrew source: mishpat (H4941, 132 co-occurrences, PMI 5.38). KJV translates primarily as 'judgment' (80%).

    Semantic categories: day of judgment eschatological, judicial process/legal setting, present cosmic condemnation, righteous character of judgment, prophetic fulfillment

    Key verses: John 5:29, Matt 10:15, Heb 9:27, John 3:19, Rev 19:2

    Relevance: Used in John 5:29 for 'resurrection of judgment/damnation.' Krisis describes a verdict-event (judicial process), not a mode of ongoing existence. No NT use of krisis implies ongoing torment -- all 47 uses are consistent with judgment-as-event.

    G2288 — thanatos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2288-thanatos.md

    Occurrences: 119

    Masculine noun from thnesko ('to die'). 119 tokens in 106 unique verses across 15 books. The standard NT word for death. Antonym: zoe (life, G2222). Used in Rev 20:14 for 'the second death' -- the identification word for the lake of fire.

    Semantic categories: physical death, death as sin's consequence, death as cosmic power, Christ's atoning death, spiritual death/passage to life, death personified/second death, shadow of death OT allusion

    Key verses: Rev 20:14, Rev 21:4, Rom 6:23, Rom 5:12, 1 Cor 15:26

    Relevance: The identification word for the lake of fire in Rev 20:14 and 21:8. The text calls it 'the second death' (thanatos), not 'the second torment.' Death is consistently cessation of life throughout 119 NT uses.

    G928 — basanizo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G928-basanizo.md

    Occurrences: 12

    Verb from basanos (G931, 'touchstone for testing metals'). 12 NT uses across 6 books. 7 non-judgment uses (sickness, sea storms, moral distress); 5 judgment uses ALL in Revelation or demonic speech. Rev 20:10 is the ONLY verse with torment for final judgment -- and all three subjects are non-human/symbolic (devil, beast, false prophet).

    Semantic categories: physical suffering/illness, demonic encounter torment, moral distress, eschatological judgment torment

    Key verses: Rev 20:10, Rev 14:10, Matt 8:29, 2 Pet 2:8

    Relevance: Critical distributional finding: 0 of 8 major judgment passages use basanizo for human wicked. Basanizo appears in judgment contexts only in Revelation's apocalyptic framework and demonic speech. Never used for generic human final fate in didactic genre.

    G929 — basanismos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G929-basanismos.md

    Occurrences: 6

    Masculine noun from basanizo (G928). ALL 6 NT uses in Revelation exclusively. 3 of 6 describe Babylon's completed destruction (Rev 18:7,10,15). Rev 9:5 has explicit 5-month limit. Rev 14:11 'smoke of their torment.' Antonym: anapausis (G372, 'rest').

    Semantic categories: demonic/supernatural torment, eschatological judgment torment, commercial/imperial torment of Babylon

    Key verses: Rev 14:11, Rev 18:7, Rev 18:10, Rev 18:15, Rev 9:5

    Relevance: Exclusively Revelation vocabulary. 3 of 6 uses describe Babylon's COMPLETED destruction -- proving basanismos can describe a destruction-event, not necessarily ongoing torment. The antonym (anapausis/rest) uses the same construction as Rev 4:8 (living creatures' uninterrupted praise).

    H1860 — dera'on¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H1860-draown.md

    Occurrences: 2

    Masculine noun from unused root meaning 'to repulse.' ONLY 2 OT occurrences (Dan 12:2, Isa 66:24). Denotes an object of visceral aversion/revulsion. In Isa 66:24, applied to peger (corpses/dead bodies). Describes what the transgressors BECOME (objects of abhorrence), not what they experience.

    Semantic categories: object of horror -- eschatological corpses, everlasting contempt -- resurrection of the wicked

    Key verses: Dan 12:2, Isa 66:24

    Relevance: Links Dan 12:2 and Isa 66:24 through shared rare vocabulary. In Isa 66:24, the objects of dera'on are explicitly peger (corpses) -- the word describes how the living view the dead, not the conscious experience of the dead.

    H6297 — peger¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H6297-peger.md

    Occurrences: 24

    Masculine noun meaning carcass/corpse/dead body. 24 OT occurrences across 11 books. ALWAYS means dead body in every occurrence. Never refers to living persons. Used in battlefield dead, divine judgment on Israel, prophetic judgment imagery, and idolatrous contamination.

    Semantic categories: battlefield dead, divine judgment on Israel, prophetic judgment imagery, idolatrous contamination

    Key verses: Isa 66:24, Isa 14:19, Isa 34:3, Num 14:29

    Relevance: Decisive E-tier fact: peger ALWAYS means dead body in all 24 OT uses. In Isa 66:24, the worm and fire act upon peger (corpses), not upon living, suffering persons.

    H5769 — olam¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H5769-owlam.md

    Occurrences: 432

    Masculine noun from unused root related to 'concealment' -- 'the vanishing point.' 432 tokens in 406 unique verses across 34 books. Core image: time that recedes beyond the reach of perception. Duration is context-dependent, ranging from 3 days (Jonah 2:6) to genuinely eternal (Psa 90:2). 20+ ended institutions labeled olam. Fire/smoke judgments called olam historically ended.

    Semantic categories: eternal duration of God, everlasting covenant/promises, perpetual cultic ordinances, immemorial past, eschatological age to come, permanent judgment/irreversible desolation

    Key verses: Dan 12:2, Psa 90:2, Isa 34:10, Josh 8:28

    Relevance: Modifies both outcomes in Dan 12:2 (chayyei olam, dera'on olam). The nature-of-subject principle: olam life for immortal beings = genuinely eternal; olam contempt for corpses = permanent result. Fire/smoke olam judgments that ended (Isa 34:10, Jer 17:4) prove olam does not always mean 'endless.'

    G3709 — orge¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G3709-orge.md

    Occurrences: 36

    Feminine noun from orego ('to reach/stretch forth'). 36 NT uses. Dual Louw-Nida domains: Anger (88.024) and Punish (38.001). Denotes both emotional state (anger) and judicial action (punishment). Heavily concentrated in Romans (12/36) and Revelation (6/36).

    Semantic categories: divine eschatological wrath, divine judicial wrath, wrath and the law, deliverance from wrath, human anger as vice

    Key verses: Rom 2:5, Rom 2:8, Rev 6:16-17, Matt 3:7, 1 Thess 5:9

    Relevance: Used in Rom 2:5 ('day of wrath') and Rom 2:8 ('indignation and wrath'). The 'day of wrath' (hemera orges) frames orge as an EVENT, not an eternal state. Orge describes God's settled judicial displeasure expressed at the day of judgment.

    G2372 — thumos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2372-thumos.md

    Occurrences: 18

    Masculine noun from thyo (G2380, 'to rush/breathe hard'). 18 NT uses. Heavily concentrated in Revelation (10/18, 56%). Root image: heated breathing, boiling fury. Distinguished from orge as the more passionate eruption vs settled judicial anger.

    Semantic categories: divine eschatological fury, corrupting passion (Babylon's wine), satanic rage, human passionate outburst, vice/work of the flesh

    Key verses: Rom 2:8, Rev 14:10, Rev 16:19, Rev 19:15

    Relevance: Paired with orge in Rom 2:8 for the fate of the disobedient. In vice lists (Eph 4:31, Col 3:8), thumos names a distinct sin alongside orge. Neither thumos nor orge specifies eternal duration of the judgment.

    G2347 — thlipsis¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2347-thlipsis.md

    Occurrences: 45

    Feminine noun from thlibo (G2346, 'to press/squeeze'). 45 tokens in 43 unique verses across 15 books. Core image: external pressure bearing down. Louw-Nida 022.001 (Trouble/Hardship). KJV: 'tribulation' (40%) and 'affliction' (40%).

    Semantic categories: tribulation/affliction

    Key verses: Rom 2:9, 2 Thess 1:4

    Relevance: Used in Rom 2:9 ('tribulation and anguish upon every soul that doeth evil'). Describes suffering vocabulary but does not specify duration or eternality.

    G2719 — katesthio¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2719-katesthio.md

    Occurrences: 20

    Verb from kata (G2596) + esthio (G2068, 'to eat'). 20 NT uses across 7 books. Means 'to eat down/devour.' Concentrated in Revelation (7/20). Used for fire consuming (Rev 11:5, 20:9), literal eating (parable of sower), figurative exploitation, and consuming zeal.

    Semantic categories: literal consumption, fire consuming/destruction, figurative exploitation, consuming zeal

    Key verses: Rev 20:9, Rev 11:5

    Relevance: Used in Rev 20:9 for fire devouring human armies. The verb conveys total, irreversible destruction by fire. In Rev 20:9, the HUMAN armies are devoured (katephagen) while in the very next verse (20:10) the DEVIL is tormented (basanisthesontai) -- vocabulary differentiation by subject.

    G4442 — pyr¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G4442-pyr.md

    Occurrences: 74

    Neuter noun (primary word). 74 tokens in 73 unique verses across 14 books. Revelation dominates (25/73). Heaviest collocations: ballo ('cast,' 19x), theion ('brimstone,' 7x), katakaio ('burn up,' 7x), gehenna (6x). Primary LXX bridge: esh (H784, 316 co-occurrences).

    Semantic categories: eschatological punishment (lake of fire/gehenna), judgment parables, divine theophany, cosmic/apocalyptic plagues, testing/refining, figurative/metaphorical

    Key verses: Rev 20:14-15, Matt 25:41, Heb 10:27, 2 Thess 1:8, Mark 9:44-48

    Relevance: Fire appears in all 8 judgment passages except John 5:28-29 and Rom 2:5-11 (which use wrath vocabulary instead). Every OT fire judgment paradigm produces destruction/ashes/cessation, never ongoing torment.

    G861 — aphtharsia¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G861-aphtharsia.md

    Occurrences: 8

    Feminine noun, alpha-privative + phthartos ('corruptible'). 8 unique verses across 5 books. Exclusively Pauline. Means incorruptibility/immortality. Used in 1 Cor 15 resurrection chapter (4x) and Rom 2:7 ('seek for immortality').

    Semantic categories: resurrection transformation, sought by believers, ethical sense (sincerity)

    Key verses: Rom 2:7, 1 Cor 15:42, 1 Cor 15:53-54, 2 Tim 1:10

    Relevance: Rom 2:7: believers SEEK (zetousin, present active participle) aphtharsia -- seeking what they do not possess. This is an anthropological prerequisite: if immortality must be sought, it is not inherent.

    G1341 — dikaiokrisia¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1341-dikaiokrisia.md

    Occurrences: 1

    Feminine noun, compound of dikaios (G1342, 'righteous') + krisis (G2920, 'judgment'). ONLY 1 NT use (Rom 2:5). Means 'righteous judgment.' Frames divine judgment as inherently just.

    Semantic categories: righteous judgment of God

    Key verses: Rom 2:5

    Relevance: Only 1 NT use -- a hapax legomenon created by Paul to emphasize the RIGHTEOUS character of God's judgment. Frames the judgment in Rom 2:5-11 as just, not capricious or disproportionate.

    G5098 — timoria¶

    Occurrences: 1

    Feminine noun. ONLY 1 NT use (Heb 10:29 'sorer punishment'). From lookup: 1 occurrence, translated 'punishment' (100%). Related to G5099 tino (verb 'to pay a penalty,' also 1 NT use in 2 Thess 1:9). Timoria = retributive penalty focused on what the offender deserves. Distinguished from kolasis (judicial/deterrent) and basanismos (experiential torment process).

    Semantic categories: retributive penalty

    Key verses: Heb 10:29

    Relevance: The only NT use is in Heb 10:29 'of how much sorer punishment.' As a retributive penalty word (not a torment-process word), it describes what is deserved, not the mode of suffering. The comparison with v.28 ('died without mercy') sets death as the baseline.

    G5099 — tino¶

    Occurrences: 1

    Verb. ONLY 1 NT use (2 Thess 1:9 'shall be punished'). From lookup: 1 occurrence, translated 'shall be punished' (100%). Means 'to pay a penalty.' The verb behind timoria (G5098).

    Semantic categories: paying a penalty

    Key verses: 2 Thess 1:9

    Relevance: Used in 2 Thess 1:9 -- the penalty paid is olethros (destruction), not basanismos (torment).

    G4663 — skolex¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G4663-skṓlēx.md

    Occurrences: 3

    Masculine noun. Only 3 NT occurrences -- ALL in Mark 9:44, 46, 48, where Jesus quotes Isaiah 66:24 ('their worm dieth not, and the fire is not quenched'). The skolex is the maggot/worm that feeds on corpses, symbolizing the unending decomposition and disgrace of those who have rebelled. The image is drawn from the Valley of Hinnom (Gehenna). KJV translates uniformly as 'worm.' Hebrew source: towla (H8438, PMI 8.57) and rimmah (H7415, PMI 10.2). Collocates: teleutao (G5053, 'dieth not') 3x, pyr (G4442, fire) 3x.

    Semantic categories: decay and corruption of the unburied dead

    Key verses: Mark 9:44, Mark 9:46, Mark 9:48

    Relevance: The worm in Mark 9:43-48 quotes Isa 66:24, where the subjects are peger (corpses/dead bodies). The worm acts upon dead bodies, not upon living, conscious persons. In the source text (Isa 66:24), peger always means dead body in all 24 OT uses.

    G762 — asbestos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G762-ásbestos.md

    Occurrences: 4

    Adjective from alpha-privative + derivative of sbennymi (G4570, 'to extinguish'). Means unquenchable, inextinguishable. Only 4 NT uses: Matt 3:12 ('burn up the chaff with unquenchable fire'), Mark 9:43 and 9:45 ('the fire that never shall be quenched'), Luke 3:17 ('unquenchable fire'). All 4 modify pyr (G4442, fire) in eschatological judgment contexts. Describes fire that cannot be put out or suppressed -- fire that is irresistible, not necessarily fire that burns forever. Cf. Jer 17:27 where Jerusalem's gates 'shall not be quenched' but are not still burning.

    Semantic categories: eschatological judgment fire

    Key verses: Mark 9:43, Mark 9:45, Matt 3:12, Luke 3:17

    Relevance: Key word in Mark 9:43 gehenna passage. 'Unquenchable' means fire that CANNOT BE PUT OUT (irresistible) -- it describes the certainty and thoroughness of divine judgment. It does not necessarily mean fire that burns for infinite duration, as shown by Jer 17:27 where the same concept is applied to Jerusalem's gates (a fire that historically ended). In Matt 3:12 and Luke 3:17, asbestos fire 'burns up' (katakaio, G2618) chaff -- the result is complete consumption, not ongoing burning.
