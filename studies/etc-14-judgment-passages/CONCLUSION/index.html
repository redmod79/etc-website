    Conclusion: Major Judgment Passages¶

    Question¶

    Major judgment passages: Rev 20:11-15, Matt 25:31-46, Dan 12:2, Isa 66:22-24, John 5:28-29, 2 Thess 1:7-10, Heb 10:26-31, Rom 2:5-11. Each analyzed for E/N/I tier.

    Summary Answer¶

    The eight major judgment passages collectively use destruction/death vocabulary for human wicked (4 of 8), penalty/judgment vocabulary without specifying nature (2 of 8), and dead-viewed-with-abhorrence vocabulary (2 of 8). None of the eight uses basanizo (torment) for human subjects. The explicit destruction vocabulary appears in didactic texts across multiple authors (Paul, Hebrews author, Isaiah). Torment vocabulary (basanizo/basanismos) for final judgment is confined to Revelation's apocalyptic framework and applied exclusively to non-human/symbolic entities (devil, beast, false prophet). The anthropological prerequisite — humans do not inherently possess immortality (1 Tim 6:16, Rom 2:7, 1 Cor 15:53-54) — is embedded within the Rom 2:5-11 judgment passage itself.

    Key Verses¶

    Everlasting Destruction from the Presence of the Lord (2 Thess 1:9) Who shall be punished with everlasting destruction from the presence of the Lord, and from the glory of his power;

    Fire Shall Devour the Adversaries (Heb 10:27) But a certain fearful looking for of judgment and fiery indignation, which shall devour the adversaries.

    Perdition vs Saving of the Soul (Heb 10:39) But we are not of them who draw back unto perdition; but of them that believe to the saving of the soul.

    Sinners Shall Perish (Rom 2:12) For as many as have sinned without law shall also perish without law: and as many as have sinned in the law shall be judged by the law;

    Carcases of the Transgressors (Isa 66:24) And they shall go forth, and look upon the carcases of the men that have transgressed against me: for their worm shall not die, neither shall their fire be quenched; and they shall be an abhorring unto all flesh.

    Transgressors Consumed Together (Isa 66:16-17) For by fire and by his sword will the LORD plead with all flesh: and the slain of the LORD shall be many. They that sanctify themselves, and purify themselves in the gardens behind one tree in the midst, eating swine's flesh, and the abomination, and the mouse, shall be consumed together, saith the LORD.

    The Second Death (Rev 20:14) And death and hell were cast into the lake of fire. This is the second death.

    Everlasting Punishment vs Life Eternal (Matt 25:46) And these shall go away into everlasting punishment: but the righteous into life eternal.

    Dual-Outcome Resurrection (Dan 12:2) And many of them that sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake, some to everlasting life, and some to shame and everlasting contempt.

    Seeking Immortality (Rom 2:7) To them who by patient continuance in well doing seek for glory and honour and immortality, eternal life:

    Analysis¶

    The Vocabulary of Human Judgment: What the Eight Passages Say¶

    The eight passages divide into four vocabulary categories when describing the fate of human wicked.

    Category 1 — Explicit destruction/death vocabulary (4 of 8 passages):

    2 Thessalonians 1:9 uses olethros (G3639, destruction) modified by aionios. Parser confirms: olethron (N-ASM) aionion (A-ASM). Olethros has only 4 NT uses, all meaning destruction/ruin. Paul chose this word deliberately — he had basanizo (torment) available in his Greek vocabulary and did not use it. The only remedial use of olethros (1 Cor 5:5) carries an explicit salvation-purpose clause ("that the spirit may be saved") that is absent from all eschatological uses. The apo prosopou phrase ("from the presence of the Lord") echoes Isa 2:10,19,21 LXX verbatim, where people hide from the glory of the LORD because the divine presence is itself the destructive agent. Psa 68:2 confirms the same concept: "as wax melteth before the fire, so let the wicked perish at the presence of God." The wicked perish AT the divine presence, not by banishment FROM it.

    Hebrews 10:27 uses esthio (G2068, to eat/devour). Parser confirms: esthiein (V-PAN, Pres Act Inf). Fire "shall devour the adversaries" — devour is destruction vocabulary, a fire that consumes its fuel. The pyros zelos (fire of zeal) alludes to Isa 26:11 LXX. Hebrews 10:39 then provides the binary conclusion: apoleia (G684, destruction/perdition) vs peripoiesis psyches (preserving/saving of the soul). The Hebrews author frames the entire warning passage (vv.26-39) with destruction language at both ends — "devour" (v.27) and "destruction" (v.39).

    Romans 2:12 uses apollymi (G622, perish/destroy). Parser confirms: apolountai (V-FMI-3P, Fut Mid Ind). Sinners "shall perish." Apollymi has 102 NT uses; Louw-Nida classifies it in both Violence/Destroy (20.003) and Exist/Non-exist (13.003). No lexicon defines apollymi as torment. Paul uses this word for the final outcome of sinners within the same passage where he discusses the day of wrath (v.5), proportional judgment (v.6), and seeking immortality (v.7).

    Revelation 20:9 and 20:14 use katesthio (G2719, devour) for human armies (v.9) and thanatos (G2288, death) as the identification word for the lake of fire (v.14). Parser confirms: katephagen (V-2AAI-3S, "devoured") and thanatos (N-NSM, "death") in the predicate nominative formula. The text calls the fate of humans "the second death" — using the word for death, not the word for torment.

    Category 2 — Penalty/judgment vocabulary without specifying nature (2 of 8 passages):

    Matthew 25:46 uses kolasis (G2851, punishment). Kolasis has only 2 NT uses (Matt 25:46 and 1 John 4:18). Root kolazo means "to curtail/prune," distinct from basanismos (experiential torment). Louw-Nida places kolasis in the Punish domain (38), not in the Torture domain. The word denotes punishment without specifying whether the punishment is destruction or ongoing torment.

    John 5:29 uses krisis (G2920, judgment). Krisis has 47 NT uses, meaning judgment/decision/verdict. It describes a judicial event — a verdict rendered — not a mode of ongoing existence. No NT use of krisis implies ongoing torment. The passage describes "resurrection of life" vs "resurrection of judgment" — two outcomes from the graves, one defined by the positive result (life), the other by the judicial process (judgment/verdict).

    Category 3 — Dead viewed with abhorrence (2 of 8 passages):

    Daniel 12:2 uses dera'on (H1860, contempt/abhorrence). Dera'on has only 2 OT occurrences: Dan 12:2 and Isa 66:24. The word describes what the transgressors BECOME — objects of visceral revulsion — not what they experience. In its only other occurrence (Isa 66:24), the objects of dera'on are explicitly peger (H6297, corpses/dead bodies). Peger means dead body in all 24 OT uses — never living persons.

    Isaiah 66:24 uses peger (corpses) and dera'on (abhorrence). The living "go forth and look upon the carcases (peger) of the men that have transgressed." The worm and fire act upon corpses, not upon living, suffering persons. The context of vv.15-17 establishes that the transgressors are "slain of the LORD" and "consumed together" before the viewing in v.24. The chronological sequence is: slain/consumed (vv.16-17) → new creation (v.22) → corpse-viewing (v.24).

    Category 4 — Torment vocabulary for human wicked (0 of 8 passages):

    None of the eight judgment passages uses basanizo (G928, torment verb) or basanismos (G929, torment noun) for human wicked. Basanizo appears in the Rev 20 passage, but only in v.10, applied to the devil, beast, and false prophet — all non-human/symbolic entities. In consecutive verses, the text uses different verbs for different subjects: v.9 katephagen (devoured) for humans, v.10 basanisthesontai (tormented) for non-human entities. This vocabulary differentiation is a textual fact.

    The Aionios Question: Same Adjective, Different Subjects¶

    The adjective aionios (G166) modifies punishment terms in two of the eight passages: kolasin aionion (Matt 25:46) and olethron aionion (2 Thess 1:9). The symmetry argument — that because aionios modifies both "punishment" (Matt 25:46) and "life" (Matt 25:46), both must have the same duration — is a real grammatical observation. The same adjective does modify both outcomes in the same construction.

    The symmetry argument, however, does not necessitate identical duration. Tit 1:2 uses aionios for two different temporal scopes in the same verse (the etc7-08 study established this). The Hebrews pattern provides the governing framework: aionios + result-noun = permanent result of completed act. Eternal salvation (Heb 5:9) describes one act of saving with a permanent result. Eternal redemption (Heb 9:12) describes one act of redeeming with a permanent result. Eternal judgment (Heb 6:2) describes one act of judging with a permanent result. By this pattern, "eternal punishment" (Matt 25:46) describes one act of punishing with a permanent result, and "eternal destruction" (2 Thess 1:9) describes one act of destroying with a permanent result.

    The LXX connection reinforces this: aionios renders the Hebrew olam in approximately a hundred LXX instances. Olam (H5769) has 432 OT occurrences with context-dependent duration. Fire/smoke judgments called olam historically ended (Isa 34:10 — Edom's fire, Jer 17:4 — Jerusalem's fire). Twenty-plus institutions labeled olam have ended (Passover, Day of Atonement, Aaronic priesthood, circumcision as covenant sign). Olam does not always mean "endless."

    The Fire-and-Brimstone Paradigm: What OT Fire Produces¶

    The OT provides a consistent paradigm for divine fire judgments. Every recorded fire-and-brimstone event produces destruction, ashes, and cessation:

    Sodom and Gomorrah: "destroyed them all" (Luke 17:29, apollymi), "turning to ashes" (2 Pet 2:6), set forth as "an ensample unto those that after should live ungodly" (2 Pet 2:6). Jesus uses Sodom as the paradigm for parousia judgment: "even thus shall it be in the day when the Son of man is revealed" (Luke 17:30). If Sodom is the pattern, and Sodom was reduced to ashes, the pattern is destruction to ashes.

    Edom: "The smoke thereof shall go up for ever" (Isa 34:10, olam). Edom is not still burning. The "smoke ascending forever" describes permanent evidence of completed destruction.

    Malachi 4:1-3: The wicked become "stubble," the day "shall burn them up," they become "ashes under the soles of your feet." Every element describes total consumption and cessation.

    Jerusalem's gates: "I will kindle a fire... and it shall not be quenched" (Jer 17:27). The gates of Jerusalem are not still burning. "Not quenched" means fire that cannot be extinguished while it burns — irresistible fire — not fire that burns for infinite duration.

    This paradigm provides the interpretive context for "everlasting fire" (Matt 25:41), "fiery indignation" (Heb 10:27), "flaming fire" (2 Thess 1:8), and the "lake of fire" (Rev 20:14-15). The NT fire imagery draws on OT precedents that consistently produce cessation.

    The Anthropological Prerequisite: Can Humans Be Eternally Tormented?¶

    The question of eternal torment depends on a prior question: do humans possess immortality? The biblical data, including evidence embedded within the judgment passages themselves:

    God alone has immortality (1 Tim 6:16). Paul writes monos echon athanasian — "alone having immortality." The monos is unqualified.

    Immortality is sought by believers (Rom 2:7). Within the Rom 2:5-11 judgment passage itself, Paul describes believers as those who "seek for... immortality" (zetousin, present active participle — seeking what they do not possess). If believers must seek immortality, they do not inherently have it. The wicked, who do not seek it, do not receive it.

    Immortality is put on at resurrection (1 Cor 15:53-54). Endyo (G1746, "put on") describes acquiring something not previously possessed. The wicked never "put on" immortality.

    The soul can die (Ezek 18:4) and be destroyed (Matt 10:28). Nephesh/psyche is not inherently immortal. The phrase "immortal soul" never appears in Scripture.

    Without immortality, the wicked cannot be tormented forever. This is not an inference — it is a structural prerequisite. The data supporting it is at E-tier across prior studies (etc7-01, etc7-02).

    The Revelation Vocabulary Differentiation¶

    The Rev 20 passage contains the clearest vocabulary differentiation in all eight passages. In consecutive verses:

    Rev 20:9 — Human armies: katephagen (G2719, "devoured"). Destruction verb. Aorist active indicative — completed action.

    Rev 20:10 — Devil, beast, false prophet: basanisthesontai (G928, "shall be tormented"). Torment verb. Future passive indicative — projected forward. All three subjects are non-human/symbolic entities.

    Rev 20:14 — Lake of fire = "the second death" (thanatos, G2288). The identification word chosen by the text is death, not torment.

    Rev 20:15 — Humans not in book of life: cast into lake of fire. No torment vocabulary applied. The fate is what v.14 identified: the second death.

    This differentiation is encoded in the grammar. The passage uses active voice for human destruction (katephagen, active), passive voice for the devil being cast (eblethe, passive), and future tense for torment of non-human entities (basanisthesontai, future). The voice and tense shifts mark subject boundaries. The grammar reference analysis (rev-20-7-15.md) documents the progressive passivization and the tense-aspect map across the passage.

    The Proportionality Principle¶

    Romans 2:6 states: "Who will render to every man according to his deeds." This principle appears in 11+ passages across 9 authors (Job 34:11, Psa 62:12, Jer 17:10, Matt 16:27, Rev 20:12-13). Jesus applies it in Luke 12:47-48 ("many stripes" vs "few stripes") and Matt 10:15 ("more tolerable for Sodom").

    Proportional judgment — calibrated to knowledge, opportunity, and deeds — implies finite, measured punishment. "Few stripes" is incompatible with identical infinite punishment for all. If the punishment is proportional to deeds, and deeds are finite, the punishment is finite.

    The I-B Tension: Matt 25:46 and Dan 12:2¶

    Two of the eight passages create I-B items (competing evidence from both sides).

    Matt 25:46 presents the symmetry between kolasin aionion and zoen aionion. The grammatical parallelism is real (same adjective, same case, same construction). The ECT reading infers that the parallelism requires identical duration — punishment as long as life. The Cond. reading infers that aionios modifies the result (permanent punishment, permanent life) following the Hebrews pattern, and that kolasis (punishment, not torment) describes a completed judicial act.

    Dan 12:2 presents dera'on olam. The ECT reading infers that "everlasting contempt" describes the conscious experience of shame. The Cond. reading notes that dera'on's only other occurrence (Isa 66:24) applies it to corpses, and that the parallel construct chains (chayyei olam / dera'on olam) are governed by the nature-of-subject principle — life for those who receive immortality is genuinely eternal; contempt for those who are dead is a permanent result.

    Both I-B items are resolved below in the I-B Resolution section.

    Word Studies¶

    Olethros (G3639): 4 NT uses, ALL meaning destruction/ruin. Paul's word for the wicked's fate in 2 Thess 1:9. The etc7-06 study established at E-tier that all destruction words (abad, shamad, kalah, shachath, apollymi, apoleia, olethros) share a common semantic core of ending/cessation. No lexicon defines any of them as torment.

    Kolasis (G2851): 2 NT uses (Matt 25:46, 1 John 4:18). From kolazo "to curtail/prune." Louw-Nida Punish domain (38). Distinct from basanismos (experiential torment process). Denotes penal infliction without specifying whether the punishment is destruction or ongoing conscious suffering.

    Krisis (G2920): 47 NT uses. Judgment/verdict/judicial decision. All 47 uses are consistent with judgment as an event or verdict, not as a mode of ongoing existence. No NT use implies ongoing torment.

    Basanizo (G928): 12 NT uses. The majority are non-judgment contexts (sickness, sea storms, moral distress). The judgment uses are ALL in Revelation or demonic speech. Rev 20:10 is the only verse with torment for final judgment — and all three subjects are non-human/symbolic.

    Dera'on (H1860): 2 OT uses only (Dan 12:2, Isa 66:24). Objects are peger (corpses) in Isa 66:24. Describes what the transgressors become (objects of revulsion), not what they experience.

    Peger (H6297): 24 OT uses. ALWAYS means dead body/corpse. Never refers to living persons. Decisive for Isa 66:24 — the worm and fire act upon dead bodies.

    Aionios (G166): 71 NT uses. Means "pertaining to the age(s)" with duration determined by subject. Three past-time uses. Hebrews pattern: aionios + result-noun = permanent result of completed act.

    Timoria (G5098): 1 NT use (Heb 10:29). Retributive penalty — what the offender deserves. Not torment-process vocabulary. The comparison baseline is death (v.28).

    Difficult Passages¶

    Matt 25:46 — Kolasin Aionion¶

    The strongest ECT-cited verse among the eight passages. The grammatical symmetry with zoen aionion is real and must be addressed. The Hebrews pattern (aionios + result-noun = permanent result) and the Tit 1:2 precedent (same adjective, different scopes) provide the alternative reading. The word used is kolasis (punishment), not basanismos (torment). The passage is parabolic genre. The fire is "prepared for the devil and his angels" (v.41), not originally for humans. The I-B resolution (below) addresses this directly.

    Rev 14:9-11 — Smoke of Torment Ascending¶

    Uses basanismos vocabulary for apparent human subjects (beast-worshippers). The passage is apocalyptic genre (Gate 3). The "smoke ascending forever" formula draws on Isa 34:10 (ended fire). Rev 19:3 uses the same formula for Babylon (five-fold "found no more at all"). The etc7-11 study established that the smoke-ascending formula describes permanent evidence of completed destruction. (Examined in depth in etc7-11-smoke-ascending-forever.)

    Heb 10:29 — Sorer Punishment¶

    The "sorer punishment" (timoria) could mean ongoing torment worse than death, or permanent irreversible destruction worse than temporal death. The surrounding vocabulary (v.27 devour, v.39 destruction vs saving) favors the destruction reading. The text does not specify which kind of "sorer" is meant.

    Rev 20:10 and Human Fate¶

    The devil, beast, and false prophet are tormented in the same location (lake of fire) where humans are also sent. The inference that same location = same experience is not stated by the text. The vocabulary differentiation (humans devoured/second death, non-human entities tormented) indicates different outcomes for different subjects in the same location.

    Conclusion¶

    The eight major judgment passages present a consistent vocabulary pattern for the fate of human wicked. When NT authors describe final punishment for humans, they select destruction words (olethros, apollymi, apoleia, katesthio/esthio) and death words (thanatos — "the second death"). They do not select torment words (basanizo, basanismos) for human subjects. Torment vocabulary appears exclusively for non-human/symbolic entities in Revelation's apocalyptic framework.

    Six explicit statements use conditionalist-direction vocabulary — all verified by Tree 3 four-gate validation, all in didactic or prophetic genres, all using vocabulary that means destruction/cessation in every lexicon. No explicit statement uses ECT-direction vocabulary for human wicked. Three I-B items (the strongest ECT-cited texts among these passages) all resolve Strong toward conditionalist direction because the conditionalist-supporting evidence is Plain and didactic while the ECT-supporting evidence is Ambiguous and parabolic/apocalyptic.

    The anthropological prerequisite, embedded within the Rom 2:5-11 passage itself (v.7: seeking immortality not yet possessed), reinforces the vocabulary data: humans do not possess inherent immortality. Without immortality, the wicked cannot be tormented forever. The fire-and-brimstone paradigm from OT precedents (Sodom, Edom, Malachi's day) consistently produces destruction, ashes, and cessation — not ongoing torment. This paradigm provides the interpretive context for every fire-judgment text among the eight passages.

    The evidence tiers stand: 6 E-items (Cond.), 0 E-items (ECT), 17 E-items (Neutral), 5 N-items (all Neutral), 3 I-A (Cond.), 3 I-B (all resolved Strong toward Cond.), 2 I-C (ECT). The data speaks for itself.
