    03-analysis.md — Origins of ECT: Historical Channels¶

    Question¶

    Historical investigation (not doctrinal): What are the historical channels through which the doctrine of eternal conscious torment (ECT) entered Christian theology? Trace the Platonic influence, intertestamental literature, early church writers, and the progression from conditional immortality to innate immortality as church doctrine.

    Part I: What Scripture Teaches (The Biblical Baseline)¶

    This section establishes what the biblical text states about human nature, immortality, the death-state, and the fate of the wicked. These are the data points against which the historical progression is measured.

    1. Biblical Anthropology: Man IS a Soul¶

    Genesis 2:7 — "And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul."

    The creation formula is: dust (material) + neshamah (breath of life, H5397) = nephesh chayyah (living soul, H5315 + H2416). The verb wayehi ("became," Qal wayyiqtol of hayah) is transformative. Man did not receive a soul as a separate component; the composite entity IS the living soul.

    Genesis 1:20-21 — The same term nephesh chayyah is applied to animals before humans. Whatever nephesh chayyah denotes, it is shared between animals and humans. What distinguishes humans is the image of God (tselem, demuth), not possession of a different kind of soul.

    Ezekiel 18:4 — "The soul (hannephesh) that sinneth, it shall die (thamuth)." Nephesh can die. The Qal imperfect of muth (H4191) applied to nephesh directly contradicts the Platonic claim that the soul is an indestructible simple substance.

    Leviticus 21:11 — Nephesh meth: nephesh used for a dead body/corpse. The term that would later be equated with the "immortal soul" is used in the OT for a dead body.

    Matthew 10:28 — "Fear him which is able to destroy (apolesai, aorist active infinitive of apollymi G622) both soul (psychen) and body (soma) in gehenna." God's ability to destroy psyche establishes that the soul is destructible. This directly counters the Platonic Phaedo argument that the soul, being simple (non-composite), cannot be destroyed.

    Ecclesiastes 12:7 — "Then shall the dust return to the earth as it was: and the spirit (ruach H7307) shall return unto God who gave it." Death reverses creation. The ruach that returns is not a conscious soul — it is the animating breath. The word used is ruach, not nephesh.

    Job 34:14-15 — "If he gather unto himself his spirit and his breath; all flesh shall perish together, and man shall turn again unto dust." Life is sustained by God's breath. Removal of that breath = perishing and return to dust.

    James 2:26 — "For as the body without the spirit (pneumatos G4151) is dead." The word is pneuma (breath/spirit), NOT psyche (soul). This verse is often cited as proof that death is "separation of soul from body," but the actual word used is breath, not soul.

    2. Biblical Conditional Immortality¶

    1 Timothy 6:15-16 — "The blessed and only (monos G3441) Potentate... who only (monos) hath immortality (athanasian G110)." Paul states that God alone possesses immortality. The word monos in doxological contexts marks unqualified divine exclusivity.

    1 Corinthians 15:53-54 — "For this corruptible must put on (endysasthai, aorist middle infinitive of endyo G1746) incorruption (aphtharsian G861), and this mortal (thneton G2349) must put on (endysasthai) immortality (athanasian G110)." Mortals acquire immortality at the resurrection. The verb endyo describes acquisition of something not previously possessed.

    Romans 2:7 — "To them who by patient continuance in well doing seek (zetousin, present active participle of zeteo) for glory and honour and immortality (aphtharsian G861), eternal life." One does not seek what one already possesses.

    2 Timothy 1:10 — Christ "hath brought life and immortality (aphtharsian G861) to light through the gospel." If all souls already possessed immortality, Christ did not bring it to light — it was already present.

    Genesis 3:22-24 — "Lest he put forth his hand, and take also of the tree of life, and eat, and live for ever." God bars access to the tree of life to prevent eternal living. If man already possessed an immortal soul, this action would be pointless.

    Romans 6:23 — "For the wages of sin is death (thanatos G2288); but the gift of God is eternal life (zoe aionios) through Jesus Christ our Lord." The contrast is death vs. life. Eternal life is a gift — conditional, not inherent.

    1 John 5:11-12 — "God hath given to us eternal life, and this life is in his Son. He that hath the Son hath life; and he that hath not the Son of God hath not life." Life is conditional on Christ. Those without Christ do not have life — the text does not say they have tormented existence.

    John 3:16 — "Should not perish (apoletai, aorist middle subjunctive of apollymi G622) but have everlasting life." The binary is perish vs. life, not torment vs. life.

    3. Biblical Death-State Testimony¶

    Psalm 146:4 — "His breath goeth forth, he returneth to his earth; in that very day his thoughts perish (abdu, Qal perfect of abad H6)."

    Ecclesiastes 9:5 — "The living know that they shall die: but the dead know not any thing."

    Ecclesiastes 9:10 — "There is no work, nor device, nor knowledge, nor wisdom, in the grave (sheol H7585), whither thou goest."

    Psalm 115:17 — "The dead praise not the LORD, neither any that go down into silence."

    Psalm 6:5 — "For in death there is no remembrance of thee: in the grave who shall give thee thanks?"

    Psalm 88:10-12 — Rhetorical questions expecting "no": the dead cannot praise God, know nothing of God's wonders, exist in a "land of forgetfulness."

    Isaiah 38:18-19 — Hezekiah: "The grave (sheol) cannot praise thee, death cannot celebrate thee: they that go down into the pit cannot hope for thy truth."

    Daniel 12:2 — "Many of them that sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake." The dead are described as sleeping — unconscious, awaiting resurrection.

    Acts 2:34 — "For David is not ascended into the heavens." Even righteous David is not in heaven — he awaits resurrection.

    1 Corinthians 15:18 — "Then they also which are fallen asleep in Christ are perished." Paul's argument works only if the dead are not already in conscious bliss. If believers at death went directly to heaven, there would be no logical connection between "no resurrection" and "perished."

    1 Thessalonians 4:13-17 — The comfort offered to grieving believers is the resurrection at the last trump, not that their dead are already in heaven.

    John 11:11-14 — Jesus calls death "sleep" (koimao G2837) and then clarifies: "Lazarus is dead."

    4. Biblical Fate of the Wicked: Destruction Vocabulary¶

    Malachi 4:1-3 — The wicked become stubble, burned up, becoming ashes under feet. The result of eschatological fire is ashes, not ongoing torment.

    Psalm 37:20 — "The wicked shall perish... they shall consume; into smoke shall they consume away."

    Obadiah 1:16 — "They shall be as though they had not been."

    2 Peter 2:6 — Sodom and Gomorrah "turning them into ashes (tephroo G5077), condemned them with an overthrow, making them an ensample." Jude 1:7 calls the same event "eternal fire" — but the result is ashes.

    2 Thessalonians 1:9 — "Who shall be punished with everlasting destruction (olethron aionion) from the presence of the Lord." Paul's eschatological punishment word is olethros (G3639, destruction), not basanizo (torment).

    Matthew 25:46 — "These shall go away into everlasting punishment (kolasin aionion); but the righteous into life eternal (zoen aionion)." The word is kolasis (punishment), not basanismos (torment). Whether "everlasting punishment" denotes ongoing process or permanent result is the interpretive question.

    Isaiah 66:24 — "They shall look upon the carcases (peger H6297) of the men that have transgressed against me: for their worm shall not die, neither shall their fire be quenched." The subjects are peger — dead bodies, corpses. The observers look at corpses, not living beings in torment.

    Revelation 20:14 — "And death and hell (hades) were cast into the lake of fire. This is the second death." Hades itself is destroyed. The lake of fire is identified as the second death.

    1 Corinthians 15:26 — "The last enemy that shall be destroyed is death." Death is destroyed, not perpetuated.

    Isaiah 25:8 — "He will swallow up death in victory." Death is abolished.

    Revelation 21:4 — "There shall be no more death." This statement is incompatible with an eternal state in which beings perpetually die without dying.

    5. Torment Vocabulary Distribution¶

    Basanizo (G928): 12 occurrences. Eschatological use confined entirely to Revelation (5x). Revelation 20:10 subjects are the devil, the beast, and the false prophet — non-human/symbolic entities. Zero epistles use basanizo for human eschatological fate. Zero OT passages use basanizo-equivalent for final human punishment.

    Basanismos (G929): 6 occurrences. All 6 in Revelation. Never co-occurs with aionios. Revelation 14:11 says the smoke of their basanismos ascends — the grammatical subject ascending is the smoke, not the people.

    Gehenna (G1067): 12 occurrences. Vocabulary used with gehenna = apollymi (destroy, Matt 10:28). Basanizo never co-occurs with gehenna in any passage.

    The destruction vocabulary (apollymi G622, olethros G3639, apoleia G684, thanatos G2288) spans all genres: OT prophets, Psalms, Wisdom literature, Gospels, Pauline epistles, General epistles, Revelation. The torment vocabulary for eschatological human fate is confined to a single genre (apocalyptic) and within that genre to non-human subjects.

    Part II: NT Warnings Against Philosophical Syncretism¶

    The New Testament contains explicit warnings about the infiltration of non-biblical philosophy into Christian teaching. These texts form the biblical framework for understanding the historical progression.

    Colossians 2:8 — "Beware lest any man spoil you through philosophy (philosophias G5385) and vain deceit, after the tradition of men, after the rudiments (stoicheia G4747) of the world, and not after Christ." This is the only NT occurrence of philosophia. The single article joining philosophia with kenes apates (vain deceit) creates a hendiadys: deceptive philosophy. Paul identifies philosophy, tradition of men, and elemental principles (stoicheia) as threats to Christological truth.

    1 Timothy 6:20 — "Avoiding profane and vain babblings, and oppositions of science (gnosis G1108) falsely so called." Paul warns Timothy against syncretic knowledge claims. The word is antitheseis tes pseudonymou gnoseos — antitheses of falsely-named knowledge.

    Acts 17:18-32 — Epicurean and Stoic philosophers (philosophoi G5386) engage Paul at Athens. When Paul proclaims the resurrection, some mock (v.32). Those who held to some form of soul-survival found bodily resurrection absurd. The apostolic message was resurrection of the dead — not the inherent immortality of the soul.

    1 Timothy 4:1 — "In the latter times some shall depart from the faith, giving heed to seducing spirits, and doctrines of devils." Paul predicts doctrinal departure from the apostolic faith.

    2 Timothy 4:3-4 — "They will not endure sound doctrine... and they shall turn away their ears from the truth, and shall be turned unto fables (mythous G3454)."

    Acts 20:29-30 — "After my departing shall grievous wolves enter in among you, not sparing the flock. Also of your own selves shall men arise, speaking perverse things, to draw away disciples after them." Paul predicts both external infiltration and internal corruption.

    2 Peter 2:1-3 — "There shall be false teachers among you, who privily shall bring in damnable heresies... and many shall follow their pernicious ways."

    Galatians 1:6-8 — Paul warns that even a different gospel preached by an angel is accursed. The apostolic teaching is the standard.

    1 Corinthians 1:22-23 — "The Greeks seek after wisdom: but we preach Christ crucified... unto the Greeks foolishness." The apostolic message was not a philosophical system — it was the proclamation of a crucified and risen Messiah.

    2 Corinthians 11:3 — "As the serpent beguiled Eve through his subtilty, so your minds should be corrupted from the simplicity that is in Christ." Paul connects the serpent's deception of Eve to the corruption of Christian minds.

    Part III: The Serpent's Lie and Its Theological Echo¶

    Genesis 3:4 — "And the serpent said unto the woman, Ye shall not surely die (lo-moth temuthun)." This is the first recorded lie in Scripture. The Hebrew uses the emphatic infinitive absolute construction (moth temuthun = "dying you shall die") negated by lo. The serpent directly contradicts God's death warning in Genesis 2:17.

    Genesis 2:17 — "In the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die (moth tamuth)."

    John 8:44 — Jesus identifies the devil as "a liar, and the father of it."

    Revelation 12:9 / 20:2 — The serpent of Genesis 3 is identified as the devil and Satan.

    The hermeneutical observation: the first lie in Scripture is "you will not die." The first extra-biblical premise required for ECT is "the soul cannot die." If the human soul is inherently immortal and indestructible, then the serpent's statement in Genesis 3:4 was factually correct — humans do not truly die but continue in conscious existence. The biblical text states the opposite: nephesh dies (Ezek 18:4), psyche can be destroyed (Matt 10:28), thoughts perish at death (Psa 146:4).

    Part IV: Olam/Aionios Semantics¶

    Olam (H5769)¶

    Derives from alam (H5956, "to conceal"). The etymological meaning is time whose endpoint is hidden from view, not time without any endpoint. Duration is determined by the nature of the subject: - God = genuinely eternal (Psa 90:2) - Slave service = one lifetime (Exo 21:6) - Jonah's confinement = 3 days (Jon 2:6) - Aaronic priesthood = ended per Heb 7:12 (Exo 40:15)

    Every OT instance of olam fire/smoke describes a judgment that historically ended: - Edom: "the smoke thereof shall go up for ever (le-olam)" (Isa 34:10) — Edom is not still burning - Jerusalem: unquenchable fire (Jer 17:27) — Jerusalem is not still burning - Sodom: "eternal fire" (Jude 1:7) — result is ashes (2 Pet 2:6)

    Aionios (G166)¶

    Derives from aion (G165, "an age"), not from aei ("always"). Three past-time uses demonstrate aionios periods can end: Rom 16:25, 2 Tim 1:9, Tit 1:2. Titus 1:2 uses aionios twice with different temporal scopes in a single verse.

    The LXX translates olam as aionios approximately 100 times, inheriting olam's context-dependent semantics.

    Aidios (G126)¶

    The strictly-eternal word. Only 2 NT occurrences. Jude uses aidios for chains (v.6) and aionios for fire (v.7) in adjacent verses — demonstrating these are not synonyms. If NT authors intended "strictly eternal" for punishment, aidios was available; they chose aionios instead.

    Basanizo/Basanismos and Aionios¶

    Basanizo (G928) and basanismos (G929) never co-occur with aionios anywhere in the NT. The claim that "eternal torment" is a biblical phrase combines two words that the biblical text never combines.

    Part V: The Four "Hell" Words and Translation Conflation¶

    Four distinct Hebrew/Greek terms were collapsed into a single "hell" concept:

    Sheol (H7585): 68 occurrences. Realm of ALL the dead (righteous and wicked). Characterized by silence (Psa 115:17), darkness, unconsciousness (Ecc 9:10). Jacob expected to go to sheol (Gen 37:35). The KJV translates sheol as both "hell" (31x) and "grave" (31x) — this inconsistency facilitated the conflation.

    Hades (G86): 11 occurrences. NT equivalent of sheol. Temporary realm destroyed at the second death (Rev 20:14). Only passage associating hades with consciousness is the parable of Luke 16:23.

    Gehenna (G1067): 12 occurrences. Almost exclusively Jesus' word (11 of 12). Vocabulary = apollymi (destroy), never basanizo (torment). OT background is the Valley of Slaughter (Jer 7:32), a place of corpse disposal.

    Tartaroo (G5020): 1 occurrence. Applied only to fallen angels (2 Pet 2:4). Not a human destination.

    The Latin Vulgate translated all four as forms of "infernus." The KJV translated sheol, hades, gehenna, and tartaroo all as "hell." A reader encountering "hell" repeatedly would naturally assume a single concept — a place of eternal torment — when the original languages distinguish four very different things.

    Part VI: The Intertestamental Bridge (Historical — I-C Tier)¶

    The historical data for this section comes from external sources (Josephus, intertestamental literature, patristic writings). The biblical text does not narrate these developments. Therefore the historical narrative is I-C tier (compatible external information that does not contradict E/N statements but adds information the text does not contain).

    The Spectrum in Intertestamental Literature¶

    The intertestamental period (c. 300 BC - 100 AD) documents the progressive Hellenization of Jewish eschatology:

    1. Sirach (c. 180 BC): Denies immortality. Sirach 17:30: "the son of man is not immortal." Aligns with OT anthropology.

    2. Wisdom of Solomon (c. 50 BC): Introduces conditional immortality language. Wisdom 2:23: "God created man for incorruption." Wisdom 9:15: "the corruptible body weigheth down the soul" — Platonic body-soul dualism explicitly entering Jewish thought. This text is the earliest Jewish document to combine biblical creation language with Platonic anthropology.

    3. 1 Enoch (c. 300-100 BC): Contains BOTH annihilationist passages (48:9: "no trace of them") and proto-ECT passages (103:8: "darkness and chains and a burning flame"). The inconsistency reflects the transitional period.

    4. 4 Maccabees (c. 50 AD): Explicitly teaches ECT: "eternal torment by fire" (9:9). This is the earliest Jewish text to teach what would become the ECT position.

    5. 2 Esdras / 4 Ezra (c. 100 AD): 7:43 — "the furnace of Gehenna shall be made manifest, and over against it the paradise of delight." Compartmentalized afterlife fully developed.

    The Pharisaic Conduit¶

    Acts 23:6-8 — The Sadducees "say that there is no resurrection, neither angel, nor spirit: but the Pharisees confess both." The theological division reflects the Pharisaic adoption of afterlife beliefs that are not explicit in the Pentateuch.

    Luke 16:19-31 — The Rich Man and Lazarus parable uses intertestamental afterlife imagery (compartmentalized hades, Abraham's bosom, great chasm) that parallels 1 Enoch 22's description of compartments for the dead. Jesus uses culturally familiar imagery for a parable whose teaching point is the sufficiency of Moses and the prophets (vv.29, 31), not afterlife geography.

    Josephus documents the Pharisaic position: - Antiquities 18.1.3: Pharisees taught "souls have an immortal vigor" with wicked in "everlasting prison." - Wars 2.8.14: Pharisees believed "all souls are incorruptible" — the righteous pass into new bodies, the wicked suffer "eternal punishment." - Wars 3.8.5: Josephus himself (a Pharisee) presents the soul as "a portion of the divinity housed in our bodies."

    The Pharisees were the conduit through which Hellenistic soul concepts entered Jewish mainstream theology. This occurred during the intertestamental period, particularly under Alexandrian influence.

    Acts 17:18-32 — The Philosophical Incompatibility¶

    When Paul preached at Athens, Epicurean and Stoic philosophers engaged him. When he proclaimed the resurrection of the dead, some mocked (v.32). The mockery reveals the incompatibility: those who accepted some form of soul immortality found bodily resurrection unnecessary or absurd. The apostolic message was resurrection — not inherent immortality. If the apostles had taught that all souls are immortal and continue consciously after death, the Athenian philosophers would have had no reason to mock.

    Part VII: Early Church Trajectory (Historical — I-C Tier)¶

    Apostolic and Sub-Apostolic Period (c. 30-150 AD)¶

    The earliest post-apostolic writers taught conditional immortality: - Clement of Rome (c. 96 AD): immortality as "gift" (1 Clement 35:1-2) - Ignatius of Antioch (c. 110 AD): immortality through the Eucharist (Eph 20, Mag 10) - Theophilus of Antioch (c. 170 AD): man made "capable of both" mortality and immortality (Ad Autolycum 2.27) - Irenaeus of Lyon (c. 180 AD): immortality as gift, not nature (Against Heresies 2.34.2-3) - Tatian, Arnobius: taught conditional immortality

    The Platonic Turn (c. 150-250 AD)¶

    Athenagoras (c. 177 AD): first Christian writer to teach natural soul immortality. He was a former Platonist.

    The Alexandrian School: - Clement of Alexandria (c. 150-215 AD): explicitly merged Platonic philosophy with Christianity. Trained in Alexandrian philosophical tradition. - Origen (c. 185-254 AD): extended the Platonic synthesis. Taught the pre-existence of souls and universal restoration (apokatastasis). His philosophical framework, while condemned in part, established Platonic categories as standard Christian vocabulary.

    The Alexandrian school is the primary documented channel through which Platonic philosophy was systematically incorporated into Christian theology. Alexandria was the center of Hellenistic Jewish thought (Philo) and subsequently the center of philosophical Christianity.

    The Augustinian Synthesis (c. 354-430 AD)¶

    Augustine of Hippo: former Neoplatonist (trained in the tradition of Plotinus via Ambrose). Augustine combined Platonic soul immortality with eternal punishment to produce the ECT synthesis that became Western orthodoxy. In City of God (books 20-22), Augustine argued against the "merciful" party (who included Christians teaching annihilation) and insisted on eternal conscious torment.

    Augustine's Neoplatonic background provided the philosophical framework. His theological authority in the Western church provided the institutional framework. The combination was decisive.

    Conciliar History¶

    • Second Council of Constantinople (553 AD): Condemned universalism (Origenist apokatastasis) but did NOT condemn conditionalism. The distinction is critical: the church formally rejected the idea that all are eventually saved but never formally condemned the idea that the wicked are destroyed.
    • Fourth Lateran Council (1215 AD): Included ECT language in its creedal statement.
    • Fifth Lateran Council (1513 AD): First council to formally dogmatize the immortality of the individual soul (Apostolici Regiminis). This is 1,483 years after the apostolic period.

    The Translation Channel¶

    The Latin Vulgate (c. 382 AD, Jerome): translated sheol, hades, gehenna, and tartaroo as forms of "infernus." This single Latin word replaced four distinct Hebrew/Greek concepts.

    The King James Version (1611 AD): translated multiple Hebrew and Greek terms as "hell." A reader of the KJV encounters "hell" as though it were a single biblical concept, when the original text uses four distinct words with four distinct meanings.

    Part VIII: The Redefinition of Death¶

    Biblical Definition¶

    • Gen 3:19: "Dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return."
    • Psa 146:4: thoughts perish at death.
    • Ecc 9:5: the dead know nothing.
    • Ecc 12:7: dust returns to earth, spirit (ruach) returns to God.

    No biblical text defines death as "the separation of the soul from the body." The word used in James 2:26 for what departs is pneuma (breath), not psyche (soul).

    Post-Biblical Definition¶

    The definition of death as "separation of soul from body" entered Christian theology through Platonic anthropology. In Plato's Phaedo, death is the release of the immortal soul from the prison of the body. This framework requires: 1. That the soul is a separable entity (contra Gen 2:7: man IS a soul) 2. That the soul is conscious when separated (contra Ecc 9:5: the dead know nothing) 3. That the soul is indestructible (contra Matt 10:28: God can destroy soul and body)

    The redefinition occurred gradually during the patristic period as Platonic categories replaced Hebrew holistic anthropology.

    Part IX: Summary of Historical Channels¶

    The biblical data (E/N tier) establishes: - Man is a holistic being (Gen 2:7) — not a body containing an immortal soul - God alone has immortality (1 Tim 6:16) — mortals acquire it at resurrection (1 Cor 15:53-54) - The dead are unconscious (Ecc 9:5, Psa 146:4) — awaiting resurrection (Dan 12:2) - The wicked are destroyed (Matt 10:28, 2 Thess 1:9, Mal 4:1-3) — using destruction vocabulary across all genres - Torment vocabulary for human eschatological fate is absent from epistles, Gospels, and OT - The serpent's first lie was "you will not die" (Gen 3:4) — the soul's indestructibility reprises this claim - The NT warns against philosophical syncretism (Col 2:8), doctrines of devils (1 Tim 4:1), and turning to fables (2 Tim 4:3-4)

    The historical channels through which ECT entered (I-C tier, requiring external historical framework): 1. Platonic philosophy (c. 360 BC): Phaedo's arguments for innate soul immortality 2. Intertestamental literature (c. 300 BC - 100 AD): progressive Hellenization from Sirach (denies) through Wisdom of Solomon (Platonic dualism) to 4 Maccabees (full ECT) 3. Pharisaic adoption (c. 200 BC - 100 AD): Josephus documents Pharisaic belief in immortal souls and eternal punishment 4. Alexandrian theology (c. 150-254 AD): Clement and Origen systematically merged Platonic philosophy with Christianity 5. Augustinian synthesis (c. 430 AD): Neoplatonic soul immortality + eternal punishment = ECT orthodoxy 6. Translation conflation: Vulgate (infernus for 4 words), KJV ("hell" for 4 words) collapsed distinct concepts into one 7. Conciliar dogmatization: Fifth Lateran Council (1513) — first formal dogmatization of soul immortality

    Each channel required overriding, reinterpreting, or ignoring specific biblical data: - Channel 1 overrides Gen 2:7 (holistic anthropology), Ezek 18:4 (soul dies), Matt 10:28 (soul destroyed) - Channel 2 displaces Ecc 9:5 (dead know nothing), Psa 146:4 (thoughts perish) - Channel 3 introduces compartmentalized afterlife (no biblical basis for hades compartments) - Channel 4 introduces "separation" definition of death (no verse uses this formulation) - Channel 5 combines Platonic immortality with eternal punishment (combining extra-biblical premise with contested interpretation) - Channel 6 obscures the distinction between sheol/hades/gehenna/tartaroo - Channel 7 dogmatizes what no apostle, no prophet, and no earlier council had stated
