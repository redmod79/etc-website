    Verses¶

    Question¶

    How does the Hebrew mind understand smoke ascending up forever and ever? What does the smoke represent in the biblical worldview? This study focuses specifically on SMOKE IMAGERY — why smoke, what did it signify, how does it function across the OT and NT? The semantic range of olam/aionios has been established in prior studies (etc7-07, etc7-08) — this study builds on that foundation.

    Abrahamic covenant theophany — smoking furnace and burning lamp¶

    And it came to pass, that, when the sun went down, and it was dark, behold a smoking furnace, and a burning lamp that passed between those pieces.

    Context: God confirms His covenant with Abraham through a theophanic vision. The smoking furnace and burning lamp pass between the severed animal pieces — the earliest smoke-and-fire theophany in Scripture.

    Notes: The word 'smoking' here is H6227 ashan (noun). The furnace (tannur) is a pottery kiln. Smoke + fire together as covenant-ratification symbols.

    Paradigmatic smoke passage — Sodom's destruction and aftermath witness¶

    Then the LORD rained upon Sodom and upon Gomorrah brimstone and fire from the LORD out of heaven; And he overthrew those cities, and all the plain, and all the inhabitants of the cities, and that which grew upon the ground. But his wife looked back from behind him, and she became a pillar of salt. And Abraham gat up early in the morning to the place where he stood before the LORD: And he looked toward Sodom and Gomorrah, and toward all the land of the plain, and beheld, and, lo, the smoke of the country went up as the smoke of a furnace.

    Context: The fire and brimstone fall in v24. In v28, Abraham sees the smoke the NEXT MORNING — the smoke is the aftermath evidence, not the destruction itself. Uses qitor (H7008) not ashan — the rarer, denser smoke word. The kibshan (furnace) simile recurs at Sinai (Exod 19:18).

    Notes: Key observations from parsing: (1) shaqaph (Hiphil wayyiqtol 3ms) = 'he looked out/down upon'; (2) ra'ah (Qal wayyiqtol 3ms) = 'he saw'; (3) alah (Qal perfect 3ms) = 'ascended' — perfect tense for the smoke's rising, indicating completed action observed; (4) qitor appears twice — once for the actual smoke, once in the furnace simile. The temporal sequence is crucial: fire (v24, past) → overthrow (v25, past) → Abraham looks (v27-28, morning after) → beholds smoke (v28, present evidence of past event).

    Sinai theophany — the mountain smoked because God descended in fire¶

    And it came to pass on the third day in the morning, that there were thunders and lightnings, and a thick cloud upon the mount, and the voice of the trumpet exceeding loud; so that all the people that was in the camp trembled. And Moses brought forth the people out of the camp to meet with God; and they stood at the nether part of the mount. And mount Sinai was altogether on a smoke, because the LORD descended upon it in fire: and the smoke thereof ascended as the smoke of a furnace, and the whole mount quaked greatly. And when the voice of the trumpet sounded long, and waxed louder and louder, Moses spake, and God answered him by a voice. And the LORD came down upon mount Sinai, on the top of the mount: and the LORD called Moses up to the top of the mount; and Moses went up.

    Context: The prototypical theophanic smoke passage. V18 is key: the mountain 'smoked' (ashan H6225, verb Qal perfect 3ms) BECAUSE God descended in fire. The cause-effect is explicit. Uses the same kibshan (furnace) simile as Gen 19:28.

    Notes: Parsing confirms: (1) ashan is verbal (Qal perf 3ms) — 'the mountain smoked as a whole'; (2) mipnei asher = 'because' — explicit causal clause; (3) yarad (Qal perf 3ms) = 'descended' — YHWH's descent is the cause of the smoking; (4) ya'al (Qal wayyiqtol 3ms) = 'ascended' — the smoke's ascent paired with God's descent; (5) ashano (smoke-of-it) uses the ashan noun (H6227) with 3ms suffix; (6) kibshan (kiln/furnace) — exact same word as Gen 19:28.

    People's response to theophanic smoke — fear and distance¶

    And all the people saw the thunderings, and the lightnings, and the noise of the trumpet, and the mountain smoking: and when the people saw it, they removed, and stood afar off.

    Context: Uses H6226 ashen (adjective, 'smoking') to describe the mountain. The people's response to the smoking mountain is fear — they stand at a distance. The smoke marks the boundary between holy and profane.

    Notes: The adjective ashen (H6226) appears here — only 2 occurrences in OT (here and Isa 7:4). The mountain is characterized as a 'smoking thing.'

    Temple theophany — house filled with smoke at seraphim's cry¶

    In the year that king Uzziah died I saw also the Lord sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up, and his train filled the temple. Above it stood the seraphims: each one had six wings; with twain he covered his face, and with twain he covered his feet, and with twain he did fly. And one cried unto another, and said, Holy, holy, holy, is the LORD of hosts: the whole earth is full of his glory. And the posts of the door moved at the voice of him that cried, and the house was filled with smoke.

    Context: Isaiah's commissioning vision. The temple fills with smoke (ashan H6227) at the seraphim's declaration of holiness. The smoke marks the boundary between the holy God and the unclean prophet. Note the seraphim (H8314, from saraph H8313 'to burn' — 'burning ones') are cognate with the burning/smoke vocabulary.

    Notes: Parsing: yimmale (Niphal impf 3ms of male') = 'was being filled' — imperfect suggests ongoing/continuous filling. ashan is the final word in the verse — smoke as the climactic element. The house (bayit) = temple. Parallels Rev 15:8.

    Eschatological cloud-and-smoke — protective theophanic presence over Zion¶

    And the LORD will create upon every dwelling place of mount Zion, and upon her assemblies, a cloud and smoke by day, and the shining of a flaming fire by night: for upon all the glory shall be a defence.

    Context: Isaiah envisions a future where God creates (bara') cloud and smoke over Zion — echoing the Exodus pillar of cloud by day and fire by night. Smoke here is protective presence, not destructive judgment.

    Notes: Uses anan (H6051, cloud) + ashan (H6227, smoke) together. The verb bara' (create) is the same used in Gen 1:1 — God will create a new Exodus-style theophanic covering.

    Heavenly temple filled with smoke from God's glory and power¶

    And the temple was filled with smoke from the glory of God, and from his power; and no man was able to enter into the temple, till the seven plagues of the seven angels were fulfilled.

    Context: Direct NT parallel to Isa 6:4 and Exod 40:34. The heavenly temple fills with smoke (kapnos G2586) from God's glory (doxa G1391). No one can enter — the 'no one could enter' motif appears in Exod 40:35, 1 Kings 8:11, and here.

    Notes: Parsing: egemisthe (aorist passive indicative 3s of gemizo G1072) = 'was filled'; kapnou (genitive singular) = 'with/of smoke'; ek tes doxes tou theou = 'from the glory of God' — ek + genitive shows SOURCE; the smoke comes FROM God's glory. edynato (imperfect middle/passive indicative 3s of dynamai G1410) = 'was able' — continuous inability to enter.

    Edom judgment — smoke ascending le-olam; THE source text for Rev 14:11¶

    And the streams thereof shall be turned into pitch, and the dust thereof into brimstone, and the land thereof shall become burning pitch. It shall not be quenched night nor day; the smoke thereof shall go up for ever: from generation to generation it shall lie waste; none shall pass through it for ever and ever.

    Context: Judgment oracle against Edom. V9: streams to pitch, dust to brimstone. V10: unquenchable fire, smoke ascending le-olam (H5769), waste from generation to generation, le-netsach netsachim (the strongest Hebrew 'forever' expression). This is the direct source for Rev 14:11's smoke-ascending formula.

    Notes: Parsing v10: laylah v-yomam = 'night and day' (time marker); lo tikhbeh (Qal impf 3fs of kabah H3518) = 'shall not be quenched'; le-olam (H5769) = 'to perpetuity'; ya'aleh (Qal impf 3ms of alah H5927) = 'shall ascend'; ashanah (ashan H6227 + 3fs suffix) = 'its smoke'; mi-dor la-dor = 'from generation to generation'; techerav (Qal impf 3fs of charab H2717) = 'shall be desolate'; le-netsach netsachim = 'to glory of glories' (strongest perpetuity expression). Key: v11-17 describes animals inhabiting this territory — the smoke ended, life returned.

    Aftermath of Edom's 'forever' smoke — animals dwelling in the ruins¶

    But the cormorant and the bittern shall possess it; the owl also and the raven shall dwell in it: and he shall stretch out upon it the line of confusion, and the stones of emptiness. They shall call the nobles thereof to the kingdom, but none shall be there, and all her princes shall be nothing. And thorns shall come up in her palaces, nettles and brambles in the fortresses thereof: and it shall be an habitation of dragons, and a court for owls. The wild beasts of the desert shall also meet with the wild beasts of the island, and the satyr shall cry to his fellow; the screech owl also shall rest there, and find for herself a place of rest. There shall the great owl make her nest, and lay, and hatch, and gather under her shadow: there shall the vultures also be gathered, every one with her mate. Seek ye out of the book of the LORD, and read: no one of these shall fail, none shall want her mate: for my mouth it hath commanded, and his spirit it hath gathered them. And he hath cast the lot for them, and his hand hath divided it unto them by line: they shall possess it for ever, from generation to generation shall they dwell therein.

    Context: Immediately after Isa 34:10 says the smoke 'shall go up for ever,' vv11-17 describe cormorant, bittern, owl, raven, wild beasts, dragons, vultures DWELLING in the territory. Animals nest, lay eggs, hatch, and rest. Life has returned. V17 says they possess it 'for ever' — the same le-olam. The smoke ended; the animals' possession also uses olam language.

    Notes: This is the decisive proof that 'smoke ascending forever' describes permanent memorial of completed destruction, not ongoing burning. The same passage uses 'forever' for both the smoke (v10) and the animal habitation (v17).

    The crux text — smoke of torment ascends eis tous aionas ton aionon¶

    The same shall drink of the wine of the wrath of God, which is poured out without mixture into the cup of his indignation; and he shall be tormented with fire and brimstone in the presence of the holy angels, and in the presence of the Lamb: And the smoke of their torment ascendeth up for ever and ever: and they have no rest day nor night, who worship the beast and his image, and whosoever receiveth the mark of his name.

    Context: The third angel's warning. Fire and brimstone echo Gen 19:24 (Sodom). The smoke-ascending formula echoes Isa 34:10. The 'day and night' phrase echoes Isa 34:10's 'night nor day.' The subjects are beast-worshippers.

    Notes: Parsing: kapnos (G2586, nom sg m) = 'smoke'; tou basanismou (G929, gen sg m) = 'of the torment'; auton (G846, gen pl m) = 'of them'; eis aionas aionon = 'into ages of ages'; anabainei (G305, present active indicative 3s) = 'ascends' — present tense for timeless/gnomic assertion; ouk echousin anapausin = 'they do not have rest' — present indicative. The present tense of anabainei is gnomic/descriptive, not necessarily continuous. Cross-reference: Rev 4:8 uses identical 'ouk echousin anapausin hemeras kai nuktos' for the four living creatures praising God.

    Second 'her smoke rose up for ever and ever' — Babylon's permanent memorial¶

    And again they said, Alleluia. And her smoke rose up for ever and ever.

    Context: Follows Rev 18's detailed description of Babylon's destruction — 'found no more at all' (18:21), fivefold cessation (18:22-23). The smoke formula is applied to an entity whose total destruction is documented in the most absolute language in Scripture.

    Notes: Parsing: kapnos (G2586, nom sg m) = 'smoke'; autes (G846, gen sg f) = 'of her'; anabainei (G305, present active indicative 3s) = 'ascends/rises' — identical verb form to Rev 14:11; eis tous aionas ton aionon = 'into the ages of the ages.' The present tense anabainei matches Rev 14:11 exactly.

    Kings see smoke of Babylon's burning — observers witness aftermath¶

    And the kings of the earth, who have committed fornication and lived deliciously with her, shall bewail her, and lament for her, when they shall see the smoke of her burning,

    Context: Kings observe smoke as testimony. The verb bleposin (G991, present active subjunctive) = 'when they see' — the smoke is visible public evidence. Parallels Gen 19:28 where Abraham sees Sodom's smoke.

    Notes: Parsing: bleposin (G991, pres act subj 3p) = 'they see'; ton kapnon (G2586, acc sg m) = 'the smoke'; tes pyroseos (G4451, gen sg f) = 'of the burning'; autes (G846, gen sg f) = 'of her.' The genitive chain: smoke OF the burning OF her.

    Merchants cry seeing smoke of burning — 'What city is like this great city?'¶

    And cried when they saw the smoke of her burning, saying, What city is like unto this great city!

    Context: Second observer group sees the same smoke. The response is the question 'What city is like this great city?' — the smoke communicates the totality of the destruction.

    Notes: Parsing: ekrazon (G2896, impf act ind 3p) = 'they were crying'; blepontes (G991, pres act ptcp nom pl m) = 'seeing' — present participle for simultaneous action; ton kapnon tes pyroseos autes = identical construction to 18:9.

    Key transience passage — the wicked consume 'into smoke shall they consume away'¶

    The LORD knoweth the days of the upright: and their inheritance shall be for ever. They shall not be ashamed in the evil time: and in the days of famine they shall be satisfied. But the wicked shall perish, and the enemies of the LORD shall be as the fat of lambs: they shall consume; into smoke shall they consume away. The wicked borroweth, and payeth not again: but the righteous sheweth mercy, and giveth. For such as be blessed of him shall inherit the earth; and they that be cursed of him shall be cut off.

    Context: Psalm 37's contrast between the righteous (inherit the earth) and the wicked (perish). V20 is the critical smoke passage: 'into smoke shall they consume away.' Smoke is the END PRODUCT of consumption — what remains after the fire has done its work.

    Notes: Parsing v20: reshaim (adj mp, 'guilty/wicked') yo'vedu (Qal impf 3mp of abad H6, 'perish') — the opening verb is 'perish'; oyevei YHWH (Qal ptcp mp const of ayab H341, 'enemies of YHWH') — enemies of YHWH are the subject; kiqar karim = 'as the preciousness/fat of pastures'; kalu (Qal perf 3p of kalah H3615, 'they are consumed/completed') — perfect tense, completed action; ve-ashan (H6227, 'in/into smoke') — smoke is the destination/end-state; kalu (repeated) = 'they are consumed.' The double kalah + be-ashan construction: consumed INTO smoke. Smoke is the cessation point.

    Wax-smoke parallel — 'as smoke is driven away' paired with wax melting¶

    As smoke is driven away, so drive them away: as wax melteth before the fire, so let the wicked perish at the presence of God.

    Context: KJV v2 = Hebrew v3. Three-part simile for the wicked's fate: (1) driven away like smoke, (2) melting like wax before fire, (3) perishing at God's presence. All three describe things that cease to exist.

    Notes: Parsing: ke-hindoph (Qal inf const of nadaph H5086) ashan = 'as the driving-away of smoke'; tindoph (Qal impf 2ms of nadaph) = 'drive them away'; ke-himes (Niphal inf const of masas H4549) donag = 'as the melting of wax'; mipnei-esh = 'from before fire'; yo'vedu (Qal impf 3mp of abad H6) = 'let them perish'; reshaim = 'the wicked'; mipnei elohim = 'from before God.' The nadaph verb (H5086) = 'to drive/scatter' — wind acts on smoke and it disperses/ceases. The presence (panim) of God is the agent.

    Personal lament — 'my days are consumed like smoke'¶

    For my days are consumed like smoke, and my bones are burned as an hearth.

    Context: Individual lament psalm. Days consumed 'like smoke' — the simile captures rapid, irreversible disappearance of time. Paired with bones burned like a hearth — fire + smoke together as images of suffering and transience.

    Notes: Uses kalah (H3615) + ashan (H6227): 'consumed like smoke.'

    Fourfold transience cluster — morning cloud, early dew, chaff, smoke through chimney¶

    Therefore they shall be as the morning cloud, and as the early dew that passeth away, as the chaff that is driven with the whirlwind out of the floor, and as the smoke out of the chimney.

    Context: Four similes for Ephraim's vanishing: morning cloud, early dew, chaff from threshing floor, smoke from chimney. All share the property of seeming substantial but quickly ceasing to exist.

    Notes: Parsing: ke-anan boqer = 'as cloud of morning'; ke-hat-tal mashkimim holekh = 'as the dew rising early, going'; ke-mots yes'oer mi-goren = 'as chaff stormed from threshing-floor'; u-khe-ashan me-'arubbah = 'and as smoke from a hole/window' — arubbah (H699) = window/lattice/chimney. The smoke rises from the chimney and disappears.

    Heavens vanish like smoke — cosmic transience¶

    Lift up your eyes to the heavens, and look upon the earth beneath: for the heavens shall vanish away like smoke, and the earth shall wax old like a garment, and they that dwell therein shall die in like manner: but my salvation shall be for ever, and my righteousness shall not be abolished.

    Context: The heavens themselves compared to smoke — the most extreme use of the smoke-as-vanishing motif. Even the cosmos is transient compared to God's salvation.

    And the beast was taken, and with him the false prophet that wrought miracles before him, with which he deceived them that had received the mark of the beast, and them that worshipped his image. These both were cast alive into a lake of fire burning with brimstone.

    Context: Non-human entities (beast, false prophet) cast 'alive' into the lake of fire. Compare: the human 'remnant' in v21 are 'slain with the sword.' Different treatment for different subjects.

    Notes: zontes (G2198, present active ptcp) = 'living/alive.' The beast and false prophet go in alive. The human remnant (v21): apektanthesan (G615, aorist passive) = 'were killed.'

    Devil tormented day and night for ever and ever — named subjects are non-human¶

    And the devil that deceived them was cast into the lake of fire and brimstone, where the beast and the false prophet are, and shall be tormented day and night for ever and ever.

    Context: The 'tormented for ever and ever' formula. All three subjects — devil, beast, false prophet — are non-human or symbolic. Contrast v9 where humans are 'devoured' by fire.

    Notes: basanisthesontai (G928, future passive indicative 3p) = 'shall be tormented.' Subjects: devil (Satan), beast (symbolic), false prophet (symbolic). Compare Ezek 28:18-19: the same being brought to ashes + 'never shalt thou be any more.'

    Smoke to the eyes — simile for irritation from a sluggard¶

    As vinegar to the teeth, and as smoke to the eyes, so is the sluggard to them that send him.

    Context: Unique proverbial use of smoke. The only OT passage using smoke purely as an irritant rather than theophanic, judgment, or transience imagery.

    Notes: ke-ashan la-einayim = 'as smoke to the eyes.' The irritation function of smoke — a practical observation.

    Wickedness burns as fire — people as fuel, smoke ascending¶

    For wickedness burneth as the fire: it shall devour the briers and thorns, and shall kindle in the thickets of the forest, and they shall mount up like the lifting up of smoke.

    Context: Wickedness compared to fire consuming a forest. The people 'mount up like the lifting up of smoke' — smoking as the visible effect of judgment consumption.

    Notes: yit'abbekhu ke-ge'ut ashan = 'they shall spiral upward like the exaltation of smoke.' The upward spiraling of smoke from a forest fire.

    Paradigmatic smoke passage — Abraham sees qitor rising the morning after¶

    And he looked toward Sodom and Gomorrah, and toward all the land of the plain, and beheld, and, lo, the smoke of the country went up as the smoke of a furnace.

    Context: Abraham sees the smoke the NEXT MORNING after Sodom's destruction. The smoke is aftermath evidence — the fire has already done its work. Uses qitor (H7008) not ashan — the rarer, denser smoke word. The kibshan (furnace) simile recurs at Sinai (Exod 19:18).

    Notes: Key observations from parsing: (1) shaqaph (Hiphil wayyiqtol 3ms) = 'he looked out/down upon'; (2) ra'ah (Qal wayyiqtol 3ms) = 'he saw'; (3) alah (Qal perfect 3ms) = 'ascended' — perfect tense for the smoke's rising, indicating completed action observed; (4) qitor (H7008) appears twice — once for the actual smoke, once in the furnace simile. The temporal sequence: fire (v24, past) → overthrow (v25, past) → Abraham looks (v27-28, morning after) → beholds smoke (v28, present evidence of past event).

    Sinai theophany — the mountain smoked because God descended in fire¶

    And mount Sinai was altogether on a smoke, because the LORD descended upon it in fire: and the smoke thereof ascended as the smoke of a furnace, and the whole mount quaked greatly.

    Context: The prototypical theophanic smoke passage. The mountain 'smoked' (ashan H6225, verb Qal perfect 3ms) BECAUSE God descended in fire. The cause-effect is explicit. Uses the same kibshan (furnace) simile as Gen 19:28.

    Notes: Parsing confirms: (1) ashan is verbal (Qal perf 3ms) — 'the mountain smoked as a whole'; (2) mipnei asher = 'because' — explicit causal clause; (3) yarad (Qal perf 3ms) = 'descended' — YHWH's descent is the cause of the smoking; (4) ya'al (Qal wayyiqtol 3ms) = 'ascended' — the smoke's ascent paired with God's descent; (5) ashano (smoke-of-it) uses the ashan noun (H6227) with 3ms suffix; (6) kibshan (kiln/furnace) — exact same word as Gen 19:28.

    Ai's smoke — military signal function; smoke tells observers destruction is complete¶

    And when the men of Ai looked behind them, they saw, and, behold, the smoke of the city ascended up to heaven, and they had no power to flee this way or that way: and the people that fled to the wilderness turned back upon the pursuers. And when Joshua and all Israel saw that the ambush had taken the city, and that the smoke of the city ascended, then they turned again, and slew the men of Ai.

    Context: The clearest military signal passage. Two groups see the smoke and respond: (1) Men of Ai look back, see smoke, lose power to flee; (2) Joshua and Israel see smoke ascending, know ambush succeeded, turn to attack. Smoke COMMUNICATES: the city is taken, the destruction is complete.

    Notes: Parsing v20: vayyiphnu (Qal wayyiqtol 3mp of panah H6437) = 'they turned'; vayyir'u (Qal wayyiqtol 3mp of ra'ah H7200) = 'they saw'; ve-hinneh alah (Qal perf 3ms of alah H5927) = 'and behold ascended'; ashan (H6227) ha-ir = 'smoke of the city'; ha-shamaimah = 'heavenward.' V21: ra'u (Qal perf 3p) = Joshua and Israel 'saw'; ki alah ashan ha-ir = 'that the smoke of the city ascended.' The alah + ashan collocation appears twice.

    Gibeah's smoke — appointed signal of rising smoke column¶

    Now there was an appointed sign between the men of Israel and the liers in wait, that they should make a great flame with smoke rise up out of the city. But when the flame began to arise up out of the city with a pillar of smoke, the Benjamites looked behind them, and, behold, the flame of the city ascended up to heaven. And when the men of Israel turned again, the men of Benjamin were amazed: for they saw that evil was come upon them.

    Context: The APPOINTED signal — explicitly a prearranged communication. V38: 'an appointed sign (moed)... that they should make a great flame with smoke rise up out of the city.' The smoke is deliberate communication: destruction is complete, reverse the retreat.

    Notes: V38: moed (appointed time/signal) + mas'eth (signal-fire/smoke column) + ashan (smoke). V40: mashleth (signal-fire) + ammud ashan (pillar of smoke) — same 'pillar' (ammud) terminology used for God's pillar of cloud.

    Key transience passage — the wicked consume into smoke¶

    But the wicked shall perish, and the enemies of the LORD shall be as the fat of lambs: they shall consume; into smoke shall they consume away.

    Context: Psalm 37's contrast between righteous (inherit earth) and wicked (perish). Smoke is the END PRODUCT of consumption — what remains after the fire has done its work. The construction 'into smoke shall they consume away' uses kalah (H3615) + be-ashan (H6227).

    Notes: Parsing: reshaim (adj mp, 'wicked') yo'vedu (Qal impf 3mp of abad H6, 'perish'); kalu (Qal perf 3p of kalah H3615, 'consumed') ve-ashan (H6227, 'into smoke') kalu (repeated). Double kalah + be-ashan: consumed INTO smoke. Smoke is the cessation point.

    Temple theophany — house filled with smoke at seraphim's cry¶

    And the posts of the door moved at the voice of him that cried, and the house was filled with smoke.

    Context: Isaiah's commissioning vision. The temple fills with smoke (ashan H6227) at the seraphim's declaration of holiness. The smoke marks the boundary between the holy God and the unclean prophet. Parallels Rev 15:8.

    Notes: Parsing: yimmale (Niphal impf 3ms of male') = 'was being filled'; ashan (H6227) is the final word — smoke as the climactic element. The seraphim (H8314, from saraph H8313 'to burn') are themselves 'burning ones.'

    Smoke from the north — invasion imagery; smoke as herald of approaching judgment¶

    Howl, O gate; cry, O city; thou, whole Palestina, art dissolved: for there shall come from the north a smoke, and none shall be alone in his appointed times.

    Context: Oracle against Philistia. Smoke coming from the north signals approaching judgment — an invading army. Smoke here functions as a herald/warning sign of destruction to come, not the aftermath.

    Notes: Uses ashan (H6227) as the visible advance signal of an invading force. The smoke precedes the invasion, functioning as communication — 'judgment is approaching.'

    Incense smoke ascending before God with prayers of saints¶

    And the smoke of the incense, which came with the prayers of the saints, ascended up before God out of the angel's hand.

    Context: Positive smoke in Revelation. Incense smoke (kapnos G2586 + thymiama G2368) ascends before God with the prayers of the saints. Uses aorist anebe (G305) — a specific, completed ascent event.

    Notes: Parsing: anebe (G305, aorist active indicative 3s) = 'went up/ascended'; ho kapnos (G2586) = 'the smoke'; ton thymiamaton (G2368, gen pl) = 'of the incenses'; tais proseuchais ton hagion = 'with the prayers of the saints'; enopion tou theou = 'before God.' Same verb anabaino as Rev 14:11 (judgment smoke), but aorist rather than present tense.
