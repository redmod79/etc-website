    Word Studies¶

    Question¶

    How does the Hebrew mind understand smoke ascending up forever and ever? What does the smoke represent in the biblical worldview? This study focuses specifically on SMOKE IMAGERY — why smoke, what did it signify, how does it function across the OT and NT? The semantic range of olam/aionios has been established in prior studies (etc7-07, etc7-08) — this study builds on that foundation.

    H6227 — ashan¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H6227-ashan.md

    Occurrences: 25

    Primary Hebrew smoke noun. 25 occurrences in 13 books (Isaiah leads with 7). Five semantic categories: (1) theophanic smoke — God's manifested presence (Gen 15:17, Exod 19:18, Isa 6:4, Isa 4:5); (2) divine wrath/judgment — smoke from God's nostrils, aftermath of judgment on nations (2 Sam 22:9, Ps 18:8, Isa 34:10); (3) transience/vanishing — wicked vanishing like smoke (Ps 37:20, Ps 102:3, Isa 51:6, Hos 13:3); (4) military signal — rising column from burning city (Josh 8:20-21, Judg 20:38,40); (5) eschatological sign — blood, fire, pillars of smoke (Joel 2:30). Key collocations: esh (fire, 8x), alah (ascend, 5x), kalah (consume, 2x).

    Semantic categories: theophanic presence, divine judgment aftermath, transience/vanishing, military signal, eschatological sign

    Key verses: Gen 15:17, Exod 19:18, Isa 6:4, Isa 34:10, Ps 37:20, Josh 8:20, Joel 2:30

    Relevance: THE primary Hebrew smoke word. All five categories may express one unified concept: smoke as the visible trace of transformation.

    G2586 — kapnos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2586-kapnos.md

    Occurrences: 13

    NT smoke word. 13 tokens in 11 verses, 2 books (Acts + Revelation). 91% in Revelation. Six categories: (1) prophetic sign/Joel quotation (Acts 2:19); (2) incense ascending before God (Rev 8:4); (3) smoke from the abyss (Rev 9:2-3); (4) fire-smoke-brimstone judgment triad (Rev 9:17-18); (5) perpetual rising — eternal consequence (Rev 14:11, 19:3); (6) divine glory filling temple (Rev 15:8); (7) smoke of Babylon's destruction (Rev 18:9, 18:18). Key collocations: anabaino (ascend, 4x), pyr (fire, 3x), theion (brimstone, 2x).

    Semantic categories: prophetic sign, incense/worship, demonic emergence, judgment triad, eternal consequence, divine glory, destruction evidence

    Key verses: Rev 14:11, Rev 19:3, Rev 15:8, Rev 8:4, Rev 9:2, Acts 2:19

    Relevance: THE NT smoke word. Serves both positive (worship, glory) and negative (judgment, destruction) functions — suggesting smoke is inherently neutral, the visible trace of divine activity.

    H7008 — qitor¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H7008-qiytowr.md

    Occurrences: 4

    Rare smoke/vapor word. Only 4 occurrences: Gen 19:28 (2x — Sodom's smoke as qitor of a furnace), Ps 119:83 (wineskin in the smoke — suffering metaphor), Ps 148:8 (vapor — natural meteorological phenomenon). Derived from qatar (H6999) root meaning to smoke/burn incense. The word for Sodom's paradigmatic smoke literally comes from the incense root — connecting judgment smoke to worship smoke at the etymological level.

    Semantic categories: destruction aftermath, suffering metaphor, natural phenomenon

    Key verses: Gen 19:28, Ps 119:83, Ps 148:8

    Relevance: Critical finding: qitor shares the q-t-r root with qatar (burn incense) and qetoreth (incense). The word for Sodom's smoke is etymologically related to the worship-incense word family. This confirms the judgment-worship smoke connection at the linguistic level.

    G822 — atmis¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G822-atmis.md

    Occurrences: 2

    Vapor/mist. Only 2 NT occurrences: Acts 2:19 (Joel quotation — 'vapour of smoke' in the apocalyptic triad) and James 4:14 (life as a vapor that appears briefly and vanishes). Connects to the Hebrew smoke-as-transience motif.

    Semantic categories: apocalyptic sign, transience of life

    Key verses: Acts 2:19, James 4:14

    Relevance: Bridges the smoke-transience concept from Hebrew (Ps 37:20, Hos 13:3) into Greek. Life is atmis — visible briefly, then vanishing entirely.

    H6225 — ashan (verb)¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H6225-ashan.md

    Occurrences: 8

    Verbal form 'to smoke.' 8 occurrences across Exodus, Deuteronomy, Psalms. Four categories: (1) theophanic smoking — Sinai smoked because YHWH descended (Exod 19:18); (2) creation trembling — mountains smoke at God's touch (Ps 104:32, 144:5); (3) divine anger — God's anger 'smokes' against his people (Ps 74:1, 80:4); (4) covenant curse — YHWH's anger smokes against the unfaithful (Deut 29:20). The verb describes what happens when the holy touches the mundane.

    Semantic categories: theophanic response, creation's response to God, divine anger, covenant curse

    Key verses: Exod 19:18, Ps 104:32, Ps 144:5, Ps 74:1, Deut 29:20

    Relevance: The verbal form reveals that smoking is not passive — it is what happens when creation encounters God. Mountains SMOKE at God's touch. This is the conceptual key.

    H6226 — ashen (adj)¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H6226-ashen.md

    Occurrences: 2

    Adjective 'smoking.' Only 2 occurrences: Exod 20:18 (the smoking mountain at Sinai — theophanic terror) and Isa 7:4 (smoking firebrands — contemptible weakness, nearly extinguished enemies). The mountain characterized AS a smoking thing.

    Semantic categories: theophanic terror, contemptible weakness

    Key verses: Exod 20:18, Isa 7:4

    Relevance: The adjective shows smoke as a defining characteristic. The mountain IS a smoking thing. In Isa 7:4, smoking = near extinction — more smoke than flame.

    H6999 — qatar¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H6999-qatar.md

    Occurrences: 139

    Verb: to cause to smoke, burn incense/sacrifice. 112 unique verses, 139 tokens. Hiphil (70x) = authorized priestly altar-burning; Piel (42x) = incense burning (often idolatrous). Key distinction from saraph: qatar produces FRAGRANT smoke ascending to God; saraph produces DESTRUCTIVE consumption. Qatar always involves turning something to smoke as a religious act. Root of qetoreth (incense) and qitor (Sodom's smoke).

    Semantic categories: sacrificial altar burning, incense worship, idolatrous incense

    Key verses: Exod 30:7-8, Lev 1:9, Jer 44:17

    Relevance: The root verb connecting incense (worship smoke) to qitor (judgment smoke). If qatar is the root of both qetoreth and qitor, then worship-smoke and judgment-smoke share etymological DNA.

    H7004 — qetoreth¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H7004-qtoreth.md

    Occurrences: 72

    Incense (fumigation noun). 58 unique verses, 72 tokens. From qatar root. The aromatic compound burned on the altar. Concentrated in Pentateuch (Exodus + Numbers = 39 of 58). Daily priestly service (Exod 30:7-8), Day of Atonement (Lev 16:12-13), offerings (Num 7), prophetic condemnation of unauthorized use (Isa 1:13, Ezek 8:11).

    Semantic categories: sanctuary incense, daily priestly service, atonement, prophetic condemnation

    Key verses: Exod 30:7-8, Lev 16:12-13, Isa 1:13

    Relevance: The worship-smoke substance. Same q-t-r root as qitor (judgment smoke). In Lev 16:13, the incense smoke is called 'cloud' (anan) — the theophanic presence word.

    G2368 — thymiama¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2368-thymiama.md

    Occurrences: 6

    NT incense. 6 verses (Luke 1:10-11, Rev 5:8, 8:3-4, 18:13). In Revelation, explicitly identified as prayers of the saints (Rev 5:8, 8:3-4). In Luke, the hour of incense when Gabriel appears. Bridge from qetoreth via LXX (52 co-occurrences, PMI 33.32).

    Semantic categories: temple liturgy, heavenly worship, prayer as incense

    Key verses: Rev 5:8, Rev 8:3-4, Luke 1:10

    Relevance: The incense smoke in heaven (Rev 8:4) uses the same verb anabaino as judgment smoke (Rev 14:11). Worship smoke and judgment smoke ascend the same way, before the same God.

    H5927 — alah¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H5927-alah.md

    Occurrences: 1122

    To ascend/go up. One of Hebrew's most frequent verbs (817 unique verses, 1122 tokens). Core categories: physical ascent, pilgrimage to Jerusalem, Exodus deliverance, cultic offering (causing sacrifice to 'go up'), military advance, growth/vegetation, smoke ascending. Cognate noun: olah (burnt offering, 'that which goes up'). The verb that makes smoke RISE — collocates with ashan 5x.

    Semantic categories: physical ascent, pilgrimage, cultic offering, military advance, smoke ascending

    Key verses: Gen 19:28, Josh 8:20, Isa 34:10, Exod 19:18

    Relevance: THE verb of smoke's upward motion. Alah + ashan appears in Sodom (Gen 19:28), Sinai (Exod 19:18), Ai (Josh 8:20), and Edom (Isa 34:10). The cognate olah (burnt offering) = 'that which ascends' in smoke. Ascending is the fundamental action of smoke.

    G305 — anabaino¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G305-anabaínō.md

    Occurrences: 86

    Greek 'to go up/ascend.' 86 occurrences. Primary LXX equivalent of alah (472 co-occurrences). Categories: pilgrimage to Jerusalem, mountain ascent, Christ's ascension, apocalyptic ascent, plant growth, baptism imagery, thoughts arising. In Revelation (13x): heavenly summons ('Come up'), smoke/incense ascending, beasts rising.

    Semantic categories: pilgrimage ascent, Christological ascension, apocalyptic ascent, smoke ascending

    Key verses: Rev 14:11, Rev 19:3, Rev 8:4, Rev 9:2

    Relevance: THE NT verb for smoke ascending. Present active indicative (anabainei) in Rev 14:11 and 19:3 — gnomic/timeless present. Aorist (anebe) in Rev 8:4 — specific historical ascent. Same verb, different aspect: judgment smoke (present/timeless) vs worship smoke (aorist/completed).

    H784 — esh¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H784-esh.md

    Occurrences: 400

    Fire. 348 unique verses, 400 tokens across 33 books. Categories: literal combustion, altar/sacrificial fire, divine presence/theophany, judgment/devouring/destruction, metaphor/radiance. Key collocations: YHWH (111x), akal/devour (90x), saraph/burn (75x). The dominant agent word — fire does the work, smoke is the evidence.

    Semantic categories: literal fire, sacrificial fire, theophany, judgment, radiance

    Key verses: Gen 19:24, Exod 19:18, Deut 4:24

    Relevance: Fire is the AGENT that produces smoke. Where fire is the active verb, smoke is the passive witness. The fire-smoke distinction: fire = the doer; smoke = the evidence of what was done.

    G4442 — pyr¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G4442-pyr.md

    Occurrences: 73

    NT fire. 73 unique verses, 74 tokens across 14 books. Revelation dominates (25/73). Categories: eschatological punishment, judgment parables, divine theophany, cosmic plagues, testing/refining, figurative. LXX bridge from H784 esh (316 co-occurrences). The fire-brimstone pair: pyr + theion (7x).

    Semantic categories: eschatological punishment, judgment parables, theophany, testing, figurative

    Key verses: Rev 20:14-15, Matt 25:41, Heb 12:29

    Relevance: NT fire word. Part of the fire-smoke-brimstone triad in Revelation. Paired with kapnos (smoke, 3x) and theion (brimstone, 7x).

    H665 — epher¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H665-epher.md

    Occurrences: 22

    Ashes. 22 unique verses in 13 books. Categories: dust-and-ashes humility (Gen 18:27, Job 42:6), mourning/humiliation (2 Sam 13:19, Est 4:1), ritual ashes of red heifer (Num 19:9), worthlessness (Job 13:12, Isa 44:20), final reduction/destruction (Ezek 28:18, Mal 4:3). The END PRODUCT after fire has consumed.

    Semantic categories: humility, mourning, purification ritual, final reduction

    Key verses: Ezek 28:18, Mal 4:3, Gen 18:27

    Relevance: The third stage in the fire→smoke→ashes process. Fire = agent, smoke = witness, ashes = residue. When something has become ashes, the process is complete.

    H1614 — gophriyth¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H1614-gophriyth.md

    Occurrences: 7

    Brimstone/sulfur. All 7 OT occurrences in judgment contexts: Gen 19:24 (Sodom), Deut 29:23 (Sodom paradigm), Ps 11:6 (cup of judgment), Job 18:15 (wicked's habitation), Isa 30:33 (Tophet), Isa 34:9 (Edom), Ezek 38:22 (Gog). No occurrence describes ongoing conscious torment. Every use describes destruction.

    Semantic categories: divine judgment on cities, eschatological judgment, individual retribution, prepared judgment

    Key verses: Gen 19:24, Isa 34:9, Ps 11:6

    Relevance: The instrument of the Sodom archetype. All 7 uses describe destructive judgment. Combined with fire (esh, 4x) and rain (matar, 3x). The brimstone intensifies fire — it does not alter the outcome from destruction to torment.

    G2303 — theion¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2303-theion.md

    Occurrences: 7

    NT brimstone. 7 occurrences (6 Revelation + Luke 17:29). Luke 17:29: Jesus says Sodom's fire and brimstone 'destroyed them all.' Revelation: Rev 9:17-18 (judgment triad), 14:10 (torment), 19:20 (lake of fire), 20:10 (devil tormented), 21:8 (second death). Etymologically related to theios (divine) and theos (God) — 'divine substance.'

    Semantic categories: Sodom archetype, eschatological torment, lake of fire, demonic warfare

    Key verses: Rev 14:10, Rev 21:8, Luke 17:29

    Relevance: NT counterpart of gophriyth. The fire-brimstone pair (pyr + theion) appears 7x. Etymological connection to theos (God) is noteworthy — brimstone understood as a 'divine substance.'

    H3615 — kalah¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H3615-kalah.md

    Occurrences: 258

    To end/complete/consume. 199 unique verses, 258 tokens. Piel (141x) = complete, finish, consume, destroy. Qal (64x) = come to end, be consumed, fail. The verb that describes what happens to things that become smoke: they are consumed to completion. Ps 37:20: 'they shall consume; into smoke shall they consume away' — kalah + ashan.

    Semantic categories: completion, divine judgment, consumption, wrath accomplished

    Key verses: Ps 37:20, Ps 102:3, Lam 3:22

    Relevance: The PROCESS verb paired with smoke. Kalah + ashan in Ps 37:20 shows smoke as the endpoint of consumption — not the process, but the destination. Things are consumed INTO smoke.

    H6051 — anan¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H6051-anan.md

    Occurrences: 92

    Cloud. 80 unique verses, 92 tokens in 18 books. Categories: guiding cloud/wilderness pillar (Exod 13:21, Num 9:17), cloud of divine communication/Sinai (Exod 19:9, 24:16), glory cloud/sanctuary (Exod 40:34, 1 Kings 8:10-11), covenantal cloud/Noah (Gen 9:13), judgment cloud/Day of LORD (Joel 2:2, Ezek 30:3), metaphorical (Job 7:9, Isa 44:22). LXX maps to nephele (G3507, 70x).

    Semantic categories: wilderness pillar, divine communication, sanctuary glory, covenant, judgment, transience

    Key verses: Exod 40:34, Lev 16:13, 1 Kings 8:10

    Relevance: The theophanic CLOUD word. In Lev 16:13, incense smoke is called 'cloud (anan) of the incense.' Cloud and smoke overlap in theophanic contexts — both are the visible presence of God. The anan + kabod (glory) complex in Exod 40:34, 1 Kings 8:11, Rev 15:8.

    H8313 — saraph¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H8313-saraph.md

    Occurrences: 124

    To burn/set on fire (total consumption). 107 unique verses, 124 tokens. Qal (109x) = burn, set on fire; Niphal (13x) = be burned. Categories: ritual burning of sacrificial remains (outside camp), disposal of unclean material, military destruction of cities, destruction of idols, judgment as penalty. Key distinction: saraph = total destruction; qatar = ascending worship-smoke. LXX maps to katakaio (G2618, 'burn down completely').

    Semantic categories: ritual burning, unclean disposal, military destruction, idol destruction, judgment penalty

    Key verses: Lev 4:12, Josh 6:24, Deut 7:5

    Relevance: The DESTRUCTIVE burning verb that produces smoke. While qatar burns TO worship, saraph burns TO destroy. Both produce smoke — but different kinds of smoke for different purposes.
