    Verses¶

    Question¶

    What does the 'day and night' (hemeras kai nuktos) formula mean in Revelation? Does 'no rest day nor night' (Rev 14:11) describe eternal conscious experience, or the thoroughgoing nature of a judgment during its execution?

    First Revelation 'day and night' — Living creatures' ceaseless worship¶

    Revelation 4:8 And the four beasts had each of them six wings about [him]; and [they were] full of eyes within: and they rest not day and night, saying, Holy, holy, holy, Lord God Almighty, which was, and is, and is to come. Revelation 4:9 And when those beasts give glory and honour and thanks to him that sat on the throne, who liveth for ever and ever, Revelation 4:10 The four and twenty elders fall down before him that sat on the throne, and worship him that liveth for ever and ever, and cast their crowns before the throne, saying, Revelation 4:11 Thou art worthy, O Lord, to receive glory and honour and power: for thou hast created all things, and for thy pleasure they are and were created.

    Context: Throne room vision. John sees the four living creatures around God's throne. Their 'rest not day and night' describes uninterrupted, positive worship — the first 'day and night' in Revelation. Echoes Isaiah 6:2-3 trishagion.

    Notes: CRITICAL: ἀνάπαυσιν οὐκ ἔχουσιν ἡμέρας καὶ νυκτὸς — same construction as Rev 14:11. ἡμέρας (N-GSF Gen Sg F) καὶ νυκτὸς (N-GSF Gen Sg F) = genitive of time. ἔχουσιν (V-PAI-3P) = present active indicative 3rd plural. ἀνάπαυσιν (N-ASF) = accusative singular. No interpreter reads this as eternal conscious torment of the living creatures. The 'no rest' here is positive — ceaseless worship.

    Second Revelation 'day and night' — Great multitude's perpetual temple service¶

    Revelation 7:13 And one of the elders answered, saying unto me, What are these which are arrayed in white robes? and whence came they? Revelation 7:14 And I said unto him, Sir, thou knowest. And he said to me, These are they which came out of great tribulation, and have washed their robes, and made them white in the blood of the Lamb. Revelation 7:15 Therefore are they before the throne of God, and serve him day and night in his temple: and he that sitteth on the throne shall dwell among them. Revelation 7:16 They shall hunger no more, neither thirst any more; neither shall the sun light on them, nor any heat. Revelation 7:17 For the Lamb which is in the midst of the throne shall feed them, and shall lead them unto living fountains of waters: and God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes.

    Context: The great multitude who came out of great tribulation serve God 'day and night in his temple.' This is the second positive 'day and night' in Revelation. Logically, this occurs in a reality where Rev 22:5 says 'there shall be no night there' — the formula is qualitative, not literal temporal.

    Notes: λατρεύουσιν (V-PAI-3P, present active indicative 3P of λατρεύω G3000) αὐτῷ ἡμέρας (N-GSF) καὶ νυκτὸς (N-GSF) — genitive of time, identical construction to Rev 4:8. The verb λατρεύω denotes sacral service/worship. The 'day and night' describes the quality of their access — perpetual, uninterrupted temple service — not a literal temporal framework (since Rev 22:5 abolishes night).

    Third Revelation 'day and night' — Satan's terminated accusation¶

    Revelation 12:7 And there was war in heaven: Michael and his angels fought against the dragon; and the dragon fought and his angels, Revelation 12:8 And prevailed not; neither was their place found any more in heaven. Revelation 12:9 And the great dragon was cast out, that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan, which deceiveth the whole world: he was cast out into the earth, and his angels were cast out with him. Revelation 12:10 And I heard a loud voice saying in heaven, Now is come salvation, and strength, and the kingdom of our God, and the power of his Christ: for the accuser of our brethren is cast down, which accused them before our God day and night. Revelation 12:11 And they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony; and they loved not their lives unto the death. Revelation 12:12 Therefore rejoice, [ye] heavens, and ye that dwell in them. Woe to the inhabiters of the earth and of the sea! for the devil is come down unto you, having great wrath, because he knoweth that he hath but a short time.

    Context: War in heaven. Satan the accuser 'accused them before our God day and night' — but this 'day and night' activity is explicitly TERMINATED. The heavenly voice declares 'NOW (Ἄρτι, G737) is come salvation... for the accuser is cast down (ἐβλήθη, aorist passive of βάλλω).' This proves 'day and night' describes the character of an activity during its occurrence, not its guaranteed eternal continuation.

    Notes: CRITICAL DATA: κατηγορῶν (V-PAP-NSM, present active participle, 'the one accusing') αὐτοὺς ἐνώπιον τοῦ Θεοῦ ἡμῶν ἡμέρας (N-GSF) καὶ νυκτός (N-GSF). The participle κατηγορῶν is present tense — describing the ongoing character of the accusation during its performance. But the ἐβλήθη (V-API-3S, aorist passive, 'was cast') shows this 'day and night' activity is now OVER. The Ἄρτι (G737, ADV, 'now/at this very moment') marks the terminus. This is the only 'day and night' in Revelation that is explicitly bounded — the activity it describes has ended.

    Fourth Revelation 'day and night' — The third angel's warning and 'no rest day nor night'¶

    Revelation 14:9 And the third angel followed them, saying with a loud voice, If any man worship the beast and his image, and receive [his] mark in his forehead, or in his hand, Revelation 14:10 The same shall drink of the wine of the wrath of God, which is poured out without mixture into the cup of his indignation; and he shall be tormented with fire and brimstone in the presence of the holy angels, and in the presence of the Lamb: Revelation 14:11 And the smoke of their torment ascendeth up for ever and ever: and they have no rest day nor night, who worship the beast and his image, and whosoever receiveth the mark of his name. Revelation 14:12 Here is the patience of the saints: here [are] they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus.

    Context: The crux text. Third angel's message. Beast-worshippers are warned of fire-and-brimstone judgment. The 'no rest day nor night' formula uses the IDENTICAL construction as Rev 4:8 — ouk echousin anapausin hemeras kai nuktos.

    Notes: MOST CRITICAL PARSING: οὐκ ἔχουσιν (V-PAI-3P) ἀνάπαυσιν (N-ASF, G372) ἡμέρας (N-GSF, G2250) καὶ νυκτός (N-GSF, G3571). IDENTICAL to Rev 4:8 in every grammatical element: same verb (ἔχουσιν, PAI-3P), same noun (ἀνάπαυσιν, ASF), same genitive of time (ἡμέρας καὶ νυκτός, both GSF). Also: ὁ καπνὸς τοῦ βασανισμοῦ (G929, N-GSM) αὐτῶν εἰς αἰῶνας αἰώνων ἀναβαίνει (V-PAI-3S, gnomic present of G305). The smoke 'ascends' in the present tense — same gnomic present as the creatures' worship in Rev 4:8.

    The anapausis antithesis — Blessed dead 'rest from their labours'¶

    Revelation 14:13 And I heard a voice from heaven saying unto me, Write, Blessed [are] the dead which die in the Lord from henceforth: Yea, saith the Spirit, that they may rest from their labours; and their works do follow them.

    Context: Immediately adjacent to Rev 14:11 — two verses later. Beast-worshippers have 'no rest (anapausis, G372) day nor night'; the blessed dead 'rest (anapauo, G373) from their labours.' Same word family, opposite outcomes, deliberately placed side by side. This defines 'no rest' by contrast: if 'rest' means cessation of labor and entry into reward, then 'no rest' means absence of relief during judgment.

    Notes: ἀναπαήσονται (V-2FPI-3P, future passive indicative 3P of ἀναπαύω G373) ἐκ τῶν κόπων αὐτῶν — 'they shall rest from their labours.' The noun in 14:11 is ἀνάπαυσις (G372), the verb here is ἀναπαύω (G373) — cognate pair from the same root (ἀνά + παύω). The form ἀναπαήσονται is future passive — they WILL BE rested. Opposite of 14:11 where they do NOT have rest.

    Smoke formula applied to destroyed Babylon¶

    Revelation 19:3 And again they said, Alleluia. And her smoke rose up for ever and ever.

    Context: Babylon's smoke rises 'for ever and ever' — but Babylon is destroyed. Rev 18:21 says she is 'found no more at all.' The smoke formula marks permanent aftermath of completed destruction, not ongoing burning of a surviving entity.

    Notes: Same 'smoke ascending for ever and ever' formula as Rev 14:11, applied to Babylon. But Rev 18:21-24 makes clear Babylon is totally destroyed — 'shall be found no more at all' (repeated 6 times). The smoke is testimony, not ongoing process.

    Fifth Revelation 'day and night' — Lake of fire with vocabulary differentiation¶

    Revelation 20:9 And they went up on the breadth of the earth, and compassed the camp of the saints about, and the beloved city: and fire came down from God out of heaven, and devoured them. Revelation 20:10 And the devil that deceived them was cast into the lake of fire and brimstone, where the beast and the false prophet [are], and shall be tormented day and night for ever and ever. Revelation 20:11 And I saw a great white throne, and him that sat on it, from whose face the earth and the heaven fled away; and there was found no place for them. Revelation 20:12 And I saw the dead, small and great, stand before God; and the books were opened: and another book was opened, which is [the book] of life: and the dead were judged out of those things which were written in the books, according to their works. Revelation 20:13 And the sea gave up the dead which were in it; and death and hell delivered up the dead which were in them: and they were judged every man according to their works. Revelation 20:14 And death and hell were cast into the lake of fire. This is the second death. Revelation 20:15 And whosoever was not found written in the book of life was cast into the lake of fire.

    Context: The fifth and final 'day and night' in Revelation. Three subjects: devil, beast, false prophet — all non-human/symbolic. The torment formula (basanisthesontai hemeras kai nuktos eis tous aionas ton aionon) is applied ONLY to these three. Humans are 'devoured' (v.9, katephagen), judged (v.12-13), and cast into the lake of fire for the 'second death' (v.14-15). Different vocabulary for different subjects.

    Notes: βασανισθήσονται (V-FPI-3P, future passive indicative 3P of βασανίζω G928) ἡμέρας (N-GSF) καὶ νυκτὸς (N-GSF) εἰς τοὺς αἰῶνας τῶν αἰώνων. Same genitive of time construction for 'day and night.' The verb is future passive indicative — different from the present active indicative in Rev 4:8 and 14:11. The subject (ὁ διάβολος) is cast (ἐβλήθη, V-API-3S, aorist passive) into the lake. Humans in v.9 are 'devoured' (κατέφαγεν, aorist) — different verb entirely.

    Eschatological dissolution of night — 'there shall be no night there'¶

    Revelation 22:5 And there shall be no night there; and they need no candle, neither light of the sun; for the Lord God giveth them light: and they shall reign for ever and ever.

    Context: The abolition of the day/night framework. νὺξ οὐκ ἔσται ἔτι — 'night shall not be any more.' If 'day and night' is the temporal framework within which activities occur, then the dissolution of this framework in the new creation proves that 'day and night' is a quality-marker for the present age, not an assertion about eternal states.

    Notes: νὺξ (N-NSF, nominative singular of νύξ G3571) οὐκ ἔσται (V-FDI-3S, future middle/deponent indicative of εἰμί) ἔτι (ADV, 'any more'). The saints βασιλεύσουσιν (V-FAI-3P, future active indicative, 'shall reign') εἰς τοὺς αἰῶνας τῶν αἰώνων — 'for ever and ever.' The saints reign forever, but without night. The day/night framework is gone. Rev 7:15's 'day and night' service continues — but in a reality without night. The formula must be qualitative.

    Eschatological dissolution — Gates never shut because no night¶

    Revelation 21:25 And the gates of it shall not be shut at all by day: for there shall be no night there.

    Context: Parallel to Rev 22:5. No night in the new Jerusalem. Confirms the abolition of the day/night cycle.

    Notes: νὺξ γὰρ οὐκ ἔσται ἐκεῖ — 'for night shall not be there.' Second affirmation of night's abolition.

    Fire-and-brimstone judgment — Context for 14:11¶

    Revelation 14:10 The same shall drink of the wine of the wrath of God, which is poured out without mixture into the cup of his indignation; and he shall be tormented with fire and brimstone in the presence of the holy angels, and in the presence of the Lamb:

    Context: Immediately precedes 14:11. The beast-worshipper 'shall be tormented' (basanisthesetai, future passive indicative 3S) with fire and brimstone. The fire-and-brimstone imagery echoes Gen 19:24 (Sodom) and Isa 34:9 (Edom).

    Notes: βασανισθήσεται (V-FPI-3S, future passive indicative 3S of βασανίζω G928). Same verb as Rev 20:10 but singular — the individual beast-worshipper is subject here.

    OT source for Rev 4:8 — Seraphim trishagion¶

    Isaiah 6:2 Above it stood the seraphims: each one had six wings; with twain he covered his face, and with twain he covered his feet, and with twain he did fly. Isaiah 6:3 And one cried unto another, and said, Holy, holy, holy, [is] the LORD of hosts: the whole earth [is] full of his glory.

    Context: The OT source for Rev 4:8's living creatures. Six-winged creatures, triple 'holy', throne room setting, divine title. John's 'rest not day and night saying Holy, holy, holy' echoes this continuous worship.

    Notes: The seraphim's continuous praise in Isaiah provides the template for Rev 4:8's 'rest not day and night.' Neither Isaiah nor anyone reads the seraphim's praise as torment.

    OT source for Rev 14:11 — Edom's fire 'not quenched night nor day' with aftermath¶

    Isaiah 34:9 And the streams thereof shall be turned into pitch, and the dust thereof into brimstone, and the land thereof shall become burning pitch. Isaiah 34:10 It shall not be quenched night nor day; the smoke thereof shall go up for ever: from generation to generation it shall lie waste; none shall pass through it for ever and ever. Isaiah 34:11 But the cormorant and the bittern shall possess it; the owl also and the raven shall dwell in it: and he shall stretch out upon it the line of confusion, and the stones of emptiness. Isaiah 34:12 They shall call the nobles thereof to the kingdom, but none [shall be] there, and all her princes shall be nothing. Isaiah 34:13 And thorns shall come up in her palaces, nettles and brambles in the fortresses thereof: and it shall be an habitation of dragons, [and] a court for owls. Isaiah 34:14 The wild beasts of the desert shall also meet with the wild beasts of the island, and the satyr shall cry to his fellow; the screech owl also shall rest there, and find for herself a place of rest. Isaiah 34:15 There shall the great owl make her nest, and lay, and hatch, and gather under her shadow: there shall the vultures also be gathered, every one with her mate.

    Context: The direct OT source for Rev 14:11's smoke-ascending formula. Edom's fire is 'not quenched night nor day; the smoke thereof shall go up for ever.' But vv.11-15 show that animals dwell in the ruins — the fire is OUT, the land is desolate but habitable by creatures. The 'night nor day' and 'for ever' language describes completed, irreversible destruction, not ongoing burning.

    Notes: Hebrew parsing: לַיְלָה (layil, noun ms Abs, 'night') וְיֹומָם (yowmam, adverb, 'by day') לֹא תִכְבֶּה (lo tikhbeh, Qal imperfect 3fs of כבה, 'go out'). The imperfect expresses the fire's character during its burning phase. לְעוֹלָם (le-owlam, 'for ever') יַעֲלֶה (ya'aleh, Qal imperfect 3ms of עלה, 'ascend') עֲשָׁנָהּ (ashana, 'its smoke'). The smoke ascends 'for ever' — but the aftermath (vv.11-17) shows animals nesting in the ruins. The fire has done its work.

    Sodom paradigm — Fire, brimstone, smoke ascending¶

    Genesis 19:24 Then the LORD rained upon Sodom and upon Gomorrah brimstone and fire from the LORD out of heaven; Genesis 19:25 And he overthrew those cities, and all the plain, and all the inhabitants of the cities, and that which grew upon the ground. Genesis 19:26 But his wife looked back from behind him, and she became a pillar of salt. Genesis 19:27 And Abraham gat up early in the morning to the place where he stood before the LORD: Genesis 19:28 And he looked toward Sodom and Gomorrah, and toward all the land of the plain, and beheld, and, lo, the smoke of the country went up as the smoke of a furnace.

    Context: The paradigmatic fire-and-brimstone judgment. God rains fire and brimstone; the cities are overthrown; Abraham sees smoke ascending. The three stages: fire falls (v.24) → cities overthrown (v.25) → smoke ascends (v.28). This is Rev 14:11's source imagery.

    Notes: 2 Pet 2:6 interprets this event: Sodom and Gomorrah were 'turned to ashes.' The fire produced destruction and ashes, not ongoing torment.

    Sodom 'turned to ashes' — Interpretive key for fire-and-brimstone outcome¶

    2 Peter 2:6 And turning the cities of Sodom and Gomorrha into ashes condemned [them] with an overthrow, making [them] an ensample unto those that after should live ungodly;

    Context: Peter interprets the Sodom fire-and-brimstone event: the cities were 'turned to ashes' (tephrosas). They are an 'ensample' (hupodeigma) of what happens to the ungodly. The fire produced ashes, not eternal burning.

    Notes: The Greek τεφρώσας (tephrosas) means 'having reduced to ashes' — the outcome of fire-and-brimstone is complete destruction, not preservation in torment.

    OT merism — 'day and night shall not cease' as creation order¶

    Genesis 8:22 While the earth remaineth, seedtime and harvest, and cold and heat, and summer and winter, and day and night shall not cease.

    Context: God's post-flood promise. The day/night cycle is paired with other merisms (seedtime/harvest, cold/heat, summer/winter) to express totality and continuity of the created order.

    Notes: Hebrew: וְיֹום (ve-yom, conjunction + noun ms Abs, 'and day') וָלַיְלָה (va-laylah, conjunction + noun ms Abs, 'and night') לֹא יִשְׁבֹּתוּ (lo yishbotu, 'shall not cease,' Qal imperfect 3mp of שׁבת H7673). The verb שׁבת ('to cease/sabbath') is the same root behind the Sabbath. Day and night are presented as a continuous created order — a merism for the totality of time.

    OT merism — 'meditate therein day and night'¶

    Joshua 1:8 This book of the law shall not depart out of thy mouth; but thou shalt meditate therein day and night, that thou mayest observe to do according to all that is written therein: for then thou shalt make thy way prosperous, and then thou shalt have good success.

    Context: 'Day and night' meditation — the OT paradigm for the merism as quality-marker. No one reads this as requiring literal 24-hour non-stop meditation. It means devotional completeness.

    Notes: The 'day and night' merism here indisputably means 'constantly, thoroughly' — not 'without any break for 24 hours.' This is the interpretive baseline for the formula.

    Notes: Same merism as Josh 1:8, same meaning: quality of devotion, not duration claim.

    OT merism — 'day and night thy hand was heavy upon me'¶

    Context: David describes divine conviction. 'Day and night' here characterizes the thoroughness of God's discipline — the quality and completeness of the pressure, not a claim that it never paused for a moment.

    Notes: Hebrew: יֹומָם (yomam, adverb, 'by day') וָלַיְלָה (va-laylah, conjunction + noun ms Abs, 'and night') תִּכְבַּד (tikhbad, Qal imperfect 3fs of כבד, 'be heavy') עָלַי (alai, 'upon me') יָדֶךָ (yadkha, 'your hand'). The 'day and night' pressure is qualitative — thoroughgoing divine discipline.

    Notes: Already covered in Isa 34:9-15 entry. This standalone reference ensures the verse is not missed.

    NT 'day and night' — Twelve tribes 'instantly serving God day and night'¶

    Acts 26:7 Unto which [promise] our twelve tribes, instantly serving [God] day and night, hope to come. For which hope's sake, king Agrippa, I am accused of the Jews.

    Context: Paul describes the twelve tribes' devotion. They serve God 'day and night' — wholehearted, constant devotion.

    Notes: CRITICAL CASE CONTRAST: νύκτα (N-ASF, ACCUSATIVE Sg F) καὶ ἡμέραν (N-ASF, ACCUSATIVE Sg F). This is ACCUSATIVE, not genitive — measuring extent of time, not characterizing the period. Paul uses the accusative of extent here (like Luke 2:37), emphasizing the duration of their service. Compare Rev 4:8 etc. which use the GENITIVE (ἡμέρας καὶ νυκτός), characterizing the temporal framework.

    Notes: νυκτὸς (N-GSF, GENITIVE Sg F) καὶ ἡμέρας (N-GSF, GENITIVE Sg F). GENITIVE of time — same construction as all 5 Revelation passages. Paul did not literally labor without rest. The formula characterizes the quality/intensity of his work ethic.

    NT 'night and day' — Anna serving God 'night and day' (ACCUSATIVE contrast)¶

    Luke 2:37 And she [was] a widow of about fourscore and four years, which departed not from the temple, but served [God] with fastings and prayers night and day.

    Context: Anna served God 'night and day' — but Luke uses the ACCUSATIVE case (nykta kai hemeran), not the genitive used in Revelation. The accusative measures extent/duration; the genitive characterizes the time-frame. This diagnostic contrast is key.

    Notes: CRITICAL DIAGNOSTIC: νύκτα (N-ASF, ACCUSATIVE Sg F) καὶ ἡμέραν (N-ASF, ACCUSATIVE Sg F). Luke uses the ACCUSATIVE of extent of time, measuring the duration of Anna's service. This contrasts with the GENITIVE used in all 5 Revelation 'day and night' passages (and in 1 Thess 2:9, Mark 5:5, etc.). The same words (νύξ, ἡμέρα) change grammatical function by case: accusative = how long; genitive = during what kind of time. The Revelation passages use the genitive — characterizing the temporal backdrop, not measuring duration.

    Creation of day/night distinction¶

    Christ's offer of rest (anapausis)¶

    Matthew 11:28 Come unto me, all [ye] that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Matthew 11:29 Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls.

    Context: Jesus offers anapausis (G372) — rest for the soul. The same word used in Rev 4:8 and 14:11. If Christ's 'rest' is spiritual refreshment and cessation of burden, then the denial of 'rest' in Rev 14:11 is the absence of this relief.

    Notes: ἀνάπαυσιν (N-ASF, G372) — same word as Rev 4:8 and 14:11. Only positive use in the NT.

    Rest (katapausis) theology — Entering God's rest¶

    Hebrews 4:1 Let us therefore fear, lest, a promise being left [us] of entering into his rest, any of you should seem to come short of it. Hebrews 4:9 There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God. Hebrews 4:10 For he that is entered into his rest, he also hath ceased from his own works, as God [did] from his. Hebrews 4:11 Let us labour therefore to enter into that rest, lest any man fall after the same example of unbelief.

    Context: Hebrews' 'rest' theology uses katapausis (G2663), a synonym of anapausis (G372). The concept of entering God's rest vs. failing to enter provides the theological backdrop for Rev 14:11's 'no rest' vs. 14:13's 'rest from their labours.'

    Notes: Different Greek word (katapausis G2663 vs. anapausis G372) but same rest concept. The theological structure is: rest is the reward of faith; absence of rest is the consequence of unbelief/rebellion.

    Notes: The lake of fire = 'the second death' (ὁ θάνατος ὁ δεύτερος). Death is the absence of life, not the continuation of conscious experience in torment.

    Eschatological endpoint — No more death, sorrow, crying, pain¶

    Revelation 21:4 And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away.

    Context: The eschatological endpoint: no death, no sorrow, no crying, no pain. If the wicked were being eternally tormented, there would be ongoing pain and sorrow in the universe forever. This verse envisions complete resolution.

    Notes: If eternal conscious torment exists, 'no more pain' is not universally true. The eschatological vision requires that pain ceases everywhere.

    Aftermath of Isa 34:10 — Animals dwell in the ruins, proving fire is out¶

    Context: Immediately follows Isa 34:10 ('not quenched night nor day; the smoke thereof shall go up for ever').     Notes: The decisive proof that Isa 34:10's 'night nor day' fire has ended: v.11 cormorant and bittern 'possess it,' owls and ravens 'dwell in it'; v.13 thorns grow in palaces; v.14 wild beasts meet, screech owl 'rests there'; v.15 owls 'make her nest, and lay, and hatch.' Living creatures inhabiting the ruins = fire is extinguished.
