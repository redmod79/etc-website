    03-analysis.md — ECT Strongest Case¶

    Question¶

    8 key ECT arguments evaluated at E/N/I tier: Rev 14:9-11, Rev 20:10, Matt 25:46, Mark 9:43-48, Luke 16:19-31, Rev 6:9-11, 2 Thess 1:9, Dan 12:2.

    Verse-by-Verse Analysis¶

    ECT Argument #1 — Rev 14:9-11: Smoke of Torment Ascending Forever¶

    Context: Third angel's message in Revelation 14. Apocalyptic genre. Part of the three angels' messages sequence (vv.6-12). The beast-worshippers are warned of divine wrath.

    Direct statement: The text says: (1) those who worship the beast will drink God's wrath unmixed; (2) they will be tormented (basanisthesetai, G928) with fire and brimstone in the presence of angels and the Lamb; (3) the smoke of their torment (basanismou, G929) ascends unto the ages of the ages; (4) they have no rest (anapausin, G372) day and night.

    ECT steelman: The plain language describes ongoing conscious torment with smoke ascending forever and no rest. This is the strongest ECT passage because it names human subjects (beast-worshippers) and uses duration language ("for ever and ever") with explicit denial of cessation ("no rest day and night").

    Original language: basanismos (G929) appears 6 times in the NT, all in Revelation. 4 of 6 occurrences are time-bounded (Rev 9:5 — five months; Rev 18:7,10,15 — Babylon's fall). The genitive chain "smoke of their torment" (kapnos tou basanismou auton) — the smoke ascends, not necessarily the torment itself. anapausis (G372) in "no rest day and night" uses the identical construction as Rev 4:8, where the living creatures "have no rest day and night" in their ceaseless praise — a quality-of-activity marker, not a guaranteed eternal duration claim.

    Cross-references: The smoke-ascending formula (eis aionas aionon) draws directly on Isa 34:10, where Edom's smoke "shall go up for ever" (le'olam ya'aleh ashannah). Isa 34:11-17 describes animals dwelling in the ruins — the fire ended, the smoke ceased, animals nest there. Rev 19:3 uses the identical smoke formula for Babylon, whose destruction is documented as total in Rev 18:8-23 with a fivefold "found no more at all."

    Relationship to other evidence: The passage is apocalyptic genre (Tree 3 Gate 3 fail). Prior study etc7-11 classified E1 as Neutral. The I-B resolution found 9+ Plain didactic items from 5 authors using destruction vocabulary governing 1 Ambiguous apocalyptic item. Resolution: Strong toward Cond.

    ECT Argument #2 — Rev 20:10: Tormented Day and Night Forever¶

    Context: Post-millennium judgment in Revelation 20. The devil is cast into the lake of fire where the beast and false prophet already are.

    Direct statement: The text says: (1) the devil that deceived them was cast into the lake of fire; (2) the beast and false prophet are already there; (3) they shall be tormented (basanisthesontai, G928) day and night unto the ages of the ages.

    ECT steelman: This is the only verse with the full "tormented day and night for ever and ever" formula. The ECT argument extends this fate to humans who are cast into the same lake (vv.14-15): same destination, same experience.

    Original language: basanisthesontai (G928, Fut Pass Ind 3P) — the torment-for-ever formula appears once in all of Scripture. The three subjects are: the devil (diabolos — supernatural being, Rev 12:9 four-fold identification chain), the beast (therion — 7 heads, 10 horns, Rev 13:1 — symbolic political-religious system), and the false prophet (pseudoprophetes — earth beast of Rev 13:11-17). All three are non-human or symbolic entities.

    Cross-references: Vocabulary differentiation in consecutive verses by the same author: Rev 20:9 — humans are devoured (katephagen, G2719); Rev 20:10 — devil/beast/false prophet are tormented (basanisthesontai); Rev 20:14-15 — the lake is the second death (thanatos, G2288). Ezek 28:18-19 describes the same being (Satan-figure) with cessation vocabulary: fire devours, becomes ashes, "never shalt thou be any more" (ayin + ad-olam).

    Relationship to other evidence: Tree 3 Gate 1 fails (non-human/symbolic subjects). Gate 3 fails (apocalyptic genre). Prior study etc7-12 classified E9 as Neutral. The extension of the torment formula to humans (I-B) was resolved Strong against — the text's own vocabulary differentiation (devoured vs. tormented for different subjects in consecutive verses) governs.

    Rev 20:9: Humans Devoured by Fire¶

    Context: One verse before the torment formula. The human armies are the direct subject.

    Direct statement: Fire came down from God out of heaven and devoured them (katephagen, G2719 katesthio, Aor Act Ind 3S).

    Original language: katesthio means to devour, eat up, consume. Different verb from basanizo. Same author (John), same passage, different vocabulary for different subjects.

    Relationship to other evidence: This is the vocabulary differentiation datum — the single most important piece of evidence from Rev 20. Humans get "devoured." The devil gets "tormented." The text differentiates.

    Rev 20:14-15: Lake of Fire IS the Second Death¶

    Context: Conclusion of the white throne judgment.

    Direct statement: "This is the second death" (houtos ho thanatos ho deuteros estin). Death and hades are cast into the lake. Whoever was not found in the book of life was cast into the lake.

    Original language: The predicate nominative formula (X = Y) identifies the lake of fire as death (thanatos, G2288), not as torment (basanismos, G929). The lake destroys death and hades — resulting in "no more death" (Rev 21:4).

    Relationship to other evidence: The self-interpretation of the lake as "the second death" controls its meaning. Death is the last enemy destroyed (1 Cor 15:26). If the lake is death, those cast into it die — they are not tormented.

    Rev 21:8: Second Death Applied to Human Sinners¶

    Context: New Jerusalem vision listing human sinners.

    Direct statement: The fearful, unbelieving, abominable, murderers, whoremongers, sorcerers, idolaters, and liars have their part in the lake burning with fire and brimstone — "which is the second death."

    Original language: Again thanatos (G2288), not basanismos (G929). Human sinners receive the second death.

    Relationship to other evidence: Confirms the pattern: humans at the lake receive death, not torment.

    Rev 19:20-21: Vocabulary Differentiation at Armageddon¶

    Context: Battle of Armageddon conclusion.

    Direct statement: Beast and false prophet cast alive into the lake (v.20). The remnant (humans) were slain (apektanthesan, G615 apokteino) with the sword (v.21).

    Original language: apokteino means to kill. Humans are killed. Non-human entities enter the lake alive. Different vocabulary, different fate, consecutive verses.

    Relationship to other evidence: The same vocabulary differentiation pattern as Rev 20:9-10. Every time humans and non-human entities appear in the same context at the lake of fire, humans receive destruction/death vocabulary while non-human entities receive torment vocabulary.

    Rev 19:3: Smoke Formula Applied to Babylon¶

    Context: Heavenly response to Babylon's fall.

    Direct statement: "Her smoke rose up for ever and ever" (ho kapnos autes anabainei eis tous aionas ton aionon).

    Original language: Identical smoke formula as Rev 14:11. But Babylon's destruction is documented as total in Rev 18:8-23 — fivefold "found no more at all" (music, craftsmen, millstone, light, voice of bridegroom).

    Relationship to other evidence: Controls interpretation of Rev 14:11. If the identical formula applied to Babylon describes total destruction (not ongoing torment), the same formula in Rev 14:11 carries the same meaning.

    Rev 4:8: Identical "Day and Night" Construction¶

    Context: Throne room vision. The four living creatures praise God.

    Direct statement: "They rest not day and night" (anapausin ouk echousin hemeras kai nuktos) saying "Holy, holy, holy."

    Original language: anapausis (G372) + "day and night" — structurally identical to Rev 14:11. The construction characterizes uninterrupted activity quality: the living creatures praise without intermission. It does not guarantee this specific activity continues eternally without change.

    Relationship to other evidence: The identical construction in Rev 4:8 demonstrates that "no rest day and night" is a quality marker — describing what happens while it happens — not a duration guarantee.

    ECT Argument #3 — Matt 25:46: Everlasting Punishment¶

    Context: Sheep and goats judgment parable. The parabolic framework is established by v.32 ("as a shepherd divideth").

    Direct statement: "These shall go away into everlasting punishment (kolasin aionion): but the righteous into life eternal (zoen aionion)."

    ECT steelman: The same adjective (aionios, G166) modifies both outcomes with identical grammatical construction (Acc Sg F). If eternal life is genuinely endless, everlasting punishment must be genuinely endless. The parallel demands equal duration.

    Original language: kolasis (G2851, Acc Sg F) means punishment/penalty, from kolazo (G2849, "to curtail/prune"). Only 2 NT occurrences: Matt 25:46 and 1 John 4:18. Jesus chose kolasis, not basanismos (G929). The word means penalty — not experiential torment. The Hebrews pattern shows aionios + result-noun = permanent result of completed act: eternal redemption (Heb 9:12) obtained once, eternal judgment (Heb 6:2) rendered once, eternal salvation (Heb 5:9) authored once. The punishment is inflicted once and the result is permanent. Tit 1:2 uses aionios twice in one verse with different temporal scopes (zoes aioniou = eternal life; chronon aionion = "eternal times" that ended), falsifying the claim that the same adjective always demands the same duration.

    Cross-references: 2 Thess 1:9 defines eschatological punishment as olethron aionion (everlasting destruction) — didactic epistle, all Tree 3 gates pass. Matt 25:41 specifies the fire was "prepared for the devil and his angels" — primary non-human designation.

    Relationship to other evidence: Prior study etc7-14 classified E13 as Neutral — both readings are grammatically possible. I-B resolved Strong toward Cond. because the Plain didactic statement (2 Thess 1:9: olethros = destruction) governs the Ambiguous parabolic statement (Matt 25:46: kolasis = punishment, reading uncertain).

    Matt 25:41: Fire Prepared for Non-Human Entities¶

    Context: Same judgment parable, five verses before v.46.

    Direct statement: "Depart from me, ye cursed, into everlasting fire, prepared for the devil and his angels."

    Original language: The fire's primary designation is for the devil and his angels — non-human entities. Humans are sent there secondarily.

    Relationship to other evidence: This verse specifies that the fire was not originally prepared for humans. Its original purpose is the destruction of supernatural/symbolic entities.

    ECT Argument #4 — Mark 9:43-48: Unquenchable Fire, Worm Dieth Not¶

    Context: Jesus' teaching on radical discipleship. Threefold repetition of the worm/fire formula.

    Direct statement: Mark 9:48 quotes Isa 66:24 directly: "Where their worm dieth not, and the fire is not quenched."

    ECT steelman: Jesus warns of a place where the fire is never quenched and the worm never dies — implying ongoing, never-ending suffering. The language of ceaseless destruction agents (fire, worm) acting continuously suggests the subjects are alive and experiencing it.

    Original language: skolex (G4663) — worm; teleutao (G5053) — dies; pyr (G4442) — fire; sbennymi (G4570) — is quenched. This is a direct quotation of Isaiah 66:24. The OT source text identifies the subjects as peger (H6297) — corpses. Peger means dead body in ALL 24 OT occurrences, never a living person. The worm (tola, H8438) is a biological organism (maggot) feeding on dead flesh. Isa 66:16-17 establishes the transgressors were "slain of the LORD" and "consumed together" before the viewing in v.24.

    Cross-references: "Unquenchable fire" in OT usage: Jer 17:27 — fire on Jerusalem's gates "shall not be quenched" (lo' tikbeh, same vocabulary) — Jerusalem's gates are not still burning. Ezek 20:47 — unquenchable fire in a forest — the forest is not still burning. "Unquenchable" means irresistible (cannot be extinguished while burning), not eternal (never stops burning).

    Relationship to other evidence: The ECT reading requires adding consciousness to subjects the OT source text identifies as corpses (I-C — Compatible External inference). Prior study etc7-21 classified as I-C at the ECT level with three I-D items. katakaio (G2618), Jesus' verb for the fate of tares in Matthew's parables, means "burn completely/consume."

    Isa 66:24: OT Source Text — Subjects Are Corpses¶

    Context: Final verse of Isaiah. The redeemed view the aftermath of judgment.

    Direct statement: "They shall go forth, and look upon the carcases (peger) of the men that have transgressed against me: for their worm shall not die, neither shall their fire be quenched; and they shall be an abhorring (dera'on) unto all flesh."

    Original language: peger (H6297, Noun mp Cst) — always means dead body (24/24 OT uses). tola'at (H8438) — worm acting on corpses. dera'on (H1860) — abhorrence/revulsion. The subjects of viewing are dead bodies, not conscious living beings.

    Relationship to other evidence: This is the source text that Jesus quotes in Mark 9:48. The worm and fire act on corpses. The dera'on is what the corpses evoke in observers — passive, not experienced.

    Isa 66:16-17: Transgressors Slain Before Viewing¶

    Context: Two verses before Isa 66:24.

    Direct statement: "By fire and by his sword will the LORD plead with all flesh: and the slain of the LORD shall be many. They... shall be consumed together."

    Original language: chalal (H2491) — slain. suph (H5486) — consumed together. The sequence: transgressors are killed and consumed, then their corpses are viewed in v.24. Death precedes the dera'on.

    Relationship to other evidence: Establishes that the objects of viewing in Isa 66:24 are already dead. The worm and fire act on dead bodies after slaughter.

    ECT Argument #5 — Luke 16:19-31: Rich Man and Lazarus¶

    Context: Part of Luke's discourse unit beginning at 15:3 with a single parabole label. The "certain rich man" (anthropos tis plousios) formula appears at Luke 12:16 (explicit parable label) and 16:1 (universally recognized parable).

    Direct statement: The rich man in hades (G86, not gehenna) is "in torments" (basanois, G931 basanos — grief vocabulary, NOT basanismos G929). He says "I am tormented" (odunomai, G3600 odunao — "I am grieved/pained," NOT basanizo G928). Abraham's bosom as post-mortem destination appears only here. The climactic teaching point is the sufficiency of Moses and the prophets (vv.29,31).

    ECT steelman: Jesus names a specific person (Lazarus) — unique among parables — suggesting literal narrative. The rich man's conscious suffering in flame is presented as factual post-mortem reality. If Jesus describes a real person in a real afterlife scenario, this teaches conscious post-mortem existence and suffering.

    Original language: basanos (G931) — torment/touchstone/pain — different vocabulary register from basanismos (G929). odunao (G3600) — grief, emotional pain — different from basanizo (G928). chasma (G5490) — chasm — hapax legomenon. The passage uses grief vocabulary, not eschatological torment vocabulary.

    Cross-references: Luke 12:16 uses the identical "certain rich man" formula with an explicit parabole label. Luke 16:1 uses the same formula — universally recognized as parable. Luke 15:3 provides a single parabole label covering the discourse unit. Same author (Luke) records David is dead and NOT ascended into the heavens (Acts 2:29,34). Paul's logic in 1 Cor 15:18 requires the dead NOT to be in conscious bliss. 7 didactic death-state passages from 6 authors describe the dead as unconscious (Ecc 9:5, Ps 146:4, Ps 6:5, Ps 115:17).

    Relationship to other evidence: Genre classified as parabolic via I-B resolution (Strong) in etc7-09 — 15+ Plain items from 8 independent lines against 1 Contextually Clear item (naming of Lazarus). Even ECT scholars acknowledge this describes hades (intermediate state), not the final hell/lake of fire.

    ECT Argument #6 — Rev 6:9-11: Souls Under the Altar¶

    Context: Fifth seal in Revelation's seal sequence. Apocalyptic vision — "I saw" (eidon) introduces the scene.

    Direct statement: John sees souls under the altar who were slain (esphagmenon, G4969 sphazo) for the word of God. They cry "How long?" and are told to rest (anapauomai, G373) for a little season.

    ECT steelman: These souls are conscious, speaking, and aware of time passing — proving the soul survives death in a conscious state. If souls are conscious between death and resurrection, they could be conscious in hell.

    Original language: sphazo (G4969) links these martyrs to Abel (1 John 3:12 — esphaxen) and the Lamb (Rev 5:6 — esphagmenon). The souls occupy the typological position of sacrificial blood poured at the base of the altar (Lev 4:7). Their "cry" follows the forensic personification pattern: Abel's blood cries (Gen 4:10), wages cry (Jas 5:4), the altar itself speaks (Rev 16:7).

    Cross-references: Rev 20:4-5 identifies the same group (five-link verbal parallel) and locates their "living" (ezesan, G2198) at the first resurrection. The "rest" (anapauo G373) parallels Rev 14:13 (blessed dead rest) and Dan 12:13 (Daniel rests, then stands at the end).

    Relationship to other evidence: Tree 3 Gate 1 fails — symbolic vision-figures, not literal humans. Gate 3 fails — apocalyptic genre, not didactic. Prior study etc7-10 classified all E-items as Neutral or Cond. ECT claims require treating apocalyptic vision as literal anthropological teaching (I-C).

    ECT Argument #7 — 2 Thess 1:7-10: Everlasting Destruction¶

    Context: Didactic epistle — Paul writing to the Thessalonians. Doctrinal statement about eschatological punishment.

    Direct statement: The disobedient "shall be punished with everlasting destruction (olethron aionion) from the presence of the Lord (apo prosopou tou kyriou), and from the glory of his power."

    ECT steelman: "Everlasting destruction from the presence of the Lord" means ongoing ruin or separation from God, not cessation of existence. "Destruction" can mean an ongoing ruinous state, and "from the presence" means banishment, not annihilation by the presence.

    Original language: olethros (G3639, Acc Sg M) — destruction/ruin. All 4 NT occurrences are in Pauline epistles. No lexicon defines olethros as torment. From ollymi (to destroy) — same root as apollymi. Paul chose olethros (destruction), not basanizo (torment). The "apo prosopou" (G575 + G4383) phrase echoes Isa 2:10,19,21 LXX, where the divine presence is the destructive agent — people hide FROM the destroying presence, not are banished from a location. Psa 68:2 confirms: "as wax melteth before the fire, so let the wicked perish at the presence of God."

    Cross-references: The Isa 2:10 LXX parallel establishes the "from the presence" construction as source-of-destruction, not banishment-from-presence. The wax metaphor in Psa 68:2 — wax melts and ceases to be wax; it does not exist in a perpetual state of melting.

    Relationship to other evidence: Prior study etc7-14 classified E7/E15 as Cond. — all four Tree 3 gates passed. This is the only E-tier Cond. classification among the 8 ECT passages. The ECT reading (ongoing ruin, not cessation) requires redefining olethros from its lexical meaning — classified I-D because it overrides the word's established meaning.

    ECT Argument #8 — Dan 12:2: Everlasting Contempt¶

    Context: The only OT text explicitly naming two resurrection outcomes.

    Direct statement: "Many of them that sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake, some to everlasting life (chayyei olam), and some to shame (cherpah) and everlasting contempt (dera'on olam)."

    ECT steelman: "Everlasting life" and "everlasting contempt" use identical olam modification in parallel grammatical construction. If life is genuinely eternal, contempt must be genuinely eternal conscious experience — the parallelism demands equal duration.

    Original language: chayyei olam (H2416 + H5769) — everlasting life. dera'on olam (H1860 + H5769) — everlasting contempt. Dera'on has only 2 OT occurrences: Dan 12:2 and Isa 66:24. In Isa 66:24, the objects of dera'on are peger (corpses — dead bodies). Dera'on describes what transgressors become (objects of visceral revulsion), not what they experience. Olam duration is governed by nature-of-subject principle: olam spans from 3 days (Jonah 2:6) to genuinely eternal (Psa 90:2).

    Cross-references: Isa 66:24 provides the only other occurrence of dera'on — applied to corpses being viewed by the redeemed. Dan 12:13 — Daniel himself will rest (nuwach) and stand (qum) at the end — rest = death, standing = resurrection.

    Relationship to other evidence: Prior study etc7-14 classified E15/E17 as Neutral — ambiguous between experienced shame and observed contempt. The I-B resolution found the only occurrence with specified objects (Isa 66:24 = corpses) governs the occurrence without specified objects (Dan 12:2). Resolution: Strong toward Cond.

    Original language: thanatos — death. The binary is death vs. eternal life. Not torment vs. eternal life.

    Relationship to other evidence: E-tier Cond. All Tree 3 gates pass. Didactic genre, universal scope.

    Original language: monos (G3441) — only/alone. athanasia (G110) — immortality. Only 3 NT occurrences of athanasia: here, 1 Cor 15:53, 1 Cor 15:54. God alone possesses immortality inherently. Humans do not.

    Relationship to other evidence: Undermines the ECT foundational assumption that human souls cannot cease to exist. If God alone has immortality, humans are mortal and can die permanently.

    Matt 10:28: God Able to Destroy Soul and Body in Gehenna¶

    Context: Jesus' teaching to the twelve. Didactic instruction.

    Direct statement: "Fear him which is able to destroy (apollymi, G622) both soul (psyche, G5590) and body in hell (gehenna, G1067)."

    Original language: apollymi with divine/authority subject in Matthew consistently means actual destruction (Matt 2:13; 12:14; 21:41; 22:7). God is able to destroy both soul and body.

    Direct statement: "The soul (nephesh, H5315) that sinneth, it shall die (muwth, H4191)."

    Original language: nephesh — soul/living being (mortal). muwth — to die. The soul dies. E-tier Cond.

    Mal 4:1-3: Wicked Become Ashes¶

    Context: Malachi's eschatological prophecy. Didactic prophetic teaching.

    Direct statement: The wicked become stubble (qash), are burned up with neither root nor branch remaining, and become ashes (epher, H665) under the feet of the righteous.

    Original language: saraph (H8313) — burn up; epher — ashes. Complete consumption vocabulary. E-tier Cond.

    John 3:16: Perish vs. Eternal Life¶

    Context: Jesus' discourse with Nicodemus. The foundational Gospel statement.

    Direct statement: "Whosoever believeth in him should not perish (apollymi, G622), but have everlasting life (zoe aionios)."

    Original language: apollymi (Aor Mid Subj 3S) — perish. The Gospel binary: perishing vs. eternal life.

    2 Pet 2:6: Sodom Turned to Ashes as Example¶

    Context: Peter's argument from historical precedent.

    Direct statement: Sodom and Gomorrah turned to ashes (tephroo — hapax legomenon) as an example (hupodeigma) for the ungodly.

    Original language: tephroo — to reduce to ashes. If Sodom is the example of what happens to the ungodly, the ungodly's fate is destruction, not ECT.

    Ezek 28:18-19: Satan-Figure Consumed — Cessation Vocabulary¶

    Context: Lamentation over the king of Tyre — addressing the supernatural being behind the earthly king.

    Direct statement: "I will bring forth a fire from the midst of thee, it shall devour thee (akal, H398), and I will bring thee to ashes (epher, H665)... thou shalt be a terror, and never shalt thou be any more" (ayin + ad-olam — permanent non-existence).

    Original language: The "ayin + ad-olam" formula describes permanent cessation: "you are nothing unto forever." This describes the same being (Satan-figure) who receives the torment formula in Rev 20:10. The OT describes his end as consumption to ashes and permanent non-existence.

    Relationship to other evidence: Ezek 28:18-19 (cessation) and Rev 20:10 (torment) describe the same entity's fate in competing ways. The OT passage is prophetic didactic; Rev 20:10 is apocalyptic vision. SIS clarity criteria: didactic > apocalyptic.

    Isa 34:9-10: OT Source of Ages-of-Ages Smoke Formula¶

    Context: Prophecy against Edom.

    Direct statement: "The smoke thereof shall go up for ever (le'olam): from generation to generation it shall lie waste."

    Original language: le'olam ya'aleh ashannah — "forever ascends its smoke." Identical formula to Rev 14:11.

    Relationship to other evidence: Isa 34:11-17 proves the fire and smoke ended — animals dwell in the ruins. The olam smoke formula describes permanent destruction, not ongoing burning.

    Isa 34:11-17: Animals Dwell in Edom's Ruins¶

    Context: Immediately follows the "smoke ascending forever" passage.

    Direct statement: Cormorant, bittern, owl, raven, wild beasts, vultures possess and dwell in the ruins "for ever" (olam). They nest, hatch, and gather there.

    Relationship to other evidence: Animals dwelling in the ruins proves the fire ended. The olam fire produced permanent desolation — the result is eternal, not the process.

    Patterns Identified¶

    Pattern 1: Torment Vocabulary Confined to Apocalyptic Genre¶

    basanizo (G928) and basanismos (G929) for human eschatological fate appear in zero epistles, zero Gospel teachings, zero OT passages. All eschatological judgment uses are in Revelation's apocalyptic framework. The only Gospel eschatological use (Matt 8:29) is demonic speech, not Jesus' teaching. Luke 16 uses basanos/odunao (grief vocabulary), not basanizo/basanismos. Matt 25:46 uses kolasis (punishment), not basanismos (torment).

    Pattern 2: Destruction Vocabulary Spans All Genres and Authors¶

    Destruction/death vocabulary for the fate of the wicked spans: - Jesus: apollymi (Matt 10:28, John 3:16), katakaio (Matt 13:30,40) - Paul: thanatos (Rom 6:23), olethros (2 Thess 1:9), apoleia (Phil 3:19) - Peter: tephroo (2 Pet 2:6) - Hebrews: esthio (Heb 10:27) - Psalms: abad, kalah, ayin (Psa 37:10,20; 68:2) - Isaiah: chalal, suph (Isa 66:16-17), epher (Isa 34; Ezek 28:18) - Malachi: epher, saraph (Mal 4:1-3) - Ezekiel: muwth + nephesh (Ezek 18:4)

    Pattern 3: Vocabulary Differentiation at the Lake of Fire¶

    In every passage where humans and non-human entities appear together at the lake of fire, different verbs are used for different subjects: - Rev 19:20-21: Beast/false prophet cast alive into lake; humans slain (apektanthesan) - Rev 20:9-10: Humans devoured (katephagen); devil tormented (basanisthesontai) - Rev 20:14-15: Lake = second death (thanatos); humans cast into = receive death

    Pattern 4: Duration Vocabulary (Olam/Aionios) Subject-Determined¶

    olam spans from 3 days (Jonah 2:6) to genuinely eternal (Psa 90:2). Ended institutions labeled olam. Explicitly revoked olam promises (1 Sam 2:30). Fire/smoke called olam that historically ended (Isa 34:10, Jer 17:27). aionios used for past time that ended (Tit 1:2 dual-scope). Hebrews pattern: aionios + result-noun = permanent result of completed act. aidios (G126) exists as the strict "everduring" word — the NT authors did not use it for eschatological punishment.

    Pattern 5: All OT Comparisons Describe Cessation¶

    Nave's "WICKED — COMPARED WITH" entry lists comparisons: chaff (blown away), stubble (burned), ashes (consumed residue), wax (melted), smoke (vanishes), grass (withers), tares (burned). Every comparison describes something that ceases to exist.

    Pattern 6: The "Ages of Ages" Formula Distribution¶

    The formula "eis tous aionas ton aionon" appears 21 times in the NT: 19 doxological (praise to God), 2 judgment uses (Rev 14:11 smoke, Rev 20:10 torment). Both judgment uses are in Revelation's apocalyptic framework for non-human/symbolic entities.

    Word Study Integration¶

    The vocabulary data transforms the English reading of every ECT passage:

    • Rev 14:11: basanismos (G929) — 6 NT occurrences, all in Revelation, 4 of 6 time-bounded. The word never appears in any didactic genre.
    • Rev 20:10: basanizo (G928) — the torment-for-ever formula appears once, applied to non-human entities.
    • Matt 25:46: kolasis (G2851) — punishment/penalty from kolazo "to curtail." 2 NT occurrences. Not torment.
    • Mark 9:48: peger (H6297) — corpse, dead body. 24/24 OT uses = dead body. The subjects are corpses.
    • Luke 16: basanos (G931) / odunao (G3600) — grief vocabulary, not eschatological torment vocabulary.
    • Rev 6:9-11: sphazo (G4969) — slaughter vocabulary linking to sacrificial blood.
    • 2 Thess 1:9: olethros (G3639) — destruction. 4 NT occurrences, all Pauline. No lexicon defines it as torment.
    • Dan 12:2: dera'on (H1860) — abhorrence/contempt applied to corpses in its only other occurrence (Isa 66:24).

    Cross-Testament Connections¶

    1. Rev 14:11 ← Isa 34:10: The smoke-ascending formula is an OT allusion. Edom's fire ended; animals dwell in the ruins.
    2. Mark 9:48 ← Isa 66:24: Direct quotation. The subjects are peger (corpses).
    3. 2 Thess 1:9 ← Isa 2:10/Psa 68:2: "From the presence" = source of destruction, not banishment.
    4. Rev 20:10 ← Ezek 28:18-19: Same entity (Satan-figure) described with cessation vocabulary in OT.
    5. Jude 1:7 / 2 Pet 2:6 ← Gen 19:24-28: Sodom's "eternal fire" ended — turned to ashes as example.
    6. Rev 6:9 ← Gen 4:10 / Lev 4:7: Forensic personification and altar-blood typology.
    7. Matt 25:46 ← Dan 12:2 ← Isa 66:24: The dera'on chain linking all three passages to corpses.

    Difficult or Complicating Passages¶

    Rev 14:9-11: Human Subjects with Torment Language¶

    This is the most difficult passage for the conditionalist position because it applies torment vocabulary to beast-worshippers (human subjects), not just to the devil/beast/false prophet. The text says "he shall be tormented" (basanisthesetai) and "they have no rest day and night." The passage names human subjects experiencing torment.

    The complication is addressed by: (1) the genre gate (apocalyptic, not didactic); (2) the identical smoke formula in Isa 34:10 describing an ended fire; (3) the identical "no rest day and night" construction in Rev 4:8 characterizing quality, not guaranteed duration; (4) Rev 19:3 applying the identical formula to Babylon's total destruction. But the difficulty remains real: this is the one passage where torment language is applied to human subjects in an eschatological context.

    Matt 25:46: The Parallel Construction¶

    The parallel "everlasting punishment / life eternal" with the same adjective in identical grammatical construction is a genuine difficulty. If the adjective means "endless" when modifying life, the parallel seems to demand the same for punishment.

    The complication is addressed by: (1) the Hebrews pattern showing aionios + result-noun = permanent result; (2) Tit 1:2 showing the same adjective with different temporal scopes in one verse; (3) the nature-of-subject principle from olam evidence. But the surface-level parallel reading is natural and must be acknowledged as a genuine exegetical consideration.

    Rev 20:10: Extension to Humans¶

    The extension argument (same lake = same experience) has surface plausibility. If the devil is tormented in the lake, and humans are thrown into the same lake, the inference that both groups experience the same thing is natural.

    The complication is addressed by the text's own vocabulary differentiation: humans get different verbs (devoured, slain, second death) from non-human entities (tormented) in consecutive verses by the same author. The text itself distinguishes the experiences.

    Preliminary Synthesis¶

    The 8 ECT arguments follow a consistent pattern when evaluated at the E/N/I tier:

    1. E-tier ECT items: 0 across all 8 passages. Not one passage produces an explicit statement that, after passing all four Tree 3 gates, classifies as ECT-direction evidence.

    2. E-tier Cond. items: 62 from prior studies, drawn from didactic passages across multiple authors and genres (Jesus, Paul, Peter, Isaiah, Malachi, Ezekiel, Psalms, Hebrews).
