    Word Studies¶

    Question¶

    8 key ECT arguments evaluated at E/N/I tier: Rev 14:9-11, Rev 20:10, Matt 25:46, Mark 9:43-48, Luke 16:19-31, Rev 6:9-11, 2 Thess 1:9, Dan 12:2.

    G929 — basanismos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G929-basanismos.md

    Occurrences: 6

    Basanismos (torment) — 6 NT occurrences, ALL in Revelation. Distribution: demonic/supernatural torment (Rev 9:5, time-bounded 5 months), eschatological judgment (Rev 14:11), commercial/imperial grief-torment (Rev 18:7,10,15, time-bounded — Babylon's fall). 4 of 6 occurrences are time-bounded. Zero epistles, zero Gospel teachings, zero OT passages. Never used by Jesus, Paul, Peter, or any didactic author for human eschatological fate.

    Semantic categories: demonic torment, eschatological judgment, commercial grief

    Key verses: Rev 9:5, Rev 14:11, Rev 18:7, Rev 18:10, Rev 18:15

    Relevance: Core ECT vocabulary word. Its exclusive confinement to Revelation's apocalyptic framework (zero occurrences in any other genre) is the single most important vocabulary datum in the debate. If torment vocabulary for human eschatological fate existed in epistles, Gospels, or OT, it would be E-tier evidence for ECT. Its absence is decisive.

    G928 — basanizo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G928-basanizo.md

    Occurrences: 12

    Basanizo (to torment/torture/vex) — 12 NT occurrences across 6 books. Distribution: Gospels physical suffering and demonic encounters (6x: Matt 8:6,29; 14:24; Mark 5:7; 6:48; Luke 8:28), epistles moral distress (1x: 2 Pet 2:8), Revelation eschatological torment (5x: Rev 9:5; 11:10; 12:2; 14:10; 20:10). The 'torment-for-ever' formula (basanisthesontai eis tous aionas ton aionon) appears ONCE — Rev 20:10 — applied only to devil/beast/false prophet (all non-human).

    Semantic categories: physical suffering, demonic encounter, moral anguish, eschatological torment

    Key verses: Matt 8:29, 2 Pet 2:8, Rev 14:10, Rev 20:10

    Relevance: Root verb of the basanizo word family. Its distribution across genres is critical: the only eschatological uses are in Revelation. Matt 8:29 (demons) is the only Gospel eschatological use — and it is demonic speech, not Jesus' teaching. Paul never uses basanizo for eschatological punishment.

    G931 — basanos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G931-basanos.md

    Occurrences: 3

    Basanos (touchstone, torment, grief) — 3 NT occurrences: Matt 4:24 (physical sickness), Luke 16:23 (rich man 'in torments'), Luke 16:28 ('place of torment'). Luke 16 uses basanos, NOT basanizo/basanismos. Different vocabulary register from Revelation's eschatological torment. basanos connotes grief/painful testing, not the eschatological torment of basanismos.

    Semantic categories: physical pain, grief, testing

    Key verses: Luke 16:23, Luke 16:28

    Relevance: Critical for Luke 16 analysis: the parable uses basanos (grief vocabulary), NOT the eschatological torment vocabulary of Revelation (basanizo/basanismos). Different word family register.

    G2851 — kolasis¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2851-kolasis.md

    Occurrences: 2

    Kolasis (punishment/penalty) — only 2 NT occurrences: Matt 25:46 ('everlasting punishment') and 1 John 4:18 ('fear hath torment/punishment'). Etymology from kolazo (G2849, 'to curtail/prune') — original sense of cutting short, deterrent penalty. Distinct from basanismos (experiential torment) and timoria (retributive penalty). Jesus chose kolasis, not basanismos — a deliberate vocabulary choice.

    Semantic categories: judicial punishment, penalty, fear's punishing quality

    Key verses: Matt 25:46, 1 John 4:18

    Relevance: The key word in Matt 25:46. ECT reads this as 'everlasting conscious torment,' but the word means punishment/penalty, not torment. Jesus had basanismos available and chose a different word. The kolazo root ('to curtail') suggests ending/cutting off, not perpetuating.

    G166 — aionios¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G166-aionios.md

    Occurrences: 71

    Aionios (eternal/everlasting/age-lasting) — 71 NT occurrences across 19 books. Dominant usage: ~43 of 69 unique verses modify 'life' (zoe). Three past-time uses prove aionios does not inherently mean 'endless': Rom 16:25, 2 Tim 1:9, Tit 1:2. Tit 1:2 uses aionios twice with different temporal scopes in the same verse. The Hebrews pattern (aionios + result-noun = permanent result) controls Matt 25:46 interpretation. aidios (G126) exists as the strict 'everduring' word, distinct from aionios.

    Semantic categories: eternal life, divine attributes, judgment/punishment, pre-temporal reference, eschatological realities

    Key verses: Matt 25:46, 2 Thess 1:9, Tit 1:2, Heb 9:12, Jude 1:7

    Relevance: Central to the ECT duration argument. ECT claims same adjective = same duration in Matt 25:46. The evidence shows aionios duration is governed by subject, not by the word itself. The Hebrews pattern and Tit 1:2 datum are decisive counter-evidence.

    G126 — aidios¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G126-aidios.md

    Occurrences: 2

    Aidios (everduring, strictly eternal) — only 2 NT occurrences: Rom 1:20 (God's 'eternal power') and Jude 1:6 (angels kept in 'everlasting chains'). Derived from aei ('always'), emphasizing continuous unbroken duration. Distinct from aionios which derives from aion ('age'). The existence of aidios as a separate word for strict eternality demonstrates that aionios does not inherently carry the same meaning.

    Semantic categories: strictly eternal, divine attribute, permanent binding

    Key verses: Rom 1:20, Jude 1:6

    Relevance: The existence of aidios as a distinct word for strict eternality is evidence that Greek had the vocabulary to express 'genuinely endless' — and the NT authors chose NOT to use aidios for eschatological punishment.

    G3639 — olethros¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G3639-olethros.md

    Occurrences: 4

    Olethros (destruction/ruin) — 4 NT occurrences, ALL in Pauline epistles: 1 Cor 5:5 (disciplinary destruction of flesh), 1 Thess 5:3 (sudden destruction), 2 Thess 1:9 (everlasting destruction), 1 Tim 6:9 (destruction and perdition). KJV renders uniformly as 'destruction.' No lexicon defines olethros as torment. From ollymi (to destroy) — the same root as apollymi.

    Semantic categories: disciplinary destruction, sudden eschatological destruction, everlasting destruction, catastrophic ruin

    Key verses: 2 Thess 1:9, 1 Thess 5:3, 1 Tim 6:9

    Relevance: Paul's chosen word for eschatological punishment in 2 Thess 1:9. He had basanizo available and chose olethros (destruction). This is the single E-tier Cond. classification among the 8 passages — all four Tree 3 gates pass.

    G622 — apollymi¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G622-apollymi.md

    Occurrences: 102

    Apollymi (to destroy/perish/lose) — 86 unique verses across 15 NT books. Active: to destroy, put to death. Middle/passive: to perish, be ruined. Jesus' primary eschatological verb: 'fear him which is able to destroy (apollymi) both soul and body in hell' (Matt 10:28). Core Gospel binary: 'should not perish (apollymi) but have everlasting life' (John 3:16). Heaviest in Synoptics (Luke 25x, Matthew 18x). Absent from Revelation. LXX equivalent of Hebrew abad (H6).

    Semantic categories: physical destruction, divine judgment, eschatological perishing, losing life/soul, losing possessions, decay

    Key verses: Matt 10:28, John 3:16, Luke 17:29, 2 Pet 3:9

    Relevance: Jesus' chosen word for what God does to the wicked in gehenna. With divine subject in Matthew, apollymi consistently means actual destruction. The perish/eternal-life antithesis (John 3:16) is the core Gospel binary — perishing is the opposite of eternal life.

    G684 — apoleia¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G684-apoleia.md

    Occurrences: 20

    Apoleia (destruction/perdition) — cognate noun of apollymi. Used for eschatological destruction: 'son of perdition' (John 17:12), 'whose end is destruction' (Phil 3:19), 'vessels of wrath fitted to destruction' (Rom 9:22). Paired with olethros in 1 Tim 6:9 ('destruction and perdition'). Part of the broad NT destruction vocabulary for final punishment.

    Semantic categories: eschatological destruction, perdition, waste

    Key verses: Phil 3:19, 2 Pet 2:1, Rev 17:8

    Relevance: NT destruction noun used across multiple authors (Paul, Peter, John) for eschatological punishment. Spans multiple genres. Part of the destruction vocabulary pattern that contrasts with Revelation's exclusive torment vocabulary.

    H5769 — owlam¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H5769-owlam.md

    Occurrences: 432

    Olam (forever/everlasting/perpetual) — 432 tokens across 406 OT verses in 34 books. From root alam (H5956, 'to conceal') — 'the vanishing point,' time that recedes beyond perception. Spans from 3 days (Jonah 2:6) to genuinely eternal (Psa 90:2). Applied to ended institutions: circumcision, Levitical priesthood, Sabbath, land promises. Explicitly revoked olam promise in 1 Sam 2:30. Fire/smoke judgments called olam that historically ended (Isa 34:10, Jer 17:27). Duration determined by nature of subject.

    Semantic categories: divine eternity, covenant permanence, cultic ordinances, judgment duration, past time

    Key verses: Psa 90:2, Isa 34:10, Dan 12:2, Jonah 2:6, 1 Sam 2:30

    Relevance: Hebrew equivalent of aionios. The extensive OT evidence that olam duration is subject-determined (not word-determined) controls interpretation of Dan 12:2 'dera'on olam' and all aionios judgment passages.

    H1860 — dera'on¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H1860-draown.md

    Occurrences: 2

    Dera'on (contempt/abhorrence) — only 2 OT occurrences: Dan 12:2 ('everlasting contempt') and Isa 66:24 ('an abhorring unto all flesh'). From unused root meaning 'to repulse.' Denotes an object that provokes active recoil/revulsion. In Isa 66:24, the objects of dera'on are peger (corpses). Dera'on describes what transgressors BECOME (objects of visceral revulsion), not what they EXPERIENCE.

    Semantic categories: eschatological revulsion, observed abhorrence

    Key verses: Dan 12:2, Isa 66:24

    Relevance: Critical for Dan 12:2 analysis. The only occurrence with specified objects (Isa 66:24 = corpses) must govern the occurrence without specified objects (Dan 12:2). If dera'on is observed revulsion at corpses, then 'everlasting contempt' is a permanent state of the objects (being abhorrent), not a conscious experience.

    H6297 — peger¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H6297-peger.md

    Occurrences: 24

    Peger (corpse/dead body) — always means dead body in ALL 24 OT occurrences. Never refers to a living person. The subjects being viewed in Isa 66:24 are peger — corpses. The worm (tola) acts on corpses. The fire burns corpses. The dera'on is observed on corpses. This is the decisive datum for Mark 9:43-48 and Dan 12:2.

    Semantic categories: dead body, corpse, carcass

    Key verses: Isa 66:24, Isa 14:19, Isa 34:3

    Relevance: Every ECT reading of Mark 9:43-48 must add consciousness to subjects the OT source text identifies as corpses. Peger never means a living person in any of its 24 OT uses.

    G5590 — psyche¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G5590-psyche.md

    Occurrences: 105

    Psyche (soul/life) — 105 NT occurrences. Semantic range: inner self/emotions (Matt 26:38), physical life-force (Matt 10:28), person/individual (Acts 2:41), destroyed in gehenna (Matt 10:28). NOT inherently immortal — can be destroyed. NT equivalent of Hebrew nephesh (H5315). Heaviest in Gospels + Acts (82%).

    Semantic categories: inner self, physical life, person, mortal entity

    Key verses: Matt 10:28, Matt 22:37, Rev 6:9

    Relevance: Central to the immortality question. If psyche can be 'destroyed' in gehenna (Matt 10:28, apollymi), then the soul is not inherently immortal. ECT requires inherent soul immortality — the biblical data contradicts this.

    G110 — athanasia¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G110-athanasia.md

    Occurrences: 3

    Athanasia (immortality/deathlessness) — only 3 NT occurrences: 1 Cor 15:53, 1 Cor 15:54, 1 Tim 6:16. Alpha-privative + thanatos = 'deathlessness.' In 1 Tim 6:16: God ALONE (monos) has immortality. In 1 Cor 15:53-54: mortal MUST PUT ON immortality (at resurrection). Never applied to inherent human nature. Absent from Gospels, Revelation, and all other NT writings.

    Semantic categories: divine attribute, resurrection transformation

    Key verses: 1 Tim 6:16, 1 Cor 15:53, 1 Cor 15:54

    Relevance: The ECT framework assumes inherent human immortality. The NT teaches the opposite: God alone has immortality, and believers receive it at resurrection. If the wicked never receive immortality, they cannot exist forever.

    G2288 — thanatos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2288-thanatos.md

    Occurrences: 119

    Thanatos (death) — 119 tokens across 106 unique verses in 15 NT books. Dominant in Romans (20x) and Revelation (15x). Semantic range: physical death, death as consequence of sin (Rom 5:12; 6:23), spiritual death, second death (Rev 20:14; 21:8). 'Wages of sin is death' (Rom 6:23) — Paul's definitive statement uses thanatos, not basanismos.

    Semantic categories: physical death, sin's consequence, second death, personified death

    Key verses: Rom 6:23, Rev 20:14, Rev 21:8, 1 Cor 15:26

    Relevance: The lake of fire is called 'the second death' (thanatos) — not 'the second torment.' Death is the final enemy destroyed (1 Cor 15:26). The consistent NT vocabulary for sin's ultimate consequence is death/destruction, not torment.

    G372 — anapausis¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G372-anapausis.md

    Occurrences: 5

    Anapausis (rest/intermission/cessation) — 5 NT occurrences: Matt 11:29 (soul rest from Christ), Matt 12:43/Luke 11:24 (demon seeking rest), Rev 4:8 (living creatures 'have no rest' in ceaseless praise), Rev 14:11 (beast-worshippers 'have no rest'). Rev 4:8 and Rev 14:11 share identical construction: 'ouk echousin anapausin hemeras kai nuktos.' In Rev 4:8, 'no rest day and night' characterizes quality of ceaseless activity, not guaranteed eternal duration.

    Semantic categories: spiritual rest, demonic restlessness, ceaseless activity

    Key verses: Rev 14:11, Rev 4:8, Matt 11:29

    Relevance: The 'no rest day and night' phrase in Rev 14:11 is central to the ECT reading. But the identical construction in Rev 4:8 (ceaseless praise of living creatures) shows this phrase characterizes uninterrupted quality, not a duration guarantee. The living creatures' praise will not literally continue forever in the new creation.

    G4969 — sphazo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G4969-sphazo.md

    Occurrences: 10

    Sphazo (to slay/slaughter) — NT occurrences linking Abel, the Lamb, and the martyrs. 1 John 3:12: Cain 'slew' (esphaxen) Abel. Rev 5:6: Lamb 'as it had been slain' (esphagmenon). Rev 6:9: 'souls of them that were slain' (esphagmenon). The sphazō vocabulary chain connects the Rev 6:9 martyrs to sacrificial death imagery, not to conscious survival.

    Semantic categories: sacrificial slaughter, martyrdom, violent death

    Key verses: Rev 6:9, Rev 5:6, 1 John 3:12

    Relevance: The Rev 6:9 souls are 'slain' (sphazō) — slaughter victims. Their position under the altar and their sphazō identification link them to sacrificial blood (Lev 4:7), not to conscious survival of death.

    G2618 — katakaio¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2618-katakaio.md

    Occurrences: 12

    Katakaio (to burn completely/consume) — Jesus' verb for the fate of the tares in Matthew's parables. 'Burn completely' — complete consumption, not preservation in fire. Used for chaff, tares, fruitless branches.

    Semantic categories: complete burning, consumptive fire

    Key verses: Matt 13:30, Matt 13:40

    Relevance: Jesus' own vocabulary for what happens to the wicked in fire is 'burn completely' (katakaio), not 'torment' (basanizo). His parabolic teaching consistently uses destruction vocabulary.

    G3600 — odunao¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G3600-odynao.md

    Occurrences: 4

    Odunao (to grieve/be in pain) — Luke 16:24,25 vocabulary. The rich man says 'I am grieved/pained' (odunomai) in this flame. This is grief vocabulary, NOT the eschatological torment vocabulary of Revelation (basanizo/basanismos). Different word family, different register.

    Semantic categories: grief, emotional pain, sorrow

    Key verses: Luke 16:24, Luke 16:25

    Relevance: Luke 16 uses odunao (grief), not basanizo (torment). This vocabulary distinction separates Luke 16 from Revelation's eschatological torment passages. The parable uses grief/sorrow vocabulary appropriate to its literary genre.

    G165 — aion¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G165-aion.md

    Occurrences: 120

    Aion (age/eon/era) — root noun from which aionios derives. 128 NT occurrences. Means 'an age' — a bounded period of time. The plural formula 'eis tous aionas ton aionon' (ages of ages) appears 21 times in the NT: 19 doxological (praise to God), 2 judgment uses (Rev 14:11 smoke, Rev 20:10 torment). Both judgment uses are in Revelation's apocalyptic framework for non-human entities.

    Semantic categories: age, era, eternity formula

    Key verses: Rev 14:11, Rev 20:10, Gal 1:5

    Relevance: The 'ages of ages' formula's distribution is critical: 19 of 21 uses are doxological (praise to God). Only 2 are for judgment — both in Revelation's apocalyptic framework. The formula is overwhelmingly a doxological expression.

    G1067 — geenna¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1067-geenna.md

    Occurrences: 12

    Geenna (Gehenna/Valley of Hinnom) — used by Jesus for the place of final punishment. Background: the Valley of Hinnom outside Jerusalem, site of child sacrifice (Jer 7:31-32), later became a garbage dump. Connotes destruction in its Valley of Hinnom background — a place where things are destroyed and consumed, not preserved.

    Semantic categories: final punishment, destruction, Valley of Hinnom

    Key verses: Matt 10:28, Mark 9:43, Matt 5:22

    Relevance: The Valley of Hinnom background connotes destruction (fire consuming refuse), not eternal preservation. No hell-word inherently requires eternal conscious torment.

    G86 — hades¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G86-háidēs.md

    Occurrences: 11

    Hades (the grave/unseen realm) — temporary holding place destroyed in Rev 20:14 when death and hades are cast into the lake of fire. Luke 16 is set in hades (intermediate state), not the final lake of fire. ECT scholars themselves acknowledge Luke 16 describes hades, not final hell.

    Semantic categories: intermediate state, the grave, temporary realm

    Key verses: Rev 20:13, Rev 20:14, Luke 16:23

    Relevance: Hades is temporary — it is destroyed in Rev 20:14. Luke 16 is set in hades (not final hell), which even ECT scholars acknowledge. The passage therefore cannot teach about the final state of the wicked.

    G2719 — katesthio¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2719-katesthio.md

    Occurrences: 20

    Katesthio (to devour/eat up/consume) — the verb used for what happens to humans at the lake of fire in Rev 20:9 ('fire came down from God out of heaven, and devoured them'). Different from basanizo (torment) used for the devil in Rev 20:10. The vocabulary differentiation between consecutive verses by the same author is critical.

    Semantic categories: consuming, devouring, total destruction

    Key verses: Rev 20:9

    Relevance: The verb used for human fate at the lake of fire is 'devoured' (katephagen), not 'tormented' (basanisthesontai). Same author, same passage, different vocabulary for different subjects.

    G373 — anapauo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G373-anapauo.md

    Occurrences: 13

    Anapauo (to rest/refresh) — cognate verb of anapausis (G372). Used for the rest of the blessed dead (Rev 14:13), the rest commanded to the souls under the altar (Rev 6:11), and Daniel's rest until the end (Dan 12:13 via LXX). Rest = death; standing/resurrection = the next event.

    Semantic categories: rest, refreshment, death-rest

    Key verses: Rev 6:11, Rev 14:13

    Relevance: The 'rest' vocabulary connects Rev 6:11, Rev 14:13, and Dan 12:13 — in all three, rest refers to the state of death before resurrection.

    H5956 — alam¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H5956-alam.md

    Occurrences: 33

    Alam (to conceal/hide) — root of olam (H5769). The verb means 'to conceal, hide from sight.' Olam derives from this root, meaning 'time whose endpoint is hidden/concealed from view' — not 'time without an endpoint.' The etymological evidence supports subject-determined duration.

    Semantic categories: concealment, hiddenness

    Relevance: Etymological root of olam. The root meaning 'to conceal' indicates olam refers to time whose end is hidden — not time that has no end. This is the linguistic foundation for the nature-of-subject principle.

    H8438 — tola¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H8438-tôwlâʻ.md

    Occurrences: 44

    Tola (worm/maggot) — biological organism that acts on dead bodies. In Isa 66:24 and Mark 9:48, the worm acts on corpses (peger), not on living conscious beings. Maggots feed on dead flesh — they do not torment living people.

    Semantic categories: biological organism, decomposition agent

    Key verses: Isa 66:24

    Relevance: The 'worm that dieth not' in Mark 9:48/Isa 66:24 is a biological organism feeding on corpses. This imagery describes post-mortem decomposition, not conscious torment.

    G2349 — thnetos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2349-thnetos.md

    Occurrences: 6

    Thnetos (mortal) — 6 NT occurrences. Describes the current state of human beings: mortal, subject to death. In 1 Cor 15:53, 'this mortal must put on immortality' — establishing that humans are currently mortal and need a transformation to become immortal.

    Semantic categories: mortality, human condition

    Key verses: 1 Cor 15:53, Rom 6:12

    Relevance: Humans are described as thnetos (mortal). If mortal, they cannot exist forever unless given immortality. ECT requires inherent immortality the Bible does not teach.
