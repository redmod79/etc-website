    ECT Argument #1: Smoke of torment ascending forever and ever¶

    And the third angel followed them, saying with a loud voice, If any man worship the beast and his image, and receive [his] mark in his forehead, or in his hand, The same shall drink of the wine of the wrath of God, which is poured out without mixture into the cup of his indignation; and he shall be tormented with fire and brimstone in the presence of the holy angels, and in the presence of the Lamb: And the smoke of their torment ascendeth up for ever and ever: and they have no rest day nor night, who worship the beast and his image, and whosoever receiveth the mark of his name.

    Context: Third angel's message in Revelation 14. Part of the three angels' messages sequence (Rev 14:6-12). Apocalyptic genre. The passage immediately follows Babylon's fall announcement (v.8) and is followed by the blessing on the dead who die in the Lord (v.13).

    ECT Argument #2: Tormented day and night forever — devil, beast, false prophet¶

    And the devil that deceived them was cast into the lake of fire and brimstone, where the beast and the false prophet [are], and shall be tormented day and night for ever and ever.

    Context: Post-millennium judgment scene in Revelation 20. After the thousand years, Satan deceives the nations (vv.7-8), fire from God devours them (v.9), then the devil is cast into the lake where beast and false prophet already are (v.10). The white throne judgment follows (vv.11-15).

    Notes: Key datum: basanisthesontai (G928, Fut Pass Ind 3P) — the torment-for-ever formula appears ONCE in all of Scripture, applied only to devil/beast/false prophet — all three are non-human/symbolic entities. The devil is supernatural (Rev 12:9). The beast has 7 heads and 10 horns (Rev 13:1) — symbolic political-religious system. The false prophet = earth beast (Rev 13:11-17). Vocabulary differentiation in consecutive verses: humans are devoured (v.9, katephagen G2719), but the devil is tormented (v.10, basanisthesontai). Prior study etc7-12: E9 classified Neutral (Gate 1 fail — non-human subjects; Gate 3 fail — apocalyptic).

    Vocabulary differentiation: Humans devoured by fire¶

    And they went up on the breadth of the earth, and compassed the camp of the saints about, and the beloved city: and fire came down from God out of heaven, and devoured them.

    Context: Same passage as Rev 20:10 — one verse prior. The human armies are the subject of katephagen (devoured), while the devil receives basanisthesontai (tormented) in the next verse.

    Notes: katephagen (G2719, katesthio) — Aor Act Ind 3S — 'devoured them.' Different verb from basanizo used for the devil in v.10. Same author (John), same passage, different vocabulary for different subjects. This is the critical vocabulary differentiation datum.

    Lake of fire IS the second death — predicate nominative formula¶

    And death and hell were cast into the lake of fire. This is the second death. And whosoever was not found written in the book of life was cast into the lake of fire.

    Context: Conclusion of the white throne judgment. Death and hades are destroyed (cast into the lake). The lake is self-interpreted via the X=Y predicate nominative formula: 'This is (estin) the second death.'

    Notes: houtos ho thanatos ho deuteros estin — 'this is the second death.' The predicate nominative formula identifies the lake of fire as death, not as ongoing torment. Death and hades are themselves destroyed in the lake — resulting in 'no more death' (Rev 21:4). Humans cast into the lake (v.15) receive the same fate as death itself — destruction.

    ECT Argument #3: Everlasting punishment — kolasin aionion vs zoen aionion¶

    Then shall he say also unto them on the left hand, Depart from me, ye cursed, into everlasting fire, prepared for the devil and his angels: For I was an hungred, and ye gave me no meat: I was thirsty, and ye gave me no drink: I was a stranger, and ye took me not in: naked, and ye clothed me not: sick, and in prison, and ye visited me not. Then shall they also answer him, saying, Lord, when saw we thee an hungred, or athirst, or a stranger, or naked, or sick, or in prison, and did not minister unto thee? Then shall he answer them, saying, Verily I say unto you, Inasmuch as ye did [it] not to one of the least of these, ye did [it] not to me. And these shall go away into everlasting punishment: but the righteous into life eternal.

    Context: Sheep and goats judgment parable in Matthew 25. The sheep/goats imagery is parabolic (v.32 'as a shepherd divideth'). The fire is 'prepared for the devil and his angels' (v.41) — primary non-human designation.

    Notes: Key parsing: kolasin aionion (G2851 + G166, Acc Sg F) vs zoen aionion (G2222 + G166, Acc Sg F) — identical grammatical construction. ECT steelman: same adjective = same duration. Counter-evidence: (1) kolasis means punishment/penalty (from kolazo 'to curtail'), NOT torment — Jesus had basanismos available and chose differently; (2) Hebrews pattern: aionios + result-noun = permanent result of completed act; (3) Tit 1:2 uses aionios twice with different temporal scopes; (4) 2 Thess 1:9 defines eschatological punishment as olethros (destruction). Prior study etc7-14: E13 classified Neutral — both readings grammatically possible.

    ECT Argument #4: Unquenchable fire and undying worm — quoting Isa 66:24¶

    And if thy hand offend thee, cut it off: it is better for thee to enter into life maimed, than having two hands to go into hell, into the fire that never shall be quenched: Where their worm dieth not, and the fire is not quenched. And if thy foot offend thee, cut it off: it is better for thee to enter halt into life, than having two feet to be cast into hell, into the fire that never shall be quenched: Where their worm dieth not, and the fire is not quenched. And if thine eye offend thee, pluck it out: it is better for thee to enter into the kingdom of God with one eye, than having two eyes to be cast into hell fire: Where their worm dieth not, and the fire is not quenched.

    Context: Jesus' teaching on radical discipleship in Mark 9. The threefold repetition of v.48 (worm dieth not, fire not quenched) is a direct quotation of Isaiah 66:24. Jesus cites the OT source text which identifies the subjects as corpses (peger).

    Notes: Mark 9:48 parsing: skolex (G4663, Nom Sg M) — worm; teleutao (G5053, Pres Act Ind 3S) — dies; pyr (G4442, Nom Sg N) — fire; sbennymi (G4570, Pres Pass Ind 3S) — is quenched. The OT source (Isa 66:24) identifies subjects as peger (H6297) — corpses/dead bodies in ALL 24 OT uses. Isa 66:16-17 establishes transgressors were 'slain of the LORD' and 'consumed together' BEFORE the viewing in v.24. 'Unquenchable fire' in Jer 17:27 and Ezek 20:47 historically ended — Jerusalem's gates are not still burning.

    OT source text for Mark 9:43-48 — subjects are corpses¶

    And they shall go forth, and look upon the carcases of the men that have transgressed against me: for their worm shall not die, neither shall their fire be quenched; and they shall be an abhorring unto all flesh.

    Context: Final verse of Isaiah. The redeemed go forth and view the aftermath of God's judgment. The subjects being viewed are identified as peger (corpses/carcasses), not living beings. Isa 66:16-17 establishes the transgressors were already slain and consumed before this viewing.

    Notes: Hebrew parsing confirms: peger (H6297, Noun mp Cst) = corpses; tola'at (H8438, Noun fs Abs) = worm acting on corpses; muwth (H4191, Qal Impf 3fs) = die; kabah (H3518, Qal Impf 3fs) = go out/be quenched; dera'on (H1860, Noun ms Abs) = abhorrence/revulsion. The subjects of dera'on are always dead bodies. The worm (tola) is a biological organism feeding on corpses, like maggots on a carcass.

    Transgressors slain and consumed BEFORE viewing in v.24¶

    For by fire and by his sword will the LORD plead with all flesh: and the slain of the LORD shall be many. They that sanctify themselves, and purify themselves in the gardens behind one [tree] in the midst, eating swine's flesh, and the abomination, and the mouse, shall be consumed together, saith the LORD.

    Context: The verses immediately preceding Isaiah 66:24. They establish the sequence: the transgressors are first slain (v.16) and consumed (v.17), then their corpses are viewed (v.24). Death precedes the dera'on.

    Notes: H2491 chalal (slain) — the slain of the LORD. H5486 suph (consumed together) — complete consumption. This sequence is critical: death/consumption BEFORE viewing. The objects of dera'on in v.24 are already dead.

    ECT Argument #5: Rich man and Lazarus — genre and vocabulary analysis¶

    There was a certain rich man, which was clothed in purple and fine linen, and fared sumptuously every day: And there was a certain beggar named Lazarus, which was laid at his gate, full of sores, And desiring to be fed with the crumbs which fell from the rich man's table: moreover the dogs came and licked his sores. And it came to pass, that the beggar died, and was carried by the angels into Abraham's bosom: the rich man also died, and was buried; And in hell he lift up his eyes, being in torments, and seeth Abraham afar off, and Lazarus in his bosom. And he cried and said, Father Abraham, have mercy on me, and send Lazarus, that he may dip the tip of his finger in water, and cool my tongue; for I am tormented in this flame. But Abraham said, Son, remember that thou in thy lifetime receivedst thy good things, and likewise Lazarus evil things: but now he is comforted, and thou art tormented. And beside all this, between us and you there is a great gulf fixed: so that they which would pass from hence to you cannot; neither can they pass to us, that [would come] from thence. Then he said, I pray thee therefore, father, that thou wouldest send him to my father's house: For I have five brethren; that he may testify unto them, lest they also come into this place of torment. Abraham saith unto him, They have Moses and the prophets; let them hear them. And he said, Nay, father Abraham: but if one went unto them from the dead, they will repent. And he said unto him, If they hear not Moses and the prophets, neither will they be persuaded, though one rose from the dead.

    Context: Part of Luke's discourse unit beginning at Luke 15:3 (single parabole label). The identical 'certain rich man' formula (anthropos tis plousios) appears at Luke 12:16 (explicit parable label) and Luke 16:1 (universally recognized parable). The climactic teaching point is the sufficiency of Moses and the prophets (vv.29,31).

    Notes: Vocabulary: basanos (G931, Dat Pl F) in v.23 — NOT basanizo/basanismos. Different vocabulary register from Revelation's eschatological torment. odunao (G3600, Pres Pass Ind 1S) in v.24 — grief/pain, NOT eschatological torment vocabulary. chasma (G5490) — hapax legomenon (chasm). The passage describes hades (intermediate state), not the final hell/lake of fire. ECT scholars themselves acknowledge this. Same author (Luke) records David is dead and NOT ascended (Acts 2:29,34). Prior study etc7-09: genre classified as parabolic via I-B resolution (Strong).

    ECT Argument #6: Souls under the altar — apocalyptic vision-figures¶

    Context: Gadarene demoniac encounter. Demons ask about torment — but this is demonic speech, not Jesus' didactic teaching about human eschatological fate.

    Notes: basanizo (G928, Aor Act Inf) — torment. Used by demons, about demons. Not in didactic teaching about human fate. This is the only Gospel occurrence of basanizo in an eschatological context — and it comes from demons, not from Jesus.

    Pauline eschatology: End is destruction¶

    Whose end [is] destruction, whose God [is their] belly, and [whose] glory [is] in their shame, who mind earthly things.)

    Context: Paul describing enemies of the cross. Their 'end' (telos) is 'destruction' (apoleia G684) — not torment.

    Notes: apoleia (G684) = destruction/perdition. Paul consistently uses destruction vocabulary for eschatological punishment: olethros (2 Thess 1:9), apoleia (Phil 3:19), thanatos (Rom 6:23). Never basanizo/basanismos.

    No more death — contradicts eternal dying¶

    And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away.

    Context: New earth description. 'No more death' (thanatos) — death itself is destroyed (Rev 20:14). If death is destroyed, the concept of eternal dying/torment is inconsistent with the new creation reality.

    Notes: ouk estai eti (G3756 + G1510 + G2089) = 'shall not be any more.' Death, sorrow, crying, pain — all cease. ECT posits eternal pain and crying continuing forever, which contradicts this promise.

    Fiery indignation to devour the adversaries¶

    But a certain fearful looking for of judgment and fiery indignation, which shall devour the adversaries.

    Context: Hebrews' warning about willful sin. The fire 'devours' (esthio G2068) the adversaries — destruction vocabulary.

    Notes: esthio (G2068) = to eat/devour. The adversaries are consumed by the fire, not sustained in it.

    Jesus cites Sodom as destruction paradigm¶

    But the same day that Lot went out of Sodom it rained fire and brimstone from heaven, and destroyed [them] all.

    Context: Jesus' teaching about the coming of the Son of Man. He cites Sodom as a model — and uses apollymi (destroyed), not basanizo (tormented).

    Notes: apollymi (G622, Aor Act Ind 3S) — destroyed them all. Jesus' own vocabulary for Sodom's fate is destruction, not torment.

    Seeking immortality — implies not already possessed¶

    To them who by patient continuance in well doing seek for glory and honour and immortality, eternal life:

    Context: Paul on divine judgment. Those who 'seek' (zeteo) immortality receive eternal life. You do not seek what you already possess.

    Notes: aphtharsia (G861) = immortality/incorruptibility. The act of seeking implies not yet possessing. If humans already had inherent immortality, seeking it would be meaningless.

    Paul's logic requires dead NOT conscious¶

    Then they also which are fallen asleep in Christ are perished.

    Context: Paul's resurrection argument. If Christ is not raised, then the dead in Christ 'are perished' (apolonto). This only works if the dead are not already consciously in bliss — if they were, the lack of resurrection would be irrelevant.

    Notes: apollymi (G622, Aor Mid Ind 3P) — have perished. Paul's entire argument in 1 Cor 15 requires that death is actual death (unconsciousness), not transfer to another conscious state.

    Wicked shall not be — cessation vocabulary¶

    For yet a little while, and the wicked [shall] not [be]: yea, thou shalt diligently consider his place, and it [shall] not [be].

    Context: David's wisdom psalm. The wicked cease to exist. Their place is empty.

    Notes: ayin (H369) — nothing, non-existence. The wicked and their place simply are not.

    Wicked shall perish, consume into smoke¶

    But the wicked shall perish, and the enemies of the LORD [shall be] as the fat of lambs: they shall consume; into smoke shall they consume away.

    Context: Continuation of Psalm 37's wisdom teaching. The wicked perish and consume away like smoke — they dissipate into nothing.

    Notes: abad (H6) = perish; kalah (H3615) = consume, come to an end; ashan (H6227) = smoke. The smoke imagery parallels Isa 34:10 and Rev 14:11 — but here it clearly means the wicked vanish like dissipating smoke.

    The altar itself speaks — personification pattern¶

    And I heard another out of the altar say, Even so, Lord God Almighty, true and righteous [are] thy judgments.

    Context: Revelation's bowl judgments. The altar itself speaks — inanimate objects given voice in apocalyptic vision.

    Notes: The altar speaks in Rev 16:7 just as souls cry from under the altar in Rev 6:10. Both are examples of apocalyptic personification — vision-figures performing literary functions.

    Aionios + result-noun: Eternal judgment rendered once¶

    Of the doctrine of baptisms, and of laying on of hands, and of resurrection of the dead, and of eternal judgment.

    Context: List of foundational doctrines. 'Eternal judgment' (krimatsos aioniou) — judgment rendered once with permanent result.

    Notes: krimatos aioniou (G2917 + G166) — eternal judgment. The judgment is not eternally in process; it is rendered once and the result is permanent. Same aionios + result-noun pattern as Heb 9:12 and Heb 5:9.

    Aionios + result-noun: Eternal salvation authored once¶

    And being made perfect, he became the author of eternal salvation unto all them that obey him;

    Context: Christ's high priesthood. 'Eternal salvation' — Christ saves once and the result is permanent. He does not perpetually keep saving.

    Notes: soterias aioniou (G4991 + G166) — eternal salvation. The pattern is consistent: aionios modifies the permanence of the result, not the duration of the process.

    Death-state: No remembrance in death¶

    For in death [there is] no remembrance of thee: in the grave who shall give thee thanks?

    Context: David's prayer during illness. In death there is no remembrance and no thanksgiving.

    Notes: zeker (H2143) = remembrance. In death there is none. Death-state passage contradicting conscious intermediate state.

    Death-state: The dead praise not the LORD¶

    The dead praise not the LORD, neither any that go down into silence.

    Context: Psalm contrasting the living and the dead. The dead do not praise and go down into silence (dumah).

    Notes: dumah (H1745) = silence. The dead go into silence — not into conscious torment or conscious bliss.

    ECT Argument #3 key verse: Kolasin aionion vs zoen aionion¶

    And these shall go away into everlasting punishment: but the righteous into life eternal.

    Context: Final verse of the sheep and goats judgment parable (Matt 25:31-46). The two outcomes are stated in parallel: everlasting punishment (kolasin aionion) vs life eternal (zoen aionion). The fire was 'prepared for the devil and his angels' (v.41).

    Notes: Greek: kolasin (G2851, Acc Sg F) aionion (G166, Acc Sg F) // zoen (G2222, Acc Sg F) aionion (G166, Acc Sg F). Identical grammatical construction. ECT steelman: same adjective = same duration. Counter-evidence: kolasis means punishment (from kolazo 'to curtail'), NOT torment; Hebrews aionios + result-noun pattern; Tit 1:2 dual-scope; 2 Thess 1:9 defines punishment as olethros (destruction). Prior study etc7-14: classified Neutral — both readings grammatically possible.

    Unquenchable fire in OT usage — irresistible, not eternal¶

    And say to the forest of the south, Hear the word of the LORD; Thus saith the Lord GOD; Behold, I will kindle a fire in thee, and it shall devour every green tree in thee, and every dry tree: the flaming flame shall not be quenched, and all faces from the south to the north shall be burned therein.

    Context: Ezekiel's prophecy against the southern forest. God will kindle a fire that 'shall not be quenched' — an unquenchable fire that historically ended. The forest is not still burning.

    Notes: lo' tikbeh (H3808 + H3518) = 'shall not be quenched.' Same vocabulary as Isa 66:24, Mark 9:43-48, and Jer 17:27. 'Unquenchable' means irresistible (cannot be put out while it burns), not eternal (never stops burning). The fire devours (akal) — consumptive, not preservative.

    Identical 'certain rich man' formula — with explicit parable label¶

    And he spake a parable unto them, saying, The ground of a certain rich man brought forth plentifully:

    Context: Jesus teaching about covetousness. The introduction uses the identical formula 'a certain rich man' (anthropos tis plousios) as Luke 16:19, but here Luke explicitly labels it 'a parable' (parabolen).

    Notes: Luke 12:16 uses 'anthropos tis plousios' (a certain rich man) with an explicit parabole label. Luke 16:19 uses the identical formula without the label. Luke 16:1 also uses the same formula (universally recognized as parable). The 'certain rich man' formula is Luke's standard parable introduction.

    Single parabole label covering discourse unit Luke 15-16¶

    And he spake this parable unto them, saying,

    Context: Introduction to the lost sheep, lost coin, and prodigal son sequence. Luke provides a single 'parable' (parabolen) label that covers the discourse unit extending through chapters 15-16.

    Notes: parabolen (G3850) — singular 'this parable.' Luke introduces the entire discourse unit (lost sheep, lost coin, prodigal son, unjust steward, rich man and Lazarus) with a single parable label. The rich man and Lazarus (16:19-31) falls within this parable-labeled discourse section.

    David NOT ascended into the heavens — same author as Luke 16¶

    For David is not ascended into the heavens: but he saith himself, The LORD said unto my Lord, Sit thou on my right hand,

    Context: Peter's Pentecost sermon, recorded by Luke. Peter explicitly states that David has NOT ascended into the heavens — David is dead and buried (Acts 2:29), not consciously alive in heaven.

    Notes: ou gar Daueid anebe eis tous ouranous — 'for David did NOT ascend into the heavens.' Same author (Luke) who records the rich man and Lazarus also records this statement about the dead not being in heaven. This is Luke's own theological position on the state of the dead.

    Sphazō vocabulary chain — Abel slain, same word as Rev martyrs¶

    Not as Cain, [who] was of that wicked one, and slew his brother. And wherefore slew he him? Because his own works were evil, and his brother's righteous.

    Context: John's teaching on love and righteousness. Cain 'slew' (esphaxen) Abel — using sphazō (G4969), the same slaughter vocabulary used for the Lamb (Rev 5:6) and the martyrs (Rev 6:9).

    Notes: esphaxen (G4969, sphazō, Aor Act Ind 3S) — slew/slaughtered. The sphazō vocabulary chain links Abel (1 John 3:12), the Lamb (Rev 5:6), and the martyrs under the altar (Rev 6:9). All three are slaughter victims — the Rev 6:9 souls are identified by their manner of death, not by ongoing conscious existence.

    Sphazō — Lamb as though slain, vocabulary link to Rev 6:9¶

    And I beheld, and, lo, in the midst of the throne and of the four beasts, and in the midst of the elders, stood a Lamb as it had been slain, having seven horns and seven eyes, which are the seven Spirits of God sent forth into all the earth.

    Context: Throne room vision. The Lamb stands 'as it had been slain' (hōs esphagmenon) — using the same sphazō vocabulary as the martyrs in Rev 6:9 and Abel in 1 John 3:12.

    Notes: esphagmenon (G4969, sphazō, Perf Pass Ptcp Acc Sg N) — as having been slain. The perfect participle indicates a past completed action with ongoing visible results. The Lamb bears the marks of slaughter. The same participle form (esphagmenon) describes the martyrs in Rev 6:9. Both Lamb and martyrs are identified as slaughter victims — linking sacrificial death imagery.
