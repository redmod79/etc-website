    Verses¶

    Question¶

    Lake of fire in Revelation. 'Second death' (Rev 2:11; 20:6,14; 21:8). Rev 20:14 ('lake of fire IS the second death'). Death/hell cast into lake (Rev 20:14). What is the lake of fire? What is the second death? What does it mean that Rev 20:14 identifies the lake of fire AS the second death? What does it mean that death and hades are cast into the lake of fire? Who are the subjects who enter the lake? What do Rev 2:11 and 20:6 say about overcomers and the second death? How does the 'second death' function as the text's own explanation of what the lake does?

    Beast and false prophet cast alive into lake; human remnant slain¶

    19:20 And the beast was taken, and with him the false prophet that wrought miracles before him, with which he deceived them that had received the mark of the beast, and them that worshipped his image. These both were cast alive into a lake of fire burning with brimstone. 19:21 And the remnant were slain with the sword of him that sat upon the horse, which [sword] proceeded out of his mouth: and all the fowls were filled with their flesh.

    Context: Armageddon battle scene. Christ on white horse defeats the beast and its coalition. First subjects cast into the lake of fire. Critical vocabulary differentiation: beast/false prophet cast 'alive' (zontes) vs human remnant 'slain' (apektanthesan).

    Notes: Rev 19:20 uses zontes (V-PAP-NPM, present active participle of zao G2198) for the beast/false prophet being cast 'alive' into the lake. Rev 19:21 uses apektanthesan (V-API-3P, aorist passive indicative of apokteino G615) for the human remnant being 'slain.' The vocabulary differentiation is stark: symbolic entities receive 'alive' language; humans receive death/slaying language.

    First resurrection and second death pairing¶

    20:5 But the rest of the dead lived not again until the thousand years were finished. This [is] the first resurrection. 20:6 Blessed and holy [is] he that hath part in the first resurrection: on such the second death hath no power, but they shall be priests of God and of Christ, and shall reign with him a thousand years.

    Context: Millennium passage. First resurrection participants are exempt from the second death. The structural pairing implies: first resurrection exempts from second death; implied second resurrection (of 'the rest of the dead') leads to second death.

    Notes: Rev 20:6: 'ho deuteros thanatos ouk echei exousian' -- the second death does not have authority/jurisdiction/power (exousia G1849) over first resurrection participants. Exousia is a delegated-authority word, not a force word. The second death's domain is limited; it has no jurisdictional reach over the blessed.

    Fire from heaven devours human armies -- destruction vocabulary for humans¶

    And they went up on the breadth of the earth, and compassed the camp of the saints about, and the beloved city: and fire came down from God out of heaven, and devoured them.

    Context: Post-millennium. Gog and Magog armies surround the beloved city. Fire from God devours them. ONE VERSE before the torment formula for the devil in v.10.

    Notes: katephagen (V-2AAI-3S, second aorist active indicative of katesthio G2719) -- 'devoured.' This is consumption/destruction vocabulary. The word means to eat down, consume utterly. Applied to human armies. The contrast with v.10 (basanisthesontai for the devil) is immediate and dramatic.

    Torment formula -- applied ONLY to devil, beast, false prophet¶

    And the devil that deceived them was cast into the lake of fire and brimstone, where the beast and the false prophet [are], and shall be tormented day and night for ever and ever.

    Context: The devil joins the beast and false prophet in the lake. The torment formula (basanisthesontai eis tous aionas ton aionon) appears here and ONLY here for lake-of-fire subjects. Never applied to any human.

    Notes: basanisthesontai (V-FPI-3P, future passive indicative of basanizo G928) -- 'they shall be tormented.' The subject is the devil, beast, and false prophet -- all non-human/symbolic entities. The future tense (only future verb in the aorist chain of vv.9-14) projects the consequence beyond the vision's timeframe. 'hemeras kai nyktos' (genitive of time) = continuous; 'eis tous aionas ton aionon' (ages of the ages) = Semitic superlative formula.

    Great white throne judgment -- death/hades cast in; humans not in book of life cast in¶

    20:13 And the sea gave up the dead which were in it; and death and hell delivered up the dead which were in them: and they were judged every man according to their works. 20:14 And death and hell were cast into the lake of fire. This is the second death. 20:15 And whosoever was not found written in the book of life was cast into the lake of fire.

    Context: The culmination of the judgment sequence. Death and hades first act as agents (deliver up the dead, v.13) then become patients (are cast into the lake, v.14). The predicate nominative identification formula defines the lake of fire AS the second death. The book of life is the criterion for human subjects.

    Notes: Rev 20:14: 'houtos ho thanatos ho deuteros estin, he limne tou puros' -- this is the X=Y identification formula. Subject: houtos ho thanatos ho deuteros (demonstrative + articular noun + articular ordinal, maximally definite). Copula: estin (V-PAI-3S). Predicate nominative: he limne tou puros (articular, definite). The text uses thanatos (death) vocabulary, NOT basanismos (torment) or kolasis (punishment). This is the text's own interpretive key: the lake of fire IS death -- the second death. Rev 20:15: first-class conditional (ei + indicative) -- the condition is assumed to have occurred.

    No more death -- the stated outcome of the lake of fire sequence¶

    And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away.

    Context: New creation passage. Immediately following the lake of fire sequence. This is the STATED OUTCOME: no more death. If death and hades have been cast into the lake (Rev 20:14), and the lake IS the second death, then the result is the abolition of death itself.

    Notes: ho thanatos ouk estai eti (the death not shall-be anymore) -- future middle indicative of eimi with negation. Death as a category of existence is abolished. The key question for ECT: if billions are being consciously tormented forever, how can there be 'no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain'?

    Eight categories of wicked -- their part IS the second death¶

    But the fearful, and unbelieving, and the abominable, and murderers, and whoremongers, and sorcerers, and idolaters, and all liars, shall have their part in the lake which burneth with fire and brimstone: which is the second death.

    Context: New creation passage. Eight categories of wicked humans identified. Their 'part' (meros G3313) is the lake. The identification formula repeats: 'ho estin ho thanatos ho deuteros' -- which IS the second death.

    Notes: to meros auton (N-NSN, their part/portion) -- meros denotes an allotted share or portion. The eight categories: deilois (fearful/cowardly), apistois (unbelieving), ebdelygmenois (abominable, perf pass ptcp of bdelyssomai), phoneusin (murderers), pornois (fornicators), pharmakois (sorcerers), eidololatrais (idolaters), pasin tois pseudesin (all liars). Again, the identification formula uses thanatos vocabulary, not basanismos.

    Overcomer promise: second death cannot hurt the overcomer¶

    2:10 Fear none of those things which thou shalt suffer: behold, the devil shall cast [some] of you into prison, that ye may be tried; and ye shall have tribulation ten days: be thou faithful unto death, and I will give thee a crown of life. 2:11 He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches; He that overcometh shall not be hurt of the second death.

    Context: Letter to Smyrna. First/second death contrast: 'faithful unto death' (first death, martyrdom) in v.10, then 'not hurt of the second death' in v.11. The overcomer who endures the first death is exempt from the second.

    Notes: Rev 2:11: 'ho nikon ou me adikethei ek tou thanatou tou deuterou.' ou me + adikethei (V-APS-3S, aorist passive subjunctive of adikeo G91) = strongest possible Greek negation. The overcomer will ABSOLUTELY NOT be hurt/wronged by the second death. adikeo means to wrong, injure, harm -- the second death's effect is described as 'hurting,' consistent with it being actual death rather than ongoing torment.

    Book of life overcomer promise -- complementary to second death exemption¶

    He that overcometh, the same shall be clothed in white raiment; and I will not blot out his name out of the book of life, but I will confess his name before my Father, and before his angels.

    Context: Letter to Sardis. Overcomer promise: not blotted from book of life. Two sides of the same coin with Rev 2:11: in the book = exempt from second death; NOT in the book = subject to second death (Rev 20:15).

    Notes: ou me exaleipso (V-FAI-1S under ou me) -- emphatic future denial with ou me + future indicative. 'I will by no means blot out.' Same emphatic negation construction as Rev 2:11.

    Christ holds keys of death and hades -- authority over entities later destroyed¶

    [I am] he that liveth, and was dead; and, behold, I am alive for evermore, Amen; and have the keys of hell and of death.

    Context: Christ's self-identification. He holds the keys (authority) of hades and death -- the very entities that are later cast into the lake of fire (Rev 20:14). Christ's authority over death is the precondition for death's eventual abolition.

    Notes: Christ's resurrection ('was dead, and am alive for evermore') gives him authority (keys) over death and hades. These are the same two entities personified as the fourth horseman's companions (Rev 6:8) and later cast into the lake (Rev 20:14).

    Death and hades personified as riders -- same entities later destroyed¶

    And I looked, and behold a pale horse: and his name that sat on him was Death, and Hell followed with him. And power was given unto them over the fourth part of the earth, to kill with sword, and with hunger, and with death, and with the beasts of the earth.

    Context: Fourth seal. Death personified as a named rider; hades follows. They are given authority (exousia) to kill. The same pair appears in Rev 20:13-14 delivering up the dead and then being cast into the lake.

    Notes: Death and hades are consistently personified in Revelation as powers/entities, not persons. Their casting into the lake of fire (Rev 20:14) abolishes them as conditions/states, resulting in 'no more death' (Rev 21:4).

    X=Y identification formula: stars ARE angels, candlesticks ARE churches¶

    The mystery of the seven stars which thou sawest in my right hand, and the seven golden candlesticks. The seven stars are the angels of the seven churches: and the seven candlesticks which thou sawest are the seven churches.

    Context: First occurrence of the X=Y self-interpreting formula in Revelation. Uses eisin (V-PAI-3P) copula to equate symbols with their referents. Same grammatical pattern as Rev 20:14.

    Notes: Pattern: subject (symbol) + copula eisin/estin + predicate nominative (referent). This is the text's self-interpretation mechanism. Rev 20:14 uses the identical construction.

    X=Y identification formulas: heads ARE mountains, waters ARE peoples, woman IS city¶

    17:9 And here [is] the mind which hath wisdom. The seven heads are seven mountains, on which the woman sitteth. 17:15 And he saith unto me, The waters which thou sawest, where the whore sitteth, are peoples, and multitudes, and nations, and tongues. 17:18 And the woman which thou sawest is that great city, which reigneth over the kings of the earth.

    Context: Three more X=Y identification formulas in Rev 17, all using eisin/estin. The same interpretive pattern as Rev 20:14.

    Notes: These formulas demonstrate that when Revelation says X IS Y, the text commits to full identity between the image and its referent. When Rev 20:14 says 'this is the second death,' it is using the identical hermeneutical mechanism.

    X=Y formula: odours ARE prayers of saints¶

    And when he had taken the book, the four beasts and four [and] twenty elders fell down before the Lamb, having every one of them harps, and golden vials full of odours, which are the prayers of saints.

    Context: Another X=Y identification: odours = prayers. Same eisin copula pattern.

    Notes: The X=Y formula appears across Revelation consistently equating symbolic images with their referents.

    OT prototype of X=Y symbol-decoding formula¶

    41:26 The seven good kine [are] seven years; and the seven good ears [are] seven years: the dream [is] one. 41:27 And the seven thin and ill favoured kine that came up after them [are] seven years; and the seven empty ears blasted with the east wind shall be seven years of famine.

    Context: Joseph interprets Pharaoh's dream using verbless nominal clauses (Hebrew NmCl). The prototype of the X=Y symbol-decoding pattern that runs through Daniel and Revelation.

    Notes: Hebrew verbless nominal clause (juxtaposition): subject (visionary element) + predicate (referent). Same pattern as Dan 2:38, Dan 8:20-21.

    OT X=Y formula: Thou ART this head of gold¶

    And wheresoever the children of men dwell, the beasts of the field and the fowls of the heaven hath he given into thine hand, and hath made thee ruler over them all. Thou [art] this head of gold.

    Context: Daniel's interpretation of Nebuchadnezzar's dream using Aramaic pronominal copula. 'ante-hu resha di dahaba' -- Thou art this head of gold.

    Notes: Aramaic pronominal copula: ante-hu (2ms pron + 3ms pron as copula). Same X=Y pattern as Revelation's identification formulas.

    The last enemy destroyed is death -- katargeo applied to death¶

    The last enemy [that] shall be destroyed [is] death.

    Context: Paul's resurrection argument. Death is an enemy that will be destroyed (katargeo G2673). The verb means to render inoperative, abolish, do away with. This is fulfilled in Rev 20:14.

    Notes: katargeitai (V-PPI-3S, present passive indicative of katargeo G2673) -- 'is being destroyed' or 'shall be destroyed.' Present tense here may be futuristic or progressive. Same word used for Christ abolishing death in 2 Tim 1:10 and destroying the devil in Heb 2:14.

    Hades = OT sheol (Psalm 16:10 quoted) -- temporary realm of the dead¶

    Notes: ouk enkataleipseis ten psychen mou eis haden -- 'thou wilt not leave my soul in hades.' Hades is a place from which the soul can be retrieved, confirming its temporary nature. Later hades itself is cast into the lake of fire (Rev 20:14).

    Book of life criterion: not found written = cast into lake of fire¶

    And whosoever was not found written in the book of life was cast into the lake of fire.

    Context: The criterion for the lake of fire: 'not found written in the book of life.' First-class conditional (ei + indicative) assumes the condition occurred. The book of life determines destiny.

    Notes: ei tis ouch heurethe en te biblo tes zoes gegrammenos, eblethe eis ten limnen tou puros.
