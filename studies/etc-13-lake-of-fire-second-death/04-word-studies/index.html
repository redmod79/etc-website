    Word Studies¶

    Question¶

    Lake of fire in Revelation. 'Second death' (Rev 2:11; 20:6,14; 21:8). Rev 20:14 ('lake of fire IS the second death'). Death/hell cast into lake (Rev 20:14). What is the lake of fire? What is the second death? What does it mean that Rev 20:14 identifies the lake of fire AS the second death? What does it mean that death and hades are cast into the lake of fire? Who are the subjects who enter the lake? What do Rev 2:11 and 20:6 say about overcomers and the second death? How does the 'second death' function as the text's own explanation of what the lake does?

    G2288 — thanatos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2288-thanatos.md

    Occurrences: 119

    Masculine noun from thnesko (to die). 119 tokens across 106 unique verses, 15 NT books. Primary meaning: death (physical, spiritual, eschatological). Semantic categories: physical/biological death, death as consequence of sin, death as cosmic power, Christ's atoning death, passage from death to life, death personified/second death, shadow of death. Central to this study: the 'second death' uses this word, NOT basanismos (torment) or kolasis (punishment). The text identifies the lake of fire's function using death vocabulary.

    Semantic categories: physical death, death as sin's consequence, death as cosmic power, Christ's atoning death, spiritual death/passage to life, death personified/second death

    Key verses: Rom 5:12, Rom 6:23, 1 Cor 15:26, Rev 6:8, Rev 20:14, Rev 21:4

    Relevance: THE central word. The 'second death' (deuteros thanatos) is the text's own identification of what the lake of fire does. The predicate nominative in Rev 20:14 uses thanatos, not basanismos. Every identification formula in Rev 20:14 and 21:8 defines the lake's function as DEATH.

    G3041 — limne¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G3041-limne.md

    Occurrences: 10

    Feminine noun. 10 occurrences: 5 in Luke (Sea of Galilee), 5 in Revelation (lake of fire). In Revelation, exclusively in the phrase 'lake of fire' (limne tou puros) or 'lake of fire and brimstone.' Identified as 'the second death' in Rev 20:14 and 21:8.

    Semantic categories: natural body of water, eschatological lake of fire

    Key verses: Rev 19:20, Rev 20:10, Rev 20:14, Rev 20:15, Rev 21:8

    Relevance: The specific word for the 'lake' of fire. Unique to Revelation. All 5 Revelation occurrences refer to the eschatological place of judgment.

    G1208 — deuteros¶

    Occurrences: 47

    Ordinal adjective, 'second.' 47 occurrences across NT. Modifies thanatos in the phrase 'second death' (deuteros thanatos) in Rev 2:11, 20:6, 20:14, 21:8. The ordinal implies a first death and a second death -- the structural pairing of first resurrection/second death frames Revelation's eschatology.

    Semantic categories: ordinal number, eschatological qualifier

    Key verses: Rev 2:11, Rev 20:6, Rev 20:14, Rev 21:8, 1 Cor 15:47

    Relevance: Modifies thanatos in 'second death.' Implies the existence of a first death (physical death) and a distinct second death (the lake of fire). The first resurrection exempts from the second death (Rev 20:6).

    G928 — basanizo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G928-basanizo.md

    Occurrences: 12

    Verb from basanos (touchstone for testing metals). 12 occurrences across 6 books. Semantic range: physical illness (Matt 8:6), laborious struggle (Matt 14:24), moral anguish (2 Pet 2:8), eschatological punishment (Rev 14:10, 20:10). In Revelation's lake-of-fire context, used ONLY for non-human/symbolic entities: devil, beast, false prophet (Rev 20:10). Never applied to human subjects at the lake.

    Semantic categories: physical suffering, demonic encounters, moral distress, eschatological torment

    Key verses: Matt 8:29, Rev 14:10, Rev 20:10

    Relevance: CRITICAL for vocabulary differentiation. In Rev 20:10, basanisthesontai (future passive, 'shall be tormented') is applied ONLY to the devil, beast, and false prophet -- non-human entities. No human is ever described with basanizo at the lake of fire. Humans receive thanatos (death) vocabulary.

    G929 — basanismos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G929-basanismos.md

    Occurrences: 6

    Masculine noun, 'torment.' All 6 NT occurrences in Revelation only. Used for: demonic locust torment (Rev 9:5), beast-worshipper torment smoke ascending (Rev 14:11), Babylon's commercial torment (Rev 18:7,10,15). Exclusively apocalyptic vocabulary.

    Semantic categories: demonic torment, eschatological judgment torment, commercial/imperial torment

    Key verses: Rev 9:5, Rev 14:11, Rev 18:7, Rev 18:10, Rev 18:15

    Relevance: All 6 uses are in Revelation. The 'smoke of their basanismos' formula in Rev 14:11 uses the same 'ascending forever' language as Isa 34:10 (Edom, fire ended) and Rev 19:3 (Babylon, destroyed). The formula describes irreversible destruction, not eternal burning.

    G2673 — katargeo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2673-katargeo.md

    Occurrences: 32

    Verb from kata + argeo. 27 tokens, overwhelmingly Pauline (25/27). Primary senses: render idle/useless, abolish, do away with. Applied to DEATH in 1 Cor 15:26 ('last enemy destroyed is death'), 2 Tim 1:10 ('abolished death'), and the DEVIL in Heb 2:14 ('destroy him that had the power of death').

    Semantic categories: render useless/idle, nullify/void, abolish powers/enemies of Christ, old covenant glory passing, cease/pass away

    Key verses: 1 Cor 15:24, 1 Cor 15:26, 2 Tim 1:10, Heb 2:14

    Relevance: The verb of death's destruction. Applied to both death (1 Cor 15:26, 2 Tim 1:10) and the devil (Heb 2:14). Rev 20:14 fulfills 1 Cor 15:26 by showing death being cast into the lake. In Louw-Nida domain 013003 (cease to exist, be abolished) alongside apollymi.

    G2719 — katesthio¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2719-katesthio.md

    Occurrences: 20

    Verb from kata + esthio. 20 occurrences. Means 'to eat down, devour utterly.' Semantic categories: literal consumption, fire consuming/destruction, figurative exploitation, consuming zeal, hostile intent to destroy. In Rev 20:9, fire from heaven 'devoured' (katephagen) human armies -- destruction vocabulary.

    Semantic categories: literal eating, fire consuming/destruction, figurative exploitation, consuming zeal, hostile intent to destroy

    Key verses: Rev 11:5, Rev 20:9, Rev 12:4

    Relevance: Rev 20:9 uses katephagen (aorist active of katesthio) for fire devouring human armies. This is consumption/destruction vocabulary -- the humans are devoured/consumed, not tormented. ONE VERSE before basanisthesontai (torment) for the devil in v.10.

    G1849 — exousia¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1849-exousia.md

    Occurrences: 103

    Feminine noun. 103 tokens across 93 verses, 16 books. Primary meaning: authority, delegated right, jurisdiction, power. Not raw force (dynamis) but vested right. In Rev 20:6, the second death has no exousia (authority/jurisdiction) over first resurrection participants.

    Semantic categories: divine/delegated authority, personal capacity/right, governmental authority, cosmic authorities, apocalyptic beast authority

    Key verses: Matt 28:18, Rev 13:2, Rev 20:6

    Relevance: Rev 20:6 states the second death 'ouk echei exousian' (does not have authority/jurisdiction) over first resurrection participants. Exousia is a legal/jurisdictional word -- the second death's domain is limited.

    G3528 — nikao¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G3528-nikao.md

    Occurrences: 28

    Verb, 'to overcome, conquer, prevail.' 28 NT occurrences, overwhelmingly in Johannine literature (86%). Dominant usage: overcomer promises in the seven churches (Rev 2-3). The overcomer (ho nikon) receives protection from the second death (Rev 2:11) and inherits all things (Rev 21:7).

    Semantic categories: eschatological victory/overcomer promises, cosmic victory/Christ overcoming, moral victory, military/hostile conquest

    Key verses: Rev 2:7, Rev 2:11, Rev 2:26, Rev 3:21, Rev 21:7

    Relevance: The overcomer (ho nikon, present active participle) in Rev 2:11 is the one exempt from the second death. The overcomer vocabulary is paired with the second death vocabulary throughout Revelation.

    G91 — adikeo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G91-adikeo.md

    Occurrences: 35

    Verb from alpha-privative + dike. 35 tokens across 23 verses, 9 books. Means: to wrong, injure, harm. Revelation accounts for 38% of all occurrences (9/24 verses). In Rev 2:11, adikethei (aorist passive subjunctive) under ou me = the overcomer will ABSOLUTELY NOT be harmed by the second death.

    Semantic categories: physical harm/injury, moral wrongdoing, legal context, relational injury, eschatological justice

    Key verses: Rev 2:11, Rev 7:2-3, Rev 9:4, Rev 22:11

    Relevance: Rev 2:11 uses ou me + adikethei (V-APS-3S) -- the strongest Greek negation with the verb 'to harm/injure.' The second death's effect is described as 'hurting/injuring' -- consistent with it being actual death rather than ongoing separation.

    G4442 — pyr¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G4442-pyr.md

    Occurrences: 74

    Neuter noun, 'fire.' 74 tokens across 73 verses, 14 books. Revelation dominates (25/73 = 34%). Primary collocate in 'lake of fire' (limne tou puros). Semantic categories: eschatological punishment, judgment parables, divine theophany, cosmic plagues, testing/purifying.

    Semantic categories: eschatological punishment, judgment parables, divine theophany, cosmic plagues, testing/refining

    Key verses: Matt 25:41, Rev 20:14-15, Jude 1:7, Heb 12:29

    Relevance: The fire element of the lake of fire. OT background (H784 esh) via LXX bridge (316 co-occurrences). In Revelation, fire functions both as instrument of judgment (Rev 20:9) and as component of the lake's designation.

    H1614 — gophriyth¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H1614-gophriyth.md

    Occurrences: 7

    Feminine noun, 'brimstone/sulphur.' 7 OT occurrences across 6 books. Associated with divine judgment on cities (Gen 19:24, Deut 29:23), eschatological judgment (Ezek 38:22, Isa 34:9), individual retribution (Job 18:15, Psa 11:6), and prepared burning (Isa 30:33). Consistently collocates with esh (fire) and haphak (overthrow).

    Semantic categories: divine judgment on cities, eschatological judgment, individual retribution, prepared burning

    Key verses: Gen 19:24, Psa 11:6, Isa 30:33, Isa 34:9, Ezek 38:22

    Relevance: The brimstone element of 'fire and brimstone.' Every OT fire-and-brimstone event produces destruction/ashes/cessation, not ongoing burning. The paradigm sets expectations for the lake 'burning with fire and brimstone' (Rev 21:8).

    G622 — apollymi¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G622-apollymi.md

    Occurrences: 102

    Verb, 'to destroy fully, perish, lose.' 102 tokens across 86 verses, 15 books. Active: to destroy, ruin, kill. Middle/passive: to perish, be lost. Primary antonym: sozo (save). The fundamental NT binary: perish (apollymi) vs. life (zoe). Used by Jesus to interpret Sodom (Luke 17:29 'destroyed them all') and for gehenna (Matt 10:28 'destroy soul and body').

    Semantic categories: physical destruction/killing, divine judgment/eschatological ruin, perishing in eternal judgment, losing life/soul, losing/finding possession, decay/perishability

    Key verses: John 3:16, Matt 10:28, Luke 17:29, 2 Pet 3:9

    Relevance: The primary destruction verb. Jesus uses it for the Sodom paradigm (Luke 17:29) and for gehenna (Matt 10:28). The apollymi/sozo binary (perish vs. save) is the NT's dominant framework for eternal outcomes -- not torment vs. life.

    G684 — apoleia¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G684-apoleia.md

    Occurrences: 20

    Feminine noun, 'destruction, perdition, waste.' 20 tokens across 19 verses, 11 books. Cognate noun of apollymi. Used for final eschatological ruin (Matt 7:13, Phil 3:19), 'son of perdition' (John 17:12, 2 Thess 2:3), the beast going into perdition (Rev 17:8,11). The NT's preferred eschatological-destiny word.

    Semantic categories: final eschatological ruin, son of perdition, beast going to perdition, waste/squandering, false teachers' ruin

    Key verses: Matt 7:13, Phil 3:19, 2 Pet 3:7, Rev 17:8

    Relevance: The noun form of apollymi. 'Broad way leads to destruction' (Matt 7:13). The beast 'goes into perdition' (Rev 17:8,11) -- destruction vocabulary for the very entity that enters the lake.

    G2851 — kolasis¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2851-kolasis.md

    Occurrences: 2

    Feminine noun, 'punishment.' Only 2 NT occurrences: Matt 25:46 ('everlasting punishment') and 1 John 4:18 ('fear hath torment'). Root kolaz0 = to curtail/prune. The Aristotelian distinction: kolasis = corrective punishment; timoria = retributive punishment. Matt 25:46 uses kolasis, NOT basanismos, for human punishment.

    Semantic categories: eschatological/judicial punishment, tormenting quality of fear

    Key verses: Matt 25:46, 1 John 4:18

    Relevance: Matt 25:46 uses kolasis (punishment) for humans, NOT basanismos (torment). Different vocabulary for humans vs. non-human entities. The etymological connection to 'curtailing' (kolazo = to prune) suggests limitation rather than endless extension.

    G166 — aionios¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G166-aionios.md

    Occurrences: 71

    Adjective from aion (age). 71 tokens across 69 verses, 19 books. Dominant usage: 'eternal life' (~43 of 69 verses). Also modifies: punishment (Matt 25:46), fire (Matt 25:41, Jude 1:7), destruction (2 Thess 1:9). Does NOT modify basanizo in any text -- aionios never directly modifies the torment verb.

    Semantic categories: eternal life, eternal judgment/punishment, divine attributes, eternal realities, eternal purpose

    Key verses: John 3:16, Matt 25:41, Matt 25:46, 2 Thess 1:9, Jude 1:7

    Relevance: Modifies kolasis (punishment, Matt 25:46) and olethros (destruction, 2 Thess 1:9) but NEVER directly modifies basanizo (torment). The 'eternal fire' of Jude 1:7 refers to Sodom -- which is not still burning. Aionios describes the permanence of the result, not necessarily the duration of the process.

    G165 — aion¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G165-aion.md

    Occurrences: 120

    Masculine noun, 'age, era, perpetuity.' 120 tokens across 95 verses, 22 books. The 'ages of the ages' formula (eis tous aionas ton aionon) appears in Rev 20:10 for the torment of the devil/beast/false prophet. This is a Semitic superlative-by-repetition idiom.

    Semantic categories: duration/perpetuity, intensified perpetuity (ages of ages), this present age, age to come, created order/universe

    Key verses: Rev 4:9-10, Rev 11:15, Rev 14:11, Rev 20:10

    Relevance: The 'ages of ages' formula in Rev 20:10 is applied ONLY to the devil, beast, and false prophet -- non-human entities. The same formula is used in doxologies for God's eternal reign (Rev 11:15). Its application to non-human entities at the lake does not automatically extend to human subjects.

    G86 — hades¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G86-háidēs.md

    Occurrences: 11

    Proper locative noun, 'Hades.' 11 NT occurrences across 5 books. The unseen world, realm of the dead. Corresponds to Hebrew sheol (H7585). Paired with thanatos (death) 4 times in Revelation. Cast into the lake of fire in Rev 20:14 -- not a permanent state but a temporary condition abolished.

    Semantic categories: realm of the dead/grave, judgment association, defeated power

    Key verses: Acts 2:27, Acts 2:31, Rev 1:18, Rev 6:8, Rev 20:13-14

    Relevance: Hades is the intermediate state, not the final state. Its casting into the lake of fire (Rev 20:14) represents the abolition of the intermediate state of death. Hades delivers up its dead (Rev 20:13) and is then destroyed.

    G1067 — geenna¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1067-geenna.md

    Occurrences: 12

    Feminine noun, transliteration of Hebrew 'Valley of Hinnom.' 12 NT occurrences: 7 in Matthew, 3 in Mark, 1 in Luke, 1 in James. Almost exclusively on Jesus's lips (11/12). Associated with fire (6x collocate with pyr). Jesus uses apollymi (destroy) with gehenna (Matt 10:28), not basanizo (torment).

    Semantic categories: fiery place of destruction, self-denial to escape it, destruction of soul and body, judicial judgment, moral corruption

    Key verses: Matt 5:22, Matt 10:28, Mark 9:43, Luke 12:5

    Relevance: Gehenna is Jesus's term for eschatological fire; 'lake of fire' is John's Revelation term. They occupy the same functional role but are never equated in the text. Jesus uses destruction vocabulary (apollymi) with gehenna, not torment vocabulary.

    G110 — athanasia¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G110-athanasia.md

    Occurrences: 3

    Feminine noun, alpha-privative + thanatos = 'deathlessness, immortality.' Only 3 NT occurrences: 1 Cor 15:53, 1 Cor 15:54, 1 Tim 6:16. God alone has immortality (1 Tim 6:16 monos echon athanasian). Humans must 'put on' immortality at the resurrection (1 Cor 15:53-54).

    Semantic categories: eschatological transformation at resurrection, exclusive divine attribute

    Key verses: 1 Cor 15:53, 1 Cor 15:54, 1 Tim 6:16

    Relevance: If God alone has inherent immortality, humans are mortal. If immortality is a gift received at the resurrection, the wicked who do not receive it remain mortal -- and mortal beings can be destroyed. The ECT position requires inherent human immortality; athanasia suggests otherwise.

    G3639 — olethros¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G3639-olethros.md

    Occurrences: 4

    Masculine noun from ollymi (to destroy). Only 4 NT occurrences, all Pauline. 'Everlasting destruction' (olethros aionios) in 2 Thess 1:9. Paired with apoleia in 1 Tim 6:9. Emphasizes catastrophic, violent ruin.

    Semantic categories: disciplinary destruction, sudden eschatological destruction, everlasting destruction, destruction paired with perdition

    Key verses: 1 Thess 5:3, 2 Thess 1:9, 1 Tim 6:9

    Relevance: 2 Thess 1:9 uses olethros aionios (everlasting destruction) for the wicked's fate. Paul uses destruction vocabulary, not torment vocabulary, for eschatological punishment of humans.

    G2920 — krisis¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2920-krisis.md

    Occurrences: 47

    Feminine noun from krino (to judge). 47 occurrences, concentrated in Matthew and John. Meaning: judgment (process), condemnation, tribunal, justice. The 'day of judgment' formula (hemera kriseos) is the most common usage.

    Semantic categories: day of judgment, judicial process, present cosmic condemnation, righteous character of judgment

    Key verses: Matt 10:15, Matt 12:36, John 5:22, Heb 9:27, Rev 19:2

    Relevance: The judgment context for the lake of fire. Rev 20:12-13 describes the judgment process (ekritthesan, aorist passive of krino) that precedes the lake of fire casting.

    H4194 — maveth¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H4194-maveth.md

    Occurrences: 170

    H7585 — sheol¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/H7585-showl.md

    Occurrences: 68

    Feminine noun. 68 tokens across 64 verses, 17 OT books. The unseen world of the dead, the grave. LXX renders sheol as hades (G86) with PMI 8.31 (score 33.87). Paired with maveth in synonymous parallelism 15 times. Hos 13:14: 'O grave (sheol), I will be thy destruction.'

    Semantic categories: realm of the dead, descent language, sheol and death paired, fate of the wicked

    Key verses: Gen 37:35, Num 16:33, Psa 89:48, Hos 13:14

    Relevance: The OT equivalent of hades. Both sheol (OT) and hades (NT) are temporary intermediate states. Rev 20:14 fulfills Hos 13:14 by casting hades (= sheol) into the lake, abolishing the intermediate state.
