    CLASSIFICATIONS.md — etc7-13 Lake of Fire / Second Death¶

    Per-item audit trail for all positional E-items (Cond. or ECT) and all N-items. Each entry runs the full Tree 3 or Tree 4 walkthrough using tool-verified parsing data.

    E-Item Classifications (Tree 3)¶

    E4 (Rev 20:9) — "Fire came down from God out of heaven, and devoured them" — Classified: Neutral

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — "devoured" (katephagen, katesthio G2719) = destruction/consumption vocabulary applied to Gog/Magog armies. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): The grammatical subject of katephagen is pyr (fire, G4442, N-NSN). The object (autous, P-APM) refers to the Gog/Magog armies — human beings. However, these are within an apocalyptic vision sequence (Rev 20:7-10). The armies represent "the nations in the four quarters of the earth" (v.8) — numberless as sand. PASS (literal humans are the object of the destruction).
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: katephagen (katesthio G2719, V-2AAI-3S, second aorist active indicative). The verb unambiguously means "devoured/consumed" with pyr as subject and autous as direct object. No alternative parsing exists. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Revelation 20:7-10 is an apocalyptic vision (Rev 1:1 semaino — "signified"). The seer reports visionary scenes using symbolic imagery. FAIL — apocalyptic genre.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Not reached (Gate 3 fails).
    • Step 3 Reclassification:
    • RC1: Genre is apocalyptic vision. The verse depicts a visionary scene of fire consuming armies in an apocalyptic sequence.
    • RC2: Corrected observation: "In an apocalyptic vision, fire from God consumes human armies — the verb katesthio (devour) is used."
    • RC3: The corrected observation uses destruction vocabulary (V1: devour), but applied within apocalyptic genre. As a genre-qualified observation, it does not constitute direct didactic teaching about human fate. Neither V1 (destruction applied to humans as literal teaching) nor V2 applies in corrected form. Result: Neutral.

    E6 (Rev 19:21) — "The remnant were slain with the sword" — Classified: Neutral

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — "slain" (apektanthesan, apokteino G615) = death/killing vocabulary applied to human remnant. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): hoi loipoi (the remnant) — human armies/followers of the beast. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: apektanthesan (apokteino G615, V-API-3P, aorist passive indicative). Unambiguously "were killed." The instrument is en te romphaia (by the sword). No alternative parsing. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Revelation 19:19-21 is an apocalyptic vision (the Armageddon battle scene). The sword proceeds from Christ's mouth (v.15, 21) — symbolic imagery. FAIL — apocalyptic genre.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Not reached.
    • Step 3 Reclassification:
    • RC1: Genre is apocalyptic vision. The sword from the mouth is symbolic (word-sword).
    • RC2: Corrected observation: "In an apocalyptic vision, human followers are described as slain by the symbolic sword proceeding from the rider's mouth."
    • RC3: Genre-qualified: does not constitute direct didactic teaching. Result: Neutral.

    E9 (Rev 21:4) — "There shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither pain" — Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — "no more death" (ho thanatos ouk estai eti) = cessation vocabulary — death itself is abolished. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): The grammatical subject is ho thanatos (death, G2288, N-NSM) — death as a category/condition, not a symbolic entity or allegory. The verse describes the new creation state. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: estai (eimi G1510, V-FDI-3S, future middle indicative) with negation ouk and adverb eti (anymore, G2089). "Death shall not be anymore." Grammar is unambiguous — future negation of death's existence. No alternative parsing. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Rev 21:1-8 is the new creation passage. While within Revelation, this section is descriptive/promissory — direct divine speech ("Behold, I make all things new," v.5). It functions as direct eschatological promise, not symbolic vision. The content echoes the didactic promise of Isa 25:8 (swallow up death, wipe away tears). PASS — promissory/didactic within Revelation.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E11 (1 Cor 15:26, last enemy destroyed is death), E12 (Isa 25:8, swallow up death), E1 (Rev 20:14, death cast into lake). No conflict with any E-item. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands. The abolition of death is directly stated.

    E10 (Rom 6:23) — "The wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life" — Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — "death" (thanatos, G2288) = death vocabulary as the penalty for sin. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): The statement is universal — about all humans who sin. Not symbolic or allegorical. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: thanatos (G2288, N-NSM) as predicate nominative in a verbless clause (ta gar opsonia tes hamartias thanatos). The predicate is unambiguously "death." The contrast is thanatos vs. zoe aionios (eternal life, G2222 + G166). No alternative parsing. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Romans is a Pauline epistle — didactic prose, direct theological teaching. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E25 (Ezek 18:4, soul that sins shall die), E21 (John 3:16, perish vs. life), E26 (2 Thess 1:9, everlasting destruction). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E15 (Gen 19:24-25) — "The LORD rained brimstone and fire... he overthrew those cities" — Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — "overthrew" (haphak H2015) = destruction vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): The inhabitants of Sodom and Gomorrah — literal human beings and their cities. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Haphak (H2015) = to overthrow, overturn. Applied to the cities and "all the inhabitants" (et kol yoshvey ha-arim). Unambiguous destruction vocabulary. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Genesis 19 is historical narrative — reporting historical events. PASS — narrative (didactic weight).
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E16 (Luke 17:29, apollymi), E17 (2 Pet 2:6, tephroo/ashes), E18 (Mal 4:1-3, ashes). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E16 (Luke 17:29) — "It rained fire and brimstone from heaven, and destroyed them all" — Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — "destroyed" (apolesen, apollymi G622) = destruction vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): The people of Sodom — literal humans. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): apolesen (apollymi G622, V-AAI-3S, aorist active indicative). "Destroyed them all" (hapantas). Unambiguous. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Jesus's eschatological discourse — direct teaching in didactic context. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E15, E17. No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E17 (2 Pet 2:6) — "Turning the cities of Sodom to ashes... an ensample unto the ungodly" — Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — "ashes" (tephroo G5077) = destruction/cessation vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): Cities and inhabitants of Sodom — literal entities. The "ensample" (hupodeigma) explicitly extends the application to future "ungodly" (asebein) persons. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): tephrosas (tephroo G5077, aorist active participle). Means "having turned to ashes." Unique NT word. Unambiguous. katastrophe (G2692) = overthrow. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): 2 Peter is an epistle — didactic teaching. Peter explicitly makes this a type ("ensample") for the eschatological fate of the ungodly. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E15, E16, E18. No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands. Peter explicitly makes the destruction-outcome a paradigm for the ungodly.

    E18 (Mal 4:1-3) — "Shall be stubble... shall burn them up... they shall be ashes under your feet" — Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — "stubble," "burn up," "ashes" (qash, lahat, epher H665) = destruction/cessation vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "All the proud, yea, and all that do wickedly" — literal human wicked. Universal scope. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Three destruction terms: qash (stubble), lahat (burn up, set ablaze), epher (H665, ashes). Grammar is direct — the wicked ARE stubble, the day SHALL burn them up, they SHALL BE ashes. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Malachi 4 is direct-speech prophecy ("saith the LORD of hosts"). PASS — didactic/prophetic.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E15-E17. No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E20 (Matt 10:28) — "Fear him which is able to destroy both soul and body in hell" — Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — "destroy" (apolesai, apollymi G622) = destruction vocabulary applied to soul and body. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): The subjects to be destroyed are "soul and body" (psychen kai soma) of human beings. Universal application. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: apolesai — not directly in the parse window, but the verse text confirms apolesai (apollymi G622, aorist active infinitive). Applied to kai psychen kai soma (both soul and body). "The one being able (ton dynamenon) to destroy both soul and body in gehenna." Grammar is unambiguous. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Matthew 10 is direct teaching of Jesus to his disciples — didactic discourse. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E25 (Ezek 18:4, soul can die), E10 (Rom 6:23, wages is death). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E21 (John 3:16) — "Should not perish, but have everlasting life" — Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — "perish" (apoletai, apollymi G622) = destruction vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "Whosoever believeth" / "whosoever does not believe" — universal human scope. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: apoletai would be V-2AMS-3S (aorist middle subjunctive of apollymi G622). In the hina clause: "in order that everyone believing in him might not perish (me apoletai) but have (eche) eternal life." The binary is apollymi vs. zoe aionios. Grammar is unambiguous. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): John 3 is Jesus's direct teaching to Nicodemus — didactic discourse. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E10 (Rom 6:23, death vs. life), E20 (Matt 10:28, destroy), E27 (Phil 3:19, end is destruction). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E24 (1 Tim 6:16) — "Who only hath immortality" — Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — "only hath immortality" implies others do NOT have immortality — cessation vocabulary by implication (humans are mortal/destructible). Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): The subject is God (monos echon athanasian). The implication concerns humans. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): monos (G3441, alone/only) + echon (V-PAP-NSM, present active participle of echo) + athanasian (G110, N-ASF, immortality). "Alone having deathlessness." The word monos unambiguously means "alone/only." PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): 1 Timothy is a Pauline epistle — didactic prose. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E35 (1 Cor 15:53, immortality as future gift), E25 (Ezek 18:4, soul can die). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E25 (Ezek 18:4) — "The soul that sinneth, it shall die" — Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — "die" (muth H4191) = death vocabulary applied to the soul. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): Nephesh (H5315, soul/person) — the literal human person. Universal statement: "all souls are mine." PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): hannephesh hachote't hi' tamuth — "the soul, the sinning one, it shall die." Muth (H4191) in Qal yiqtol (imperfect), expressing future certainty. Grammar is unambiguous. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Ezekiel 18 is direct-speech divine oracle ("saith the Lord GOD"). PASS — didactic/prophetic.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E10 (Rom 6:23), E20 (Matt 10:28). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E26 (2 Thess 1:9) — "Punished with everlasting destruction from the presence of the Lord" — Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — "everlasting destruction" (olethron aionion) = destruction vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "Those who know not God, and obey not the gospel" — literal human wicked. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): olethron (G3639, N-ASM, destruction) + aionion (G166, A-ASM, everlasting). Olethros means catastrophic ruin/destruction. Grammar is unambiguous — the penalty is destruction, qualified as everlasting. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): 2 Thessalonians is a Pauline epistle — didactic prose. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E10, E20, E21, E27. No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E27 (Phil 3:19) — "Whose end is destruction" — Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — "destruction" (apoleia G684) = destruction vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "Enemies of the cross of Christ" — literal human persons. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): hon to telos apoleia — "of whom the end [is] destruction." Apoleia (G684) is the cognate noun of apollymi. Grammar is unambiguous. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Philippians is a Pauline epistle — didactic prose. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E10, E20, E21, E26. No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E28 (Ezek 28:18-19) — "I will bring thee to ashes... never shalt thou be any more" — Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — "ashes" (epher H665), "devour" (akal H398), "never any more" (ayin H369) = destruction/cessation vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): The "anointed cherub" — identified as a supernatural being (the Satan-figure). Not a literal human being. However, unlike Revelation's symbolic beast, this is a non-human spirit being addressed directly by God in a prophetic oracle. FAIL — subject is a non-human spirit being.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Not reached.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Not reached.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Not reached.
    • Step 3 Reclassification:
    • RC1: Subject is a non-human spirit being (the anointed cherub / Satan-figure).
    • RC2: Corrected observation: "God's direct oracle describes the Satan-figure's fate as becoming ashes and existing never any more — destruction vocabulary applied to the same non-human entity tormented in Rev 20:10."
    • RC3: V1 (destruction vocabulary) applies, but to a non-human entity. As an observation about a non-human entity's fate, both sides can accept what the text says while disagreeing about its implications. Result: Neutral.

    E31 (Psa 37:10,20) — "The wicked shall not be... shall consume into smoke" — Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — "shall not be" (ayin H369), "perish" (abad H6), "consume" (kalah H3615) = non-existence/cessation vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "The wicked" — literal human persons. Universal. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): ayin (H369) = non-existence; abad (H6, Qal yiqtol) = shall perish; kalah (H3615, Qal qatal) = consume/be finished. "Into smoke shall they consume away." All terms unambiguously denote cessation. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Psalm 37 is wisdom poetry, functioning as didactic instruction. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E18 (Mal 4:1-3, ashes), E25 (Ezek 18:4, shall die), E32 (Obad 1:16, not been). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E32 (Obad 1:16) — "They shall be as though they had not been" — Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — "as though they had not been" (kelo hayu) = non-existence vocabulary. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "All the heathen" (kol-haggoyim) — literal human nations. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): kelo hayu — "as though they had not been." Perfect tense of hayah with negation and comparative. Unambiguous non-existence language. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Obadiah is direct-speech prophecy. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E31 (Psa 37:10, shall not be), E18 (Mal 4:1-3, ashes). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E33 (Rev 14:10-11) — "He shall be tormented with fire and brimstone... smoke ascendeth forever" — Classified: Neutral

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V2 — "tormented" (basanisthesetai, basanizo G928), "no rest day nor night" = conscious-ongoing-existence vocabulary. Candidate: ECT.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "The same" (autos) — referring to "if any man worship the beast and his image" (v.9). Human beast-worshippers. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: basanisthesetai (basanizo G928, V-FPI-3S, future passive indicative). "He shall be tormented." Grammar supports ECT reading at surface level. However, the "smoke ascending forever" formula (kapnos anabainei eis aionas aionon) draws on Isa 34:10 (same formula for Edom — fire ended). The grammar itself does not resolve whether the formula describes duration of process or permanence of result. PASS (grammar supports the surface reading).
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Revelation 14 is an apocalyptic vision — the three angels' messages delivered within the visionary framework of Rev 1:1 (semaino, "signified"). FAIL — apocalyptic genre.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Not reached.
    • Step 3 Reclassification:
    • RC1: Genre is apocalyptic vision. The passage uses the "smoke ascending forever" formula that derives from Isa 34:10 (Edom, where the fire ended — Isa 34:11-17).
    • RC2: Corrected observation: "In an apocalyptic vision, the third angel uses the 'smoke ascending forever' formula — the same formula applied to Edom (Isa 34:10, fire ended) and Babylon (Rev 19:3, completely destroyed)."
    • RC3: The corrected observation is a genre-qualified statement about apocalyptic formula usage. Neither side's reading survives the genre gate without qualification. Result: Neutral.

    E35 (1 Cor 15:53) — "This mortal must put on immortality" — Classified: Cond.

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 — "mortal" (thnetos) / "put on immortality" (athanasian endysasthai) = mortality vocabulary implying destructibility. Candidate: COND.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): The subject is the resurrected believer — literal humans. The implication extends to all humans (mortals by nature). PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): endysasthai (endyo G1746, aorist middle infinitive) = "to put on." athanasian (G110, N-ASF) = immortality. The construction "must put on" (dei endysasthai) treats immortality as something external that is received, not inherent. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): 1 Corinthians is a Pauline epistle — didactic prose. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E24 (1 Tim 6:16, God only has immortality). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    N-Item Classifications (Tree 4)¶

    N1 — "The text uses different vocabulary for different subject categories at the lake of fire" — Based on: E3, E4, E5, E6 — Classified: Neutral

    • Gate 0 (Foundation):
    • N-Test 1 (Universal agreement): Would an ECT scholar agree that the text uses different vocabulary for different subjects?
