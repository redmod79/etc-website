    CONCLUSION.md — etc7-17: God's Character and Justice¶

    Study Question¶

    God's love, justice, holiness, mercy, wrath. Proportionality of punishment. Consistency of infinite torment vs. destruction with biblical justice.

    Necessary Implications (N)¶

    # Necessary Implication Based on Position Why it is unavoidable Master ID
    N1 God's self-revealed character places mercy as predominant over judgment: 6 mercy-attributes vs 2 justice-attributes, mercy to thousands vs judgment to 3rd/4th generation E1, E2 Neutral The numerical ratio is in the text itself — counting the attributes and the scope terms is arithmetic, not interpretation N078
    N2 Biblical punishment is proportional to deeds — stated in 11+ passages across 9 authors, both testaments E11, E22, E24, E25, E26, E27, E28, E29, E30, E49, E57 Neutral Each passage independently states proportionality; the pattern is observable across genres and authors N079
    N3 The stated penalty for sin is named "death" (thanatos/muth), not "torment" (basanizo) E16, E17, E18, E34, E53 Cond. Each passage names the penalty as death; no passage names the penalty as torment — this is an observation about which word appears N080
    N4 God's anger is explicitly described as temporary: "not for ever" (Psa 103:9, Isa 57:16, Mic 7:18) E3, E6, E8 Cond. Three passages explicitly deny perpetual divine anger using le-olam/la-netsach/la-ad N081
    N5 Every recorded divine judgment in Scripture results in death/destruction — none results in ongoing torment E42, E48, E58, E59 Neutral Observable pattern across all recorded judgments; verifiable by checking each result N082
    N6 God does not take pleasure in executing the death penalty on the wicked — stated three times, once at oath level E13, E14, E15 Neutral Three passages state this directly; Ezek 33:11 adds "As I live" oath formula N083

    Inferences (I)¶

    # Claim Type Position What the Bible actually says Why this is an inference Criteria
    I1 Infinite conscious torment for finite sin is inconsistent with the biblical principle of proportional justice I-A Cond. E11 (Job 34:11 — render according to works), E22 (Rom 2:6 — according to deeds), E24 (Luke 12:47-48 — many/few stripes), E25-26 (Matt 10:15; 11:22,24 — more tolerable), E27 (Heb 2:2 — just recompense), E28 (Heb 10:29 — sorer punishment), E29 (Col 3:25 — for the wrong done), E30 (Rev 20:12-13 — according to works), E49 (Deut 25:1-3 — proportional with 40-stripe limit), N2 (proportionality principle) Systematizes 11+ proportionality texts into a conclusion about ECT's consistency; the individual passages state proportionality; the inference draws the conclusion about infinite torment I105
    I2 Destruction (cessation) as the final fate is consistent with God's self-revealed character of mercy, temporary anger, and the stated penalty of death I-A Cond. E1-E2 (Exo 34:6-7 — mercy predominates), E3 (Psa 103:9 — not keep anger forever), E6 (Mic 7:18 — retains not anger forever), E8 (Isa 57:16 — not contend forever), E16 (Ezek 18:4,20 — soul dies), E34 (Rom 6:23 — wages = death), E42 (2 Pet 2:6 — ashes), E44 (2 Thess 1:9 — everlasting destruction), N3 (stated penalty = death), N4 (anger temporary) Systematizes character, penalty, and wrath-duration texts into a consistency conclusion; each text speaks to a different aspect; the inference draws them together I106
    I3 The "no more death" promise (Rev 21:4) is incompatible with ECT — if the wicked are tormented eternally, death/sorrow/pain have not passed away I-A Cond. E35 (Rev 21:4 — no more death, sorrow, crying, pain), E20 (1 Cor 15:26 — last enemy destroyed is death), E54 (Isa 25:8 — swallow up death), E55 (Rev 20:14 — death cast into lake of fire = second death) Systematizes the "no more death" constellation into an incompatibility claim; each text individually describes death's end; the inference draws the corollary about ongoing torment I107
    I4 Christ's penalty on the cross was death, not eternal torment; this reveals the nature of the penalty for sin I-A Cond. E41 (Rom 5:8 — Christ died for us), E34 (Rom 6:23 — wages of sin = death), E40 (Rom 3:25-26 — demonstration of righteousness), N3 (stated penalty = death) Systematizes cross-as-death passages with stated penalty passages; the conclusion that Christ's death reveals the penalty's nature draws on both sets but adds the systematizing step I108
    I5 ECT reverses the biblical temporal asymmetry between wrath and mercy — making wrath eternal and mercy finite (limited to this life) I-A Cond. E9 (Psa 30:5 — anger a moment, favor a lifetime), E10 (Isa 54:7-8 — small moment of wrath vs everlasting kindness), E3 (Psa 103:9 — not keep anger forever), N4 (anger temporary) Systematizes temporal asymmetry texts into a corollary about ECT; each text states wrath is momentary and mercy lasting; the inference draws the implication I109
    I6 The Anselmian argument (sin against infinite God = infinite punishment) is not found in Scripture — no verse measures punishment by the status of the offended party I-C Neutral E11 (Job 34:11 — according to work), E22 (Rom 2:5-6 — according to deeds), E24 (Luke 12:47-48 — according to knowledge), E29 (Col 3:25 — for the wrong done), N2 (proportionality principle) Observes the absence of a philosophical argument from the text; the Bible measures punishment by the offender's deeds, not the offended party's nature; the Anselmian framework is external to the text I110
    I7 God's holiness demands ECT as the necessary consequence of sin I-D ECT-direction E48 (Lev 10:1-2 — holiness violation → death, not torment), E16 (Ezek 18:4,20 — soul that sins dies), E34 (Rom 6:23 — wages = death), E52 (Psa 145:17,20 — righteous God, wicked destroyed). Every OT holiness violation results in death, not ECT. Requires overriding the explicit penalty (death) with an external concept (holiness necessitates infinite punishment). No text connects holiness to eternal torment. The text connects holiness violations to death. I111
    I8 God's statements about not retaining anger "for ever" apply only to His covenant people, not to the final fate of the wicked I-C ECT-direction E3 (Psa 103:9 — context is Israel), E6 (Mic 7:18 — context is Israel), E8 (Isa 57:16 — context is Israel; however, the anthropological reason "spirits would fail" applies to all humans). The texts do not state "for Israel only." Adds a scope limitation the text does not state; Isa 57:16 gives an anthropological reason that applies universally; but the original covenant-audience context makes this a reasonable external observation I112
    I9 The redeemed's affirmation of God's justice (Rev 15:3-4; 16:5-7; 19:1-2) confirms that God's actual method of judgment is recognized as just and true I-B Neutral FOR: E36-39 (redeemed and angels affirm justice after seeing judgment), E50 (Psa 89:14 — justice is the foundation of God's throne). AGAINST: Both ECT and conditionalist models claim the redeemed would affirm justice; the text states the affirmation without specifying the model. Both sides claim the redeemed's affirmation supports their model; the text does not specify which judgment method is being affirmed I113

    I-B Resolution: I9 — The Redeemed Affirm God's Justice¶

    Step 1 — Tension: - FOR (both models): E36-39 (redeemed affirm justice), E50 (justice is God's throne-foundation) - AGAINST: No E/N items oppose the affirmation itself. The tension is between two competing interpretations of what judgment model the redeemed are responding to.

    Step 2 — Clarity Assessment:

    Item Level Rationale
    E36-39 Plain Directly state the redeemed affirm God's justice — the affirmation is unambiguous
    E50 Plain Directly states justice and judgment are the foundation of God's throne

    Step 3 — Weight: All items are Plain, but they do not specify the model of judgment being affirmed. The affirmation itself is clear; the question of what judgment model it covers is not addressed by these texts.

    Step 4 — SIS Application: No clearer passage resolves which model the redeemed are responding to. The affirmation is neutral as to the mechanism.

    Step 5 — Resolution: Unresolved (Neutral) The redeemed's affirmation of God's justice is a textual fact. Both models claim it. The text does not specify the mechanism being affirmed. Classification remains Neutral.

    What CAN Be Said (Scripture explicitly states or necessarily implies)¶

    1. God's self-revealed character places mercy first and extensively (6 mercy-attributes vs 2 justice-attributes), with mercy extending to "thousands" and judgment limited to "the third and fourth generation" (E1, E2, N1).
    2. God will not keep His anger forever (E3, E6, E8, N4). Isa 57:16 gives an anthropological reason: human spirits (which He made) would fail.
    3. God does not afflict from His heart; judgment is His "strange work" — alien to His character (E7, E19).
    4. The stated penalty for sin is death (thanatos/muth), not torment (basanizo) — stated in Gen 2:17, Ezek 18:4,20, Rom 6:23, 2 Thess 1:9, Jas 1:15 (E16, E17, E34, E44, E53, N3).
    5. Punishment is proportional to deeds — stated in 11+ passages across 9 authors, both testaments, including Jesus' own teaching on "many stripes" vs. "few stripes" and "more tolerable" (E11, E22, E24-30, E49, E57, N2).
    6. God has no pleasure in the death of the wicked — stated three times, once at oath level: "As I live" (E13, E14, E15, N6).
    7. Every recorded divine judgment in Scripture results in death/destruction — flood, Sodom, Nadab/Abihu, Beth-shemesh — none results in ongoing torment (E42, E48, E58, E59, N5).
    8. The paradigmatic divine judgment (Sodom) produced ashes and is designated as the example (hupodeigma) for future ungodly judgment (E42).
    9. Death will be abolished: "no more death, sorrow, crying, pain" (E35, E20, E54, E55).
    10. The redeemed affirm "just and true are thy ways" after seeing God's judgments executed (E36-39).
    11. God is love (agape) — love is His essential nature, not merely an attribute (E45).
    12. Christ bore the penalty of death (apothnesko), not eternal torment (E41).
    13. The cross demonstrates God's righteousness: He is both just and justifier (E40).
    14. God is able to destroy both soul and body in gehenna — the verb is apollymi (destroy), not basanizo (torment) (E46).
    15. God's stated desire is salvation, not punishment: "not willing that any should perish" (E31, E33).

    What CANNOT Be Said (not explicitly stated or necessarily implied by Scripture)¶

    1. No text states that God's holiness requires eternal conscious torment. Every recorded holiness violation in the OT resulted in death. The "holiness demands ECT" argument adds a concept the text does not contain (I7, I-D).
    2. No text states that sin against an infinite God deserves infinite punishment. The Bible measures punishment "according to deeds" (kata ta erga), not according to the nature of the one offended. The Anselmian argument is external to the text (I6, I-C).
    3. No text states that God's wrath is eternal against the wicked. Three passages explicitly deny that God keeps anger forever (E3, E6, E8). The counterclaim that these apply only to Israel is an external scope limitation (I8, I-C).
    4. No text uses torment vocabulary (basanizo/basanismos) for the eschatological fate of generic human wicked in didactic passages. The torment vocabulary for human subjects appears only in parabolic (Luke 16:23-25, using basanos/odunao) and apocalyptic (Revelation) contexts. Didactic passages consistently use death/destruction vocabulary.
    5. No text states that the penalty for sin is eternal conscious torment. The stated penalty is death (Gen 2:17, Ezek 18:4,20, Rom 6:23, 2 Thess 1:9).
    6. Neither side can claim that the redeemed's affirmation of God's justice (Rev 15:3-4; 16:5-7; 19:1-2) exclusively supports their model. The text states the affirmation without specifying the judgment mechanism (I9, I-B, Unresolved/Neutral).
