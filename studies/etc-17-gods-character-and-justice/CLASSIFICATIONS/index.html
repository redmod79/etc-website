    CLASSIFICATIONS.md — etc7-17: God's Character and Justice¶

    Per-item audit trail using Classification Decision Trees (Trees 3-5) with tool-verified parsing for Gate 2.

    E-Item Classifications¶

    E3 (Psa 103:9) -- "He will not always chide: neither will he keep his anger for ever" -- Classified: Cond.¶

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- no destruction vocabulary. V2 -- no conscious-torment vocabulary. However, the verse explicitly denies perpetual divine anger. The denial of perpetual wrath has directional relevance: it limits the duration of divine anger, which bears on the fate question.
    • Re-assessment: This is not V1/V2 vocabulary per se. The statement is about the duration of divine wrath. A denial of perpetual wrath is consistent with conditionalist direction (wrath ends) and inconsistent with ECT direction (wrath is eternal). Candidate: Cond.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): Subject is God (YHWH). Statement is about God's character/behavior toward humans generally. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: lo (neg) + la-netsach (to perpetuity) + yariv (Qal Impf 3ms, "contend"); ve-lo (and not) + le-olam (to eternity) + yittor (Qal Impf 3ms from natar, "be angry/keep"). Two synonymous parallel clauses, both negated, both with durational adverbs (netsach, olam). Grammar unambiguously supports "will not contend forever / will not keep anger forever." PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Psalmic poetry — worship/devotional. Didactic in function (teaching about God's character). PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E6 (Mic 7:18 — retains not anger forever), E8 (Isa 57:16 — not contend forever). No conflicting E-items. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E6 (Mic 7:18) -- "He retaineth not his anger for ever, because he delighteth in mercy" -- Classified: Cond.¶

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: Denial of perpetual anger + statement that God delights in chesed. Directional: wrath is temporary, mercy is God's delight. Candidate: Cond.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): Subject is God. Statement about His character. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: lo (neg) + hecheziq (Hiphil Perf 3ms from chazaq, "be strong" — Hiphil = "to hold fast/retain") + la-ad (to perpetuity) + appo (his anger, from aph + 3ms suffix). Ki (because) + chaphets (Qal Ptcp, "desires/delights") + chesed (loyalty/mercy) + hu (he). Grammar is unambiguous: "He does not hold fast his anger forever because he delights in chesed." PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Prophetic — direct teaching about God's character. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E3 (Psa 103:9) and E8 (Isa 57:16). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E8 (Isa 57:16) -- "I will not contend for ever, neither will I be always wroth: for the spirit should fail before me, and the souls which I have made" -- Classified: Cond.¶

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: Denial of perpetual contention/wrath + anthropological reason (spirits would fail). Candidate: Cond.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): Speaker is God (1st person). Subject of the wrath limitation is God Himself. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: ki (conj) + lo (neg) + le-olam (to eternity) + ariv (Qal Impf 1s, "I contend"); ve-lo (and not) + la-netsach (to perpetuity) + eqtsoph (Qal Impf 1s from qatsaph, "I am angry"). Ki (because) + ruach (spirit/wind) + milliphanay (from before me) + yaatoph (Qal Impf 3ms from ataph, "faint/be overwhelmed"); u-neshamoth (and breaths/souls) + ani (I) + asithi (Qal Perf 1s, "I made"). Grammar unambiguously: "I will not contend forever, nor be angry perpetually, because the spirit would faint before me, and the souls that I have made." The anthropological reason (human spirits would fail) is part of the explicit statement. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Prophetic direct speech ("I will not..."). Didactic. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E3 and E6. No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E16 (Ezek 18:4,20) -- "The soul that sinneth, it shall die" -- Classified: Cond.¶

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "die" (muth) = death/cessation vocabulary applied to the wicked (the sinning soul). Candidate: Cond.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "The soul [nephesh] that sins" — generic human subject. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output (Ezek 18:4): ha-nephesh (the soul, fs Abs) + ha-chotet (Qal Ptcp fs from chata, "the one sinning") + hi (she/it) + tamuth (Qal Impf 3fs from muth, "she shall die"). Grammar is unambiguous: the subject is nephesh (soul), the predicate is tamuth (shall die). The soul itself dies. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Prophetic didactic — God's direct speech about individual justice. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E17 (Gen 2:17 — mot tamut), E34 (Rom 6:23 — wages of sin is death), E53 (Jas 1:15 — sin brings forth death). No conflicting E-item. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E31 (2 Pet 3:9) -- "Not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance" -- Classified: Cond.¶

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "perish" (apollymi) = destruction vocabulary. The fate God wants to prevent is apollymi. Candidate: Cond.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "Any" (tinas) — generic human subject. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: me (neg) + boulomenos (Pres M/P Ptcp Nom Sg M from boulomai, "wishing/willing") + tinas (Acc Pl M, "any/some") + apolesthai (Aor Mid Inf from apollymi, "to perish"). Grammar unambiguous: God is "not wishing any to perish." The infinitive apolesthai is aorist middle — punctiliar, referring to the act of perishing. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Epistle — didactic. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E33 (John 3:16 — not perish), E34 (Rom 6:23 — wages = death), E44 (2 Thess 1:9 — everlasting destruction). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E33 (John 3:16-17) -- "whosoever believeth in him should not perish but have everlasting life... God sent not his Son into the world to condemn the world" -- Classified: Cond.¶

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "perish" (apollymi) = destruction vocabulary. The binary is perish vs. eternal life. Candidate: Cond.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "Whosoever believeth" / "the world" — generic human subjects. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: pas ho pisteuon (pres act ptcp, "everyone believing") + me apoletai (Aor Mid Subj 3s from apollymi, "should not perish") + all' (but) + eche (Pres Act Subj, "may have") + zoen aionion (eternal life). Grammar unambiguous: the contrast is between apollymi (perish) and zoe aionios (eternal life). Two mutually exclusive outcomes. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Gospel narrative — Jesus' direct teaching (didactic). PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E34 (Rom 6:23 — death vs. life), E31 (2 Pet 3:9 — not willing any perish). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E34 (Rom 6:23) -- "The wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life" -- Classified: Cond.¶

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "death" (thanatos) = death/cessation vocabulary as the penalty for sin. Candidate: Cond.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): Generic — "sin" produces death for sinners generally. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: ta opsonia (Nom Pl N, "the wages") + tes hamartias (Gen Sg F, "of sin") + thanatos (Nom Sg M, "death"). To de charisma (Nom Sg N, "but the gift") + tou Theou (Gen Sg M, "of God") + zoe aionios (Nom Sg F, "eternal life"). Grammar is a verbless equative clause: wages of sin = death. Gift of God = eternal life. The two are in explicit antithetical parallelism. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Epistle — didactic. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E16 (Ezek 18:4 — soul dies), E17 (Gen 2:17 — mot tamut), E33 (John 3:16 — perish vs. life), E53 (Jas 1:15 — sin brings death). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E42 (2 Pet 2:6) -- "Turning the cities of Sodom and Gomorrha into ashes... making them an ensample unto those that after should live ungodly" -- Classified: Cond.¶

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "ashes" (tephroo = to turn to ashes) = destruction vocabulary. "Ensample" (hupodeigma) = this is the paradigm for future judgment. Candidate: Cond.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): Cities of Sodom and Gomorrah and their inhabitants — literal historical entities. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: tephrosus (Aor Act Ptcp Nom Sg M from tephroo, "having turned to ashes") + katastrophe (Dat Sg F, "with overthrow") + katekrinen (Aor Act Ind 3s from katakrino, "condemned"). Hupodeigma (Acc Sg N, "example/pattern") + mellonton (Pres Act Ptcp Gen Pl M from mello, "of those about to") + asebein (Pres Act Inf from asebeo, "live ungodly") + tetheikos (Perf Act Ptcp Nom Sg M from tithemi, "having set/placed"). Grammar unambiguous: God condemned Sodom by turning them to ashes, having set them as a paradigm for future ungodly. The aorist participle tephrosus denotes completed action. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Epistle — didactic. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E58 (Jude 1:7 — eternal fire → ashes), E59 (Isa 34:10 — smoke forever but animals dwell). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E44 (2 Thess 1:9) -- "Everlasting destruction" (olethros aionios) -- Classified: Cond.¶

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "destruction" (olethros) = destruction vocabulary. Candidate: Cond.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): "Those who know not God and obey not the gospel" (v.8) — generic human wicked. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: hoitines (Nom Pl M, "who indeed") + diken (Acc Sg F from dike, "penalty/justice") + tisousin (Fut Act Ind 3P from tino, "they shall pay") + olethron (Acc Sg M from olethros, "destruction") + aionion (Acc Sg M from aionios, "everlasting/age-lasting"). Grammar: the penalty (dike) they will pay is olethros aionios (everlasting destruction). Paul chose olethros (destruction, G3639), not basanismos (torment, G929). The word is in accusative as the content of the penalty. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Epistle — didactic. PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E34 (Rom 6:23 — wages = death), E16 (Ezek 18:4 — soul dies), E33 (John 3:16 — perish vs. life). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E46 (Matt 10:28) -- "God is able to destroy both soul and body in gehenna" -- Classified: Cond.¶

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "destroy" (apollymi) = destruction vocabulary applied to soul and body. Candidate: Cond.
    • Step 2 Validation Gates:
    • Gate 1 (Subject): Generic human — "soul and body" of the wicked in gehenna. PASS.
    • Gate 2 (Grammar): Parser output: ton dunamenon (Pres M/P Ptcp Acc Sg M from dunamai, "the one being able") + kai psychen kai soma (both soul and body) + apolesai (Aor Act Inf from apollymi, "to destroy"). Apollymi is aorist active infinitive — punctiliar aspect (completed action). The verb is apollymi (G622, "to destroy fully"), not basanizo (G928, "to torment"). The kai...kai construction ("both...and") emphasizes totality. PASS.
    • Gate 3 (Genre): Gospel — Jesus' direct teaching (didactic). PASS.
    • Gate 4 (Harmony): Consistent with E34 (Rom 6:23), E44 (2 Thess 1:9), E16 (Ezek 18:4). No conflict. PASS.
    • Result: All gates pass. Classification: Cond. stands.

    E47 (Heb 10:27) -- "A fiery indignation which shall devour the adversaries" -- Classified: Cond.¶

    • Step 1 Vocabulary Scan: V1 -- "devour" (esthio = eat/consume) = destruction/consumption vocabulary. Candidate: Cond.
