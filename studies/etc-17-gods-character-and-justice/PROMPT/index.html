    Bible Study: God's love, justice, holiness, mercy, wrath. Proportionality of punishment. Consistency of infinite torment vs. destruction with biblical justice.¶

    Question¶

    God's love, justice, holiness, mercy, wrath. Proportionality of punishment. Consistency of infinite torment vs. destruction with biblical justice.

    Primary Verses (34 — full analysis required)¶

    Old Testament: - Exo 34:6-7 — God's self-description formula — mercy attributes outnumber judgment attributes 6:2; mercy extends to 'thousands', judgment to 'third and fourth generation' - Psa 103:8-14 — Echoes Exo 34:6-7; 'not always chide', 'not keep anger forever', 'remembers we are dust' - Mic 7:18 — Retains not anger forever, delights in mercy (chesed) - Lam 3:33 — Does not afflict willingly — 'milibo' (from His heart). Judgment is not God's delight. - Isa 57:16 — Will not contend forever — human spirits would fail. God limits His own wrath for anthropological reasons. - Jer 3:5 — Rhetorical question: Will He keep anger le-olam? - Psa 30:5 — Temporal asymmetry: anger for a moment, favor for a lifetime - Isa 54:7-8 — 'Small moment' of wrath vs. 'everlasting kindness' — explicit asymmetry between wrath duration and mercy duration - Job 34:11-12 — Render according to ways; God will not do wickedly nor pervert judgment - Gen 18:25 — Abraham's appeal: 'Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?' — God's justice is the premise Abraham argues from - Ezek 18:23,32 — God has no pleasure in the death of the wicked — explicit statement about God's character toward judgment - Ezek 33:11 — As I live, I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked — oath-level statement - Ezek 18:4,20 — The soul that sins shall die (muth) — the penalty stated in the most explicit terms - Gen 2:17 — Thou shalt surely die (mot tamut) — the original penalty announcement. No mention of eternal torment. - Gen 3:19 — Dust thou art, unto dust shalt thou return — the penalty enacted: dissolution, not eternal conscious suffering - Isa 28:21 — God's strange act / strange work — judgment is alien to God's character, not natural to it - 1 Cor 15:26 — Last enemy destroyed is death — death must cease entirely - Psa 145:8-9 — Gracious, full of compassion, slow to anger, great in mercy. The LORD is good to ALL; tender mercies over ALL his works.

    New Testament: - Rom 2:5-11 — Righteous judgment (dikaiokrisia); render 'according to deeds'; embeds anthropological prerequisite — 'seek for immortality' (v.7) implies non-possession - Luke 12:47-48 — Many stripes vs. few stripes — punishment graduated by knowledge. Proportionality is intrinsic to God's justice. - Matt 10:15 — 'More tolerable' for Sodom than cities that rejected the gospel — degrees of judgment - Matt 11:22,24 — 'More tolerable' for Tyre/Sidon than Chorazin/Bethsaida — repeated proportionality principle - Heb 2:2 — Every transgression received 'just recompense' (misthapodosia) — proportionality built into vocabulary - Heb 10:29 — 'Sorer punishment' (axios timoria) — degrees of punishment exist - Col 3:25 — Receive for the wrong done; no respect of persons — punishment matches the specific wrong - Rev 20:12-13 — Judged kata ta erga (according to their works) — final judgment is proportional - 2 Pet 3:9 — Not willing that any should perish (apollymi) — God's desire is salvation, not punishment - 1 Tim 2:4 — Will have all men to be saved — universal salvific will - John 3:16-17 — God so loved the world; sent Son not to condemn but to save — love is the motive, not retribution - Rom 6:23 — Wages of sin = death (thanatos), gift = eternal life — the stated penalty is death, not torment - Rev 21:4 — No more death, sorrow, crying, pain — if ECT is true, death and sorrow exist eternally, contradicting this promise - Rev 15:3-4 — The redeemed affirm 'just and true are thy ways' AFTER seeing judgment — God's justice is vindicated by the manner of judgment - Rom 3:25-26 — God's justice demonstrated at the cross: 'just, and the justifier' — the cross reveals both justice and mercy - 2 Pet 2:6 — Sodom reduced to ashes — an example (hupodeigma) for the ungodly. The paradigmatic judgment is ashes, not torment.
