    Verses¶

    Question¶

    What does the Bible teach about divine vengeance? How is God's vengeance executed across Scripture, and what is the consistent outcome? How does the martyrs' cry for vengeance (Rev 6:10) get answered (Rev 19:2), and what does the answer reveal about the nature of final judgment? Does biblical vengeance support ongoing torment or completed destruction? Additionally: What is the OT pattern for how affliction/torment vocabulary relates to destruction vocabulary?

    Deut 32:35¶

    Notes: Covenantal declaration of divine vengeance. Quoted in Rom 12:19 and Heb 10:30. The foundational text establishing vengeance as God's prerogative. H5359 naqam (noun) — vengeance.

    Deut 32:36¶

    Notes: Judgment paired with mercy — YHWH judges His people but also relents for His servants. Establishes the judicial framework for the Song of Moses.

    Deut 32:41¶

    Notes: Hebrew parsing: naqam (H5359, noun) = vengeance; shub Hiphil = 'render/return.' Vengeance rendered to adversaries. Sword imagery = lethal, not torment.

    Deut 32:42¶

    Notes: Outcome vocabulary: akal (H398) = devour/eat flesh. The sword DEVOURS — destruction vocabulary, not torment. 'Revenges' = par'ot (H6546, wild/loose hair), referring to vengeance on the enemy's heads.

    Deut 32:43¶

    Notes: Climax of Song of Moses. Hebrew: dam avaday yiqqom (he will avenge the blood of his servants). This is the covenantal backbone quoted/alluded to in Rev 6:10 and answered in Rev 19:2. Also kipper admato ammo — he will make atonement for his land and people. The naqam vocabulary is paired with kipper (atonement) and mercy.

    Deut 32:41-43¶

    Notes: Combined range: Song of Moses vengeance climax. Vengeance vocabulary (naqam H5359) + outcome vocabulary (akal H398 = devour). Every outcome described is lethal destruction, not ongoing torment.

    Num 31:1-2¶

    Notes: naqam vocabulary — divine command to avenge. The outcome is described in vv.7,10.

    Num 31:7¶

    Notes: Outcome of the vengeance command (Num 31:2): slew all males. Vengeance result = death.

    Num 31:10¶

    Notes: Outcome of the vengeance command continued: burnt all cities with fire. Vengeance result = destruction by fire.

    2 Kgs 9:7¶

    Notes: naqam vocabulary — avenge the blood of servants/prophets. Same blood-vengeance formula as Deut 32:43 and Rev 6:10/19:2.

    2 Kgs 9:33¶

    Notes: Execution of vengeance on Jezebel. Thrown down, blood spilled, trodden underfoot. Physical death.

    2 Kgs 9:35¶

    Notes: Final outcome: consumed by dogs, nothing left. Vengeance result = total consumption. No ongoing torment.

    Jer 50:15¶

    Notes: Vengeance of the LORD against Babylon. Result: foundations fallen, walls thrown down. Proportional: 'as she hath done, do unto her.'

    Jer 50:40¶

    Notes: Babylon's vengeance outcome linked explicitly to Sodom paradigm: overthrown (haphak H2015), no inhabitant. Total destruction.

    Jer 51:6¶

    Notes: The time of the LORD's vengeance (neqamah H5360). The instruction to flee implies a destructive event, not an ongoing state.

    Jer 51:11¶

    Notes: Explicit equation: vengeance = to DESTROY (shamad H8045). The purpose of the LORD's vengeance is destruction, stated explicitly.

    Jer 51:36-37¶

    Notes: Vengeance outcome: heaps, desolation, without inhabitant. Total destruction and abandonment.

    Nah 1:2¶

    Notes: Triple naqam (noqem x3, Qal active participle) — densest concentration of vengeance vocabulary in the OT. God characterized as THE avenger. Hebrew parsing confirms: noqem (Verb.Qal.Ptcp) appears three times.

    Nah 1:8¶

    Notes: Hebrew: kalah (H3615, noun = destruction/utter end). The vengeance from v.2 results in kalah — complete end. Not torment but termination.

    Nah 1:9¶

    Notes: Hebrew parsing: kalah (noun, destruction) + tsarah (H6869, noun = distress/affliction). Key statement: tsarah does not rise again because kalah has occurred — the affliction terminates because the subjects are consumed. Process (tsarah) does not continue because the outcome (kalah) is final.

    Nah 1:10¶

    Notes: Hebrew: akal (H398, Pual.Perf = devoured/consumed) + qash yavesh male (stubble fully dry). The mechanism of the utter end: consumed like dry stubble. Complete consumption.

    Nah 1:8-10¶

    Notes: Combined range: The vengeance-to-outcome chain. Vengeance (v.2) → utter end (kalah, v.8-9) → devoured as stubble (akal, v.10). Affliction does not rise again (v.9) because the subjects are consumed (v.10). This is the clearest OT example of the process→outcome pattern.

    Isa 1:24¶

    Notes: naqam vocabulary — God will avenge Himself of His enemies. The outcome is described in vv.28,31.

    Isa 1:28¶

    Notes: Outcome of vengeance: destruction (sheber) and consumed (kalah). Not torment but cessation.

    Isa 1:31¶

    Notes: Vengeance result: burn together like tow (ne'oreth = dry flax fiber). Unquenchable = cannot be put out before completing its work. Strong becomes tow — consumed completely.

    Isa 1:28-31¶

    Notes: Combined range for the outcome of Isa 1:24 vengeance declaration.

    Isa 34:8¶

    Notes: yom naqam (H5359) — the day of the LORD's vengeance. Explicit vengeance vocabulary. This is the source text for Revelation's smoke-ascending imagery.

    Isa 34:9-10¶

    Notes: Source imagery for Rev 14:11 and Rev 19:3. Smoke ascending forever. But vv.11-15 show animals dwelling in the ruins — the fires have ceased, only the ruins remain as permanent evidence.

    Isa 34:11-15¶

    Notes: Animals dwelling in the ruins after the fires — proof the fire has gone out. The smoke formula in v.10 describes the permanent aftermath of completed destruction, not ongoing burning. This is the interpretive key to Rev 14:11 and Rev 19:3.

    Gen 19:24-25¶

    Notes: The paradigmatic destruction event. haphak (H2015) = overthrew. Total destruction of inhabitants, cities, vegetation. The model for all subsequent vengeance-by-fire passages.

    Gen 19:28¶

    Notes: Smoke ascending like a furnace — the aftermath evidence. Abraham sees the smoke AFTER the destruction is complete. Source imagery for Isa 34:10, Rev 14:11, Rev 19:3.

    Jude 1:7¶

    Notes: Greek parsing: diken (G1349, dikē, Acc) + pyros (G4442, pyr, Gen) + aioniou (G166, aiōnios, Gen). Sodom = 'vengeance of eternal fire.' Yet the result is ashes (2 Pet 2:6). The genitive construction pyros aioniou modifies the quality/finality of the fire (eternal result), not its duration. prokeitai deigma = 'are set forth as an example' — Sodom's destruction exemplifies what awaits the ungodly.

    2 Pet 2:6¶

    Notes: tephroo (G5077) = 'turning to ashes.' This is the NT interpretation of Sodom's fate: ashes. Not ongoing torment. 'ensample' (hypodeigma) = example/pattern for what happens to the ungodly.

    Isa 13:8¶

    Notes: Hebrew parsing: tsirim (H6735, pangs/convulsions) + chavalim (labor pains) seize them; yechilun (H2342, chuwl, Qal.Impf = writhe in pain). Process vocabulary: pain as the experience DURING judgment. This is the process stage; v.9 gives the outcome.

    Isa 13:9¶

    Notes: Hebrew parsing: yashmid (H8045, shamad, Hiphil.Impf = he shall destroy/exterminate). The OUTCOME: destroy (shamad) the sinners. Process vocabulary (v.8: pangs/pain) precedes outcome vocabulary (v.9: shamad/destroy).

    Isa 13:19¶

    Notes: Babylon's judgment linked to Sodom paradigm: overthrew (mahpekat, from haphak H2015). The process→outcome chain: pangs (v.8) → destroy (v.9) → overthrew like Sodom (v.19).

    Psa 103:9¶

    Notes: Hebrew parsing: lo lanetsach (not + to perpetuity, H5331) yarib (contend, H7378, Qal.Impf); welo l'olam (not + to eternity, H5769) yittor (keep anger, H5201 natar, Qal.Impf). Double negative with two duration terms: netsach (perpetuity) and olam (eternity). Explicit denial of perpetual divine wrath.

    Isa 57:16¶

    Notes: Hebrew parsing: lo l'olam (not + to eternity) ariv (contend, riv H7378, Qal.Impf.1s); welo lanetsach (not + to perpetuity) eqqetsoph (be wrathful, qatsaph H7107, Qal.Impf.1s). Second explicit denial of perpetual divine wrath. God gives the REASON: the spirit would fail — implying wrath that never ends would annihilate the created spirit, which contradicts God's creative purpose.

    Mic 7:18¶

    Notes: Third explicit denial of perpetual divine wrath. lo hecheziq la'ad appo = he does not hold (chazaq H2388) his anger (aph H639) forever (la'ad H5703). Reason: because he delights (chaphets H2654) in mercy (chesed H2617). God's character is mercy-oriented, not wrath-oriented.

    Rev 6:10¶

    Notes: Greek parsing: krineis (G2919, krinō, V-PAI-2S = Present Active Indicative 2nd Sg) + ekdikeis (G1556, ekdikeō, V-PAI-2S = Present Active Indicative 2nd Sg). Both verbs are present tense — imperfective aspect, portraying the action as ongoing/unresolved. The martyrs' cry is for an action that HAS NOT YET HAPPENED. This is the opening of the vindication arc that Rev 19:2 will answer with aorist (completed) verbs.

    Rev 19:1¶

    Notes: The answer to Rev 6:10 begins. The multitude praises because the vindication is DONE.

    Rev 19:2¶

    Notes: Greek parsing: ekrinen (G2919, krinō, V-AAI-3S = Aorist Active Indicative 3rd Sg) + exedikēsen (G1556, ekdikeō, V-AAI-3S = Aorist Active Indicative 3rd Sg). Both verbs are aorist — perfective aspect, viewing the action as a completed whole. The tense shift from present (Rev 6:10 ekdikeis) to aorist (Rev 19:2 exedikēsen) marks narrative resolution. The avenging is DONE. Also echoes Deut 32:43: 'he will avenge the blood of his servants.'

    Rev 19:3¶

    Notes: Greek parsing: anabainei (G305, anabainō, V-PAI-3S = Present Active Indicative 3rd Sg). The smoke 'rises' — present tense, describing permanent state/evidence of completed destruction. This is the smoke-ascending formula from Isa 34:10 and Gen 19:28: permanent aftermath evidence, not ongoing burning.

    Rev 19:1-3¶

    Notes: Combined range: The answer to the martyrs' cry (Rev 6:10). Aorist verbs (ekrinen, exedikēsen) = completed actions. Smoke ascending = permanent aftermath.

    Rev 20:9¶

    Notes: Greek parsing: anebēsan (G305, V-2AAI-3P = Aorist), ekykleusan (G2944, V-AAI-3P = Aorist), katebē (G2597, V-2AAI-3S = Aorist), katephagen (G2719, katesthiō, V-2AAI-3S = second Aorist Active Indicative 3rd Sg = devoured). Four consecutive aorist verbs: went up, surrounded, fire came down, devoured. All completed actions. The devouring is as completed as the other three. katephagen = 'ate down/devoured completely.' Applied to humans.

    Rev 20:10¶

    Notes: Greek parsing: eblēthē (G906, ballō, V-API-3S = Aorist Passive = was cast) + basanisthēsontai (G928, basanizō, V-FPI-3P = Future Passive Indicative 3rd Pl = shall be tormented). The ONLY future-tense verb in the entire aorist chain of Rev 20:9-15. Different grammar for different subjects: humans (v.9) receive devoured (aorist); non-human/symbolic entities — devil, beast, false prophet (v.10) — receive tormented (future). Subject differentiation is grammatically encoded.

    Rev 20:12-13¶

    Notes: Proportional judgment: 'according to their works' — repeated twice. This establishes the proportionality principle in the final judgment: each person judged individually based on deeds. Implies graduated, measured punishment.

    Rev 20:14¶

    Notes: X=Y predicate nominative: 'This IS the second death.' The lake of fire is DEFINED as death vocabulary (thanatos G2288), not torment vocabulary (basanizo). Revelation's own interpretive formula identifies the lake of fire with death, not perpetual torment.

    Luke 12:47-48¶

    Notes: Graduated, proportional, finite punishment. 'Many stripes' vs. 'few stripes.' Stripes are countable and finite. This teaching implies that punishment varies in degree — incompatible with infinite equal torment for all.

    Rom 12:19¶

    Notes: Direct NT quotation of Deut 32:35. ekdikountes (G1556, Present Active Participle) with negative = do not keep avenging yourselves. ekdikēsis (G1557) = vengeance. The divine prerogative of vengeance is restated in the NT.

    2 Thess 1:7-9¶

    Notes: Greek parsing: ekdikēsin (G1557, ekdikēsis, N-ASF = vengeance, accusative). didontos ekdikēsin = 'giving/dealing out vengeance.' v.9: dikēn (G1349, dikē, N-ASF = penalty) + olethron (G3639, olethros, N-ASM = destruction) + aiōnion (G166, aiōnios, A-ASM = everlasting). NT passage explicitly linking vengeance to destruction (olethros). The vengeance IS everlasting destruction — not everlasting torment.

    Matt 10:15¶

    Notes: Degrees of judgment: 'more tolerable.' Implies graduated punishment — some fare better/worse. Proportionality.

    Matt 11:22¶

    Notes: Graduated judgment: 'more tolerable.' Same proportionality principle.

    Matt 11:24¶

    Notes: Graduated judgment applied even to Sodom — destroyed by fire, yet judgment will be MORE tolerable for them than for Capernaum.

    Heb 2:2¶

    Notes: misthapodosia (G3405) = just recompense. 'Just' (endikos G1738) = proportional. Every transgression received calibrated penalty.

    Rom 2:5-6¶

    Notes: Proportional judgment: 'according to his deeds.' Each person's punishment corresponds to their actions. Implies finite, measured judgment.

    Heb 10:30-31¶

    Notes: NT quotation of Deut 32:35-36. ekdikēsis (G1557) = vengeance. Confirms the Song of Moses template remains active in NT theology.

    Psa 30:5¶

    Notes: rega b'apo = 'a moment in his anger.' God's anger is temporal: a moment. His favor is life. Supports the pattern of temporary wrath.

    Isa 54:7-8¶

    Notes: 'A small moment' of wrath vs. 'everlasting kindness.' Wrath is temporary; mercy is permanent. The asymmetry is explicit.

    Zeph 1:15¶

    Notes: Process vocabulary: tsarah (H6869, trouble) + metsuwqah (H4691, distress). These describe the experience DURING judgment. The outcome comes in v.18.

    Zeph 1:18¶

    Notes: Outcome vocabulary: akal (H398, devoured) + kalah (H3615, 'speedy riddance'). The process (tsarah/metsuwqah, v.15) terminates in destruction (akal/kalah, v.18).

    Ezek 30:4¶

    Notes: chalchalah (H2479, great pain) occurs WHEN the slain FALL. Pain accompanies killing; it does not replace it. The pain is the process; the falling is the outcome.

    Ezek 30:9¶

    Notes: chalchalah (H2479) again — great pain. Coming upon them like the day of Egypt (destruction reference).

    Ezek 30:16¶

    Notes: chalchalah (H2479) paired with fire and tearing asunder. Pain is the experience during destruction, not a separate eternal state.

    Rev 18:7-8¶

    Notes: basanismos (G929, torment) IS Babylon's destruction process. Her torment consists of: plagues, death, mourning, famine, and being utterly burned (katakaiō G2618) with fire — all in ONE DAY. Torment is the process; complete burning is the outcome.

    Rev 18:21¶

    Notes: Five-fold 'no more at all' (ou mē heurethē eti) follows in vv.21-23. Total cessation. Babylon's torment terminates in non-existence.

    Deut 25:1-3¶

    Notes: Maximum 40 stripes — capped punishment. Exceeding would 'degrade' the brother. Proportional, finite, capped punishment is a biblical principle.

    Acts 7:24¶

    Notes: ekdikēsen (G1556, aorist) — avenged by smiting. A single lethal act. Vengeance = death, not ongoing torment.

    Luke 17:29¶

    Notes: Jesus' own interpretation of Sodom: apōlesen (G622, apollymi, aorist) = 'destroyed them all.' Jesus uses destruction vocabulary for Sodom, not torment vocabulary.

    Luke 18:3¶

    Notes: ekdikēson (G1556, aorist imperative) — avenge me. Parallel to Rev 6:10 martyrs' cry. The widow seeks vindication/justice.

    Luke 18:7-8¶

    Notes: ekdikēsis (G1557, vindication) — God will avenge His elect SPEEDILY. The vindication is portrayed as swift, not perpetual.

    Lev 26:25¶

    Notes: naqam (H5359) — sword that avenges the covenant. Vengeance as covenantal enforcement mechanism. Result: sword, pestilence, enemy conquest — lethal consequences.

    1 Thess 4:6¶
