    Word Studies¶

    Question¶

    What does the Bible teach about divine vengeance? How is God's vengeance executed across Scripture, and what is the consistent outcome? How does the martyrs' cry for vengeance (Rev 6:10) get answered (Rev 19:2), and what does the answer reveal about the nature of final judgment? Does biblical vengeance support ongoing torment or completed destruction? Additionally: What is the OT pattern for how affliction/torment vocabulary relates to destruction vocabulary?

    G1556 — ekdikeō¶

    Occurrences: 6

    Relevance: Central verb for the vindication arc: present tense in Rev 6:10 (unresolved) → aorist in Rev 19:2 (completed). All 6 occurrences are active voice. Primary Hebrew source: naqam H5358 (22 LXX co-occurrences, PMI 8.04).

    G1557 — ekdikēsis¶

    Occurrences: 9

    Relevance: The noun form of the vindication concept. Used in 2 Thess 1:8 (vengeance → destruction), Heb 10:30 (quoting Deut 32:35), Luke 18:7-8 (God will avenge speedily). Hebrew source: neqamah H5360 (19 LXX co-occurrences, PMI 26.61).

    G1558 — ekdikos¶

    Occurrences: 2

    Relevance: Adjective completing the ekdik- family. Rom 13:4: civil authority as God's avenger. 1 Thess 4:6: the Lord IS the avenger. Establishes God's role as the one who executes vengeance.

    H5358 — naqam (verb)¶

    Occurrences: 44

    Relevance: The primary Hebrew vengeance verb. Triple noqem (Qal participle) in Nah 1:2 = densest concentration in OT. Jeremiah's Hithpael formula 'shall not my soul be avenged?' (5:9, 5:29, 9:8). Maps to G1556 ekdikeō in LXX (22 co-occurrences).

    H5359 — naqam (noun)¶

    Occurrences: 17

    Relevance: Appears in the 'day of vengeance' formula (yom naqam: Isa 34:8, 61:2, 63:4). Deut 32:35,41,43: the Song of Moses vengeance template. In Deut 32:43, paired with dam (blood) = 'he will avenge the blood of his servants' — quoted in Rev 19:2.

    H5360 — neqamah¶

    Occurrences: 28

    Relevance: Feminine counterpart completing the n-q-m family. Used in Jer 51:6 (time of the LORD's vengeance), Ezek 25:12,15 (hostile human vengeance). Maps to ekdikēsis G1557 in LXX (19 co-occurrences, PMI 8.88). Combined n-q-m family: 76 OT occurrences.

    G928 — basanizō¶

    Occurrences: 12

    Relevance: Semantic range spans physical illness (Matt 8:6), toil (Matt 14:24), moral distress (2 Pet 2:8), and eschatological torment (Rev 14:10, 20:10). In judgment contexts, confined to Revelation's apocalyptic framework or demonic speech (Matt 8:29). Rev 20:10 basanisthēsontai (future passive) is the ONLY future-tense verb in the Rev 20:9-15 aorist chain.

    G929 — basanismos¶

    Occurrences: 6

    Relevance: All 6 occurrences exclusively in Revelation. Time-bounded in 4 of 6 uses. Rev 18:7,10,15: Babylon's torment IS her destruction process (in one day/one hour). Rev 14:11: smoke of torment — aftermath formula from Isa 34:10.

    H6869 — tsarah¶

    Occurrences: 79

    Relevance: Key OT process vocabulary. Nah 1:9: 'affliction (tsarah) shall not rise up the second time' — because the subjects are consumed (v.10). Zeph 1:15: 'a day of trouble (tsarah) and distress' — outcome in v.18 is devoured (akal). Tsarah describes the experience DURING judgment; destruction vocabulary describes the outcome.

    H2479 — chalchalah¶

    Occurrences: 4

    Relevance: Process vocabulary in Ezek 30:4,9,16. 'Great pain shall be in Ethiopia WHEN the slain shall fall' (Ezek 30:4) — pain accompanies killing; it does not replace it. Pain is the process; death is the outcome.

    H2342 — chuwl¶

    Occurrences: 70

    Relevance: Process vocabulary in Isa 13:8: yechilun (Qal.Impf = they shall writhe in pain) — labor-pain metaphor for judgment. The writhing (v.8) precedes the destroying (shamad, v.9). Pain is the process; destruction is the outcome.

    H3615 — kalah¶

    Occurrences: 258

    Relevance: Outcome vocabulary in Nah 1:8-9: kalah = utter end/destruction. In Zeph 1:18: 'speedy riddance' (kalah). The word means total completion/termination. When applied to judgment, it means the judgment reaches its complete end — the subjects are finished/consumed.

    H8045 — shamad¶

    Occurrences: 101

    Relevance: Outcome vocabulary in Isa 13:9: yashmid (Hiphil.Impf = he shall destroy/exterminate). Almost exclusively causative Hiphil (69 of 91 forms) — inherently transitive: destruction done TO someone. LXX maps to apollymi G622 (23 co-occurrences). The process→outcome chain: chuwl (writhe, v.8) → shamad (destroy, v.9).

    H398 — akal¶

    Occurrences: 947

    Relevance: Outcome vocabulary: Nah 1:10 (devoured as stubble), Deut 32:42 (sword shall devour flesh), Zeph 1:18 (whole land devoured). When paired with vengeance vocabulary, akal consistently describes total consumption. The sword devours — not torments.

    G622 — apollymi¶

    Occurrences: 102

    Relevance: Primary NT destruction verb. Luke 17:29: Jesus on Sodom — 'destroyed (apōlesen) them all.' The perish/save antithesis (John 3:16, 2 Pet 3:9) frames apollymi as the opposite of eternal life. Hebrew source: abad H6 (141 LXX co-occurrences). Absent from Revelation — replaced by cognate apoleia G684.

    G3639 — olethros¶

    Occurrences: 4

    Relevance: Exclusively Pauline. 2 Thess 1:9: 'everlasting destruction (olethros aiōnion)' = the penalty for those who do not know God. This verse explicitly links ekdikēsis (vengeance, v.8) with olethros (destruction, v.9). Vengeance = destruction.

    G684 — apoleia¶

    Occurrences: 20

    Relevance: The noun form of apollymi. Used for 'son of perdition' (John 17:12, 2 Thess 2:3), the beast 'goeth into perdition' (Rev 17:8,11), 'broad way that leadeth to destruction' (Matt 7:13). Names both the event and state of ruin.

    G2719 — katesthiō¶

    Occurrences: 20

    Relevance: Rev 20:9: katephagen (second aorist active indicative 3S = devoured) — fire came down and devoured them. Applied to humans at final judgment. Total, irreversible consumption. Part of the 4-aorist chain: went up, surrounded, fire came down, devoured.

    G2618 — katakaiō¶

    Occurrences: 11

    Relevance: Rev 18:8: Babylon 'shall be utterly burned (katakaiō).' Matt 3:12/Luke 3:17: chaff burned up with unquenchable fire. Jesus' interpretive verb for the tares: katakaio (Matt 13:40). The kata- prefix indicates completeness: burned DOWN, consumed.

    G2920 — krisis¶

    Occurrences: 47

    Relevance: Rev 19:2: kriseis (judgments) — 'true and righteous are his judgments.' The judicial framework for divine vengeance. In Louw-Nida domain 38.001 (Punish), krisis neighbors ekdikēsis, basanizō, dikē, kolasis.

    G3405 — misthapodosia¶

    Occurrences: 3

    Relevance: Heb 2:2: 'every transgression received a just (endikos) recompence (misthapodosia).' The word emphasizes proportionality — the recompense is precisely calibrated to the offense. Supports graduated, measured punishment.

    G1349 — dikē¶

    Occurrences: 4

    Relevance: Root of the entire dik- family (dikaios, dikaiosynē, ekdikeō, ekdikēsis, ekdikos). Jude 1:7: dikēn hypechousai = 'suffering the penalty/vengeance.' 2 Thess 1:9: dikēn tisousin olethron = 'shall pay the penalty of destruction.' Both link dikē to destruction, not ongoing torment.
