    Word Studies¶

    Question¶

    What does 2 Corinthians 5:1-8 teach about the intermediate state? Does "unclothed" (ekdysasthai) mean disembodied? What is Paul's actual preference and what does the clothing metaphor mean? Does this passage support conscious existence between death and resurrection, or is Paul expressing preference for resurrection without dying?

    G1902 — ependyomai¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1902-ependýomai.md

    Occurrences: 2

    Unique Pauline compound verb: epi (upon) + endyo (clothe). Only in 2 Cor 5:2,4. Means to put on OVER what is already worn — superimposition of the resurrection body on the mortal body without passing through death. Paul's stated preference.

    Semantic categories: eschatological transformation

    Key verses: 2 Cor 5:2, 2 Cor 5:4

    Relevance: THE decisive vocabulary item. The epi- prefix determines Paul's preference: he wants transformation (clothing over), not exchange (strip then re-clothe). This eliminates the ECT reading that Paul desires death as positive.

    G1562 — ekdyo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1562-ekdyo.md

    Occurrences: 5

    To strip off, unclothe. 5 NT verses (7 tokens). Every Synoptic use involves violent stripping: soldiers stripping Jesus (Matt 27:28,31; Mark 15:20), thieves stripping a traveler (Luke 10:30). Paul's sole use (2 Cor 5:4) is aorist middle infinitive — he explicitly says he does NOT want this.

    Semantic categories: forcible stripping, violent stripping, being unclothed (eschatological)

    Key verses: 2 Cor 5:4, Matt 27:28, Matt 27:31, Mark 15:20, Luke 10:30

    Relevance: Every other NT use involves violence against a victim. Paul explicitly negates the desire for ekdysasthai — 'we do NOT want to be unclothed.' This is the state ECT interpreters claim Paul desires.

    G1746 — endyo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1746-endyo.md

    Occurrences: 31

    Base clothing verb: to put on, to clothe. Every Pauline use describes acquisition: put on Christ (Rom 13:14, Gal 3:27), put on new man (Eph 4:24), put on armor (Eph 6:11), put on immortality (1 Cor 15:53-54). The endyo metaphor demands that the thing put on is received, not revealed.

    Semantic categories: literal clothing, metaphorical clothing — spiritual qualities, eschatological clothing — resurrection body, endowment — power

    Key verses: 1 Cor 15:53, 1 Cor 15:54, 2 Cor 5:3, Rom 13:14, Gal 3:27

    Relevance: Establishes that the clothing metaphor in Paul means acquisition/reception of something not previously possessed. Immortality is PUT ON at resurrection, not revealed as something already present.

    G1553 — ekdemeo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1553-ekdēméō.md

    Occurrences: 3

    To be away from home, to emigrate. Only in 2 Cor 5:6,8,9. From ek (out of) + demos (people/homeland). The body is the current 'home'; being ekdemeo from it means being away from one's earthly dwelling.

    Semantic categories: absence from home/body, pilgrimage/sojourning

    Key verses: 2 Cor 5:6, 2 Cor 5:8, 2 Cor 5:9

    Relevance: One of the two paired verbs central to the intermediate state debate. The word describes a state of being (absence from home), not a specific temporal moment.

    G1736 — endemeo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1736-endemeo.md

    Occurrences: 3

    To be at home, to be among one's own people. Only in 2 Cor 5:6,8,9. Direct antonym of ekdemeo. The body is the current home; the Lord is the ultimate home.

    Semantic categories: at home in the body, at home with the Lord, aspiration to please regardless of state

    Key verses: 2 Cor 5:6, 2 Cor 5:8, 2 Cor 5:9

    Relevance: Paired with ekdemeo — always appear together. The home/abroad metaphor does not specify WHEN the transition occurs. The aorist infinitive endemesai in v.8 presents the state-transition as a complete whole.

    G4636 — skenos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G4636-skenos.md

    Occurrences: 2

    Tent, temporary dwelling. Only in 2 Cor 5:1,4. Exclusively Pauline. The mortal body as a fragile, temporary hut. Contrasted with oikodome (building, G3619) — permanent structure. Related to but distinct from skene (G4633, tabernacle) and skenoma (G4638, Peter's body-tent word).

    Semantic categories: the mortal body as fragile dwelling

    Key verses: 2 Cor 5:1, 2 Cor 5:4

    Relevance: Core metaphor for the mortal body. The tent/building contrast (skenos vs. oikodome) captures the temporary/permanent, earthly/heavenly dualism of the passage.

    G2666 — katapino¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2666-katapino.md

    Occurrences: 7

    To drink down, swallow entire, devour. 7 NT verses. In 2 Cor 5:4: katapothE (V-APS-3S, aorist passive subjunctive). In 1 Cor 15:54: KatepothE (V-API-3S, aorist passive indicative). Both from Isa 25:8 (Hebrew bala H1104). Same verb, same resurrection event.

    Semantic categories: literal swallowing, eschatological victory — death/mortality swallowed up, overwhelmed by grief, destruction/drowning, predatory devouring

    Key verses: 2 Cor 5:4, 1 Cor 15:54, Isa 25:8

    Relevance: Links 2 Cor 5:4 directly to 1 Cor 15:54 and Isa 25:8. The identical vocabulary confirms the same eschatological event is in view — the resurrection, when mortality is swallowed by life/victory.

    G4727 — stenazo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G4727-stenazo.md

    Occurrences: 6

    To groan, sigh. 6 NT verses. Paul uses it in 2 Cor 5:2,4 and Rom 8:23 — all three with the same present active indicative form stenazomen (1st plural). In Romans, the groaning is for 'the redemption of our body.' This defines the groaning in 2 Cor 5 as longing for bodily resurrection.

    Semantic categories: groaning for redemption of the body, physical/emotional sigh, grief from responsibility, complaining

    Key verses: 2 Cor 5:2, 2 Cor 5:4, Rom 8:23

    Relevance: Same verb, same form, same author, same concept. The groaning is for bodily transformation/resurrection, not for death or disembodied existence.

    G728 — arrabon¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G728-arrabon.md

    Occurrences: 3

    Earnest, down payment, pledge. 3 NT occurrences: 2 Cor 1:22, 5:5, Eph 1:14. Loanword from Hebrew H6162. A commercial first installment guaranteeing full payment later. The Spirit IS the arrabon — a present foretaste guaranteeing the full inheritance (resurrection body).

    Semantic categories: Spirit as God's pledge of completed redemption, OT background: material pledge

    Key verses: 2 Cor 5:5, 2 Cor 1:22, Eph 1:14

    Relevance: The arrabon metaphor in 2 Cor 5:5 confirms the eschatological framework: the Spirit is a present guarantee of a FUTURE completion. The full payment (resurrection body) has not yet been received.

    G360 — analyo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G360-analyo.md

    Occurrences: 2

    To break up, to loosen back, to depart. 2 NT uses: Luke 12:36 (master returning from a wedding) and Phil 1:23 (Paul's desire to depart). Military/nautical background: loosing from moorings, striking camp. The cognate noun analusis (G359) appears in 2 Tim 4:6.

    Semantic categories: physical return/departure, departure from life (euphemism for death)

    Key verses: Phil 1:23, Luke 12:36

    Relevance: The departure verb in Phil 1:23. The metaphor pictures death as striking camp or unmooring — an unloosing. The aorist infinitive analysai presents departure as a complete event; the kai syn Christo einai that follows does not specify temporal relationship.

    G2349 — thnetos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2349-thnetos.md

    Occurrences: 6

    Mortal, subject to death. 6 NT occurrences, all Pauline. Used substantivally in 1 Cor 15:53-54 and 2 Cor 5:4 ('this mortal,' 'mortality'). Paired with athanasia as direct antonym. The shared vocabulary confirms both passages describe the same resurrection event.

    Semantic categories: mortal body under sin, mortal body awaiting resurrection, mortal-to-immortal transformation, life manifested in mortal flesh

    Key verses: 1 Cor 15:53, 1 Cor 15:54, 2 Cor 5:4, Rom 8:11

    Relevance: Links 2 Cor 5:4 to 1 Cor 15:53-54. The substantival use 'this mortal' in both passages identifies the same transformation event.

    G110 — athanasia¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G110-athanasia.md

    Occurrences: 3

    Immortality, deathlessness. Alpha-privative + thanatos. 3 NT uses: 1 Cor 15:53,54 (put on at resurrection), 1 Tim 6:16 (God alone has it). Immortality is not inherently possessed by humans — it must be 'put on' (endyo) at the resurrection.

    Semantic categories: eschatological transformation at resurrection, exclusive divine attribute

    Key verses: 1 Cor 15:53, 1 Cor 15:54, 1 Tim 6:16

    Relevance: The endyo+athanasia construction in 1 Cor 15:53-54 proves immortality is received at resurrection, not inherently possessed. God ALONE has immortality (1 Tim 6:16). If humans had immortal souls, this verse would be false.

    G3619 — oikodome¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G3619-oikodomē.md

    Occurrences: 18

    Building, edification. 18 NT occurrences. In 2 Cor 5:1: the permanent 'building from God' contrasted with the temporary 'tent.' Physical structure + spiritual edification.

    Semantic categories: physical structure, spiritual edification, church as building, apostolic authority for building up

    Key verses: 2 Cor 5:1, 1 Cor 3:9, Eph 2:21

    Relevance: The 'building of God' in 2 Cor 5:1 = the permanent resurrection body. The tent/building contrast captures the temporary/permanent, dissoluble/eternal duality.

    G3613 — oiketerion¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G3613-oiketerion.md

    Occurrences: 2

    Dwelling-place, habitation. Only 2 NT uses: 2 Cor 5:2 (heavenly dwelling = resurrection body) and Jude 1:6 (angels' proper habitation). From oikeo (to dwell) + -terion (place suffix). Both uses describe a divinely assigned proper dwelling.

    Semantic categories: heavenly/resurrection body, assigned spiritual domain

    Key verses: 2 Cor 5:2, Jude 1:6

    Relevance: The rare oiketerion in 2 Cor 5:2 identifies what believers groan to receive — their heavenly dwelling/habitation (the resurrection body). Its only parallel (Jude 1:6) confirms the 'proper dwelling' sense.

    G886 — acheiropoietos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G886-acheiropoietos.md

    Occurrences: 3

    Not made with hands. Alpha-privative + cheiropoietos. 3 NT uses: Mark 14:58 (temple Jesus would build), 2 Cor 5:1 (building from God), Col 2:11 (circumcision of Christ). All three describe divine vs. human origin.

    Semantic categories: divine-origin structure, spiritual transformation

    Key verses: 2 Cor 5:1, Mark 14:58, Col 2:11

    Relevance: In 2 Cor 5:1, acheiropoieton characterizes the resurrection body as divine workmanship — not of human making. The Mark 14:58 parallel links the 'body-as-temple' to the 'body-as-building' in 2 Cor 5:1.

    G2106 — eudokeo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2106-eudokeo.md

    Occurrences: 25

    To think well of, to be pleased, to be willing. 25 NT occurrences. In 2 Cor 5:8: eudokoumen (V-PAI-1P, present active indicative) = 'we are willing/pleased.' Present tense expresses ongoing disposition, not a decisive act. Distinguished from thelo (general will) by its element of considered satisfaction.

    Semantic categories: divine approval, divine sovereign pleasure, human willing/readiness, human pleasure/delight

    Key verses: 2 Cor 5:8, Mat 3:17, 1 Thess 2:8

    Relevance: In 2 Cor 5:8, eudokoumen is weaker than thelo — it means 'we are willing/content with' rather than 'we desire.' Paul is expressing willingness to accept death (if necessary) with confidence in resurrection, not eagerness for death.

    G2309 — thelo¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2309-thelo.md

    Occurrences: 211

    To will, wish, desire, prefer. 211 NT occurrences. In 2 Cor 5:4: ou thelomen (V-PAI-1P, negated present active indicative) = 'we do NOT wish/want.' Paul explicitly negates the desire for ekdysasthai (being unclothed/death).

    Semantic categories: personal desire, settled resolve, refusal (negated), divine sovereign choice

    Key verses: 2 Cor 5:4

    Relevance: The negated thelo in 2 Cor 5:4 is Paul's explicit statement of non-preference for death. If ou thelomen ekdysasthai means 'we do NOT want to be unclothed,' then Paul is NOT expressing a desire for death/disembodiment.

    G2837 — koimao¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2837-koimao.md

    Occurrences: 19

    To put to sleep, to fall asleep. 19 NT occurrences. Dominant as a euphemism for death: Acts 7:60, Acts 13:36, 1 Cor 15:6,18,20,51, 1 Thess 4:13-15. The sleep metaphor implies: (a) unconsciousness, (b) temporary state, (c) expected awakening/resurrection.

    Semantic categories: literal sleep, death as sleep (euphemism), resurrection context

    Key verses: 1 Thess 4:13-14, 1 Cor 15:6, 1 Cor 15:18, 1 Cor 15:51, Acts 7:60

    Relevance: Paul's consistent vocabulary for death is 'sleep' (koimao). Sleep implies unconsciousness. When Paul describes the dead as 'asleep,' this determines how 'absent from the body' in 2 Cor 5:8 should be understood.

    G5590 — psyche¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G5590-psyche.md

    Occurrences: 105

    Soul, life, self, person. From psycho (to breathe). 105 NT occurrences. LXX equivalent of nephesh (H5315). Semantically wide: inner self, physical life, person (counting), living creature, mind/will. Can die (Matt 10:28), be destroyed — NOT inherently immortal.

    Semantic categories: inner self/emotions, physical life, person/individual, living creature, mind/will

    Key verses: Matt 10:28, Rev 6:9

    Relevance: The souls 'under the altar' in Rev 6:9 uses psyche in the 'person/life' sense, not as a detachable immortal substance. Matt 10:28 says God can DESTROY psyche — incompatible with inherent immortality.

    G4151 — pneuma¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G4151-pneuma.md

    Occurrences: 385

    Spirit, breath, wind. From pneo (to blow). 385 NT occurrences. In 2 Cor 5:5, pneuma appears as the content of the arrabon — the Spirit IS the down payment. Pneuma in 'receive my spirit' (Acts 7:59) parallels Ecc 12:7 (spirit returns to God).

    Semantic categories: Holy Spirit, human spirit, wind/breath, demonic spirits

    Key verses: 2 Cor 5:5, Acts 7:59, 1 Thess 5:23

    Relevance: The Spirit as arrabon in 2 Cor 5:5 is the guarantee of future bodily resurrection. The spirit returning to God at death (Ecc 12:7, Acts 7:59, Luke 23:46) does not prove conscious survival.

    G166 — aionios¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G166-aionios.md

    Occurrences: 71

    Eternal, everlasting, pertaining to the age(s). In 2 Cor 5:1: aionion qualifies the building from God as 'eternal in the heavens' — permanent, divine quality contrasted with the temporary earthly tent.

    Semantic categories: eternal life, eternal punishment, eternal God/Spirit

    Key verses: 2 Cor 5:1

    Relevance: The 'eternal in the heavens' describes the resurrection body's permanence, contrasted with the dissoluble tent.

    G4983 — soma¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G4983-soma.md

    Occurrences: 146

    Body. 146 NT occurrences. Paul's primary term for the physical frame. In 2 Cor 5:6,8,10: the soma is the current dwelling from which believers are 'absent from the Lord.' In Phil 3:21: the soma tes tapeinoseos (body of humiliation) will be changed.

    Semantic categories: physical body, dead body, Christ's body, body under sin, body as temple, church as body, resurrection body

    Key verses: 2 Cor 5:6, 2 Cor 5:8, 2 Cor 5:10, Phil 3:21, 1 Cor 15:35-44

    Relevance: Paul does not view the body as a prison from which the soul escapes. The body is the arena of moral action (2 Cor 5:10) and the object of resurrection transformation (Phil 3:21).

    G861 — aphtharsia¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G861-aphtharsia.md

    Occurrences: 8

    Incorruption, imperishability. Alpha-privative + phtheiro. 8 NT uses, all Pauline. Paired with athanasia in 1 Cor 15:53-54. Something 'put on' at resurrection, not presently possessed.

    Semantic categories: resurrection transformation, object of aspiration, ethical purity

    Key verses: 1 Cor 15:42, 1 Cor 15:50, 1 Cor 15:53, 1 Cor 15:54

    Relevance: Parallel to athanasia — incorruption is received at resurrection. The corruptible/incorruptible pair in 1 Cor 15 uses the same conceptual framework as the tent/building pair in 2 Cor 5.

    G386 — anastasis¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G386-anastasis.md

    Occurrences: 40

    Resurrection, a standing up again. 40 unique verses. Paul's consistent hope — Phil 3:11 uses the compound exanastasis (G1815) for the resurrection of the dead.

    Semantic categories: bodily resurrection, first/second resurrection

    Key verses: Phil 3:11, 1 Cor 15:12-13, Acts 24:15

    Relevance: Resurrection is Paul's stated hope. If conscious intermediate existence were the norm, resurrection would be anti-climactic — merely adding a body to an already-conscious spirit.

    G2222 — zoe¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G2222-zoe.md

    Occurrences: 134

    Life. 134 NT occurrences. In 2 Cor 5:4: 'that mortality might be swallowed up of life (zoe).' Life is the agent that absorbs mortality — the resurrection life.

    Semantic categories: eternal life, present spiritual life, physical life

    Key verses: 2 Cor 5:4, John 11:25

    Relevance: In 2 Cor 5:4, zoe is the force that swallows mortality — pointing to resurrection life, not a disembodied spirit-existence.

    G1131 — gymnos¶

    Source: D:/bible/bible-studies/word-studies/G1131-gymnos.md

    Occurrences: 15

    Naked, bare. 15 NT occurrences across 9 books. Physical nakedness (Matt 25:36, Acts 19:16), partially unclothed (Mark 14:51-52, John 21:7), bare/unadorned (1 Cor 15:37 'bare grain'), exposed before God (Heb 4:13), spiritual exposure/shame (2 Cor 5:3, Rev 3:17, Rev 16:15). In 2 Cor 5:3 the 'naked' state is what Paul hopes NOT to be found in — the death-state without a resurrection body.

    Semantic categories: physical nakedness, partially unclothed, bare/unadorned, exposed before God, spiritual exposure/shame

    Key verses: 2 Cor 5:3, 1 Cor 15:37, Rev 3:17, Rev 16:15

    Relevance: In 2 Cor 5:3, gymnoi ('naked') is the undesirable state — being found without the resurrection body. In 1 Cor 15:37, gymnon ('bare') grain describes the seed sown before receiving its new body. Both uses identify the death-state-without-resurrection-body as the 'naked' condition Paul wants to avoid.
