    Bible Study: What does 2 Corinthians 5:1-8 teach about the intermediate state? Does "unclothed" (ekdysasthai) mean disembodied? What is Paul's actual preference and what does the clothing metaphor mean? Does this passage support conscious existence between death and resurrection, or is Paul expressing preference for resurrection without dying?¶

    Question¶

    What does 2 Corinthians 5:1-8 teach about the intermediate state? Does "unclothed" (ekdysasthai) mean disembodied? What is Paul's actual preference and what does the clothing metaphor mean? Does this passage support conscious existence between death and resurrection, or is Paul expressing preference for resurrection without dying?

    Prior Research Summary¶

    Eleven prior studies were consulted (see 00-references.md). The prior 2-corinthians-5-unclothed-intermediate-state study (2026-02-20) established that Paul's stated preference is ependysasthai (transformation) not ekdysasthai (death/disembodiment), and that the katapothE/thnEton vocabulary links to 1 Cor 15:54. The etc7-04-state-of-the-dead study (2026-06-03) classified E43 (2 Cor 5:8) and E44 (Phil 1:23) as Neutral after Tree 3 gate tests. The etc7-01-what-is-man study established holistic anthropology (man IS a nephesh, not HAS a soul). The etc7-02-who-has-immortality study showed endyo vocabulary demands that immortality is received at resurrection. The etc7-03-biblical-death study defined death as reversal of creation, not separation of soul from body. All prior studies agree on the subjective-instantaneity model for explaining 2 Cor 5:8. Gaps identified: deeper study of analysai (G360) in Phil 1:23, the oikia/oikodome contrast in 2 Cor 5:1, the proleptic present echomen, the textual variant in 5:3, the arrabon metaphor in 5:5, and the broader 2 Cor 4:7-5:10 literary unit.

    Primary Verses (35 — full analysis required)¶

    Core passage: 2 Corinthians 5:1-10: The primary passage under study. Retrieve with full chapter context for both chapters 4 and 5 - 2 Cor 5:1 — Core passage v.1 — earthly tent-house vs. building from God, 'eternal in the heavens' (aionios), oikia/oikodome contrast - 2 Cor 5:2 — Core passage v.2 — groaning (stenazomen), desiring to be 'clothed upon' (ependysasthai) with dwelling from heaven (oiketerion) - 2 Cor 5:3 — Core passage v.3 — 'if so be that being clothed we shall not be found naked' — textual variant endysamenoi vs. ekdysamenoi - 2 Cor 5:4 — Core passage v.4 — three-part preference: ou thelomen ekdysasthai / all ependysasthai / hina katapothE to thnEton hypo tEs zOEs — resurrection vocabulary from 1 Cor 15:54 - 2 Cor 5:5 — Core passage v.5 — God prepared us (katergasamenos), gave earnest (arrabon) of the Spirit — guarantee of future resurrection body - 2 Cor 5:6 — Core passage v.6 — confidence language, endemoumen/ekdemoumen pair — 'at home in the body, absent from the Lord' - 2 Cor 5:7 — Core passage v.7 — 'we walk by faith not by sight' — parenthetical explaining present condition - 2 Cor 5:8 — Core passage v.8 — eudokoumen/tharrountes — 'willing rather to be absent from the body and present with the Lord' — key verse for intermediate state debate, aorist infinitives - 2 Cor 5:9-10 — Immediate following context — judgment seat of Christ, 'whether present or absent we aim to please him'

    Immediate preceding context: The argument leading into the clothing metaphor — suffering, resurrection hope, temporal/eternal contrast - 2 Cor 4:14 — Immediate preceding context — 'He which raised up the Lord Jesus shall raise up us also by Jesus' — resurrection as the explicit hope - 2 Cor 4:16 — Immediate preceding context — 'outward man perish, inward man renewed day by day' — what perishing/renewal means - 2 Cor 4:17-18 — Immediate context — 'eternal weight of glory' vs. 'light affliction' — temporal/eternal contrast leading into ch.5

    Same-author resurrection vocabulary: Passages where Paul uses the identical vocabulary found in 2 Cor 5:1-8 — katapino, thnetos, endyo, stenazo - 1 Cor 15:35-44 — Same-author — natural body vs spiritual body, sown in corruption raised in incorruption — the clothing metaphor's conceptual framework - 1 Cor 15:51-54 — Same-author parallel — 'this mortal must put on (endysasthai) immortality,' 'death is swallowed up (katepothE) in victory' — identical vocabulary to 2 Cor 5:4 - Rom 8:10-11 — Same-author — Spirit as guarantee of bodily resurrection: 'shall also quicken your mortal (thneta) bodies' - Rom 8:23 — Same-author parallel — groaning (stenazomen, same verb as 2 Cor 5:2,4), waiting for 'the redemption of our body' (apolutrosin tou somatos)

    Parallel 'depart/be with Christ' passages: Other Pauline statements about death and being with Christ, with their immediate contexts - Phil 1:21-24 — Same-author parallel — 'to depart (analysai) and be with Christ, which is far better' — parallel preference expression for being with Christ after death - Phil 3:11 — Same-letter context for Phil 1:23 — Paul's hope is 'the resurrection of the dead' (exanastasin), not immediate post-death presence - Phil 3:20-21 — Same-letter context — Saviour who 'shall change our vile body' (metaschEmatisei) — transformation, not soul-release - 2 Tim 4:6-8 — Same-author — Paul's departure language: 'crown of righteousness at that day' — future reception at parousia, not at death - 1 Thess 4:13-17 — Same-author — the dead in Christ rise first, then caught up together, 'so shall we ever be with the Lord' — being with Lord placed at parousia/resurrection - 1 Thess 5:10 — Same-author — 'whether we wake or sleep, we should live together with him' — Paul envisions both possibilities (alive or dead at parousia)

    OT death-state testimony: Explicit OT statements about the unconsciousness of the dead - Ecc 9:5-6 — OT death-state — 'the dead know not any thing' — explicit statement on unconsciousness in death - Psa 146:4 — OT death-state — 'his breath goeth forth, he returneth to earth; in that very day his thoughts perish' - Psa 6:5 — OT death-state — 'in death there is no remembrance of thee' - Psa 115:17 — OT death-state — 'the dead praise not the LORD' — going down into silence - Isa 38:18-19 — OT death-state — Hezekiah: 'the grave cannot praise thee, death cannot celebrate thee' — the dead do not praise God - Job 14:12-14 — OT death-state — 'man lieth down, and riseth not: till the heavens be no more' — death as sleep until resurrection - Ecc 12:7 — OT death-state — 'the spirit shall return unto God who gave it' — reversal of Gen 2:7 creation, parallel to Stephen's prayer

    Counter-evidence passages: Passages cited in favor of conscious intermediate state — require genre/context analysis - Rev 6:9-11 — Comparison passage — 'souls under the altar' crying out — ECT-direction text for conscious dead, requires genre analysis (apocalyptic symbol) - Luke 23:43 — Comparison passage — 'today shalt thou be with me in paradise' — punctuation debate, temporal question - Acts 2:34 — NT — 'David is not ascended into the heavens' — explicit that OT saint is not in heaven consciously - Heb 11:39-40 — NT — OT faithful 'received not the promise' — they await resurrection, not already in heaven

    Additional Primary Verses: - 1 Cor 15:18 — Same-author — 'they which are fallen asleep in Christ are perished' — incompatible with conscious intermediate state (if they are already consciously with Christ, how perished?) - 1 Cor 15:19-20 — Same-author — if only in this life we have hope, 'most miserable' — resurrection as THE hope, not intermediate conscious existence
